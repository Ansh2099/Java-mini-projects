# Workflows SMBs Will Pay For: India and Europe (Sept 2026)

## The short answer

Build one product and sell it in both regions: **"Order inbox to ERP."** A customer order arrives by email (PDF, Excel, or plain text) or by WhatsApp. Today a person reads it, finds each item in the system, checks price and stock, and types a sales order. You automate the reading, the matching, the checks, and the draft order. A person approves anything odd.

Why this one wins:

1. It is a real, paid job today. UK firms hire "sales order processors" at about £26,800 a year on average ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)).
2. The tools that do it well are priced for big firms. Rossum's published Starter plan is about 18,000 USD a year, with a one year minimum ([Floowed](https://www.floowed.com/insights/rossum-pricing), [Capterra Rossum pricing](https://www.capterra.com/p/193772/Rossum/pricing/)). Conexiom is aimed at midsize and enterprise firms ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/205134/Conexiom/)). Cheap extractors like Parseur pull text out of documents, but they do not match a customer's part codes to your SKUs, check prices, or write the order ([Parseur pricing](https://parseur.com/pricing)).
3. It is the same job in Ludhiana and in Leeds. Indian distributors get orders as WhatsApp messages, photos, and voice notes, and staff retype them into Tally ([Sunray](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)). So your Indian pilot becomes a real case study for European buyers.
4. Europe's e-invoicing rules make supplier invoices structured, but they do not touch customer purchase orders. The messy PDFs stay at the order desk.

**The big warning you must hear first.** ERP makers are now building this feature in. Microsoft's Business Central has a **Sales Order Agent** that is generally available. It watches a mailbox, reads PDF and image purchase orders, finds the customer and items, checks stock, and drafts a quote or order for a person to approve ([Microsoft Learn overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent), [Microsoft Learn FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/faqs-sales-order-taker-agent)). Epicor sells its own version too. So:

1. **Do not lead with Business Central shops.** Lead with **Sage 200** (UK), then inventory apps that small wholesalers pair with Xero or QuickBooks (**Unleashed**, **Cin7 Core**), and **Exact Online** (Netherlands and Belgium). I found no built in order reading agent for these.
2. Treat Business Central as a small side market: only firms whose orders break Microsoft's agent (limits listed in the Europe section).
3. Your window is short. Sage may ship the same feature. Move in the next 6 to 12 months.

Your second product, later: **"Remittance to cash allocation"** for Sage 200 firms. A customer pays several invoices in one lump and emails a remittance PDF. Someone reads it and allocates the money invoice by invoice. RemitClear already does this for Xero and QuickBooks ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/multi-invoice-payment-matching-xero)), so you sell it only where that tool does not reach.

What I tell you not to build: supplier invoice capture, payment reminders, GST reconciliation, bank statement entry into Tally, and dealer payment allocation in Tally. Cheap or built in tools already do them. Details below.

## Assumptions

1. **"Would pay for" means the buyer gets back at least twice the year one price within a year**, in saved staff time. I apply one formula for this everywhere (see "The 2x rule" below). If the numbers do not reach 2x, I say walk away or cut the price.
2. You want the fewest products that can pay you. So I rank a narrow list, not a long menu.
3. Money conversion for rough math: 1 GBP is about 1.3 USD, 1 EUR is about 1.1 USD, 1 USD is about 88 INR. Check the day's rate before you quote.
4. **About my sources.** I opened the Microsoft Learn pages in full. Most other pages I could only see through search result summaries, because my web fetch tool was blocked for those sites. Each such link is a page I found in search and is marked in the sources list. Prices change often. Open every competitor pricing page yourself before you quote it to a client.
5. Any volume or time per task without a link is my estimate and is labeled "estimate". You measure the real number in discovery.
6. "Touches 2 to 3 software tools" means client software that holds or receives the data (inbox, WhatsApp, ERP). The pipe you use to reach a tool (a WhatsApp API provider, a Tally connector program) is not a new tool for the client's staff. Your review screen is the product itself.

## How I weighed it: three experts, one answer

I ran every idea past three views.

1. **The buyer** (an operations head at a 60 person UK wholesaler). Asks: does one person own this pain, and will the boss sign for it?
2. **The market analyst.** Asks: does a free feature or a cheap tool already do this? If yes, kill it.
3. **The delivery lead.** Asks: can two part timers build it in a few weeks, keep it running on under 50 USD a month, and not get paged every night?

Where they disagreed, and how I settled it:

| Question | Buyer said | Analyst said | Delivery lead said | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supplier invoice capture? | "Everyone hates it" | Xero includes capture free; Dext and many others; BC has a Payables Agent | Easy to build | **Reject.** Free and cheap tools own it. |
| Business Central shops for order entry? | Biggest pool, best API | Microsoft's Sales Order Agent is GA and paid per use, about 0.15 to 0.20 USD an order | Great API, but we would fight Microsoft inside its own product | **Demote to niche.** Only where the agent fails on their real orders. |
| Sage 200 shops? | Common UK mid market ERP | No built in order agent found; a few UK service firms (Zestcode) and US platforms offer it | Real API, UK partner network | **First target in Europe.** |
| Odoo shops? | Growing fast | Cheap AI "PO to quotation" apps already in the Odoo App Store | Easy API | **Reject.** Cheap add ons exist. |
| WhatsApp orders in India? | Huge volume | Order apps exist but make dealers change habits | Coexistence mode now lets the client keep the phone app | **Yes, for firms with 2 or more order clerks.** |
| Dealer payment allocation in Tally? | Real pain | TallyConnects already matches bank lines to parties and bills, pay per use | Easy to build, but little gap | **Reject.** |
| One product or several? | Offer what each client asks | Focus beats breadth | You are two part timers | **One product, two ERP connectors to start.** |

## The 2x rule (used for every price in this report)

This replaces any rule of thumb. Use it in every discovery call.

**Europe (order desk or credit control).** A UK clerk costs about £31,000 to £34,000 a year with employer costs (estimate from the £26,800 average salary above). That is about 22.5 USD an hour over 1,875 working hours. If you cut 60 percent of the work (estimate; measure it in the pilot), each minute of daily work you touch saves about 56 USD a year.

**So: take the year one price in USD (setup plus 12 months of fees) and divide by 28. That is how many "order minutes a day" the client must have for a 2x return.** Order minutes a day = orders per day times minutes per order.

| Package | Year one price | Order minutes a day needed | Orders a day at 6 min | Orders a day at 10 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small entry: 1,500 USD setup + 300 USD a month | 5,100 USD | 182 | 30 | 18 |
| Small full: 3,000 USD + 600 USD a month | 10,200 USD | 364 | 61 | 36 |
| Medium entry: 5,000 USD + 800 USD a month | 14,600 USD | 521 | 87 | 52 |
| Medium full: 7,500 USD + 1,200 USD a month | 21,900 USD | 782 | 130 | 78 |

**What this means, plainly:** price by order volume, not by company size. A 150 person firm with 40 simple orders a day only justifies the small entry package. A 40 person firm with 120 messy orders a day justifies the medium package.

**India.** An order clerk in Ludhiana earns about ₹14,000 to ₹25,000 a month (see India section). Take ₹18,000 as the middle, about ₹2.16 lakh a year. Cut 60 percent of the desk's work (estimate). For 2x, year one cost (your fees plus the WhatsApp provider) must be at most **30 percent of the desk's yearly salary cost**.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ₹2.2 lakh | ₹65,000 | Free or near free pilot only |
| 2 | ₹4.3 lakh | ₹1.3 lakh | Small: ₹25,000 to ₹40,000 setup + ₹3,000 to ₹5,000 a month |
| 3 | ₹6.5 lakh | ₹1.9 lakh | Small full: up to ₹60,000 setup + ₹8,000 a month |
| 4 to 5 | ₹8.6 to ₹10.8 lakh | ₹2.6 to ₹3.2 lakh | Medium: ₹60,000 to ₹1,00,000 setup + ₹8,000 to ₹12,000 a month |
| 6 or more | ₹13 lakh and up | ₹3.9 lakh and up | Medium full: up to ₹1,50,000 setup + ₹15,000 a month |

## Region 1: India

### The honest picture first

Indian labor is cheap. Order processing, billing, and coordinator jobs in Ludhiana pay about ₹14,000 to ₹20,000 a month at entry level and about ₹25,000 for experienced billing executives, based on job listings I found in search ([Indeed Ludhiana order processing](https://in.indeed.com/q-order-processing-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html), [PlacementIndia billing executive Ludhiana](https://www.placementindia.com/job-search/billing-executive-jobs-in-ludhiana.htm), [Glassdoor Ludhiana coordinator](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/ludhiana-coordinator-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,8_IC2939907_KO9,20.htm), [Indeed Ludhiana billing](https://in.indeed.com/q-billing-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)). A data entry operator in Punjab earns about ₹9,000 to ₹20,000 ([Glassdoor Punjab](https://www.glassdoor.com/Job/punjab-data-entry-operator-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,6_IS4951_KO7,26.htm)). So in India you do not win on salary alone. You win when:

1. Volume already needs two or more clerks, or jumps in season.
2. Mistakes cost real money: wrong item shipped, missed order, return freight.
3. Speed matters: same day dispatch, dealer waiting for confirmation.
4. The owner wants to stop depending on one key person who "knows all the codes."

Treat most Indian work as proof of work. Charge something, even a small amount, because a paying client makes a stronger case study.

### India workflow 1: WhatsApp orders into Tally or Busy (recommended)

**a. Who has it.** Distributors and wholesalers (hardware, auto parts, electricals, FMCG, pharma distribution, paints), and manufacturers who sell to dealers. Ludhiana is full of these: auto parts, hand tools, bicycle parts, hosiery. Fits **medium** best (50 to 250 staff, a real order desk with 4 or more clerks). **Small** firms (5 to 49) fit only if they have 2 or more people typing orders; see the 2x table.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A dealer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp: text, a photo of a handwritten list, a voice note, or an Excel file. Some come by email.
2. An order clerk reads it and works out which item the dealer means ("10 box 6mm bolt" means which stock item?).
3. The clerk checks stock and the dealer's rate, and asks back on WhatsApp when something is unclear.
4. The clerk types a sales order or sales invoice in Tally or Busy.
5. The clerk tells dispatch and replies to the dealer.

WhatsApp is the order channel for most Indian distributors, and orders arrive as voice notes, photos, and casual messages that get retyped ([Sunray](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/what-is-whatsapp-order-management), [CampaignHQ](https://blog.campaignhq.co/whatsapp-for-manufacturing-india)).

**c. Software on each side.** In: WhatsApp Business (and Gmail if over 20 percent of orders come by email). Out: TallyPrime (most common), Busy, Marg (pharma), sometimes Zoho Books. That is 2 client tools, or 3 with email. Start with 2.

TallyPrime can take orders by API. It runs a small HTTP server on port 9000 on the client's PC and accepts XML to create vouchers ([Tally XML integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/), [TallyPrime API notes on GitHub](https://github.com/NoumaanAhamed/tally-prime-api-docs/blob/main/index.md)). TallyPrime 7.0, released December 2025, added JSON data exchange too ([Tally release notes 7.0](https://help.tallysolutions.com/release-notes-tallyprime-7-0/)). The catch: Tally lives on a desktop in the client's office. You install a small connector program on that PC that pulls approved orders from your cloud and pushes them into Tally. Developer B can write it in Node. The client's staff never touch it.

**WhatsApp access: easier than it used to be.** Meta's **coexistence** mode lets a business link its existing WhatsApp Business app number to the Cloud API. The clerk keeps using the phone app and chat history as today, and your system receives the same messages through the API. Search sources report it live in India and globally by mid 2026 ([Meta developer docs on onboarding app users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users/), [360dialog coexistence docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/onboarding/whatsapp-coexistence), [Chakra](https://chakrahq.com/article/whatsapp-coexistence-live-eu-uk-europe-whatsapp-business-for-api-live/), [Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence)). Limits reported in those sources: someone must open the app on the phone at least once every 14 days or the API link pauses, and the number must not already be on another API account. You still sign up through a provider that supports coexistence (for example 360dialog, Interakt, AiSensy, Gupshup, WATI). Budget roughly ₹1,000 to ₹3,000 a month for the provider (estimate; check current plans and Meta's per message rules). Test coexistence on the pilot client's number in week one.

**d. How often, how long.** Estimate: a medium distributor gets 50 to 300 orders a day, 2 to 8 minutes each, more for photos and voice notes. Measure it in discovery.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Order clerks, billing executives, or sales coordinators at about ₹14,000 to ₹25,000 a month each (sources above). A 4 clerk desk costs about ₹8 to ₹10 lakh a year in salary.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Order taking apps (for example [RetailCore](https://www.retailcore.in/sales-order/mobile-ordering-system-for-distributors-and-traders-in-india)) and WhatsApp catalogue bots ([Fast2SMS guide](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)) exist. Their gap: they make the dealer change habits, use an app or tap through a bot menu. In my judgment many small dealers will not do that; ask in discovery how many dealers use the firm's app today. Your version lets dealers keep sending free text, photos, and voice notes, and does the understanding on your side. That fits your rule that the process must already be part digital.

**g. Realistic price.** Use the India 2x table above. Short version: 2 to 3 clerks, ₹25,000 to ₹60,000 setup plus ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 a month. 4 or more clerks, ₹60,000 to ₹1,50,000 setup plus ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 a month. One clerk: walk away, or do it as a free pilot for the case study.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

## Region 2: Europe and the UK

### Context that shapes everything

1. **Supplier invoices are becoming structured data.** Belgium made B2B e-invoicing over Peppol mandatory from 1 January 2026. Poland's KSeF became mandatory for large firms in February 2026 and most others in April 2026. France requires every business to be able to receive e-invoices from 1 September 2026. Germany requires issuing for firms above 800,000 EUR from 1 January 2027 ([Finbite](https://finbite.eu/en/e-invoicing-mandates-europe-2026/), [OpenText](https://blogs.opentext.com/e-invoicing-europe-2026-lessons-from-poland-belgium-greece-france-and-germany/)). Invoice OCR is a shrinking market. Purchase orders and remittances are not covered, so they stay as PDFs and emails.
2. **Xero includes invoice and receipt capture free** in its plans ([Xero](https://www.xero.com/us/accounting-software/capture-data-with-hubdoc/), [Xero Central](https://central.xero.com/0/article/Hubdoc-in-Xero)). Do not compete with free.
3. **ERP makers are adding AI agents.** Business Central has a generally available Sales Order Agent and a Payables Agent, both paid per use in Copilot Credits ([Microsoft Learn billing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/administration/tenant-admin-center-manage-consumption-billing)). Epicor sells order entry automation. Sage's 2026 agent announcements cover Intacct, X3 and HR, not Sage 200 order entry, from what I found ([Sage April 2026 news](https://www.sage.com/en-gb/company/digital-newsroom/2026/04/sage-expands-ai-agents-across-finance-hr-and-operations-to-automate-workflows/)). Pick ERPs where the maker has not shipped this yet.

### Europe workflow 1: Emailed purchase orders into sales orders (top pick)

**a. Who has it.** Wholesale distributors (building supplies, industrial parts, food service, packaging, electrical, medical consumables), manufacturers who sell to trade customers, and 3PLs that take client orders by email. **Medium** fits best. **Small** firms fit if they have enough order minutes a day (see the 2x table; about 30 orders a day at 6 minutes for the small entry package).

**b. Steps today.**
1. A customer emails a PO: a PDF from their ERP, an Excel file, or lines typed in the email.
2. An order processor opens it, maps the customer's part numbers to their own SKUs, checks price against the agreed price list, checks stock and delivery date.
3. They type the sales order into the ERP.
4. They email an order confirmation and chase anything wrong (old price, unknown part, missing delivery address).

Vendors in this space describe the same flow: read the email and attachment, extract lines, match to the item master, and queue an ERP ready order for human review ([Y Meadows](https://ymeadows.com/en/insights/blog/ai-order-entry-for-distributors/), [Logistify](https://www.logistify.ai/blog/ai-order-entry-software), [Lido](https://www.lido.app/blog/best-sales-order-automation-software)). Job ads for the role list exactly this work: checking orders, preparing order documents, working in an ERP and a shared inbox ([Prime Appointments](https://prime-appointments.co.uk/sales-order-processor), [Indeed UK jobs](https://uk.indeed.com/q-sales-order-processor-jobs.html)).

**c. Software on each side.** In: Outlook or Microsoft 365 (most common in UK and EU B2B), Gmail. Out: one ERP or inventory app. That is 2 client tools. Target order, with API status:

**What Microsoft's agent does, and where it stops.** From Microsoft's own docs, the Sales Order Agent: reads a Microsoft 365 mailbox; reads PDF and image attachments up to 50 pages; finds the customer by the sender's email; matches items by item number, vendor item number, GTIN, item references, attributes, and fuzzy search; checks stock and can promise dates; and drafts a quote that a person approves before anything goes out ([overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent), [FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/faqs-sales-order-taker-agent)). It costs about 14 to 19 Copilot Credits per order by Microsoft's billing examples ([Microsoft Learn billing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/administration/tenant-admin-center-manage-consumption-billing)). Copilot Credits are about 0.01 USD each pay as you go (found in search: [Azure Copilot Studio pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/copilot-studio/), [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/copilot-studio-pricing/)). That is roughly 0.15 to 0.20 USD an order. You cannot beat that on price.

Its documented limits, which are the only places you can sell to a BC shop:
1. Up to 15 item lines per document; more may lower quality.
2. It does not fill the item Variant Code.
3. It reads only a Microsoft Exchange shared inbox.
4. It finds customers only by an exact match on the sender's email. Forwarded orders and portal emails need a person.
5. It only handles item lines, not charges or resources.
6. Partners cannot change its instructions.
7. The docs do not say it checks the price on the customer's PO against your agreed price. Test that.

**Rule for BC prospects:** before you pitch, ask them to switch on the agent (or let their partner do it) and run 50 real orders through a sandbox. If it handles most of them, walk away. Sell only if their orders are long (wholesale orders of 30 to 100 lines), use variants, or arrive in ways the agent cannot read.

**d. How often, how long.** Estimate: 30 to 300 emailed orders a day at a 20 to 150 person distributor; 3 to 15 minutes each depending on the number of lines and how clean the PDF is. One vendor claims order reps spend up to 40 percent of their time on data entry ([Y Meadows](https://ymeadows.com/en/insights/blog/ai-order-entry-for-distributors/)); treat vendor numbers as marketing until you measure.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Sales order processors or customer service reps. UK average about £26,800 a year, typical range £26,000 to £29,000 ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)). With employer costs, call it £31,000 to £34,000 per person (estimate). Example: 100 orders a day at 6 minutes each is 10 hours a day, about 1.3 people at 7.5 hours a day, or roughly £40,000 to £45,000 a year.

### Europe workflow 2: Remittance advice into cash allocation (second product)

**a. Who has it.** B2B firms that invoice many trade customers: wholesalers, manufacturers, logistics firms, contract cleaners, staffing firms. **Medium** mostly; small firms rarely have enough volume.

**b. Steps today.** A customer pays one amount for many invoices and emails a remittance PDF. A credit controller opens it, reads each invoice number, finds it in the ledger, checks the amount, and allocates the payment line by line. Short payments and deductions go to a query list. Xero has no built in way to read a remittance PDF and split a lump payment across invoices ([RemitClear on Xero](https://remitclear.com/blog/how-to-reconcile-remittance-advice-in-xero), [RemitClear multi invoice](https://remitclear.com/solutions/multi-invoice-payment-matching-xero)).

**c. Software.** In: Outlook inbox (the remittance). Out: Sage 200. That is 2 tools; the bank line is already in Sage.

**d. How often, how long.** Estimate: 20 to 200 remittances a week, 5 to 20 minutes for multi invoice ones. RemitClear says its cheaper tool pays off above about 10 to 20 remittances a week ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/multi-invoice-payment-matching-xero)).

**e. Who does it.** Credit controllers or sales ledger clerks at about £27,000 to £31,000 a year outside London, £36,750 to £45,000 in London ([Robert Walters](https://www.robertwalters.co.uk/our-services/salary-survey/credit-controller-salaries.html), [Robert Half](https://www.roberthalf.com/gb/en/job-details/credit-controller)). Remittance chasing and cash allocation are listed duties.

**f. Existing tools and gap.**
1. RemitClear covers Xero and QuickBooks ([RemitClear pricing](https://remitclear.com/pricing)). Do not sell to those firms.
2. Business Central has payment application rules plus a Copilot bank reconciliation assist that can match one lump sum bank line to several invoices from dates, amounts and descriptions ([Microsoft Learn bank reconciliation with Copilot](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/bank-reconciliation-with-copilot)). It works from bank text, not the remittance PDF, but that shrinks the gap. **Skip BC here too.**
3. Sage 200: I found no cheap equivalent. Verify with two Sage 200 resellers before you build.

**g. Price, with the 2x rule.** At about 22.5 USD an hour and 60 percent saved (estimates), the year one price must be at most about 6.75 USD per hour of remittance work a year. So:
1. 1,500 USD setup + 250 USD a month (4,500 USD year one) needs about 13 hours a week of remittance work, about 55 multi invoice remittances a week at 15 minutes.
2. 3,000 USD setup + 500 USD a month (9,000 USD year one) needs about 27 hours a week, about 107 a week at 15 minutes.

Below about 50 a week, it does not pay 2x. Say so.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

**Rules check:** you propose allocations, a person approves. You never move money.

## Workflows in both regions (your case study bridge)

| Workflow | India version | Europe version | How strong is the bridge? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Order inbox to ERP** | WhatsApp orders into Tally or Busy | Emailed PDF and Excel POs into Sage 200, Unleashed or Cin7 Core | **Strong.** Same hard part: understand the order, match items to SKUs, check price, send exceptions to a person. The numbers you measure in Ludhiana (orders a day, minutes saved, first pass match rate, errors caught) are exactly what a UK operations head asks for. |
| **Payment to allocation** | Not worth building (TallyConnects covers it) | Remittance PDFs allocated in Sage 200 | **Weak.** No Indian twin. Sell Pick 2 on European evidence and on your order desk relationship with the client. |

How to write the case study so a European buyer trusts it: show before and after numbers, the first pass match rate, the share of orders a person still had to fix, and a short screen video of the review screen. Do not hide that it was an Indian distributor. Say "Punjab based auto parts distributor, 140 orders a day, Tally." A UK buyer cares that the matching works on messy real orders, and WhatsApp photos and voice notes are messier than UK PDFs. That is a selling point. Add one European pilot as soon as you can, because some buyers will still want a local reference.

## Pricing in Europe: your guess, corrected

Your guess: 1,500 to 4,000 USD for a first workflow, 400 to 1,500 USD a month.

| Buyer (by order minutes a day, not headcount) | Setup | Monthly | Year one | Needs at least |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low volume (about 30 to 60 orders a day at 6 min) | 1,500 to 3,000 USD | 300 to 600 USD | 5,100 to 10,200 USD | 182 to 364 order minutes a day |
| Mid volume (about 60 to 90 a day) | 3,000 to 5,000 USD | 600 to 800 USD | 10,200 to 14,600 USD | 364 to 521 |
| High volume (about 130 a day or more), after 2 case studies | 5,000 to 7,500 USD | 800 to 1,200 USD | 14,600 to 21,900 USD | 521 to 782 |

My corrections:

1. **Setup:** your 1,500 to 4,000 USD is right for first clients. Go up to 7,500 USD only for high volume clients once you have proof.
2. **Monthly:** 1,500 USD a month is too high for one workflow at this size. It fails the 2x rule unless the client has about 115 to 130 orders a day at 6 minutes. 300 to 1,200 USD is realistic. Justify the fee with things a tool does not give: you fix broken matches, update rules when a customer changes their PO layout, and report the numbers each month.
3. **The math that sells it:** 100 orders a day at 6 minutes is 600 order minutes. That saves about 33,700 USD a year at 60 percent. The mid package at 14,600 USD year one gives about 2.3 times back. That is a real yes.
4. **Charge LLM and API costs at cost** to the client, or have them use their own keys. This keeps you under your 50 USD a month budget.

## Final top picks

### Pick 1: Order inbox to ERP. Do this now.

**Reasons:** a paid role exists in both regions; enterprise tools are too expensive and cheap tools stop at extraction; one owner (order desk lead or customer service manager); 2 client tools (inbox or WhatsApp, plus the ERP); easy to count (orders a day, minutes per order); a person approves every exception.

**Who to target first:**
1. India pilot: a Ludhiana distributor or manufacturer with 2 or more order clerks on TallyPrime, taking orders on WhatsApp. Connect with coexistence. Charge ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 for the pilot. 4 to 6 weeks.
2. Europe: UK distributors with 20 to 150 staff on **Sage 200** first, then wholesalers on **Unleashed** or **Cin7 Core**. Two connectors only for the first year. Qualify every lead with the 2x rule.
3. Business Central: only when a prospect has already tried Microsoft's agent and it failed on their orders.

**What you build (split to your skills):**
1. Developer A (Spring Boot, PostgreSQL, Spring AI): inbox reader (Microsoft Graph for Outlook, Gmail API), LLM extraction into a fixed order schema, item matching (customer part number table, then fuzzy text, then vector search with pgvector), rule checks (price against price list, minimum quantity, unknown item, credit hold), ERP writers for Sage 200 and Unleashed or Cin7 Core. Long orders (30 lines and up) are your edge, so test on them.
2. Developer B (Node, TypeScript, n8n): review screen where the order desk approves or fixes each order, the confirmation email draft, the Tally desktop connector and WhatsApp webhook for India, and glue flows in n8n.
3. Hosting: one small EU region server running n8n, Postgres and the Spring app. Estimate about 10 to 25 USD a month. EU buyers will ask where data sits and want a data processing agreement under GDPR.

**How to sell in Europe from Ludhiana:** go through **Sage 200 resellers** and **Unleashed and Cin7 partners**. They have the clients and often lack AI builders. Offer a referral fee or white label. Do not use Business Central partners: they are the people switching on Microsoft's agent. The trade off: you give up 15 to 30 percent of revenue and some client contact, but you skip cold outreach, which is slow for an unknown two person team in India.

### Pick 2: Remittance to cash allocation. Add after 2 paying order clients.

**Reasons:** same buyers, same ERP, same tech (read PDF, match to open items, propose, approve). You sell it as a second workflow to a client who already trusts you, which is the cheapest sale you will make. **Only for Sage 200 users with about 50 or more multi invoice remittances a week.** RemitClear covers Xero and QuickBooks, and Business Central's Copilot matching covers much of it there.

### Trade offs you are accepting

| Choice | What you get | What you give up |
|---|---|---|
| One product, not a menu | Faster build, sharper case studies, reusable code | Clients who want something else. You will say no a lot. |
| Distributors and manufacturers only | Buyers who all speak the same language (SKUs, POs, price lists) | Services firms, clinics, agencies, and accountants |
| Sage 200, Unleashed, Cin7 Core first | No built in agent to fight, real APIs | The biggest pool (Business Central), and the best documented API |
| Pricing by volume with a 2x rule | Deals the buyer can justify | Many small leads; you will walk away from firms with few orders |
| India as pilot ground | Fast, cheap proof on messy real data | Little Indian income, and only one Indian workflow |
| Selling through Sage and inventory app partners | Warm leads | 15 to 30 percent of revenue and some control |

### What could make me wrong

1. **Sage ships its own order agent for Sage 200**, as Microsoft and Epicor did. Watch Sage's product news every quarter. If it happens, move to Unleashed, Cin7 Core and Exact Online, and to long or messy orders the built in agents fail on.
2. AI order entry platforms (Y Meadows, Logistify, Lido, WizCommerce) drop to small business prices. Check their pricing every quarter.
3. Microsoft lifts the agent's limits (more than 15 lines, variants, other inboxes). Then the BC niche disappears. That does not change Pick 1, because BC is no longer your main market.
4. Coexistence has limits I could not test (for example on voice notes or groups). Test it on the pilot number in week one.
5. The 60 percent time saving is my estimate. If your pilot shows 40 percent, every threshold in the 2x tables goes up by half. Measure it and reprice.

## Discovery call: measure before you quote

Ask these in the first call:

1. How many orders (or remittances) a day, and in the busiest week?
2. How many minutes does one order take, start to finish? Time five of them live.
3. How many people touch them, and for how many hours a day?
4. What share come as PDF, Excel, email text, WhatsApp, or photos? How many lines per order?
5. Do customers use your SKUs or their own part numbers? Do you use item variants?
6. How many orders last month had an error (wrong item, wrong price, missed)? What did one error cost?
7. Which ERP and version? Cloud or on premise? Who holds admin access? If Business Central: have you tried the Sales Order Agent?
8. Who signs off on spending, and who owns the order desk?

**Walk away rule, matching the prices above.** Multiply orders a day by minutes per order. Divide your planned year one price in USD by 28. If the first number is smaller, cut the package or walk away. In India, if the desk has one clerk, walk away or run a free pilot. If it has 2 or 3, quote only the small package.
