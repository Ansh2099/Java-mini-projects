ATTACK 1 [FATAL] Top pick targets Business Central first, but Microsoft already ships a Sales Order Agent in Business Central that does this exact workflow
Where: Final top picks, Pick 1: "Europe: UK and Irish distributors with 20 to 150 staff on **Business Central** first (best API, largest pool)" and "How to sell in Europe from Ludhiana: go through Business Central and Sage partners (resellers)". Also "What could make me wrong" item 2: "Business Central and Sage may ship their own order reading features, like Epicor already did. If they do, move to the remittance workflow".
Problem: This has already happened, and the report never mentions it. Business Central has a "Sales Order Agent" that watches a mailbox, reads customer order requests, finds the customer, checks inventory, asks the customer about missing details by email, and creates a sales quote that a person reviews and converts to an order. It launched in preview in 2025 release wave 1 (version 26) in the US and UK and is now listed as generally available. It is included in the BC subscription and billed per use through Copilot credits (sources: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent , https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent-process , https://erpsoftwareblog.com/2026/06/business-central-sales-order-agent/ , https://yzhums.com/68484/). By the report's own rule (it tells the user to "Skip Epicor shops" because Epicor has built in order automation, and to pivot if BC ships the same), the first target market of Pick 1 is already disqualified. Worse, the chosen sales channel is BC resellers, who are the people rolling out Microsoft's agent. Concrete scenario: the team pitches a 60 person Leeds distributor on BC through a BC partner; the partner answers "we are turning on Sales Order Agent for them next month." The report's Europe competitor table ("Existing tools and the gap") lists Conexiom, Rossum, Parseur, Epicor and startups, but omits the one competitor that is free to switch on inside the recommended ERP. That breaks the "reject if a built in feature already solves it" check the task demands for the headline recommendation. The pick may survive only for Sage 200 or for cases the agent handles badly (customer part numbers, messy multi page PDFs), but the report does not say that and does not tell the user to test it.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The walk away threshold contradicts the report's own 2x payback rule and its own prices
Where: Assumptions item 1: "'Would pay for' means the buyer gets back at least twice what they pay within a year ... If the math does not show that, I rejected the idea." Discovery section: "If the answers show fewer than about 30 orders a day in Europe, or only one clerk in India, the savings will not cover your price. Say so and walk away."
Problem: Run the report's own numbers at 30 orders a day. 30 orders x 6 minutes = 3 hours a day = 0.4 of a 7.5 hour person. At the report's £31,000 to £34,000 loaded cost that is £12,400 to £13,600 a year. Cutting 60 percent saves £7,400 to £8,200, about 9,700 to 10,600 USD. The report's own medium price example (5,000 USD setup + 800 USD x 12) is 14,600 USD in year one, a 0.7x return. Even its cheapest medium row (3,000 + 600 x 12 = 10,200 USD) gives about 1x. To hit the promised 2x at 14,600 USD you need about 29,200 USD of savings, which by the same math is roughly 85 to 90 orders a day, three times the stated cutoff. India has the same bug: "2 or more order clerks" at ₹12,000 to ₹25,000 each is ₹2.9 to 6 lakh a year; 60 percent is ₹1.7 to 3.6 lakh, against a medium year one cost of ₹1.3 to 3.3 lakh (₹60,000 to ₹1,50,000 setup + ₹6,000 to ₹15,000 x 12) plus the ₹12,000 to ₹36,000 WhatsApp provider fee. A 2 clerk firm at the low salary end gets about 1.2x, not 2x. The user will qualify prospects with this 30 order / 2 clerk rule and quote deals the report itself says buyers will not sign.
