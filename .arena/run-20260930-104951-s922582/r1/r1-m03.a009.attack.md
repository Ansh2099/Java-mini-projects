ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Pick 2's "gap" on Business Central is false: a common BC add-on already matches supplier order confirmations to POs
Where: Europe workflow 2, row f: "Gap: 50 to 250 person firms on Business Central, Odoo or Sage that will not buy an enterprise document platform." Row c: "I found no built in Business Central agent for this job." Pick 2: "The competition sells mostly to SAP customers."
Problem: Continia Document Capture, one of the most widely installed Business Central add-ons, has a Purchase Order document category (PURCHORDER) that reads a vendor's order confirmation and creates or updates the BC purchase order. It updates Vendor Order No., Promised Receipt Date, Quantity and Direct Unit Cost, and shows price, cost and delivery date changes on a matching page ([Continia docs: Purchase Order category](https://docs.continia.com/en-us/continia-document-capture/business-functionality/document-categories/the-purchase-order-category/), [Continia Document Capture](https://www.continia.com/solutions/document-capture/)). That is the exact job in row b ("matches each line to the PO... flags differences... writes approved dates back to the ERP"), sold inside BC, the first ERP the answer names as the gap. Checking for a built in agent only and not the BC AppSource add-ons does not meet the task's rule: "Whether existing software already solves it. If it is already solved by a built in feature or a cheap tool, reject it." BC firms need to be taken out of the Pick 2 target list, and the claim that competitors "mostly target SAP" is wrong for BC.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] Pick 1's "gap" ERPs (Sage 200, Odoo) already have ready order to ERP products that the answer never checked
Where: Europe workflow 1, row f: "Your gap: UK and EU SMEs on Sage 200, Xero plus inventory apps, Odoo or Exact; firms whose customers send Excel orders; firms with customer specific codes and price rules." Pick 1: "choosing clients on Sage 200 ... Odoo or Exact ... owning the customer specific rules (codes, prices, delivery rules), which built in agents handle poorly."
Problem: The competitor check covers only Conexiom, OrderDrafter (US, Epicor), Parseur and the BC agent. It skips products built for the very ERPs it calls the gap. For Sage 200, Zestcode sells Sage 200 sales order automation that reads "PDF, CSV, Excel, Word and email-body orders", matches "each line to your Sage 200 stock items" and applies "the price band, customer price or discount Sage holds, validated line by line" ([Zestcode](https://zestcode.co.uk/services/sage-200-order-automation/)). That covers all three "gap" features: Sage 200, Excel orders, and customer price rules. For Odoo, the Odoo Apps Store sells an AI Sales Order OCR module that turns a customer PO into a draft sale order ([Odoo Apps](https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/jjro_ai_sales_capture)), and LevelOps offers "PDF to Order for Odoo, POs in, sales orders out" by email ([LevelOps](https://www.levelops.co/odoo-levelops-pdf-to-order)). The answer even cites Document Genetics, a UK vendor that sells this exact product, but only as a volume source, never as a competitor. So the "gap" that Pick 1 depends on is claimed without the check the task requires, and part of it is false. The user would walk into Sage 200 and Odoo sales calls against cheaper packaged tools they were never warned about.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The recommended first India build breaks the 50 USD a month budget and costs more to run than the price charged, by the answer's own numbers
Where: Assumption 4: "LLM calls (estimate: 1 to 5 US cents per order document). This holds until you have several busy clients." India workflow 1, row d: "30 to 300 orders a day for a distributor." Row g: "₹3,000 to ₹8,000 a month ... charge little or nothing" to the first clients. 30 day plan step 1: "Offer the build free or at cost."
Problem: The task says: "Our budget is under 50 USD a month until we have revenue." Take ONE distributor at 300 orders a day: 9,000 documents a month times 1 to 5 cents is 90 to 450 USD a month in LLM calls alone. Even a middle case (100 orders a day at 3 cents) is 90 USD a month. That is over budget with the very first client, not "several busy" ones, and that first client is paying "little or nothing." At full price, ₹8,000 is about 91 USD at the answer's own ₹88 rate, so the top of the India price range does not cover the top of its own cost estimate. Voice notes and handwritten photos, which the answer says the product must read, cost more per order than plain text. The answer never flags this or gives a fix (a cheaper model, a daily order cap for free pilots, or pricing per order).
