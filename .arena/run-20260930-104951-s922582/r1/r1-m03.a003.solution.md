# Workflows Worth Selling: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

1. **Build one thing first: "customer order arrives by email or WhatsApp, and it becomes a checked sales order in the client's system."** It has the clearest proof of paid pain (a full time salary spent on typing), a clean human approval step, and it is the same job in India and Europe. That gives you a real case study.
2. **Be clear eyed: this is a crowded, proven market, not an empty gap.** Almost every common ERP already has a tool or an agency selling this. Business Central has a built in agent. Sage 200, Odoo, Exact Online and Unleashed all have named sellers. You will win on price, fast setup and handling each customer's messy rules, not on being the only option. That is still the best bet I found, because every other workflow I checked is either more crowded or has weaker proof of paid demand.
3. **Your second product, sold only to your first clients: "supplier order confirmation arrives, it is checked against our purchase order, and missing ones are chased."** It reuses most of the same code. Checking confirmations is already sold for Business Central and Odoo, so the part worth selling is the chasing and tracking of late suppliers.
4. **Stay away from invoice entry, bank entry, GST reconciliation, payment reminders and supplier statement matching.** Cheap tools and built in features already do these.
5. **Your Europe price guess needs one correction.** 1,500 to 4,000 USD is right only as an intro price for your first 2 or 3 clients. After that, list at 5,000 to 8,000 USD. A Dutch agency publishes order intake builds from EUR 8,500. Upkeep is 400 to 800 USD a month. 1,500 a month is too high except for heavy volume.
6. **The hardest part is not the code. It is getting the first European client to trust a two person team in Ludhiana.** An Indian case study with real numbers, plus your Finland client, is your way in.

## How to read this

* **Confidence labels.** Every workflow has a confidence level: High, Medium or Low. It says how sure I am that businesses will pay you for it.
* **"Estimate"** means I worked the number out myself. It is not from a source. Check it on your first 5 sales calls.
* **Sources.** All links were found through web search on 30 September 2026. Many vendor pages could be seen only as search results, not opened in full, so I treat vendor numbers as vendor claims.
* **Money.** I use about ₹88 per USD, about 1.33 USD per GBP and about 1.15 USD per EUR. Check the rate on the day you quote.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK, the EU, Norway and Switzerland. The UK, the Netherlands, German speaking countries and the Nordics are the best targets because they buy software, answer email in English and pay on time.
2. "Build in a few weeks part time" means about 60 to 120 hours for the first client, and about half that for the second client with the same workflow.
3. You run the core pipeline in your own code (Java or Node). Use n8n only for glue, if at all. Check n8n's Sustainable Use License before you host it for clients.
4. **Running costs, checked against your 50 USD a month budget:**
   * Server: about 5 to 20 USD a month.
   * AI model: use a small, cheap model for most orders, such as Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite at 0.10 USD per million input tokens and 0.40 USD per million output tokens ([Google pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). An order with item list lookups is about 3,000 tokens in and 600 out. That is about 0.05 cents. Photos and voice notes cost more (estimate: up to 0.2 cents). Send only the hard 10% to a stronger model. Blended estimate: about 0.1 to 0.2 cents per order.
   * WhatsApp: from 1 October 2026, outgoing service messages in India cost about 0.0014 USD each after the first 1,000 a month ([Wati pricing guide](https://www.wati.io/en/blog/whatsapp-api-pricing-guide/), [Pepper Cloud](https://blog.peppercloud.com/whatsapp-api-pricing-everything-you-need-to-know/)).
   * **Worked example, one big distributor at 300 orders a day (9,000 a month):** about 9 to 18 USD for the model, about 11 USD for WhatsApp confirmations, plus the server. Total: about 30 to 50 USD. That fits, but only just.
   * **Three rules keep you safe:** (1) cap every free pilot at 100 orders a day; (2) paying clients pay model and WhatsApp costs at cost, on top of your fee; (3) set India monthly prices by volume band (below) so the fee always covers running costs many times over.

## Part 1: India

**The honest picture.** Indian owners will pay, but little, and only when the gain is obvious. A fresher Tally operator costs about ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 a month ([PW Skills](https://pwskills.com/blog/tally/tally-operator-salary-work-job-for-freshers)). Your yearly price must be well below one salary, or the owner will just hire one more person. Cheap Indian SaaS products already cover the most common jobs. **Bottom line: India has one well evidenced workflow for you, and it is crowded. Treat India mainly as proof of work for Europe.**

**Two technical facts that shape every Indian build.**

1. **Tally is local.** TallyPrime talks to other software through XML over HTTP on port 9000 on the client's own computer. There is no cloud login ([AI Accountant guide](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/tally-api-integration-guide), [TallyHelp JSON integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime-integration-using-json-1/), [TallyPrime API Explorer](https://tallysolutions.com/tallyprime-api-explorer/)). It only answers while TallyPrime is open with the right company loaded. So build a small agent program for the client's Windows PC that:
   * pulls approved entries from your server into a local queue;
   * names the target company in every XML request, and checks first that Tally is running with that company loaded;
   * retries later if Tally is closed, and sends the operator a WhatsApp alert if an entry waits more than, say, 30 minutes;
   * logs every posted entry so nothing is posted twice.
   Best setup: run it on the PC that keeps Tally open all day, usually the accounts PC. BUSY has no default integration option and sends you to its channel partners ([BUSY FAQ](https://busy.in/faqs/third-party-services/integration/1/)), though third parties sell BUSY APIs ([BusyNotify](https://busynotify.in/solutions/custom-apis), [RootFi](https://www.rootfi.dev/integrations/busy-accounting)). Plan for Tally first. Treat BUSY as extra work.
2. **WhatsApp needs the Cloud API, but staff can keep their app.** The WhatsApp Business app has no API for reading messages. Use Meta's **Coexistence** feature: the client's existing number is connected to the Cloud API through Meta or a provider, and the Business app keeps working on the same number. Messages sync both ways, and up to 180 days of one to one history is imported ([YCloud](https://www.ycloud.com/blog/whatsapp-business-app-coexistence-meta-update), [360dialog docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/phone-numbers/coexistence), [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users/)). Limits to tell the client: the app must be opened at least once every 13 days, and **group chats do not sync** ([InstantReply](https://www.instantreply.co/blog/whatsapp-coexistence-what-actually-syncs-2026)). If dealers send orders in a WhatsApp group, that client is not a fit until the dealers message the number directly. Staff keep working the same way, which is what you want.

## Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

**The honest picture.** Europe pays well, and the order workflows below are clearly paid for today. But they are not secret. Tools and small agencies already sell them for most common ERPs. Microsoft Business Central now has a built in **Sales Order Agent** that reads customer emails and PDF purchase orders and creates quotes and orders ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent)), and a **Payables Agent** for supplier invoices ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/payables-agent)). **Rule for every sales call: find out which ERP the prospect runs, then search "[their ERP] sales order automation" and know the rival's name before you talk.**

## Part 4: My top picks for you

### Pick 1: Order to ERP (Europe workflow 1, proven first in India workflow 1)

* **Why:** It has the clearest proof of paid pain (a £27,000 a year role that mostly types), published prices you can undercut, and a clean human approval step. The fact that agencies like Werkritme and Zestcode sell exactly this as a service proves your business model works. It fits both of you: Developer A builds matching and ERP posting in Spring Boot with Spring AI; Developer B builds email and WhatsApp intake and the approval screen in TypeScript.
* **Main risk:** you are one seller among many, and Microsoft or the ERP vendor adds the feature. Reduce it by targeting UK firms on Xero plus Katana or Cin7, and on Sage 200; by skipping Business Central and Odoo firms; and by owning each customer's specific codes, prices and delivery rules.
* **Chance it works:** about 30 to 45% chance of landing a paying European client within 6 months, if you get one strong Indian case study and use your Finland contact for introductions. This is a judgement, not a sourced number. It is lower than you may hope because the market is crowded. It is still the best odds of anything I checked.

### Pick 2: Supplier confirmation check and chasing (Europe workflow 2)

* **Why:** It reuses most of Pick 1's code (my estimate: about two thirds): reading documents, matching lines, the approval screen and ERP APIs. The chasing and supplier slip tracking are the parts ready tools cover least.
* **When:** only as the second workflow for your Pick 1 clients. Do not lead with it. Skip Business Central firms that use Continia, and Odoo firms.

### Pick 3: Nothing else yet

Freight quotes and the India dispatch chasing idea stay on the "only with a warm lead" list. Everything in the rejected tables is off the list. Two focused products beat five half built ones for a part time team.

### What to do in the next 30 days

1. Find 2 Indian firms for Pick 1: one Tally distributor whose dealers message the business number directly (not in groups) and who dislikes WhatsApp order platforms, and one manufacturer receiving emailed PDF POs. Offer the build free or at cost for case study rights, capped at 100 orders a day.
2. Before building, ask each for one week of real orders. Count them and time 20 by hand. That checks my estimates.
3. In parallel, message 30 UK wholesalers and distributors on Xero plus Katana or Cin7, or Sage 200. Ask one question: "How many orders a day does your team retype from email?" Before any call, search "[their ERP] sales order automation" and know the rival.
4. Prepare a simple data protection agreement and use an EU hosted model endpoint for European data. European buyers will ask about GDPR before price.
