ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The #1 pick is already sold as a cheap built-in add-on for one of its two named ERPs, and has ready-made tools for the others
Where: Final top picks, "1. E1, email PO to sales order, for UK wholesalers on Unleashed or Cin7 Core", and E1 f: "The gap: small and medium firms on Unleashed, Cin7 Core, Sage 200, Odoo or Xero ... A service that sets up the mapping and the checks fits them better than a US SaaS."
Problem: The task says "If it is already solved by a built in feature or a cheap tool, reject it." Cin7 sells its own Intelligent Document Recognition (IDR) add-on for Cin7 Core. It reads an emailed PDF purchase order, maps lines to Cin7 products and creates a draft sales order for review. It is reported at about $79 a month for up to 600 documents (Cin7 Core help "Extract sale data with Intelligent Document Recognition (IDR)" https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/10553586503439-Extract-sale-data-with-Intelligent-Document-Recognition-IDR ; Cin7 feature page https://www.cin7.com/features/sales/idr/). That price is below the $300 to $600 a month upkeep E1 wants to charge, so a Cin7 Core buyer will pick the vendor's own button. For Unleashed, Logistify AI already advertises "AI PDF Sales Order Entry for Unleashed Software" (https://logistify.ai/blog/ai-pdf-sales-order-entry-for-unleashed-software). For Odoo, the Odoo App Store already lists AI sale-order digitisation modules that turn a customer PO PDF or photo into a draft quotation (https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/18.0/dx_sale_digitization , https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/jjro_ai_sales_capture). The doc searched for competitors only on Business Central, Epicor and SAP. It calls the exact ERPs it recommends "the gap" and never checks them. So half of the #1 pick breaks the task's own reject rule, and the rest has a named competitor the user is never warned about.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The I1 "gap" is false: cheap-looking WhatsApp tools already read voice notes and handwritten photos and push them to Tally

Problem: Karvan.ai sells "WhatsApp Order Automation for Wholesale" and says it handles text, voice notes and photos of handwritten orders (https://www.karvan.ai/). Zotok.ai says it handles unstructured WhatsApp orders with low-touch Tally/ERP integration (https://www.zotok.ai/). YuVerse describes AI order handling for Indian FMCG distributors with support for regional-language voice (https://www.yuverse.ai/resources/posts/ai-fmcg-distributor-communication-order-management-india). The doc never names or prices any of them. Its only competitors are the catalogue-app and DMS tools, which it pushes aside. So the "gap" in the India pick is claimed without being checked, and the task's required step "Whether existing software already solves it" was not done for the product it tells the user to build first.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The "under $50 a month" budget check breaks on its own numbers
Where: "Budget check (under $50 a month): n8n self hosted on a small VPS or n8n Cloud Starter at about $20 a month ... WhatsApp coexistence keeps messages sent from the phone app free"
Problem: (a) n8n Cloud Starter includes only 2,500 executions a month (https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/n8n-pricing/ , https://costbench.com/software/ai-automation/n8n/). The doc's own I1 volume is "30 to 300 orders a day". A pilot distributor at 100 orders a day, 26 working days, is about 2,600 order runs, which is over the cap before any E1 client, retries or confirmation messages. The next tier costs more than the whole budget. (b) The doc's WhatsApp route cites 360dialog for coexistence. 360dialog charges a licence of about €49 a month per number (https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/360dialog/prices-plans-and-payment-options , https://360dialog.com/pricing). That fee alone is about the whole $50, and the plan tells the user to run the first Indian client "near free", so nobody else pays it. (c) The GDPR section also asks for EU hosting and an EU-resident LLM, and none of that is priced. The one hard money constraint in the brief is claimed as met, but it is not.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] The WhatsApp intake silently drops common ordering patterns
Where: I1 c: "Since Meta's coexistence feature, the same number can stay on the phone app and also feed the Cloud API", and I1 b step 1: "A retailer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp."
Problem: Under coexistence, group chats are not synced to the API. Broadcast lists, view-once and disappearing messages are not supported either (https://saleshiker.com/kb/whatsapp-coexistence-connect-whatsapp-business-apps-to-whatsapp-api/limitations-of-whatsapp-co-existence-business-app-cloud-api/ , https://www.instantreply.co/blog/whatsapp-coexistence-what-actually-syncs-2026). Counterexample: a Ludhiana distributor whose field salesmen post the day's orders in a "Sales Team" WhatsApp group, which is a very common setup. The engine never sees those orders. The clerk must keep punching them, and the "% auto matched" case study number is wrong from day one. The doc does not mention this limit or give a fallback, such as asking salesmen to forward orders 1:1 to the number.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The Tally connector fails in exactly the scenario the doc uses to sell it, and Busy has no route at all
Where: India intro: "Automation only wins where ... orders arrive at night and the business wants to dispatch first thing". I1 c: "Tally takes XML over HTTP on port 9000, but only while Tally is open on a local PC with the company loaded ... So you need a tiny local agent". I1 title: "WhatsApp order to Tally or Busy sales order".
Problem: At night the Tally PC is normally off or Tally is closed. The step 3 checks ("check stock and the party's outstanding balance") and the step 4 write both need a live Tally, so they cannot run then. The design says nothing about queuing, retrying, or caching stock and outstanding balances. So the "night orders ready at opening" pitch cannot happen as described. It also ignores two cases: a PC shared with the accountant who has a different company loaded, and a Tally on Remote Access or TallyPrime Server. "Busy" is named in the title, the table and the build plan, but no Busy API or import route is given anywhere. The task says the team can integrate "any tool that has a public API", and the doc never shows Busy has one. A Busy-using distributor, common in Punjab, cannot be served on this plan.
