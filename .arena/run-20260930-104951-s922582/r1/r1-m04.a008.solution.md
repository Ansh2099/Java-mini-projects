# Workflow automations to sell: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Sell one engine: **a messy order message comes in, and a checked draft goes into the business system for a human to approve.** It has a clear start and end, touches 2 tools plus your approval screen, has one owner, and is easy to count.

Now the brutal part, which you need before anything else. **Email order entry is not an empty market in Europe.** Funded products already sell it: Hyperfox (about €0.07 per approved order line, native Exact Online, Odoo and Business Central connectors), Turian, Conexiom, Y Meadows, Ordermind, Logistify, cheap Odoo App Store modules, and Microsoft's own Sales Order Agent inside Business Central. A UK firm, Zestcode, already sells it as a service for Sage 200. The same is true of supplier confirmation checking (Turian, Doxis, Tradecloud). So you cannot pitch "nobody does this". You can only win in the narrow gaps below, and you must walk away from everyone else.

1. **Lead offer in Europe, medium firms only: "Customer order email to draft sales order".** Target three gaps and nothing else:
   * **High line volume.** A distributor keying 15,000+ order lines a month pays roughly €1,000+ a month on Hyperfox's per line fee and signs a 12 month contract. Your flat fee of $500 to $1,200 a month is cheaper.
   * **UK firms on Sage 200.** It has a documented REST API that can create sales orders, and the only rival I found selling this for Sage 200 is one small UK service firm.
   * **Business Central firms whose orders break Microsoft's agent:** more than 15 lines, item variants, or orders sent from many email addresses.
2. **Proof of work in India: "WhatsApp order to Tally sales order"** for Ludhiana distributors whose retailers will not use an ordering app. It is the same engine with a harder input. Charge a token price for the first 1 or 2 clients. This is a case study, not income.
3. **Second workflow in Europe, sold to the same medium clients: "Supplier order confirmation check and chasing".** Email only, no supplier portal, priced below the mid market products.

**Walk away from:** small European firms on Exact Online, Odoo, Business Central or Xero with Unleashed (cheap tools cover them), generic "AI invoice processing" (Microsoft's Payables Agent and e-invoicing laws are killing it), and any prospect that already uses one of the products above.

If your first 10 European discovery calls show that most prospects already use or have trialled Hyperfox, Turian or a similar product, stop and rethink. That is the test that tells you whether the gap is real.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the EU plus the UK, Switzerland and Norway. First targets: the UK, the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany and the Nordics (Finland first, because Developer A already has a Finnish client). English works in these markets. The Microsoft agents also cover English, German, Dutch, Finnish and more, so you win on fit and price, not language.
2. "A few weeks of part time work" means 3 to 6 weeks for two people at about 15 hours a week each, so about 90 to 180 hours per build.
3. Where a volume or time per task has no link, it is a labeled **estimate** to check on the first sales call.
4. **Research limits, stated plainly.** In this session, page fetch was blocked for almost every site except Microsoft Learn and GitHub. So the Microsoft facts come from pages I opened and read. Every other fact (prices, salaries, competitors, job ads) comes from the text of web search results, and the Sources section marks this. I did not invent any source. My web search budget is now used up, so the list in "What I could not verify" is what you check next.
5. Currency: I use about ₹88 per US dollar and about $1.30 per pound. Check the day's rate before you quote.

## How I judged each idea

A workflow made the list only if it passed all of these:

1. It meets your 7 rules: one process with a clear start and end, 2 or 3 tools, one owner, a human approves exceptions, a few weeks to build, countable, and already partly digital.
2. Businesses already spend money on it: salaries, job ads that list the task, paid tools, or paid services.
3. **No built in feature or cheap tool already covers it for that buyer.** I checked this per buyer segment, not per workflow. When a cheap tool covers a segment, that segment is rejected even if the workflow survives for others.
4. It does not move money or make legal, medical or lending decisions.
5. It survives the e-invoicing laws rolling out across Europe. Those laws turn invoices into structured data, which kills PDF reading for invoices. They do **not** cover customer orders or supplier order confirmations. That is why my picks sit on the order side.

## Part 1: India

### India workflow 1: WhatsApp order to Tally sales order (distributors and wholesalers)

**a. Who has it, and size.** FMCG, pharma, hardware, auto parts, electrical and textile distributors and wholesalers who take retailer orders on WhatsApp and phone and bill in Tally (also Busy or Marg). Fits **both** sizes. Small (5 to 49 staff): the owner decides, and 1 or 2 billing staff key orders. Medium (50 to 250): the sales operations or billing head owns it. Vendor blogs describe order desks of 10 to 15 people at the larger end.

**b. The steps today.**
1. A retailer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp. It may be a text list, a voice note, a photo of a handwritten list, or a forwarded PDF. Local item names, pack sizes ("5 peti", "2 dozen") and mixed Hindi, Punjabi and English are normal.
2. A billing person reads it, works out which items and pack sizes are meant, checks rate and stock, and keys a sales order or invoice into Tally.
3. They reply on WhatsApp to confirm, often late, and send the invoice PDF.
4. The data ends up in Tally. The WhatsApp chat is the only record of what was asked for.

Vendor blogs describe this exact pattern: orders from calls and WhatsApp keyed into Tally by hand, "error-prone and hours late", and at mid market firms "hundreds of messages per day" handled by teams of 10 to 15 people. These vendors sell a fix, so treat their numbers as a claim.

**c. Software on each side.** In: WhatsApp Business app, or the WhatsApp Business API through a provider. Out: TallyPrime. Tally accepts data through XML over HTTP (TallyPrime must be running with its HTTP server on, for example port 9000, with a company loaded). Since TallyPrime 7.0 it also accepts native JSON. Tally is a desktop program, so you need a small connector on the client's PC that talks to Tally locally. That is 2 tools plus your approval screen.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** Small distributor: 30 to 150 orders a day. Medium: 200 to 1,000 or more. Keying one order of 5 to 20 lines takes about 2 to 6 minutes, plus back and forth when an item is unclear. Example: 100 orders a day at 4 minutes each is about 6.7 hours a day, close to one full time person. **Measure it on day one:** count a week of WhatsApp orders and time 20 being keyed.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Billing executives. Live Ludhiana job ads show ₹10,000 to ₹19,000 a month (Toyz Island), ₹13,000 to ₹15,000 (Commercial Traders, Miller Ganj), and ₹14,000 to ₹18,000 on Apna. So one clerk costs about ₹15,000 a month. **Labor savings alone will not sell this in India.** Sell speed (confirm and dispatch within the hour), fewer wrong items and returns, and no bottleneck in peak season.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. TallyPrime (Release 4.0 and later) can **send** invoices, orders and reminders on WhatsApp. It does **not** read free text orders and turn them into vouchers.
2. **SalesOn** costs ₹208 per user a month on a yearly plan, with no setup fee, and posts orders to Tally. But it is a salesman order booking app: someone has to enter the order in the app. It does not read the free WhatsApp messages retailers already send. A distributor whose retailers will not use an app is not served by it, and forcing an app on them breaks your rule 7.
3. **Zotok** says customers can order by text, image or voice note on WhatsApp and that it connects to Tally. A software listing says its pricing starts at about ₹6,000. **This is the real competitor, and it is cheap.** HublerX makes the same claim for larger manufacturers. Interakt lists a Tally integration.
4. **The honest gap is narrow.** It is a custom fit to one distributor's messy item names, their Busy or Marg setup, and their approval rules, delivered in person by people 20 minutes away, for firms that have not bought Zotok. If a prospect already uses Zotok, HublerX or SalesOn, walk away.

**g. Realistic price.** Your price must sit near Zotok's, not above it.
* First 1 or 2 clients: free or a ₹10,000 token setup, in exchange for a written case study, permission to share the numbers, and a video testimonial.
* After that: setup ₹15,000 to ₹30,000, and ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 a month, plus WhatsApp API message costs at cost.
* At that price India does not pay for your time. That is fine. The job of this workflow is to produce the numbers that sell the European offer.

**h. Sources.** Tally XML, JSON and WhatsApp pages; Interakt, Fast2SMS, HublerX and Sunray Datalinks blogs; SalesOn pricing and order taking app pages; Zotok distributor page and the Techjockey listing; Apna and WorkIndia job ads. Links are in the Sources section.

### India workflow 2: Supplier PO confirmation and dispatch follow up (manufacturers)

**Verdict: do not build this unless a plant asks for it.** I checked it properly this time, and it is weak in India.

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers that buy many inputs from many suppliers: bicycle parts, auto components, hosiery, hand tools, machinery (all big in Ludhiana). Mostly **medium**.

**b. The steps today.** A purchase order is made in Tally (or Excel) and sent to the supplier as a PDF on WhatsApp or email. The supplier replies on WhatsApp with a confirmation, a changed rate, a part quantity or a new date. A purchase executive reads it, updates an Excel tracker (sometimes the Tally due date), and phones suppliers who are late. Data ends up in Excel and Tally.

**c. Software.** WhatsApp or Gmail in, Tally or Google Sheets out. 2 tools.

**d. Frequency and time (estimate).** 20 to 100 open POs a week at a medium plant, 3 to 5 minutes per confirmation, plus daily chasing.

**e. Who does it and cost (estimate).** A purchase executive. They do not do this full time. The real cost is a stopped line when a late supplier was not chased.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. Tally already has a **Pending Purchase Orders** report that lists open POs by party and date. The "which POs are late" half is built in.
2. A Tally WhatsApp plugin (Fast2SMS) already **sends** POs to suppliers from Tally; the plugin is free and you pay per message.
3. HublerX claims to handle unstructured WhatsApp messages with ERP and Tally links for larger manufacturers.
4. What is left is reading the supplier's reply and updating the PO. That is a thin gap, at a firm where "my purchase boy does this" costs ₹18,000 to ₹35,000 a month (estimate, not sourced).
5. **No demand source.** I found no Indian job ad, study or paid service that shows firms spend money on this exact step.

**g. Price (estimate).** Only if a plant has lost production days to late material and asks: setup ₹20,000 to ₹40,000, monthly ₹3,000 to ₹6,000.

**h. Sources.** Tally Pending Purchase Orders help page, Fast2SMS PO from Tally page, HublerX manufacturers blog. No India demand source. Validate before you build.

## Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

### What already exists (read this before pitching anything)

**Microsoft, inside Dynamics 365 Business Central (BC).** BC is one of the most common ERPs for European firms with 20 to 250 staff.

1. **Sales Order Agent** watches a shared Exchange mailbox, reads the email body and PDF or image attachments, finds the customer by email address, checks stock, and creates a sales quote and then an order. A human reviews each step. Microsoft's cost example is 1,650 Copilot Credits for 100 order emails a month, about **$16.50 a month** at the $0.01 per credit rate in its examples. It works in English, German, Dutch, French, Finnish, Danish, Swedish, Norwegian, Italian and Spanish.
2. **Its documented limits:** at most **15 item lines** per document (more "might result in lower-quality output"), item lines only, **no variant codes**, no new customers or items, customers matched **only by the sender's email address**, only an Exchange shared inbox, no price or discount changes.
3. **Payables Agent** turns emailed PDF invoices into draft purchase invoices, focused on overhead costs. Microsoft's example is 6,500 credits for 100 three line invoices, **about $65 a month.** Better PO matching was "moved to the next release wave" in August 2026.
4. **Procurement Agent (supplier communications)** reads supplier confirmations and chases late POs, but only in **Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management**, Microsoft's large company ERP, as a "production-ready preview". I found no BC equivalent in BC's update 29.0 feature list.
5. **E-invoicing:** Belgium B2B from 1 January 2026, Poland KSeF from 1 February 2026 for large taxpayers and 1 April 2026 for all, France from 1 September 2026. BC has supported German XRechnung and ZUGFeRD since January 2025. Invoice PDF reading is a shrinking market in these countries.

### Europe workflow 1: Customer order email to draft sales order

**a. Who has it, and size.** B2B wholesalers and distributors (industrial supplies, packaging, food service, building materials, spare parts, electrical) and make to stock manufacturers that get orders by email as PDF purchase orders, Excel sheets or plain text.

* **Medium (50 to 250 staff): the target.** The customer service or sales operations manager owns it, with 2 to 6 order entry staff.
* **Small (10 to 49): mostly rejected.** Small firms run Exact Online, Odoo, BC, or Xero plus an inventory app such as Unleashed (Xero has no full sales order module; Unleashed does, has a SalesOrders API, and syncs to Xero). Each of those is already covered cheaply: Hyperfox at low volume costs about €176 a month (worked below), the Odoo modules are a one time purchase, Logistify covers Unleashed, and BC has Sales Order Agent. Take a small firm only if it runs an ERP none of these cover and its volume is real.

**Target only these medium buyers:**

| Segment | Why the gap is real | How to check on the first call |
|---|---|---|
| A. High line volume, any ERP with an API | At Hyperfox's published €0.07 per line, a firm keying 80 orders a day at 12 lines (about 20,000 lines a month) pays about €1,400 a month and signs for 12 months. Your flat $600 to $1,200 is cheaper. | Orders a day times average lines. Above about 12,000 lines a month, per line pricing costs more than you. |
| B. UK firms on Sage 200 | Sage 200 has a documented REST API with a POST for sales orders (Sage developer portal). The only Sage 200 rival I found is Zestcode, a small UK service firm. Hyperfox names no Sage 200 connector in what I found. | Which Sage 200 edition and version? Is the API enabled? |
| C. BC firms whose orders break Sales Order Agent | More than 15 lines, item variants, many sender addresses, customer part numbers. | Ask for 20 real orders and count how many break a documented limit. If under a third, walk away. |

**Kill rule.** If a prospect already uses or has signed with Hyperfox, Turian, Conexiom, Y Meadows, Ordermind or Logistify, do not compete. If your first 10 calls show most prospects already have one, the gap is smaller than this report assumes, and you should stop and rethink.

**b. The steps today.**
1. An order arrives in a shared mailbox (orders@...). It may be a PDF from the customer's ERP, an Excel sheet, or a typed email.
2. A clerk finds the customer in the ERP, translates the customer's part numbers or descriptions to their own item codes (often from an Excel cheat sheet), checks price, stock and delivery date, and types each line into a new sales order.
3. They email an order confirmation back.
4. The data ends up in the ERP sales order. Mismatches (wrong price, unknown or discontinued item) are sorted out by phone or email.

**c. Software and APIs.**

| Tool | Usable API? | How I know |
|---|---|---|
| Microsoft 365 / Outlook (input) | Yes | Microsoft Graph sends change notifications for new messages; shared mailboxes need the Mail.Read application permission. (Opened) |
| Gmail (input) | Yes, Gmail API | Not re-verified here |

| Odoo (output) | **Only on "Custom" plans** | Odoo docs: no external API on One App Free or Standard. (Opened, GitHub) |
| Unleashed (small firm output) | Yes | SalesOrders resource can list, view and create; JSON or XML; no partial updates. (Search result) |

| SAP Business One, Access Dimensions, Pegasus Opera, weclapp, Xentral | Not verified | Check before you pitch |

That is 2 tools (mailbox and ERP) plus your approval screen. Teams or Slack alerts would be a third.

**What you build.** Mailbox webhook, then text extraction (PDF, Excel, email body), then an LLM that pulls out customer, lines, quantities, dates and PO number. Next **a matching layer**: a customer part number to item code table that learns from every human correction, plus price and stock checks through the ERP API. Then a draft order on an approval screen next to the original document. Nothing posts until a human clicks approve. Developer A builds the core in Spring Boot with Spring AI and PostgreSQL. Developer B builds the approval screen and mail connectors in TypeScript.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** Medium: 50 to 300 orders a day. Keying a 10 to 30 line order takes about 4 to 10 minutes, more when part numbers need translating. Tech Ultra Solutions, which sells an Odoo module, claims order entry drops "from 8 to 15 minutes per PO to 1 to 2 minutes" (vendor claim). Example: 60 orders a day at 7 minutes is 7 hours a day, about one full time person. **Measure it:** export one month of the orders@ mailbox and time 20 orders being keyed.

**e. Who does it and what it costs (evidence of spend).**
* **UK salaries (Reed, search results):** average sales processor salary £28,921. Live ads: Worsley £16.24 an hour (about £31,500 a year), Stockport £24,000 to £30,000, North London £28,000 to £32,000 plus 20% bonus, senior sales order processor £35,000 to £40,000. Reed lists sales order processor jobs across London, Leeds and remote, so firms hire people for exactly this task.
* **Netherlands (Werkzoeken, search result):** orderverwerker average €2,545 gross a month, range €2,165 to €2,930, from 10,000+ salary entries.
* Employer costs (National Insurance, pension, holiday) add more on top. I could not open a source for the exact UK rates, so assume 15 to 25% extra and confirm.
* **Paid tools and services:** Hyperfox, Turian, Conexiom, Y Meadows and Zestcode all sell this, and Hyperfox raised €3.2M to do it. Companies are paying for the fix today.
* So one full time UK order clerk costs a firm about £2,400 a month in salary alone (£28,921 divided by 12), and a medium firm with 3 clerks spends over £7,000 a month on this job.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** See the landscape table above. In short: BC Sales Order Agent is cheap for simple BC orders; Hyperfox and the Odoo modules cover Exact, Odoo and BC cheaply at low volume; Logistify covers Unleashed; Zestcode covers some Sage 200 firms as a service. Your gap is segments A, B and C. EDI solves this only for large customers; the long tail still sends PDFs. E-invoicing laws do not cover purchase orders, so the pain stays.

**g. Realistic price.** Medium first workflow: setup $3,000 to $4,000, upkeep $600 to $1,200 a month tied to volume. See Part 3 for how this was checked.

### Europe workflow 2: Supplier order confirmation check and PO chasing

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers, wholesalers and importers that place many POs with many suppliers: engineering, food production, furniture, electrical and industrial distribution. Mainly **medium** (1 to 5 buyers). The owner is the purchasing manager or head buyer. Small firms rarely have enough POs.

**b. The steps today.** The buyer sends a PO from the ERP as a PDF by email. The supplier replies with an order confirmation that may confirm, change the price or quantity, split the delivery, or move the date. The buyer finds the PO, compares line by line, updates the expected receipt date and flags price changes. Unconfirmed or late POs get chased by email. Data ends up in the ERP PO lines. Price changes nobody noticed become invoice disputes later.

Microsoft describes this pain in its own documentation: "Purchasing personnel spend a large part of their day on manual tasks that are related to communicating with vendors, following up with them, and maintaining changes that are related to purchase orders," and this communication "is often unstructured and conducted via email, even for companies that implement electronic data interchange (EDI)."

**c. Software and APIs.** Mailbox (Graph or Gmail) and the ERP. In BC the purchaseOrderLine resource has an expectedReceiptDate field and supports PATCH (opened). Exact Online has PurchaseOrder entities (opened). Unleashed documents a Purchase Orders resource (search result). 2 tools plus your approval screen.

**What you build.** Classify supplier emails (confirmation, change, delay, other). Extract lines. Match to the open PO. Show a diff (old against new: price, quantity, date). The buyer approves, then you PATCH the PO line. On a schedule, draft chasers for POs unconfirmed after N days or overdue, and the buyer sends them. Price changes above a threshold always go to a human.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** 30 to 200 POs a week at a medium firm, 3 to 8 minutes per confirmation, plus chasing. Example: 100 confirmations a week at 5 minutes is about 8 hours a week, plus 2 to 4 hours of chasing. Count one month of supplier replies and time 20.

**e. Who does it and cost (evidence of spend).**
* Indeed UK: purchasing assistant average £25,869 a year. Reed: London £27,500, Leeds £30,250 (search results).
* UK job descriptions for purchasing administrators list "expediting order confirmations and agreeing delivery dates with suppliers", "using in-house systems to reschedule purchase orders" and "checking order confirmations to ensure all information is correct" (Indeed job description page and Glassdoor listings, search results). Firms hire for this task.
* Turian, Doxis and Tradecloud sell products for it, which is spend.
* The bigger cost is late material and missed price increases. Ask each client for one real example of each; that story sells better than hours.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. **Turian** processes supplier confirmations, handles partial deliveries and price unit conversions, and sends only critical deviations to people. Price on request, mid market. This is the closest rival.
2. **Doxis** compares confirmations to the PO line by line. It is an enterprise document platform.
3. **Tradecloud** moves confirmation onto a supplier portal. Suppliers must log in, which many small suppliers will not do.
4. **Microsoft Procurement Agent** is only in D365 Supply Chain Management, in preview, and does not yet support split deliveries or site changes. No BC version found.
5. **Your gap:** medium firms on BC, Sage 200 or Exact that want email only (no supplier portal), a flat fee, and no mid market sales cycle. It is a real gap but a narrow one, which is why this is the second workflow, sold to clients who already trust you.

**g. Realistic price.** As a second workflow: setup $2,000 to $3,000 (connector and approval screen are reused), upkeep $400 to $900 a month. As a first workflow: $3,000 to $4,000.

**h. Sources.** Microsoft Learn: Procurement Agent overview, supplier communications, follow up; BC update 29.0; BC purchaseOrderLine. Search results: Turian order confirmations and supplier communication pages; Doxis; Tradecloud; Indeed and Reed salaries; Indeed job description; Glassdoor listings.

## Part 3: Pricing check for Europe (confirm or correct)

**Your guess:** $1,500 to $4,000 for the first workflow, $400 to $1,500 a month for upkeep.

**My verdict: the setup range holds, but you will mostly sell at the top of it, because your buyers are medium firms. The upkeep range is right at the bottom and too high at the top for one workflow. Quote from this one table.**

| Deal | Setup | Upkeep a month |
|---|---|---|
| Medium firm, first workflow (the normal deal) | **$3,000 to $4,000** | **$600 to $1,200**, tied to volume |
| Small firm, first workflow (rare, only on an uncovered ERP) | $1,500 to $2,500 | $250 to $500 |
| Second workflow for an existing client | $2,000 to $3,000 | $400 to $900 |
| First 1 or 2 European clients (case study deal) | Up to 30% off, in exchange for a named case study | Same |

Do not go above $4,000 for setup until you have two European case studies.

**Why these numbers. Five checks, each one you can repeat:**

1. **What UK agencies charge (search results, agency marketing, so treat as a range not a fact).** UK pricing guides for 2026 put a single automation at £1,000 to £5,000 (about $1,300 to $6,500), a custom AI agent with system integration at £6,000 to £25,000, and ongoing support at £300 to £997 a month. Your $3,000 to $4,000 sits below a UK agency for a custom ERP integration, which is right for a new offshore team with no case studies yet.
2. **What freelancers charge.** Upwork n8n experts go for $40 to $100 an hour, and senior AI and API specialists for $125 to $250. At your 90 to 180 hours per build, $3,000 to $4,000 is $17 to $44 an hour. That is cheap for the buyer and still good money in Ludhiana. It also tells you not to go below $3,000 for a medium build.
3. **What the buyer's clerk costs.** Reed's UK average is £28,921 a year, so about £2,400 a month per clerk before employer costs. Worked example: if the workflow saves 60% of one clerk's order time, that is about £1,450 a month of value. Setup of $3,500 (about £2,700) pays back in about 2 months. Upkeep of $800 (about £615) leaves the client about £835 a month ahead. Upkeep of $1,500 (about £1,150) leaves them only about £300, and they will cancel. With 3 clerks the value triples, which is where $1,000 to $1,200 a month is fair.
4. **What the product rival costs at the buyer's volume.** For non BC buyers the anchor is Hyperfox, not Microsoft. At €0.07 per approved line: 20 orders a day at 6 lines (about 2,500 lines a month) is about €176 a month, which you cannot beat, so you do not sell there. 80 orders a day at 12 lines (about 20,000 lines) is about €1,400 a month on a 12 month contract, so your $600 to $1,200 is cheaper and needs no lock in. I could not confirm whether Hyperfox adds a platform fee on top; a reseller listing mentions a $2,000 starter pack. Ask prospects what they were quoted.
5. **What Microsoft costs, for BC buyers only.** Sales Order Agent is about $16.50 per 100 orders. A BC buyer compares you to that, so you only sell to segment C (orders that break the agent) and justify the fee by fit and service: new customer formats, mapping upkeep, monitoring, fixes within a day, and a monthly report of orders and minutes saved.

**Tie upkeep to volume.** Include up to N documents a month and charge per extra 100. It is fair, it scales, and it covers your LLM costs.

**India comparison (corrected).** India prices are now ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 setup (about $170 to $340) and ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 a month (about $34 to $68). Against the normal European deal ($3,000 to $4,000 setup, $600 to $1,200 a month), comparing midpoints, India setup is about one fourteenth of Europe ($255 against $3,500) and India upkeep is about one eighteenth ($51 against $900). The India price is this low because Zotok sets the ceiling. India is for proof, not profit.

## Part 5: Final top picks, with reasons

**Pick 1. Europe: customer order email to draft sales order, for medium firms in segments A, B and C only.**
* Why: partly digital (email plus ERP), countable, one owner, 2 tools, human approval, untouched by e-invoicing laws. UK firms hire sales order processors at about £29,000 a year, and funded products prove firms pay for the fix.
* Why it is still risky, honestly: this is a funded category. You are a service team with no case study competing against products. You win only on the three segments, and you need the kill rule.
* Fit to your team: Developer A's Spring Boot, PostgreSQL and fintech work (document checks, audit trails) is the matching and approval core. Developer B builds the screen and connectors.
* Where to start: your Finnish client. Ask for 3 introductions to medium wholesalers or manufacturers. Then UK distributors on Sage 200, found through Sage 200 partner and user communities.

**Pick 2. India: WhatsApp order to Tally, as proof of work only.**
* Why: the fastest path to a real case study with real volume, 20 minutes from your desk, on the same engine as Pick 1.
* Be honest with yourselves: Zotok starts at about ₹6,000 and a clerk costs about ₹15,000 a month. Do 1 or 2 clients at a token price, then stop selling in India unless someone pays the full ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 setup.

**Pick 3. Europe: supplier confirmation check and chasing, as the second workflow for Pick 1 clients.**
* Why: medium firms hire purchasing staff partly for it, Microsoft covers it only in its large company ERP, the product rivals (Turian, Doxis, Tradecloud) are mid market, enterprise or portal based, and it reuses your connector and approval screen, so your margin improves.

**Do not build:** order intake for small European firms, generic AI invoice processing, payment reminders, anything for BC users with simple orders, or the Indian supplier follow up unless asked. Each is covered by built in or cheap tools, which is the trap that killed your earlier idea.

### Practical rules that protect you

1. **n8n licence.** The n8n Sustainable Use License allows use "only for your own internal business purposes", and providing it to others only "free of charge for non-commercial purposes". Do not host one n8n instance and charge clients for workflows on it. Install n8n in the client's own account, or write the product in your own Spring Boot and Node code. Email license@n8n.io before you sell anything that runs on n8n.
2. **GDPR.** EU and UK client data processed by you in India is a transfer out of the EEA or UK. India is not on the European Commission's adequacy list (check the Commission list linked from the Microsoft guide in Sources). Sign a Data Processing Agreement with **Standard Contractual Clauses** (plus the UK addendum for UK clients). Keep data in an EU region such as AWS Frankfurt or Ireland. Keep a written list of subprocessors, including your LLM provider.
3. **Budget under $50 a month.** One small EU cloud VM or container, one PostgreSQL database, and a pay as you go LLM API. With small models, the cost per order document should be around 1 US cent or less. That is my estimate; log real token use from day one. Charge each client's LLM costs to that client once they pay.
4. **Tally connector.** Tally must be running on the client's machine with the HTTP server on. Build a small Windows service that pulls approved orders from your cloud and posts them to Tally locally. Use JSON on TallyPrime 7.0, XML otherwise.
5. **Constraints.** Nothing here moves money or makes legal, medical or lending decisions. Make "human approves before anything posts or sends" a hard rule in the code, not a setting.

### First two weeks: exact steps

1. Days 1 to 3: visit 5 Ludhiana distributors. Ask whether they use Zotok, SalesOn or HublerX (if yes, skip them). Ask for a week of forwarded WhatsApp orders and time 20 entries. Pick the one with the most orders and the messiest item names.
2. Days 4 to 5: Developer A builds the extractor and matcher against their Tally item master (XML or JSON export). Developer B builds the approval screen.
3. Week 2: shadow mode. The clerk keys as usual while the system drafts. Compare them. Track match rate and minutes.
4. In parallel, send the Finnish client a one page brief and ask for 3 introductions. On each European call ask: orders a day? lines per order? minutes each? which ERP, edition and plan? any orders over 15 lines or with variants? who owns it? **have you looked at Hyperfox, Turian or a similar tool, and what were you quoted?** Log every answer. After 10 calls, apply the kill rule.
