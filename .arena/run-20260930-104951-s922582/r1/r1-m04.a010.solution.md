# Workflows worth selling: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Build one engine and sell one workflow: **turning customer orders that arrive by email (PDF, Excel, or plain text) into a checked draft sales order in the client's stock or ERP system, which a person approves.**

1. Sell it in the UK to small wholesalers and distributors that run **Unleashed**. Later, sell it to medium firms on **Sage 200**, but only once a signed deal pays for Sage's developer fee.
2. Do **not** target Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central or Cin7 Core. Both now ship this feature inside the product. Under the task's own rule, those buyers are already solved.
3. Build it first for one Indian supplier that runs Tally, so you have a real case study with real numbers.
4. Treat the mirror workflow (checking supplier order confirmations against your purchase orders) as a question to ask clients, not a plan.

**Brutal truth.** This category is being absorbed into ERPs. Microsoft and Cin7 already did it. Others will follow. You have maybe 12 to 24 months where "the ERP does not do this yet" is true for Unleashed and Sage 200. So build the engine so the ERP connector is the only part that changes, and get paying clients fast.

Everything else we checked is either already sold cheaply by a tool, or too small to be a workflow. Details and sources follow.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK plus EU countries. Most evidence is from the UK. I add Dutch evidence for Exact Online, and it points to "later, not now".
2. Currency: about $1.30 per £1 and about ₹88 per $1 for rough conversions. Check the rate on the day you quote.
3. Many numbers come from vendor blogs and job boards. Vendors inflate pain to sell tools. I mark vendor claims and my own estimates. Your first discovery calls must replace my estimates with real counts.
4. Web search worked. Direct page opening was blocked for most sites in my session. So every link below is a page I found through search and read in the search results. Open each one before you quote it to a client.

## How I chose (the argument in two rounds)

**Round 1.**

**Thesis: sell supplier invoice (accounts payable) automation.** Every firm gets bills. The steps are clear. The APIs are good.

**Antithesis: that market is solved and cheap.** Dext captures bills from about £24 to £30 a month ([BookkeeperTools](https://bookkeepertools.uk/dext-pricing-uk-2026/)) and also sells supplier statement reconciliation ([Dext](https://dext.com/en/business/products/supplier-statements-reconciliation)), as does Yooz ([Yooz](https://www.getyooz.com/en-gb/yooz-vendor-statements)). In India, Suvit (now Vyapar TaxOne) turns purchase and sales PDFs and bank statements into Tally entries and does GST reconciliation, from about ₹10,000 ([Techjockey](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/suvit), [Suvit pricing](https://www.suvit.io/pricing)). You cannot beat a £25 tool with a $3,000 service.

**Synthesis: go to the other side of the same document problem.** Bills coming in are standard. Orders coming in from customers are not. Each customer has its own codes, units, and price deals, and every order must be matched to the seller's own items and prices. Enterprise tools do this for a lot of money (Conexiom about $10,000 to $100,000+ a year per [ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom); Rossum Starter $18,000 a year per [Floowed](https://www.floowed.com/insights/rossum-pricing)).

**Round 2.**

**Thesis: sell order entry into Business Central first.** Its API is the cleanest and its clients pay for software.

**Antithesis: Business Central already does it.** Microsoft's Sales Order Agent watches a shared mailbox, reads the email body and PDF or image attachments, finds the customer, matches items (including item references and customer part numbers set up as references), checks availability, and drafts a quote or order that a person reviews ([Microsoft Learn: Sales Order Agent](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent), [FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/faqs-sales-order-taker-agent), [QuickDynamics on item references](https://quickdynamics.co.uk/make-your-business-central-sales-agent-an-item-number-guru/)). Microsoft's own example prices 100 order emails at about 1,650 Copilot Credits a month ([Microsoft Learn billing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/administration/tenant-admin-center-manage-consumption-billing), [yzhums](https://yzhums.com/68484/)). At roughly one cent a credit that is about $16 a month. Check the current credit price. Cin7 Core also turns PDF purchase orders into sales orders with its built in Intelligent Document Recognition ([Cin7 IDR](https://www.cin7.com/features/sales/idr/)).

**Synthesis: keep the workflow, change the target.** Sell into systems that have a usable API but no built in agent. The Microsoft agent also has limits: it supports up to 15 item lines per document and struggled with split units in a user test ([Microsoft Learn FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/faqs-sales-order-taker-agent), [Dynamics Communities test](https://dynamicscommunities.com/ug/dynamics-business-central-nav-ug/testing-limits-sales-order-agent-in-business-central/)). That niche is real but too narrow to lead with.

# Region 1: India

**Brutal truth first.** A Ludhiana sales operations or billing role with Tally pays about ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month ([Indeed Ludhiana Tally jobs](https://in.indeed.com/q-tally-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html), [PlacementIndia accounts Tally jobs Ludhiana](https://www.placementindia.com/job-search/accounts-tally-jobs-in-ludhiana.htm), [Indeed Ludhiana billing jobs](https://in.indeed.com/q-billing-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)). Order apps with Tally sync cost about ₹3,999 per user per year ([SoftwareSuggest on Bizmitra](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/bizmitra-erp)). Indian owners compare you to one more junior hire and to those app prices. Treat India as your lab. Take paid work when it comes, but do not plan your income on it.

## I2. Emailed purchase orders from bigger buyers into Tally (the case study)

**a. Who has it.** Small manufacturers and suppliers that sell to larger companies: auto and bicycle parts, hosiery and garments, packaging, hand tools. Ludhiana is full of them. Mostly **small** (owner decides), some **medium** (sales coordinator or accounts head owns it).

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: a buyer emails a PO as a PDF or Excel file.
2. A staff member finds the customer in Tally and reads each line.
3. They map the buyer's part code to their own stock item, check the rate against the agreed rate, and note the delivery date.
4. They type a sales order (or later a sales invoice) into Tally.
5. End: the order is in Tally and the owner knows what to make and ship.

Sales coordinator ads in India list this duty: "booking sales orders in ERP" and generating invoices in Tally from orders ([Glassdoor Gurgaon sales coordinator jobs](https://www.glassdoor.com/Job/gurgaon-sales-coordinator-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,7_IC2921225_KO8,25.htm), [Glassdoor Maharashtra](https://www.glassdoor.com/Job/maharashtra-sales-coordinator-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,11_IS4944_KO12,29.htm)).

**c. Software.** Intake: Gmail or Outlook. Destination: TallyPrime (sometimes Busy). Tally takes data through XML over HTTP on port 9000; stock items and ledgers must exist first ([TallyHelp XML Integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/), [AI Accountant Tally API guide](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/tally-api-integration-guide)). Tally runs on the client's PC, so you need a small Node script there that pulls approved orders from your server and posts them to Tally. One extra moving part. Accept it.

**d. How often and how long.** My estimate: 5 to 40 POs a day, 5 to 20 minutes each. Guides report 4 minutes for a simple order up to 60 to 90 minutes for a 100 line PO (vendor figures, not India specific: [Infrrd](https://www.infrrd.ai/blog/order-entry-automation), [Hyperfox](https://www.hyperfox.com/insights/the-real-cost-of-manual-order-entry)).

**India qualify rule.** A year of my price below (₹35,000 setup plus ₹4,000 a month) is about ₹83,000. A ₹18,000 clerk costs ₹2,16,000 a year. If you remove 60% of the keying, keying must fill at least 4 to 5 hours of that clerk's day before you beat a hire. That is roughly 20+ POs a day at 12 to 15 minutes each. **Or** the firm has lost real money on wrong rates or missed lines. At the low end (5 POs a day), a clerk absorbs it for free. Walk away.

**e. Who does it and the cost.** A sales operations, billing, or accounts executive at ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month (sources above). Often the owner checks rates himself.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Indian order tools (Bizmitra, Rupyz, Takkada, Nexxio) are built around their own app or salesman flow ([Bizmitra](https://bizmitra.io/), [Rupyz](https://rupyz.com/blog/order-management-system-with-whatsapp-tally-integration/), [Takkada](https://takkada.com/), [Nexxio](https://nexxio.in/dealer-connect.html)). A big buyer will not use its supplier's app. It sends its own PDF. Suvit reads PDFs into Tally ([Suvit data entry](https://www.suvit.io/data-entry-automation-feature)) but is sold to CA firms for accounting entries, not for matching a buyer's part codes and checking agreed rates. **Honest caveat:** I found no hard Indian data on how many suppliers have this at volume. Prove it in 10 calls before you build.

**g. Realistic price.** ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 setup, ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 a month, and only above the qualify rule. This is my estimate, anchored above Suvit (from about ₹10,000 a year) and Bizmitra (₹3,999 per user per year) because you do custom matching they do not. For the first pilot, charge little or nothing. That is marketing spend, not income, so it does not need to beat a hire. In return get written permission to name them, before and after numbers, and a short video of the owner.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

## I1. WhatsApp orders from retailers into Tally (paid Indian work only)

**a. Who has it.** FMCG, pharma, hardware, and electrical distributors. Mostly **small**.

**b. Steps today.** Retailers and salesmen send orders on WhatsApp as text, voice notes, and photos of handwritten lists. Staff retype them into Tally and send the bill back on WhatsApp ([Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)).

**c. Software.** WhatsApp Business Platform through a provider (from ₹0.20 per message per [Chat Mitra](https://chatmitra.com/pricing/); [Meta pricing](https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/platform-pricing/)) and TallyPrime or Busy.

**d. How often and how long.** My estimate: 30 to 150 orders a day, 2 to 6 minutes each. Vendors claim 60 to 80% less time after automation (vendor claim, HublerX above).

**e. Who and cost.** One or two billing executives at ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Crowded and cheap: Bizmitra, Rupyz, Takkada, Nexxio, Fast2SMS (links above). They want retailers to order through a catalogue. Voice notes and handwritten photos from retailers who will not change are still retyped. Real gap, small, price sensitive buyer.

**g. Realistic price.** ₹20,000 to ₹40,000 setup, ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 a month. Only if they pay.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

# Region 2: Europe (including the UK)

## E1. Emailed customer orders into Unleashed or Sage 200 (your main product)

**a. Who has it.** B2B wholesalers and distributors (food and drink, building and electrical supplies, industrial parts, packaging, cleaning supplies) and small manufacturers selling to trade customers. **Small** firms on Unleashed: the owner or MD buys. **Medium** firms on Sage 200: the customer service or sales operations manager owns it.

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: a customer emails an order: PDF, Excel, or lines in the email body.
2. The order processor finds the customer in the system.
3. For each line, they translate the customer's code or description to their own SKU and unit.
4. They check price against the customer's price list and check stock.
5. They key the sales order line by line.
6. End: sales order in the system and a confirmation email to the customer.

UK job ads describe this: processing orders received by phone, email, and website into Sage, and entering sales orders following customer instructions ([Jobsite](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor), [Totaljobs](https://www.totaljobs.com/jobs/sales-order-processor), [DWP Find a Job](https://findajob.dwp.gov.uk/details/16605000), [Central Recruitment](https://centralrecruit.co.uk/job/sales-order-processor/)). **Honest gap:** the ads say "Sage", not which Sage. Many will be Sage 50, which you cannot reach. In discovery, ask the system name first and hang up politely on Sage 50.

**c. Software on each side and APIs.**

Who does it and the cost.** UK sales order processors earn about £20,000 to £27,000 a year ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries), [PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/UK/Job=Order_Processor/Salary)). The many rivals are also good news: they prove firms pay for this.

**g. Realistic price.** Your guess of $1,500 to $4,000 for the first workflow is **right for the build on Unleashed.** UK agencies quote £1,000 to £5,000 for one automation ([Axivon](https://www.axivon-ai.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-cost-uk)). Your upkeep guess of $400 to $1,500 a month is **too high at the top for small firms.** UK small business retainers sit around £300 to £900 a month ([AI Workforce](https://www.aiworkforce.co.uk/blogs/ai-automation-pricing-uk-small-businesses-2026)).

| Buyer | Build | Monthly (hosting, AI usage, fixes, new customer formats) |
|---|---|---|
| Small, Unleashed | $2,500 to $3,500 | $400 to $700 |
| Medium, Sage 200 | $5,000 to $7,000 | $900 to $1,500 |

The Sage 200 build is higher because the first one must cover the £4,995 (about $6,500) developer fee. With one Sage 200 client you roughly break even on that fee. You need 2 to 3 before it pays.

**Sanity check for a small client (fixed).** One processor at £24,000. Year one price at $3,000 plus $550 a month is $9,600, about £7,400.
1. At the qualify line (5 hours of a 7.5 hour day), keying costs about £16,000 a year. Remove 60% and the client saves about £9,600. That beats £7,400.
2. At half a day (£12,000), removing 60% saves £7,200. That is less than £7,400. The client loses. That is why the line is 5 hours, not 4.
3. Break even: keying must cost at least £12,300 a year (at 60% removal). Employer costs on top of salary only widen your margin.

## E2. Supplier order confirmations checked against purchase orders (ask, do not plan)

**a. Who has it.** Manufacturers and distributors that buy a lot of stock. Mostly **medium**; the purchasing manager owns it.

**b. Steps today.** The firm sends a PO. The supplier replies with an order confirmation, usually a PDF by email. A purchasing assistant compares price, quantity, and date with the PO, updates the delivery date in the system, flags differences, and chases suppliers who never confirmed. End: every open PO has a confirmed date and price, or a flagged exception. UK ads list these duties ([Jobsite](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/purchasing-assistant), [DWP](https://findajob.dwp.gov.uk/details/16396322), [Lucy Walker Recruitment](https://www.lucywalkerrecruitment.com/job/purchasing-assistant-2/)). A vendor claims up to half of mid market POs never get a formal confirmation (vendor claim, [Leverage](https://tryleverage.ai/blog/pf/po-acknowledgement-automation-hybrid-edi-1)).

**c. Software.** Outlook or Gmail plus Unleashed or Sage 200 (same APIs as E1, purchase side).

**d. How often and how long.** Unknown. My guess of 20 to 100 a day at 3 to 10 minutes has no source. Measure it.

**e. Who and cost.** Purchasing assistants at about £17,000 to £29,000 (ads above).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Netfira, Doxis, DocBits, Hyperfox, and Leverage all do this ([Netfira](https://netfira.com/solutions/process-purchase-order-confirmations-automatically/), [Doxis](https://www.doxis.com/en/solutions/order-confirmation-automation), [DocBits](https://docbits.com/en/order-confirmation-automation/), [Hyperfox](https://www.hyperfox.com/solutions/use-cases/ai-order-confirmation-agent)). None publish prices that I found. So I do not know whether a cheap tool covers SMBs. That is why E2 is a question, not a plan.

**g. Realistic price.** If an E1 client asks for it: $2,000 to $3,500 build (you reuse the engine), $400 to $900 a month.

# Final top picks

1. **E1 on Unleashed for small UK wholesalers.** Named job role with £20,000 to £27,000 salaries, no built in agent, API at no cost to you, and a buyer who already pays hundreds a month for software. It fits your skills: Developer A's fintech review flows match "draft, check, approve"; Developer B's Node skills fit the mail watcher and connector.
2. **I2 in Ludhiana as the case study, free or cheap, above the India qualify rule.** Same engine, Tally connector.
3. **E1 on Sage 200 for medium UK firms, only after a signed deal covers the developer fee.**

**Not recommended:** anything on Business Central or Cin7 Core (built in agent), Sage 50 (no usable API), supplier bill entry, statement reconciliation, GST reconciliation, payment reminders (cheap tools, sources in the matrix). Exact Online waits until you can sell in Dutch.

## What to do next, in order

1. **Weeks 1 to 2: 10 calls.** 5 Ludhiana suppliers that get emailed POs, 5 UK wholesalers on Unleashed. Ask: which system, orders per day, minutes per order, who keys them, what goes wrong, have they seen Logistify. Walk away from anyone under the qualify rules.
2. **Also call 3 Unleashed partners (implementers) in the UK.** They know which clients drown in emailed orders.
3. **Weeks 3 to 6: build for the Indian pilot.** These parts, nothing more:
   1. Mail inbox watcher (one mailbox per client).
   2. LLM extraction into a fixed JSON order schema.
   3. Matching against the client's customers, items, and a customer code table you maintain.
   4. Checks: unknown item, price differs, unusual quantity.
   5. One review screen where the processor edits and approves.
   6. One connector: Tally XML (India) or Unleashed API (UK). Keep it the only part that changes.
4. **Weeks 7 to 10: run the pilot for 4 weeks and write the case study.**
