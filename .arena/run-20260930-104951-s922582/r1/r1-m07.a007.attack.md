ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The lead product is already a built-in feature on two of the five ERPs it names as targets
Where: Europe pick 1, section c table ("Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central ... Yes", "Odoo ... Yes"), section f "Gap: small and medium firms on Exact, Business Central, Odoo, weclapp, or Unleashed", and the warning "Expect ERP vendors and startups to cover more of it within 12 to 24 months (Assumption)".
Problem: The task says: "If it is already solved by a built in feature or a cheap tool, reject it." Business Central already has this. Its Sales Order Agent went generally available in April 2026 (https://erpsoftwareblog.com/2026/06/business-central-sales-order-agent/, https://tigunia.com/blog/complete-guide-to-business-central-ai-agents/). It reads customer orders from a shared Microsoft 365 inbox, drafts the quote or order in BC, and a human reviews it. It supports English, Dutch, German, French, Danish, Swedish, Finnish and other languages. It costs about 14 Copilot credits per order at $0.01 each, so roughly $0.14 an order (https://erpsoftwareblog.com/2026/09/what-does-it-cost-to-run-ai-agents-in-business-central/). Odoo has several Apps Store modules that turn a customer PO PDF into a draft quotation with AI line matching (https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/jjro_ai_sales_capture). So the "gap closing in 12 to 24 months" is not a future risk. It had already closed before the date of this report. A BC Online client quoted $600 to $1,200 a month will compare that with about $6 a month in credits for 40 orders and say no. The report missed this because its only Business Central link is the purchaseOrderLine update page, which says nothing about sales orders. It never checked what BC itself ships. Pick 1, the matching India to Europe case study story, and the 90-day plan ("ask them which ERP they use ... qualify out Sage 50 desktop") all send the user toward BC and Odoo buyers, where the product is already solved.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The recommended prices fail the report's own ROI rule, including at its own qualifying volume
Where: Europe pick 1 e ("the saving is about £13,500 a year, or £1,100 a month. That supports a £400 to £700 monthly fee"); price check ("Your fee should stay under about a third of the measured monthly saving"); break case 3 ("Europe: at least 15 POs a day ... Price below a third of the measured saving"); pick 1 g ("Small ... $300 to $500 a month").
Problem: A third of £1,100 is about £365. So even the report's own best case example cannot support its £400 to £700 range. Now take its minimum qualifying client: 15 POs × 8 min = 2 hours a day, or about 0.27 of a full time person. At £32,200 fully loaded, that is about £8,700 a year. At 60 percent removed, the saving is about £5,200 a year, or £435 a month. A third of that is about £145 a month, yet the small tier is $300 to $500 a month. India has the same problem. At the 100 orders a day threshold, 100 × 4 min = 6.7 hours, about 0.85 of a ₹15,000 to ₹17,000 clerk. A 60 percent saving is about ₹8,000 a month, and a third of that is about ₹2,700, below the ₹3,000 floor of the ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 range. A user who follows the price table will quote fees that fail the report's own "one third of saving" test. The table that "confirms or corrects" the user's price guess cannot be trusted.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Exact Online is marked "Yes" for sales order creation, but most small Exact customers do not have the sales order API
Where: Europe pick 1 c table: "Exact Online | Netherlands, Belgium | Yes. REST SalesOrders endpoint".
Problem: Exact's own knowledge base says the sales order API methods only apply to Exact Online Wholesale Distribution or Manufacturing subscriptions (https://support.exactonline.com/community/s/article/All-All-DNO-Content-rest-api-business-cases-rest-bsncs-slsord). A small Dutch firm on an accounting-only Exact plan, the most common Exact customer, has no SalesOrders endpoint. The report's rule "qualify on the ERP first" checks only the ERP name, not the edition. So the user will book a sales call, promise a build, and find there is nowhere to write the order. Add Attack 1, which removes BC and Odoo, and the realistic target ERP list gets much smaller than the table shows.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The India builds cannot reach Tally the way the report sets them up, and the report gives no bridge
Where: India pick 1 c: "TallyPrime accepts XML over HTTP on port 9000 on the local machine; it ... runs on a local PC". Tooling: "self hosted n8n community edition and your own Spring Boot service on one small VPS". The 90-day plan, weeks 1 to 6, goes live with Ludhiana Tally clients.
Problem: A VPS cannot call localhost:9000 on a shop's office PC behind a home or office router. To work, you need a small agent on the client PC that polls the VPS and posts XML to Tally, or a tunnel. TallyPrime must also be open with the right company loaded, and the XML port must be turned on. The report does not describe any of this, but both India picks and the first six weeks of the plan depend on it. Break case 2 says "Tally PC switched off" and offers only an alert, not a way to connect at all. The user, who has to build this, would be guessing at the main design choice for the proof-of-work clients.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The support time zone advice goes stale in under four weeks
Where: "India is 4.5 hours ahead of the UK in summer and 3.5 hours ahead of Central Europe. You will cover their mornings well and their afternoons badly."
Problem: The report is dated 30 September 2026. European clocks go back on 25 October 2026. After that, India is 5.5 hours ahead of the UK and 4.5 hours ahead of Central Europe until late March. That is about five months of the first year, which is when the first paid European pilot (weeks 8 to 12) will run. The overlap the support promise depends on is an hour smaller than stated for most of that period.
