Choco OrderAgent does the same for food distributors in the UK and Europe (https://choco.com/us/orderagent), and "food and drink wholesale" is one of the answer's own target segments. For Odoo, the Odoo Apps Store already sells AI modules that turn an uploaded customer PO PDF into a draft quotation (https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/jjro_ai_sales_capture, https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/18.0/dx_sale_digitization). Odoo is one of the five named targets, and these are exactly the "cheap tool" the task says to reject. The main selling reason for pick 1 ("a big price gap below Rossum and Conexiom") compares against the wrong competitors. The user would walk into sales calls with Exact or Odoo distributors and hear "we already looked at Hyperfox" or "there's an Odoo app for that". The pick might still work in a narrower niche, but the answer never names that niche.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The India workflow 2 price is higher than the labour it saves, which breaks the answer's own pricing rule

Problem: Use the answer's own numbers at its walk away floor. 10 POs × 10 minutes = 100 minutes a day, about 21% of an 8 hour day. 21% of a ₹25,000 salary is about ₹5,200 a month saved. Under the answer's own "a third to a half" rule, the owner pays about ₹1,700 to ₹2,600 a month. The quoted upkeep is ₹5,000 to ₹12,000 a month, plus ₹40,000 to ₹1,00,000 setup. The answer ran this value check for India workflow 1 and skipped it for workflow 2. Yet workflow 2 is named the first case study (Part 7 pick 2) and the thing to build in "Weeks 1 to 3". That leaves the user holding a price list that an Indian owner can beat just by keeping the clerk. That is the exact "hire another person" test the task tells us to apply to India.
