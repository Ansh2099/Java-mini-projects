# Workflows Worth Selling: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Here is the part you will not like: in the UK, almost every clean finance workflow on a mainstream ledger (Xero, QuickBooks, Sage 50, Sage 200, Business Central) already has a cheap product. I checked the ideas people suggest first, and then I checked my own best idea, and it failed too on those ledgers. What is left is narrower, but it is real.

**Pick 1 (build this first): supplier statement reconciliation, only on ledgers the cheap tools skip.** A supplier sends a monthly statement. Someone checks every line against the ledger, finds missing invoices, missing credit notes and wrong amounts, and emails the supplier. On Xero and QuickBooks, Dext already does this inside its normal plans. On Sage, Business Central and NetSuite, Lightyear does it, including the chase email, from about 149 USD a month. So you do **not** sell it there. You sell it to medium firms on **Exact Online in the Netherlands** (Exact serves 675,000+ businesses, mainly in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany, and neither Dext nor Lightyear lists it), and to any other firm whose ledger passes the 4 step competitor check in Step 2. You prove it first in India as "party ledger reconciliation" in Tally, which is the same matching engine.

**Pick 2 (India only, local income): export shipment document cross-check** for Ludhiana exporters. It checks the customs agent's checklist and the draft bill of lading against your invoice and packing list before anyone approves them. Useful and local. It will not help you sell in Europe.

**Not a pick, but know it: emailed purchase order to draft sales order.** Strongest labour case of anything here, and the most crowded. Business Central, QuickBooks, Cin7, Unleashed and Exact Online all have a product or add-on for it already. Only take it if the Step 2 check comes back empty for that client's ERP.

**Your Europe price guess is half right.** 1,500 to 4,000 USD for the first workflow is fine for medium firms. 400 to 1,500 USD a month for upkeep is too high for one workflow. Plan on 290 to 530 USD a month (€250 to €450) for statement reconciliation.

**The hardest part is not the build.** It is getting a Dutch or British finance manager to trust two unknown developers in Ludhiana with their ledger. The plan at the end deals with that directly.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK plus EU countries where finance teams work comfortably in English (UK, Ireland, Netherlands, Nordics, Belgium). Germany and France are harder for you because of language and local accounting tools.
2. Exchange rates used, rounded: 1 GBP = 1.33 USD, 1 EUR = 1.17 USD, 1 USD = 88 INR. Check the day's rate before you quote.
3. "Loaded" staff cost = salary plus about 20% for employer taxes and pension in the UK. 1,950 working hours a year.
4. Anything marked **(estimate, measure in discovery)** has no source. Do not quote it to a buyer until you have timed it at a real client.
5. About sources: every link came up in my web searches in September 2026. Open the three or four numbers you plan to put in a sales deck and check them yourself first.

**What survives, and why.** The pattern is simple. Once a workflow is generic enough to describe in one sentence, a product builds it for the three or four biggest ledgers first (Xero, QuickBooks, Sage, Business Central). Products get to the smaller and regional ledgers late, because each connector costs them the same work for fewer customers. That gap is your only honest opening in Europe: **the same proven workflow, on a ledger the products have not reached yet.** It is a smaller market, and it closes over time, so you move fast and pick one ledger.

## Step 2: Europe (including the UK)

### The 4 step competitor check (run it before every sales call)

The brief says reject anything a built in feature or cheap tool already solves. For your workflows, the answer depends on the client's ledger, so you check per client, not once.

1. Is the ledger Xero or QuickBooks Online? Then Dext covers statement reconciliation. Stop.
2. Is the ledger on Lightyear's integrations list (Xero, QBO, MYOB, Sage 50, Sage 200, Sage Intacct, Business Central, NetSuite, AccountsIQ, Iplicit and others)? Open the current list on lightyear.cloud. If it is there, stop.
3. Does the ledger vendor sell its own add-on? Search the vendor's app store for "statement" and "reconciliation" (or "order" and "PDF" for order entry).
4. Search "<ledger name> supplier statement reconciliation" and the local language version. If a product under about 300 USD a month shows up, stop.

If all four come back empty, the client qualifies. Save a screenshot of each search in the client folder. When the finance manager asks "why not just buy a tool?", you show them.

### E1. Supplier statement reconciliation on uncovered ledgers (TOP PICK)

**a. Who has it, and size.**
Wholesalers, distributors, manufacturers, construction contractors and multi-site hospitality groups that buy on credit from many suppliers. **Medium (50 to 250 staff): the target.** Small (5 to 49): only if they pass the same qualifier as everyone else, which is **100 or more supplier statements received a month.** That one number is the qualifier everywhere in this document.

Where to find them:
* **Netherlands, on Exact Online.** Exact serves more than 675,000 businesses, mainly in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany ([Exact about us](https://www.exact.com/about-us), [Wikipedia on Exact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_(company))). Neither the Dext statements feature (Xero and QBO) nor the Lightyear lists I found include Exact. Dutch B2B e-invoicing is only mandatory from 1 July 2030, so PDF invoices and PDF statements stay common for almost four more years ([Sovos](https://sovos.com/blog/global-vat/netherlands-b2b-e-invoicing-mandate-2030/), [vatcalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/netherlands/netherlands-reluctant-on-domestic-e-invoicing/), [Meijburg](https://www.meijburg.com/news/dutch-government-opts-mandatory-e-invoicing-and-digital-reporting-b2b-transactions)). Belgium is weaker: e-invoicing has been mandatory there since January 2026, so fewer invoices go missing.
* **Other ledgers to run the check on:** AFAS and Twinfield (Netherlands), Visma and Fortnox (Nordics), Procountor and Netvisor (Finland, where Developer A already has a client), Odoo, and older UK mid-market ledgers. I have **not** verified these. Run the 4 step check for each.

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: a supplier emails a statement (PDF, sometimes Excel) to the accounts inbox, usually in the first week of the month.
2. A purchase ledger clerk opens that supplier's account in the ledger.
3. They tick each statement line against the ledger: invoices, credit notes, payments.
4. For lines on the statement but not in the ledger, they search email for the invoice or ask the supplier for a copy.
5. For amounts that differ, they check the original invoice and the purchase order.
6. They email the supplier about missing credits or wrong amounts.
7. End: the reconciliation is saved (often in Excel), and the account is marked "agreed" or "differences open."

A UK vendor in this space, Fiscal, says most teams only manage to reconcile their top 10 to 20 suppliers and the rest go unchecked ([Fiscal guide](https://fiscaltec.com/supplier-statement-reconciliation-ultimate-guide/), [Fiscal on the process](https://fiscaltec.com/supplier-statement-reconciliation-what-it-is-why-it-matters-for-ap/)).

**c. Software on each side.**
* Inbox: Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace. In the default design you need **no access to it** (see build step 1).
* Ledger:
  * **Exact Online:** REST API. Standard limits are 60 calls per company per minute and 5,000 per day, which is plenty for one reconciliation run a month ([Exact API limits](https://support.exactonline.com/community/s/article/All-All-DNO-Simulation-gen-apilimits?language=en_GB), [Apideck Exact guide](https://www.apideck.com/blog/guide-to-exact-online-api-integration), [Peliqan on Exact API](https://peliqan.io/blog/exact-online-api/)). API access follows the rights of the Exact user who authorises the app, so the client creates a user with read only rights on financial data **(check the exact role names in the client's Exact admin during setup)**.
  * Odoo Online: external API only on the Custom plan, not Standard. Check before you quote ([Odoo external API docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/developer/reference/external_api.html), [Odoo forum](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/does-the-external-api-really-require-the-custom-plan-on-odoo-online-or-is-there-a-trial-window-306032)).
* That is 2 tools (inbox plus ledger), 3 if they keep a shared Excel reconciliation file.

**d. How often and how long.**
Each supplier, once a month. Fiscal puts an average manual statement at about 30 minutes and a complex one at around 2 hours ([Fiscal on cost of errors](https://fiscaltec.com/the-cost-of-errors-in-statement-reconciliation-what-ap-teams-must-know/), [Fiscal fact sheet, May 2026](https://fiscaltec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Statement-Reconciliation-Fact-Sheet-May-2026.pdf), [Klearstack](https://klearstack.com/blogs/supplier-invoice-reconciliation-process-best-practices)).

**e. Who does it and what it costs.**
A purchase ledger clerk. UK proof that firms pay for this role: average salary £26,802 (Indeed, updated 1 August 2026), £26,000 to £32,750 outside London and £35,250 to £44,500 in London (Robert Half), and job ads name "supplier statement reconciliations" as a duty ([Robert Half](https://www.roberthalf.com/gb/en/job-details/purchase-ledger-clerk), [Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/purchase-ledger-clerk/salaries)). Westgate Moore sells supplier statement reconciliation as an outsourced service, which shows firms pay outsiders to do it ([Westgate Moore](https://www.westgatemoore.co.uk/statement-recs/)).
For the Netherlands, I did **not** find a sourced salary for a "crediteurenadministrateur." Use about €25 an hour loaded as a working figure **(estimate, measure in discovery)** and check Dutch job ads before your first call.

**The brutal math.** At 30 minutes a statement:

| Case | Statements a month | Manual hours | Labour (UK, £16/h) | Labour (NL, €25/h estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qualifier floor, all reconciled | 100 | 50 h | about £800 | about €1,250 |
| Same firm, only top 20 done today | 20 | 10 h | about £160 | about €250 |

So at the floor, your fee (€250 to €450) is about a third of the cost of doing the job properly, and about equal to what they spend today on the top 20. You sell on two things:
1. **Coverage:** every statement checked, not the top 20.
2. **Money found:** Fiscal says its users find missing credits averaging £13,000 and missing invoices averaging £8,000. These are a vendor's own marketing claims, not audited facts, but they show where the value is ([Fiscal fact sheet](https://fiscaltec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Statement-Reconciliation-Fact-Sheet-May-2026.pdf)).

Below 100 statements a month, the fee is higher than the full job's labour saving and you lose the renewal. Walk away.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* Dext Supplier Statements: Xero and QBO only. Included in all Dext plans.
* Lightyear: statement matching plus supplier chase emails on Xero, QBO, Sage 50, Sage 200, Business Central, NetSuite, Sage Intacct and more. Not Exact Online on the lists I found.
* Fiscal, Xelix, Basware, Kefron: "any ERP" statement tools, sold inside full accounts payable platforms and aimed at larger finance teams ([Fiscal](https://fiscaltec.com/solutions/statement-reconciliation/), [Xelix](https://xelix.com/xelix-supplier-statement-reconciliation), [Basware](https://www.basware.com/en/solutions/ap-automation/statement-matching), [Kefron](https://kefron.com/finance-automation/accounts-payable-automation/features/automated-supplier-statement-reconciliation)).
* SupplierRecon: a browser tool that matches a CSV or Excel statement against a ledger export you upload by hand. No PDF reading, no ledger connection, no supplier email ([SupplierRecon](https://supplierrecon.com/)).
* Exact Online itself: matches bank statements to open items ("afletteren"), not supplier statements ([Exact docs on afletteren](https://www.exactsoftware.com/docs/DocView.aspx?DocumentID=%7B91e11175-f81f-4644-a9d0-751a18115e0e%7D)).

**The gap, stated honestly:** a medium Dutch firm on Exact Online can buy an enterprise AP platform or a manual Excel matcher, and nothing in between that I could find. That gap is real today. It will close if Lightyear or Dext adds an Exact connector, so check their lists every quarter, and lock in clients on 12 month terms.

**Biggest open risk:** I found strong UK evidence that clerks spend time on supplier statements. I did not find Dutch evidence of the same volume. Your first 5 Dutch calls must answer one question before anything else: "How many supplier statements do you receive a month?" If the answers are mostly under 100, Dutch firms do not work this way and you move to the next ledger on the list.

**How you would build it (3 to 4 weeks part time).**

1. **Intake with no mailbox permission.** The client adds one Outlook or Gmail rule that forwards supplier statements to `statements+<client>@yourdomain.com`. An n8n IMAP trigger on your own mailbox picks up the attachment. You never touch the client's Microsoft tenant.
2. **Extraction and the right balance check.** The LLM returns JSON: `supplier_name`, `supplier_vat_or_kvk`, `statement_date`, `statement_type` (`open_item` or `balance_forward`), `opening_balance` (null for open item), `rows[] {doc_date, doc_number, doc_type, amount}`, `closing_balance`. Then plain code checks:
   * open item statement: sum of rows = closing balance (within 0.01)
   * balance forward statement: opening balance + sum of rows = closing balance
   If the check fails, a human sees it. The brought forward line is a field, never a row, so it does not break the sum.
3. **Find the supplier.** Match the statement to one ledger account by VAT or KvK number, then IBAN, then fuzzy name. Save the mapping so it only happens once per supplier.
4. **Pull the ledger for that one supplier, as of the statement date.** In Exact Online, read the transaction lines filtered to that supplier account and dated up to the statement date plus 10 days (for payments in transit). Check the field names in Exact's REST reference when you build it.
   * For an **open item** statement: rebuild which invoices and credit notes were still open on the statement date (document amount minus payments dated on or before that date). Compare the statement rows to that open list. Do not compare to "this month's entries." That is what caused false alarms in naive designs: an unpaid May invoice on a September statement is correct, not missing.
   * For a **balance forward** statement: compare the ledger balance on the day before the period to the statement's opening balance, then compare the period's entries line by line.
5. **Matching engine (Developer A, Spring Boot and PostgreSQL, plain code, no AI).** Normalise document numbers (strip spaces, prefixes, leading zeros). Match on number plus amount, then amount plus date within 5 days. Tag every leftover as one of: missing invoice, missing credit note, amount difference, **payment in transit** (we paid within 10 days before the statement date, supplier has not booked it), payment not on statement (older than 10 days), on our ledger but not on their statement, or possible duplicate.
6. **Exception screen (Developer B, Node and TypeScript).** The clerk sees only the differences. Buttons: Approve, Edit, Ignore. Every click is logged with user and time.
7. **Reply to the supplier.** On approve, the screen shows a ready email (in Dutch or English) with the missing documents listed, and an "Open in Outlook" button that fills a new message. The clerk sends it from their own mailbox. You never send email.
   * If a client insists on drafts appearing in a shared mailbox, register an app with Graph Mail.ReadWrite, but **scope it**: in Exchange Online, create a management scope for that one mailbox (`New-ManagementScope`), assign the Application Mail.ReadWrite role to your app with that scope (`New-ManagementRoleAssignment ... -CustomResourceScope`), test with `Test-ServicePrincipalAuthorization`, and **remove the unscoped Mail.ReadWrite consent in Entra ID**. Microsoft warns that Entra and Exchange grants add together, so leaving the Entra consent in place means no scoping at all. RBAC for Applications replaces the older Application Access Policies ([Microsoft Learn: RBAC for Applications](https://learn.microsoft.com/exchange/permissions-exo/application-rbac), [Microsoft Learn: Application Access Policies (legacy)](https://learn.microsoft.com/exchange/permissions-exo/application-access-policies)).
8. **Read only on the ledger.** You never post, change or pay anything.

**g. Realistic price.**
* Build: €1,800 to €3,000 (about 2,100 to 3,500 USD).
* Monthly: €250 to €450 (about 290 to 530 USD), 12 month term, including hosting, model costs, new supplier formats and a monthly "money found" summary.
* Pitch line: "We check every supplier statement against Exact every month, and your clerk only sees the differences and the reply emails."

**h. Sources:** inline above, all listed at the end.

### E2. Emailed customer purchase order to draft sales order (conditional only, do not lead)

**a. Who has it, and size.** Wholesalers and distributors (electrical, packaging, industrial supplies, building materials, food service) and B2B manufacturers. **Medium: strong fit** (30 to 200 emailed orders a day). Small: only with 15 or more emailed orders a day.

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: a customer emails a purchase order as PDF, Excel or plain text to orders@.
2. A sales order processor finds the customer account.
3. They translate the customer's part numbers into their own product codes.
4. They check prices against the price list or contract.
5. They key the order into the ERP line by line.
6. End: they send an order confirmation back.

**c. Software.** Outlook or Gmail, plus the ERP. Business Central has a `salesOrders` API endpoint ([Knit BC guide](https://getknit.dev/blog/microsoft-business-central-api-guide-in-depth/), [iPaaS BC endpoints](https://support.ipaas.com/en/articles/12314582-microsoft-dynamics-365-business-central-api-endpoints)). Xero's accounting API has no sales order object, so Xero firms run a separate stock system. Exact Online has a REST API (see E1).

**d. How often and how long.** Every order, every day. **(Estimate, measure in discovery): 4 to 8 minutes per order.**

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** A sales order processor. UK averages: £20,999 (Jobsite), £26,832 (Indeed), London about £24,233 (Glassdoor) ([Jobsite](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor), [Indeed](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries), [Glassdoor London](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Salaries/london-united-kingdom-order-processor-salary-SRCH_IL.0,21_IM1035_KO22,37.htm)). Example: 60 orders a day at 5 minutes is about 105 hours a month, roughly £1,500 of labour. The money case is much stronger than E1.

**f. Existing tools and the gap. This is why it is not a pick.**
* ERP built in or vendor add-on: Cin7 IDR turns a PDF PO into a draft sales order inside Cin7 Core ([Cin7 IDR](https://www.cin7.com/features/sales/idr/), [Cin7 help](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/10553586503439-Extract-sale-data-with-Intelligent-Document-Recognition-IDR)).
* ERP specific: Logistify for Unleashed; Tamboer AI and virtualworkforce.ai for Exact Online ([Logistify](https://logistify.ai/blog/ai-pdf-sales-order-entry-for-unleashed-software), [Tamboer AI](https://www.tamboerai.nl/en/order-processing-wholesale), [virtualworkforce.ai](https://virtualworkforce.ai/data-entry-automation-for-exact-orders/)).
* General: Conexiom (40+ prebuilt ERP integrations, enterprise), OrderPier (self serve, Business Central, QuickBooks or webhook), Ordermatic, Elentaria, StackCube, ChannelFlex, Xorosoft, Lido ([ERP Research on Conexiom](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom), [OrderPier](https://orderpier.com/), [Ordermatic](https://ordermatic.co/), [Elentaria](https://elentaria.ai/compare/elentaria-vs-conexiom), [StackCube](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors), [ChannelFlex](https://channelflex.com/blog/sales-order-automation-for-distributors-and-manufacturers/), [Xorosoft](https://xorosoft.com/sales-order-automation-distributors/), [Lido](https://www.lido.app/blog/best-sales-order-automation-software)).

**The gap:** I could not name an ERP where it is clearly open. The OrderPier style webhook products can reach almost any ERP with an API. Only take this job if the 4 step check (with "order" and "PDF" as search words) comes back empty for that client's ERP **and** the client has messy part number mapping that a self serve tool will not handle.

**Build notes.** Same intake as E1. LLM extracts header and lines. Plain code maps customer part numbers through a table built from the last 6 months of orders, checks prices, and creates a **draft** sales order. Exceptions go to the same approval screen.

**g. Realistic price (only if it qualifies).** Build £2,000 to £3,500. Monthly £400 to £800 at 50+ orders a day. Aim for payback within 3 months, because a product will undercut you later.

## Step 3: India

**The honest frame first.** Indian SMB software is priced very low: Vyapar TaxOne from ₹899 a month, Takkada ₹2,900 to ₹8,500 a year. A data entry operator in Ludhiana earns roughly ₹10,000 to ₹23,000 a month, a Tally accountant ₹15,000 to ₹45,000 ([Suvit](https://www.suvit.io/pricing), [Takkada](https://takkada.com/), [Glassdoor Ludhiana data entry](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/ludhiana-data-entry-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,8_IC2939907_KO9,19.htm), [Apna Ludhiana data entry](https://apna.co/jobs/data_entry-jobs-in-ludhiana), [Jooble Ludhiana Tally accountant](https://in.jooble.org/jobs-tally-accountant/Ludhiana)).

So in India you will not win on "we replace a salary." A monthly fee above ₹8,000 is a hard sell for a small firm. You win only where **errors or speed cost more than a salary**, or where the work is a rehearsal for what you sell in Europe.

### I1. Party ledger reconciliation in Tally (TOP INDIA PICK, mirror of E1)

**a. Who has it, and size.** Traders, distributors, manufacturers, and their CA firms. **Small and medium.** Businesses reconcile big parties quarterly and everyone at year end. CA firms need party balance confirmations for audits, which run from April to the tax audit deadline. This year CBDT moved the tax audit report date from 30 September to **21 October 2026** and the ITR date for audit cases to **21 November 2026** ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/cbdt-extends-ay-2026-27-tax-audit-date-21-october-2026.html), [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-cbdt-extends-itr-due-date-tax-audit-cases-ay-2026-27-47142.html/), [CAclubindia](https://www.caclubindia.com/news/cbdt-extends-tax-audit-due-date-for-ay-2026-27-to-21st-october-2026-itr-deadline-moved-to-21st-november-26821.asp)). So CA firms are in their final crunch now and will not try a new tool until December. Plan around that (Step 8).

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: a supplier or customer sends their ledger of your account (PDF or Excel) by email or WhatsApp, or the auditor asks for it.
2. The accountant exports the same party's ledger from Tally to Excel.
3. They match line by line in Excel.
4. They explain differences. Common Indian causes: TDS deducted by one side and not booked by the other, GST adjustments, debit or credit notes not booked, payments in transit, typing errors ([OneFinOps](https://onefinops.com/blog/vendor-payment-reconciliation-guide), [Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/debtors-creditors-reconciliation-india/), [ClearTax on 26AS](https://cleartax.in/s/26as-reconciliation)).
5. End: correction entries in Tally (after owner or CA approval) and a confirmation or difference list sent to the party.

**c. Software.** WhatsApp or Gmail on one side, TallyPrime on the other. Tally talks XML over HTTP, by default on port 9000 of the PC running Tally. **Practical catch:** Tally usually runs on an office PC, so you need a small connector on that PC, or the client uses Tally on a cloud server. Sort this out in the first meeting.
Same two statement types as Europe: if the firm keeps bill-wise details in Tally, compare an open item ledger against the party's bill-wise outstanding as on the statement date. If not, treat it as balance forward: opening balance plus the period's vouchers.

**d. How often and how long.** Big parties quarterly, all parties at year end. **(Estimate, measure in discovery): 20 to 60 minutes per party.**

**e. Who does it.** The in-house accountant (₹15,000 to ₹45,000 a month in Ludhiana), or article assistants at the CA firm.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* Tally Auditor edition has an external confirmation report, but it shows only the balance difference, not which lines cause it ([TallyHelp](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/Tally.ERP9/Auditor_Edition/India/Statutory_Audit/External_Confirmation_third_Party.htm)).
* Takkada keeps customer ledgers close to matched by linking UPI payments to bills, but only on the receivable side for distributors ([Takkada](https://takkada.com/blog/ledger-reconciliation-tally-distributor/)).
* Terra Insight and AI Accountant sell reconciliation tools for India ([Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/debtors-creditors-reconciliation-india/), [AI Accountant](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/automated-statement-reconciliation-india)).
* **Gap:** reading the other party's own PDF or WhatsApp ledger, matching it line by line to Tally with Indian reason codes (TDS, GST, debit note), and drafting the reply. **Honest warning:** tools exist here too. Treat India as your test bed and case study source, not your profit.

**g. Realistic price.**
* Trader or manufacturer: ₹20,000 to ₹40,000 setup, ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 a month.
* CA firm: ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 per audit season (April to October) for up to about 500 party ledgers.
* First 2 clients: free or at cost, **in writing, in exchange for the right to publish real numbers and name the client.**

**h. Sources:** inline above.

### I2. Export shipment document cross-check (India only)

**a. Who has it.** Ludhiana and Jalandhar exporters of hand tools, hosiery and bicycle parts. **Small and medium.** Local press puts about ₹10,000 crore of Ludhiana exports at risk from US tariffs, which pushes these firms toward Europe and new buyers with new document rules ([Tribune Ludhiana](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/ludhiana/citys-export-lifeline-under-threat-10k-cr-at-risk-amid-us-tariff-shock/)).

**b. Steps today.** The exporter already makes the invoice and packing list (in Export EMS, ExpoMaster or Excel). Then the customs agent (CHA) sends a shipping bill checklist and the forwarder sends a draft bill of lading. The documentation executive checks every field (buyer name, HS code, quantities, weights, value, container numbers) against the invoice, packing list and buyer's PO, then approves or asks for changes.

**c. Software.** Email or WhatsApp, the export document tool or Excel, and Tally for the export invoice.

**d. Frequency and time.** 5 to 40 shipments a month. **(Estimate, measure in discovery): 30 to 60 minutes per shipment.**

**e. Who does it.** An export documentation executive, ₹12,000 to ₹35,000 a month in Ludhiana. Local job ads for this exact role show firms pay a salary for it ([PlacementIndia Ludhiana](https://www.placementindia.com/job-search/export-documentation-executive-jobs-in-ludhiana.htm), [Indeed Ludhiana export jobs](https://in.indeed.com/Export-Import-jobs-in-Ludhiana,-Punjab)).

**f. Existing tools and gap.** The tools in Step 1 **generate** export documents. None I found **checks the documents other companies send back** against your own. A mistake caught after the bill of lading is issued means amendment fees and delay.

**g. Price.** ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 setup, ₹3,000 to ₹5,000 a month.

**h. Sources:** inline above.

**Why it is only a local pick:** it earns local money and referrals, but a European buyer does not care about Indian shipping bills.

### Unproven, test in discovery only: WhatsApp or email PO to sales order in Tally or Busy

Ludhiana factories whose business customers send POs as PDFs or photos could use the E2 engine on Tally or Busy. Ludhiana has about 1.5 lakh registered MSMEs ([SME Communities](https://smecommunities.com/ludhiana-punjabs-industrial-powerhouse-driving-indias-next-sme-revolution/), [DC MSME Ludhiana profile](https://dcmsme.gov.in/old/dips/Ludhiana.pdf)). But I found **no job ads, paid tools or outsourcing** that prove firms spend money on this step, and an owner can hire another clerk for about ₹15,000. So it does not meet your evidence test. Ask about it in every Ludhiana visit. If three owners name a person who spends 2+ hours a day on it and a real cost from wrong dispatches, it becomes a workflow. Until then, do not build it.

## Step 4: Workflows in both regions (your case study bridge)

| India version | Europe version | Same parts you reuse | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1 Party ledger reconciliation in Tally | E1 Supplier statement reconciliation on Exact Online (or another uncovered ledger) | Statement reading, two type balance check, "as of date" open item logic, matching engine, exception screen, reply draft | Ledger connector (Tally XML to Exact REST), reason codes (TDS and GST vs VAT and credit notes), reply channel (WhatsApp to Outlook), language (English and Hindi to Dutch and English) |

**Why this is a believable bridge:** the European buyer's real question is "does your matching work on messy statements from many different suppliers?" An Indian case study answers that honestly, because Indian party ledgers are messier (TDS, GST, handwritten notes, WhatsApp photos). Write it with numbers only: "412 party ledgers, 87% of lines matched with no human touch, 61 differences found worth ₹X, accountant time cut from Y hours to Z hours."

**Capture these from day one** (in PostgreSQL): statements processed, statement type, lines per statement, percent of lines matched with no human touch, exceptions per statement by tag, false alarms (exceptions the clerk marked Ignore), minutes of review per statement (time the approval screen), and money value of differences found. The false alarm rate is the number a European finance manager will ask about.

There is no honest bridge for the order entry workflow, because it is not a pick in either region.

## Step 7: Final top picks and why

**1. Supplier statement reconciliation, on Exact Online in the Netherlands first, proven in India on Tally.**
* It passes the brief's rejection test only on ledgers the cheap tools skip. On Xero, QuickBooks, Sage and Business Central it fails, and I say so plainly.
* Dutch PDF invoices stay legal until July 2030, so the problem lasts long enough to earn back your work.
* The core is plain matching code Developer A can make reliable. AI only reads the statement, and the type aware balance check catches its mistakes.
* Read only on the ledger, and no access to the client's mailbox in the default design. That makes the security conversation short.
* The India version is the same engine, so your Indian case study is honest proof.
* Weaknesses: the gap can close when Lightyear or Dext adds Exact; I have no Dutch proof of statement volume yet; and you must sell to Dutch firms from Ludhiana. The first 5 calls test all three.

**2. Export document cross-check (India only).** Take it if a Ludhiana exporter will pay. Do not spend European sales time on it.

**What I would not do:** invoice capture, supplier statements on Xero, QuickBooks, Sage or Business Central, order entry on any ERP with a product, WhatsApp order bots, GST reconciliation, freight quoting, or supplier confirmation checks. Each has a cheap or funded product, or no proof of spend.

## Step 8: The next 90 days, with dates

1. **1 to 14 October 2026:** Build the extractor (both statement types), the "as of date" ledger logic and the matching engine against sample data. Get 3 real statements and ledgers from a friendly business. Target: 80% of lines matched with no human touch and under 1 false alarm per statement.
2. **15 October to 15 November: India pilot 1, a trader, not a CA firm.** Pick a Ludhiana distributor or manufacturer with 100+ parties on Tally. Reconcile its top 30 parties as of 30 September 2026 (their quarter end). Free, with written permission to publish numbers. Stay away from CA firms until 21 November: they are filing audit reports and audit ITRs.
3. **22 November to 31 December: India pilot 2, a CA firm, as a back test.** After the audit ITR date, CA firms have slack. Run the tool on this season's finished balance confirmation files, with the auditee's written consent, where the right answers are already known. That gives you a clean accuracy number. Then sign the firm for the April to October 2027 season at ₹15,000 to ₹30,000.
4. **1 to 15 December:** Write one case study page with real numbers and a 3 minute screen recording of the exception screen.
5. **Weeks 6 to 13 (mid November to end of December): Europe discovery, through channels.**
   * Ask Developer A's Finnish client for 3 introductions to finance managers, and run the 4 step check on their ledgers.
   * Contact Exact Online partners and Dutch administratiekantoren (bookkeeping firms). Offer white label work: they have the clients and the trust.
   * Ask every prospect: "How many supplier statements do you receive a month, and how many do you reconcile?" **Under 100 received: move on.**
6. **Stop rule:** if 10 calls with firms that pass the 4 step check and the 100 statement qualifier produce no paid pilot, E1 is dead in Europe. Do not fall back to order entry by default. Run the 4 step check for the next ledger on the list (AFAS, Visma, Fortnox, Procountor) and try once more. If that fails too, the honest conclusion is that there is no product shaped gap for you, and you should sell build hours to Exact and Odoo partners instead of a workflow.
