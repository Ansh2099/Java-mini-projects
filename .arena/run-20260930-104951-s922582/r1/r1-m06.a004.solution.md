# Workflow automations worth selling in India and Europe

Research date: 30 September 2026. Written for a two person, part time team in Ludhiana.

## The short answer

1. **Build one product first: "order inbox to ERP."** Customer orders arrive by email, PDF, Excel or WhatsApp. Today a person reads each one and types it into the accounting or ERP system. You automate the reading, the matching of products and prices, and the checks. A staff member approves each draft order with one click. This is the only idea I found that is strong in both regions and has clear proof of pain and spend. But it only passes the "no built in feature, no cheap tool" test for **some** ERPs, so where you sell it matters as much as what you sell.
2. **Do not sell it to firms on Business Central online.** Microsoft now ships a built in Sales Order Agent in Business Central. It reads a mailbox, finds the customer, matches items (including customer item references), and drafts quotes and orders for a human to review. It costs roughly 7 to 14 Copilot Credits an order at 0.01 USD a credit. You cannot beat that. Odoo also has cheap AI order capture apps in its app store. Reject both as targets.
3. **Get proof in India, earn in Europe.** Do it first for a Ludhiana distributor or manufacturer on Tally or Busy (WhatsApp and email orders). Then sell the same engine to UK and EU wholesalers and manufacturers on ERPs with no built in order agent: **Sage 200** (UK, my first choice), **Exact Online** (Netherlands, Belgium), **Nordic systems** like Procountor, and Business Central **on premises** (the agent is online only).
4. **Second product, for medium European manufacturers:** supplier order confirmations checked against purchase orders. Big pain, proven by funded startups, and the Business Central agent does not touch purchase documents. It is a longer sale.
5. **Do not sell** supplier invoice entry, payment reminders, temp staff timesheets, freight quoting, or customs entries. Cheap or specialist tools already cover them, or the risk is wrong for you.
6. **Your price guess is half right.** 1,500 to 4,000 USD for a first workflow holds for small firms. Your upkeep guess of 400 to 1,500 USD a month is too high for small firms (plan on 300 to 600) and fair for medium firms (800 to 1,500).
7. **The hard truth.** ERP vendors are building this feature into their own products. Microsoft already did. Sage or Exact could follow within a year or two. Your edge is not the AI. It is doing the messy setup work (customer product codes, price rules, units, credit holds) that a built in agent or self serve tool leaves to the customer, on ERPs the vendors have not covered yet. Pick one ERP and one trade, go deep, and recheck the vendors' release notes every quarter.

## Where you should be in 12 months, and the path back to today

I started from the finished result and worked backwards, so each step has a reason to exist.

**The finish line (September 2027):**
* 4 European clients pay a monthly fee for "order inbox to ERP": 2 small firms at 300 to 600 USD a month and 2 medium firms at 800 to 1,500 USD a month. That is about 2,200 to 4,200 USD of monthly revenue. Be honest with yourselves: if all four are small firms, it is only 1,200 to 2,400 USD a month. You need at least one medium client to make this a real business.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK, the EU, and nearby countries like Norway and Switzerland. I give extra weight to the UK, the Netherlands and the Nordics, because their SMEs use ERPs with public APIs and English works for sales there.
2. A workflow "qualifies" only if it passes all 7 of your tests and both checks (proof of spend, and no built in feature or cheap tool already solving it). I list rejected ideas too, with the reason.
3. **How the sources were gathered.** I found every link through web search in September 2026. Opening full pages was blocked in my research setup (I retried on 30 September and it was still blocked), so the figures come from the search result text for each page. Where a key number rests only on that text, I say so. Treat vendor numbers (time saved, error rates) as marketing claims. Open the page and check any number before you put it in a pitch.
4. Where I give a number without a source (for example orders per day at a typical distributor), I label it as an estimate. Confirm it in your first discovery calls.
5. Prices exclude VAT and GST.

## Part 1: India

**The honest picture.** Indian owners will compare you to a Tally operator. Those salaries run about ₹12,000 to ₹16,000 a month at entry level and ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 with 1 to 3 years of experience ([Glassdoor job listings](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/india-tally-operator-and-data-entry-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,5_IN115_KO6,35.htm), [Jooble Tally salary](https://in.jooble.org/salary/tally), [Jooble Tally operator salary](https://in.jooble.org/salary/tally-operator)). So your monthly fee must be well below one salary, or you must win on speed and errors. Orders are where speed and errors matter most, because a wrong order means wrong goods dispatched, returns, and an angry dealer. That is why orders beat invoices in India.

### India workflow 1: Dealer and customer orders from WhatsApp and email into Tally or Busy

**a. Who has it, and size.** Distributors and wholesalers (FMCG, pharma, hardware, electrical, auto parts, paints), and manufacturers that sell to dealers. In Ludhiana this fits hosiery and garment makers, bicycle and auto parts makers, and hand tool and fastener makers. **Fits both sizes.** Small firms (5 to 49) feel it once order volume passes about 30 a day. Medium firms (50 to 250) often have a whole order desk.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A dealer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp: a text list, a photo of a handwritten list, a voice note, or a PDF. Some come by email.
2. A staff member reads it, works out which product each line means ("blue 10mm, 5 box"), checks stock and the dealer's price list, and types a sales order into Tally or Busy.
3. They reply on WhatsApp to confirm, and pass it to dispatch.
4. The data ends up as a sales order and later a sales invoice in Tally or Busy.

Industry writing describes exactly this: orders arrive "as voice notes, photos of handwritten lists, and casual messages at odd hours," and someone then transfers them into a system, checks stock, confirms with the client and updates dispatch ([HublerX: what is WhatsApp order management](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/what-is-whatsapp-order-management), [Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [Wydely](https://www.wydely.io/whatsapp-automation-wholesale-trading-businesses.html)).

**c. Software on each side.**
* In: WhatsApp Business app (kept, see the WhatsApp section below), Gmail or Outlook.
* Out: TallyPrime, Busy, or Marg for pharma.
* TallyPrime can be integrated. It runs an HTTP server (default port 9000) that accepts XML to create vouchers, and it returns a log of what was created or rejected ([Tally XML interface](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/DeveloperReference/integration-capabilities/using_XML_interface.htm), [Tally XML request and response formats](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/DeveloperReference/integration-capabilities/case_study_1.htm), [Integration with TallyPrime](https://help.tallysolutions.com/integrate-with-tallyprime/)). Tally usually runs on a PC in the office, so you need a small local connector (a Java or Node service on that PC) that pulls approved orders from your cloud and posts them to Tally.
* Busy: I did not confirm a public API for Busy. Plan on its import features or a local connector, and check before you promise it.
* Tally has no built in AI order reader that I found, so the "built in feature" test passes here.

**d. How often and how long.** Estimate, to confirm in discovery: a small distributor gets 30 to 150 order messages a day; a medium one gets several hundred. A simple WhatsApp order with 5 to 15 lines takes a few minutes to type; photos and voice notes take longer. So 50 orders a day at 4 to 6 minutes each is 3 to 5 hours of one person's day. A vendor claims mid market firms run "a manual processing team of 10 to 15 people" with a 12 to 18 percent error rate from retyping ([HublerX top WhatsApp order tools 2026](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)). Treat that as a vendor claim. Time five real orders on day one of a pilot.

**e. Who does it, and the cost.** An order desk clerk or Tally operator, and sometimes the owner's family member after hours. Cost is ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 a month per person (sources above). The hidden costs are late orders (orders sent at night wait till morning) and wrong dispatches.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* **HublerX** reads text, audio and photo orders and posts to SAP, Oracle, D365 and Tally. It targets mid market manufacturers with ₹200 to ₹2,000 crore turnover ([HublerX top tools](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026), [HublerX order management](https://www.hublerx.ai/order-management-software)). No public price. **Gap:** firms below that size.
* **Bizom, FieldAssist and similar sales force apps** make salesmen or retailers order in an app. They are priced per user (Bizom is reported at 60 USD per user a month, or ₹8,000 to ₹15,000) ([Software Finder on Bizom](https://softwarefinder.com/field-service/bizom), [Sortstring comparison](https://sortstring.com/blogs/fieldassist-vs-bizom-vs-beatroute-vs-salesport-2026-matrix)). **Gap:** dealers must change habits. Your rule 7 says avoid that.
* **Fast2SMS Tally plugin and similar** send outgoing invoices, confirmations and reminders from Tally to WhatsApp ([Fast2SMS for distributors](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)). **Gap:** outgoing only. They do not read messy incoming orders.
* **Rupyz, Nexxio** offer order apps and dealer portals with Tally links ([Rupyz](https://rupyz.com/blog/order-management-system-with-whatsapp-tally-integration/), [Nexxio Dealer Connect](https://nexxio.in/dealer-connect.html)). **Gap:** dealers must use a portal or a structured catalogue.
* **Logistify** lists TallyPrime among its ERP integrations for AI order entry, with quote based pricing ([Logistify](https://logistify.ai/), [Logistify order entry guide](https://www.logistify.ai/blog/ai-order-entry-software)). Its material is aimed at email and PDF orders. Watch it as a competitor.
* **The real gap you fill:** accept orders in whatever form dealers already send, on the **same WhatsApp number** they already use, match them to the item master, check price and stock, and put a draft in Tally for a human to approve. No change for the dealer.

**WhatsApp: how to read orders without changing the dealer's habit.** Reading messages from the normal WhatsApp Business phone app with unofficial WhatsApp Web scrapers is against the rules, and the number can be banned. Do not do it. The right answer is Meta's **coexistence** feature (Meta calls it "API Solutions for Business App Users"). It runs the WhatsApp Business app and the Cloud API on the same number, and it is available in India ([DoubleTick on coexistence](https://learn.doubletick.io/co-existence/co-existence-vs-whatsapp-business-api), [MSG91 coexistence guide](https://msg91.com/guide/unlock-whatsapp-coexistence-use-whatsapp-business-app-and-cloud-api-together), [360dialog coexistence docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/phone-numbers/coexistence)). Incoming messages reach your webhook, and the staff keep using the phone app. Onboard it through a provider that supports coexistence. Know the limits before you promise anything ([Clientify](https://clientify.com/en/blog/communication/whatsapp-coexistence), [Sandra](https://sandra.ch/en/blog/whatsapp-coexistence-the-business-app-and-the-api-on-the-same-number), [InstantReply on what syncs](https://www.instantreply.co/blog/whatsapp-coexistence-what-actually-syncs-2026)):
* The business app must be opened at least once every 13 days, or the link lapses.
* **Group chats do not sync.** If dealers post orders in a WhatsApp group, you will not see them. Ask this in discovery. If a client runs orders through groups, start with the dealers who message one to one and with email orders, and do not claim full coverage.
* Broadcast lists, disappearing messages, view once media and live location are switched off for 1 to 1 chats. Tell the owner before you switch it on; some owners use broadcast lists for price updates.
* Throughput is capped at 20 messages a second, which is far above what an SME needs.
There is no need for a separate "orders" number, which would break rule 7.

**g. Realistic price in India.**
* Small (5 to 49): ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 setup, then ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 a month. This is a third to a half of one operator's salary.
* Medium (50 to 250): ₹75,000 to ₹1,50,000 setup, then ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 a month. Firms already paying per user for a DMS will see this as cheap.
* Pass WhatsApp message costs through. 2026 India rates are reported at about ₹0.115 per utility message and ₹0.86 per marketing message, plus 18 percent GST and the provider's markup. One 2026 guide also reports that from 1 October 2026 Meta charges service replies at the utility rate, which touches order confirmations ([MyOperator India pricing 2026](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026), [ChatMaxima India rates](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/india/), [AiSensy pricing](https://aisensy.com/pricing)). These come from search result text; check Meta's own rate card before you quote.
* **For your first 1 or 2 clients:** charge only your running costs, in return for a written case study, permission to share numbers, and a reference call with a European prospect.

**h. Sources.** Listed inline above.

### India workflow 2: Enquiry (RFQ) to quotation for small manufacturers

**a. Who has it, and size.** Job shops and component makers: fasteners, forgings, castings, machined parts, hand tools, textile machinery parts, packaging. Very common in Ludhiana. **Mostly small**, some medium.

**b. Steps today.** A buyer emails or WhatsApps an enquiry with a part list, sizes, quantities and sometimes a drawing. The owner or a sales coordinator looks up past quotes in Excel or Tally, applies raw material rates and margins, types a quotation in Excel or Word, and sends it. Many enquiries never get a reply fast enough.

**c. Software.** Email and WhatsApp in; Excel price sheets or Tally item masters; a quotation template out (Excel, Word or Tally quotation voucher).

**d. How often and how long.** Estimate: 5 to 40 enquiries a week at a small maker, 20 to 90 minutes each depending on how many lines and how much lookup. That is 2 to 60 hours a week, a wide range you must narrow in discovery.

**e. Who does it, and the cost.** Usually the owner or a senior salesperson, which is the most expensive time in the firm. Bigger firms hire sales coordinators for it: Ludhiana job listings name "quotation preparation" as a required skill, with coordinator pay around ₹14,000 to ₹20,000 a month ([Glassdoor Ludhiana coordinator jobs](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/ludhiana-coordinator-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,8_IC2939907_KO9,20.htm), [Indeed Ludhiana coordinator jobs](https://in.indeed.com/q-coordinator-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html); figures from search result text). Slow replies lose orders, so speed matters more than salary here.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** HublerX markets "RFQ to order automation" for manufacturers ([HublerX manufacturing operations](https://www.hublerx.ai/manufacturing-operations-software)), which shows the pain is real, but it aims at larger firms. Generic CRMs store quotes but do not read enquiries or fill in prices. **Gap:** a draft quote built from the firm's own price sheet and past quotes, sent only after the owner approves.

**g. Realistic price.** ₹20,000 to ₹40,000 setup, ₹4,000 to ₹8,000 a month. Harder to sell than workflow 1 because the value is "more orders won," which owners believe less than "fewer hours spent."

**h. Sources.** Above. **Confidence: medium to low.** The evidence of spend is weaker than for workflow 1. Offer it only as an add on to a workflow 1 client who also makes goods, and only after you have counted their enquiries for two weeks.

### India workflow 3: Supplier dispatch and delivery date follow up for manufacturers

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers that buy many parts and raw materials. **Medium mostly.**

**b. Steps today.** Purchase staff send POs from Tally or Excel, then chase each supplier on WhatsApp and phone for confirmation and dispatch dates, and update a tracking sheet.

**c. Software.** Tally or Busy (POs), Excel (tracking), WhatsApp and email.

**d. How often and how long.** Estimate, to confirm in discovery: a medium manufacturer has 30 to 150 open POs at any time, each needing 1 to 3 follow ups at 3 to 10 minutes each (message, wait, read reply, update the sheet). That is roughly 5 to 30 hours a week.

**e. Who does it, and the cost.** A purchase executive. Ludhiana job postings list "follow up with suppliers for timely delivery of materials" as a core duty, at ₹15,000 to ₹20,000 a month ([Indeed Ludhiana purchase executive jobs](https://in.indeed.com/q-purchase-executives-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html), [Glassdoor Ludhiana purchasing jobs](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/ludhiana-purchasing-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,8_IC2939907_KO9,19.htm); figures from search result text). The bigger cost is a production line waiting for a part.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** The same problem is solved in Europe by funded startups (see Europe workflow 2). I found no Indian SME tool for it. **Gap:** everything, but that also means no proof yet that Indian firms will pay.

**g. Price.** ₹30,000 to ₹60,000 setup, ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 a month, only for medium firms. Note that the fee is close to one purchase executive's salary, so the pitch must be "fewer line stoppages," not "fewer staff."

**h. Sources.** Above. **Confidence: low.** The pain is proven by local job ads, but the willingness to pay is not. Validate it in local visits before you build anything.

### Rejected in India, and why

* **Purchase bill entry into Tally, with GST checks.** Vyapar TaxOne (formerly Suvit) already covers data entry automation, client document collection and follow ups, and GST reconciliation, with Tally integration and WhatsApp included. It is reported at about ₹5 to ₹10 per invoice ([Suvit / Vyapar TaxOne pricing](https://www.suvit.io/pricing), [GeekChamp review](https://geekchamp.com/suvit-pricing-reviews-2026/), [Suvit on AI GST reconciliation](https://www.suvit.io/post/ai-gst-reconciliation-tally-automation)). Other tools compete on flat pricing ([TrulyInvoice](https://www.trulyinvoice.com/vs/suvit-alternative)). You cannot beat ₹5 per invoice. Reject.
* **Payment reminders and ledger confirmations from Tally.** Free or cheap Tally WhatsApp plugins already do this ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Takkada](https://takkada.com/blog/whatsapp-invoice-format-tally/)). Reject. It is also a single task, not a workflow.
* **CA firm document chasing.** Included in Vyapar TaxOne (source above). Reject.

## Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

**The honest picture.** Europe pays well for labour saved, but this market is squeezed from two sides. At the top, enterprise tools are strong: Rossum's cheapest plan is reported at about 18,000 USD a year ([Floowed on Rossum pricing](https://www.floowed.com/insights/rossum-pricing), [JustPrompt Rossum review](https://justprompt.io/review/rossum-review)), and Coupa announced it had acquired Rossum on 12 May 2026 ([Coupa newsroom](https://www.coupa.com/newsroom/coupa-acquires-rossum-to-accelerate-end-to-end-autonomous-spend-management/), [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/News/2026/05/Rossum-Leader-in-AI-Document-Processing-Acquired-by-Coupa)). From inside, ERP vendors are adding AI agents to their own products. The space left for you is: small and medium firms, on ERPs with no built in agent, whose orders are messy enough that a self serve tool is not enough.

### Europe workflow 1: Emailed customer orders into the ERP (the top pick)

**a. Who has it, and size.** Wholesale distributors (building and electrical supplies, food service, packaging, industrial parts, beauty and pharmacy wholesale), and manufacturers selling to trade customers. **Both sizes**, best at 15 to 250 staff. Small firms: the owner or sales manager decides. Medium firms: the head of customer service or operations owns it.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A customer emails a purchase order as a PDF, an Excel file, or text in the email body. Each customer uses their own format and their own product codes.
2. A sales order processor opens it, finds the customer, translates the customer's codes into the firm's codes, checks prices, stock, credit limit and delivery dates, and types it into the ERP.
3. They send an order confirmation.
4. The data ends up as a sales order in the ERP.

**How to make the Indian case study believable to a European buyer:**
1. **Pick an Indian client that also receives orders by email with PDF or Excel attachments**, not only WhatsApp. A Ludhiana manufacturer that supplies OEMs or exporters is ideal. European buyers must see their own situation in it.
2. **Measure before you switch it on.** Log one week of manual handling: orders per day, minutes per order, number of lines, errors found later. Then report the same numbers after 6 weeks.
3. **Report the numbers European buyers care about:** share of orders approved with no edits, minutes per order, time from email to order in the system, errors caught before dispatch. Lead with minutes and accuracy, not rupees.
4. **Show the approval screen and the audit trail.** European buyers (and GDPR) care that a human approves and that every change is logged.
5. **Build a Sage 200 demo** with the same engine, so the UK buyer sees their own ERP, not Tally. Ask a Sage 200 reseller for a demo company in return for a referral share; check Sage's developer programme terms for test access. Do not build a Business Central demo for order entry; a BC buyer will rightly say "our ERP already does this."

## Part 4: Final top picks, ranked

### Pick 1: "Order inbox to ERP" (India for proof, Europe for revenue)

**Why it wins:**
* It passes every one of your 7 rules. One owner (the order desk lead), one process (order arrives to order approved), 2 to 3 tools (email or WhatsApp, the ERP, your approval screen), and very countable (orders per day times minutes each).
* Proof of spend is strong in both regions: hundreds of UK job listings for order processors, and whole order desks in Indian distribution.
* It passes the "built in feature or cheap tool" test **on the right ERPs**: Tally and Busy in India, Sage 200, Exact Online and Nordic ERPs in Europe. It fails on Business Central online and Odoo, and you will not sell there.
* Enterprise tools start near 18,000 USD a year. The cheap tools either stop at a spreadsheet (Lido at 29 USD) or suit clean, simple orders (StackCube). Messy orders on an uncovered ERP are your space.
* In India it keeps the dealer's habit intact: coexistence lets you read orders from the same WhatsApp number the dealers already use.
* It matches your skills: Developer A builds the engine and ERP connectors in Spring Boot with Postgres (the KYC document flows you built are the same pattern). Developer B builds the intake in n8n and the approval screen in TypeScript.
* It is the same product in both regions, so the Indian case study directly sells the European version.

**The honest risks:**
* **ERP vendors are the biggest threat.** Microsoft already built this into Business Central. If Sage or Exact ships the same thing, your market in that ERP shrinks fast. Check release notes quarterly, keep contracts monthly, and be ready to move to the next uncovered ERP or to Pick 2.
* Competition from SMB tools is growing. Specialise in one ERP (Sage 200 for the UK) and one trade (for example building and electrical wholesale). Sage 200 resellers are your best channel: they meet the buyers and do not want to build AI themselves.
* Product matching is the hard part. Budget most of your build time for it: customer product codes, synonyms, units, and a "learn from corrections" table in Postgres.
* European buyers will ask about data protection. Host in an EU or UK cloud region, sign a data processing agreement, and never train models on client data.

### Pick 2: Supplier order confirmations (Europe, medium firms)

Clear pain, proven by funded startups, enterprise priced competitors, and not covered by Business Central's agents. Sell it from month 9 to clients who already trust you, or as a first product to a medium Business Central firm that you cannot serve with order entry. Longer sale to a purchasing head.

### Pick 3: Quote requests to draft quotation (conditional add on)

Same engine, same buyer, same ERP connection. The proof of spend is thinner than for Pick 1, so only sell it to a happy Pick 1 client after you have counted their quote requests for two weeks. Never on Business Central online.

### What not to do

* Do not build invoice capture, payment reminders, timesheet tools, freight quoting, or customs tools.
* Do not sell order entry to Business Central online or Odoo users.
* Do not sell "general AI automation." Sell one named workflow with a number attached: "We turn your emailed orders into approved Sage 200 orders. Clients approve most drafts with no edits."
* Do not chase many ERPs at once. Every new ERP is weeks of work.
* Do not use unofficial WhatsApp Web tools in India. Use coexistence through a provider.

## Practical checklist

**Discovery questions (ask every prospect, to prove the workflow is countable and not already solved):**
1. How many orders arrive a day, and through which channels? (India: how many come in WhatsApp groups rather than one to one chats?)
2. How many lines does a typical order have?
3. How long does one order take to enter? Can we time five?
4. Who does it, and how many people?
5. How many orders had mistakes last month, and what did a mistake cost?
6. Which ERP and version? Cloud or on premises? Who holds admin access? (If Business Central online or Odoo: stop and point them to the built in option.)
7. Do customers use your product codes or their own? How many different order formats do you see?
8. Have you tried an AI order tool or your ERP's own AI features? What happened?

**Running costs under 50 USD a month:**
* One small cloud server for n8n (self hosted, free software), your Spring Boot service, and Postgres.
* LLM calls paid per use, passed through to clients once they pay.
* A Sage 200 or Exact Online test company through a reseller or the vendor's developer programme (check the terms for each). Xero's Starter API tier is free for up to 5 connections if you later add Xero with an inventory add on ([Xero developer pricing](https://developer.xero.com/pricing)).
* WhatsApp messages in India are paid per message and billed to the client.

**Pilot rules:**
* Draft only. Nothing is posted to the ERP without a human click, until the client asks for auto approval of simple orders.
* Every draft shows the original message next to the extracted lines, with low confidence fields highlighted.
* Keep a log of every correction; it is your training data and your case study.
