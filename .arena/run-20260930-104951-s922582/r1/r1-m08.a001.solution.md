# Workflows Worth Selling in India and Europe (30 September 2026)

## The short answer

1. **Sell one workflow: customer order intake.** Trade customers send orders by email as a PDF, an Excel file or plain text (in India, often on WhatsApp). A person retypes each order into the accounting, inventory or ERP system. You read the order, find the right customer, items and prices, and put a draft order into the system. A person at the client approves anything odd. Both regions have this problem, it touches 2 or 3 tools, and it is easy to count.
2. **Build it first for one Ludhiana business on Tally that types at least 2 hours of orders a day.** Do it free or at cost, measure it, and turn it into a case study with real numbers. Then sell the same workflow to UK wholesalers and distributors on Xero with Unleashed or Cin7 Core. Both have sales order APIs.
3. **Leave Sage 50 until you have a quote for its API connector,** and skip Business Central shops, because Microsoft already ships this feature there.
4. **A later add on for medium European firms: supplier order confirmation check.** Suppliers confirm purchase orders in their own PDF formats, and a purchasing assistant checks each line by hand. The evidence is thinner than for order intake, so sell it only to a client who already trusts you.
5. **Do not sell** supplier invoice entry, bank statement entry, payment reminders, export document sets, temp timesheets or freight quotes. Cheap tools already do them, they break the 2 to 3 tool rule, or the field is full of funded products.
6. **Your price guess is close, with one fix.** Build fees of 1,500 to 4,000 USD are right for your first clients and small firms. Upkeep of 400 to 1,500 USD a month is too high for small firms: expect about 270 to 600 USD. Only medium firms pay 800 to 1,600 USD a month. The full table is in the Europe section.

Be ready for two hard truths. First, order intake is a real market with real competitors, and at least one of them (Logistify AI) says it already writes orders into Xero, Cin7, Unleashed and TallyPrime. You will not win on technology. You will win on a fixed low price, doing the setup on the client's messy data, and fast replies. Second, India gives you one strong workflow, not a menu. That is fine: you need one case study, not five.

## Open questions and how I settled them

| Open question | How I settled it |
| --- | --- |
| What does "would pay for" mean? | The business already spends money on the task today (a salary, part of a salary, or a tool), and our fee is well under that cost. |
| When is a problem "already solved"? | When the client's own software does it as a built in feature, or a tool under about 50 USD (or ₹4,000) a month does the whole workflow without help. If a tool exists but is quote based, costs enterprise money, or needs setup the owner cannot do, it is not solved for them. It is still competition, and I name it. |
| How much volume is enough? | One floor for every workflow: **at least 2 hours of manual typing a day at the client.** For orders that is roughly 25 orders or 150 order lines a day. Below that, the fee is too small to matter to you and the savings too small to matter to them. |
| Which part of Europe first? | The UK, then Ireland, the Netherlands and the Nordics. English works there, and UK 9am is 1:30pm or 2:30pm in Ludhiana, depending on the season. Germany and France come later because of language. |
| How much time do you have? | I assume 15 to 20 hours a week between you. The first build is about 90 hours, so about 5 to 6 weeks. The plan at the end is 12 weeks, not 30 days, because that is what the work takes. |
| Where do volumes and times come from? | Salaries, prices and tool facts come from the linked sources. Numbers marked **estimate** are mine. Check them in your first meeting with each client before you promise savings. |
| How good are the sources? | I found every link through web search on 30 September 2026. My tool could not open several vendor pages directly (Hyperext, OrderPier, Logistify), so some facts come from the search result text. Open the page and check any figure before you quote it to a client. |
| What exchange rate? | About 1.34 USD per pound, only to compare with your USD guess. Check the rate on the day you quote. |
| Data protection in Europe? | You will be a data processor under GDPR. Sign a data processing agreement with each client and host their data in an EU or UK region. |

## How this plan could fail, and what I did about it

1. **You build something a tool already does.** Every workflow has an "existing tools and the gap" line, and I name the tools that come closest, even when that hurts the pitch. I rejected eight ideas.
2. **The client's software has no usable API.** I checked the route for each target system: Tally (XML over HTTP), Xero, Unleashed, Cin7 Core and Business Central have usable APIs. Sage 50 desktop needs a paid, quote only connector, so it waits. Busy, Marg, Sage 200, Odoo and Exact Online are not checked: read their docs before you quote.
3. **The client has to change how they work.** Customers keep sending orders exactly as they do today. Staff move from typing to approving.
4. **Volume is too low to pay for it.** Every client, including the free Indian one, must pass the 2 hour a day floor. Below it, walk away.
5. **An Indian case study does not impress European buyers.** Pick an Indian client whose inbox looks like a UK distributor's: English PDF or Excel orders by email. Report the numbers a UK buyer asks for.
6. **The AI enters a wrong order.** Nothing posts on its own for the first 60 days. Every order is a draft that a person approves.
7. **A cheaper or bigger rival already covers the client's stack.** Microsoft covers Business Central, so avoid it. Logistify covers Xero, Cin7, Unleashed and Tally, so get a sense of its real price before you pitch (step in the plan).
8. **The plan takes longer than you think.** The timeline below budgets hours, not wishes.
9. **You blow the 50 USD budget.** The client pays their own software, connector and messaging fees. Your own costs are listed at the end.

## India

### The honest math first

A data entry operator in Ludhiana earns about ₹15,000 to ₹23,000 a month ([apna.co Ludhiana data entry jobs](https://apna.co/jobs/data_entry-jobs-in-ludhiana), [PayScale Ludhiana](https://www.payscale.com/research/IN/Job=Data_Entry_Operator/Salary/5096a870/Ludhiana), [Glassdoor Punjab](https://www.glassdoor.com/Job/punjab-data-entry-operator-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,6_IS4951_KO7,26.htm)). An owner will hire another person before paying you, unless you replace a big part of a person's day or stop errors that cost more than a salary. So in India, only chase clients with high volume or costly mistakes. That is why the 2 hour floor matters most here.

### India workflow 1: Customer orders into Tally (or Busy)

**a. Who has it, and size.** Distributors and wholesalers (FMCG, pharma, hardware, electricals, auto parts, bicycle parts). Also manufacturers that sell to many dealers or to large buyers, which Ludhiana has plenty of (auto parts, bicycle parts, hand tools, hosiery). Fits **both** sizes, if they pass the floor:
1. Small distributor: 25 or more orders a day, or 50 or more if most are short WhatsApp texts.
2. Medium manufacturer or distributor: at least 150 order lines a day arriving by email (POs, schedules or amendments).
A manufacturer that gets 3 POs a week from 2 OEM buyers does **not** qualify. The saving would be a few minutes a week.

**b. Steps today.** Retailers send orders on WhatsApp as text, a photo of a handwritten list, or a voice note. Large buyers email PO PDFs or Excel files. A staff member reads each one, finds the party in Tally, matches loose item names ("5 wala pack", a customer's own part code) to the item master, checks the rate and scheme, types a sales order or invoice into Tally, and replies to confirm. The order ends up in Tally. The status lives in WhatsApp chats and memory.

**c. Software on each side.** In: Gmail or Outlook (start here), WhatsApp Business later. Out: TallyPrime for most, Busy or Marg for some. That is 2 tools, 3 if you add WhatsApp. TallyPrime accepts XML over HTTP (default port 9000) to create vouchers and masters ([Tally XML integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/), [Integrate with TallyPrime](https://help.tallysolutions.com/integrate-with-tallyprime/)). Tally runs on the client's own PC, so you install a small local connector that pulls approved drafts from your server. Never open port 9000 to the internet. I did not check Busy or Marg import options; test them before you quote a Busy or Marg client.

**d. How often and how long (estimate).** 25 to 300 orders a day for a qualifying distributor. 3 to 6 minutes for a clean typed order, longer for photos, voice notes and long PDFs. At 50 orders and 5 minutes, that is about 4 hours a day, half an operator.

**e. Who does it and the cost.** Order entry or data entry staff at ₹15,000 to ₹23,000 a month each (sources above). One vendor claims mid market FMCG firms use teams of 10 to 15 people for manual order processing with 12 to 18% error rates ([HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/what-is-whatsapp-order-management)). That is a sales claim, so do not repeat it to clients as fact. Job ads for sales coordinators in Ludhiana list order processing and Tally ([Naukri](https://www.naukri.com/sales-coordinator-jobs-in-ludhiana)).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. WhatsApp ordering tools with Tally links: [Fast2SMS for Tally distributors](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [Delta Sales App](https://deltasalesapp.com/blog/dealer-app-for-distributors), HublerX. Most push retailers to order through a catalog, a bot menu or an app. That is a behaviour change, and many retailers keep sending free text anyway.
2. AI order entry: Logistify AI says it reads orders from email, PDF and WhatsApp and creates sales orders in ERPs including TallyPrime, with quote based pricing that charges by successful tasks ([Logistify AI](https://www.logistify.ai/blog/ai-order-entry-software), [SpotSaaS on Logistify pricing](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/logistify-ai/pricing)).
3. Cheap Tally AI tools I found (TrulyInvoice, AI Accountant) do **purchase** bill entry, not customer orders ([TrulyInvoice](https://www.trulyinvoice.com/blog/automate-purchase-entry-tally), [AI Accountant](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/purchase-bill-entry-in-tally)).

The gap: nothing cheap and self serve reads what customers already send, learns this client's own aliases for items and parties, and drafts into their own Tally for approval, with someone local to set it up. Be honest with yourself: this gap is closing, and your edge is a done for you setup and local support, not secret tech. That is fine for India, where the main goal is a case study. **Start with emailed PDF and Excel orders.** WhatsApp needs the WhatsApp Business Platform through a provider, which adds cost and setup. Utility messages cost about ₹0.115 plus 18% GST each in India, and one provider reports that Meta starts charging for service messages from 1 October 2026 ([MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026), [Meta pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). Check Meta's page before you price a WhatsApp build, and bill these fees to the client.

**g. Realistic price.** Rule: keep the monthly fee under half the salary it frees up.
1. Small distributor (frees half an operator, about ₹9,000 a month): ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 setup, ₹3,000 to ₹4,500 a month.
2. Medium firm (frees one to two operators): ₹60,000 to ₹1,20,000 setup, ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 a month. Only charge the top of this range if you free at least 1.5 people.
3. Your first client: free or at cost, in return for permission to publish their numbers and a named quote.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### India ideas I rejected

1. **Buyer order to export document set.** Real pain: Ludhiana ads for export documentation staff list invoices, packing lists and shipping bills, at up to ₹30,000 to ₹35,000 a month ([ABP Empower Ludhiana](https://www.abpempower.in/job-openings-for-export-import-documentation-executive-ludhiana-1299973.htm), [PlacementIndia Ludhiana](https://www.placementindia.com/job-search/export-documentation-executive-jobs-in-ludhiana.htm)). But it fails your rules. It touches email, Excel, Tally and a document tool (4 tools). It makes several outputs at different times (proforma before production, the rest after), so it is not one clean process. And [ovrseas](https://ovrseas.io/blog/export-documentation-software-small-exporters) already makes 12 document types from one master file from 20 USD a month. What is left, typing the PO into that tool, is a single task. Take it only as paid local work if an exporter asks.
2. **Purchase bills and bank statements into Tally.** Vyapar TaxOne (formerly Suvit) already converts bank statements and purchase and sales invoices into Tally entries, from around ₹10,000 ([Suvit pricing](https://www.suvit.io/pricing), [Techjockey](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/suvit)). TrulyInvoice and AI Accountant also do purchase bills (links above).
3. **GST return reconciliation.** Included in the same Vyapar TaxOne plans ([Suvit](https://www.suvit.io/)).
4. **Payment reminders from Tally on WhatsApp.** Cheap tools do it ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)), and it is a single task, not a workflow.

### Europe workflow 1: Customer order intake to sales order

**a. Who has it, and size.** Wholesalers, distributors and B2B manufacturers that take orders from trade customers: building supplies, electrical, food and drink wholesale, packaging, industrial parts. Fits **both**. Medium is best (more volume, and a sales office or customer service manager owns it). Small works at 20 or more orders a day (the 2 hour floor).

**b. Steps today.** An order arrives by email as a PDF purchase order, an Excel sheet or plain text. A clerk finds the customer account, matches the customer's product codes or descriptions to their own items, checks the price against the agreed price list, types the order into the system, and sends a confirmation. Exceptions: unknown item, wrong price, missing PO number, new delivery address. The order ends up in the inventory, accounting or ERP system.

**c. Software on each side, and APIs.** In: Outlook or Gmail. Out, in the order you should target them:

| System | Usable API? | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Xero with Unleashed | Yes. Unleashed's API lists, views and creates sales orders in JSON or XML. You must send totals yourself, and updates replace the whole record, so always send every field ([Unleashed API sales orders](https://apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/SalesOrders), [Unleashed API FAQ](https://apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/Faq)) | **First target.** Cloud, documented, no local install. |
| Xero with Cin7 Core | Yes. Cin7 Core has a Sale POST endpoint and recommends API V2. Some sale features are restricted through the API, and it silently creates a backorder if stock is short ([Cin7 Core Sale POST](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034512161423-Sale-POST), [Cin7 Core developer portal](https://dearinventory.docs.apiary.io/)) | **First target.** Test the restricted fields in week one of any build. |
| Xero alone | Yes. From March 2026 Xero charges app developers by tier, but custom integrations built for a single client stay free, and the free Starter tier allows 5 connections ([Databuzz](https://www.databuzz.com.au/changes-to-the-xero-developer-pricing/), [Truto](https://truto.one/blog/xero-api-pricing-changes-2026-costs-tiers-and-how-to-minimize-egress/), [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/xero-shifts-to-tiered-pricing-model-for-developers)) | Xero has quotes and invoices but no sales order screen. Draft invoice only, so it suits small firms. |
| Sage 50 Accounts (UK desktop) | No native public REST API. HyperAccounts adds one: an annual subscription, price by quote only, installed by Hyperext on the server that holds the Sage 50 data ([Hyperext on Sage 50 APIs](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-integration/does-sage-50-have-an-api-yes-heres-what-you-need-to-know/), [Hyperext Sage 50 Accounts API](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-rest-apis/sage-50-accounts-api/), [HyperAccounts docs](https://sage-50-accounts-api-v1-docs.hyperext.com/)). Unified APIs such as Chift also list Sage 50 ([Chift](https://www.chift.eu/tools/sage-50-accounts-uk)) | **Wait.** Get a Hyperext quote first. If the licence plus your fee breaks the one third rule for a small firm, do not pitch Sage 50 small firms. |
| Dynamics 365 Business Central | Yes, including a sales order endpoint ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/api/dynamics_salesorder_create)) | **Avoid.** Microsoft's own Sales Order Agent reads order emails and drafts orders for approval ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent)), at roughly 7 Copilot credits per order ([MSDynamicsWorld](https://msdynamicsworld.com/blog-post/business-centrals-ai-agents-run-consumption-billing-heres-what-means-your-2027-budget)). |
| Sage 200, Odoo, Exact Online | Not checked | Read the developer docs before you quote. |

**d. How often and how long (estimate).** 20 to 200 orders a day. 3 to 10 minutes each, more for messy PDFs and new customers.

**e. Who does it and the cost.** A sales order processor or order clerk. Average UK pay is about £26,832 a year for an order processor ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)) and £22,764 for an order clerk ([CheckASalary](https://www.checkasalary.co.uk/salary/order-clerk)), plus employer National Insurance and pension. Firms still hire for it ([Indeed UK sales order processing jobs](https://uk.indeed.com/q-sales-order-processing-clerk-jobs.html), [DWP Find a Job listing](https://findajob.dwp.gov.uk/details/14397721), [Jobsite](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor)).
Worked example: 40 orders a day at 6 minutes is 4 hours a day, about half a clerk. That is about £13,400 in salary, or roughly £15,500 with employer costs (estimate). A third of that is about £430 a month, so quote £350 to £430. At 20 orders a day, the same rule gives about £200 a month.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. Enterprise: Conexiom, about 10,000 to 100,000+ USD a year ([ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom)).
2. Self serve: [OrderPier](https://orderpier.com/) writes native orders into Business Central and QuickBooks and sends a webhook to anything else. Its free plan covers 15 orders a month, with a 14 day trial of 250 orders (search result text). So for a Xero, Unleashed or Cin7 shop, someone still has to build the webhook side. Others are listed by [Ordermatic](https://ordermatic.co/blog/conexiom-alternatives) and [OrderDrafter](https://orderdrafter.com/alternatives/conexiom-alternative/).
3. Closest rival for your target stacks: **Logistify AI** says it reads PDF orders from email and creates sales orders in Unleashed, and names Xero, Cin7, Brightpearl, MYOB and TallyPrime among its ERPs. It markets to UK Unleashed users. Pricing is quote based and charged by successful tasks ([Logistify on Unleashed](https://logistify.ai/blog/ai-pdf-sales-order-entry-for-unleashed-software), [Logistify on Unleashed in the UK](https://logistify.ai/blog/ai-sales-order-entry-for-unleashed-software-in-uk), [SpotSaaS](https://www.spotsaas.com/product/logistify-ai/pricing)).
4. Agencies: Aucta AI sells AI order automation into Sage, from about £1,000 a month (above).

The honest gap: the problem is **not** unsolved. It is solved at agency prices (£1,000+ a month), at enterprise prices, or by quote based tools whose real price small firms cannot see. I found no tool under 50 USD a month that drafts orders into Xero, Unleashed or Cin7 without extra build work. Your offer is a fixed, published price under £450 a month for small firms, done for you, with each customer's codes and prices mapped by you. Before your first UK pitch, find out what Logistify really charges (ask openly, or ask prospects who have had a quote). If it is near your price with setup included, your only edge is service and speed, and your price must drop. If a prospect runs Business Central or QuickBooks and is happy to set up a tool, point them to it and move on. You will earn trust and save weeks.

**g. Realistic price.** Use the price table above. With the one third rule: small firms (20 to 60 orders a day) pay £200 to £450 a month; medium firms pay £600 to £1,200.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### Europe workflow 2 (later add on): Supplier order confirmation check

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers and distributors that buy from many suppliers. **Medium** only. Small firms rarely send enough purchase orders to pass the floor (20 or more confirmations a day).

**b. Steps today.** The buyer sends a PO from the ERP. The supplier replies with an order confirmation in its own PDF format. A purchasing assistant checks each line (part number, quantity, price, delivery date) against the PO, updates the expected date in the ERP, chases suppliers who have not confirmed, and warns the planner when a date slips. UK job descriptions list checking order acknowledgements, updating ETAs and prices, and chasing suppliers ([Indeed UK purchasing assistant](https://uk.indeed.com/hire/job-description/purchasing-assistant), [Lucy Walker Recruitment](https://www.lucywalkerrecruitment.com/job/purchasing-assistant-2/), [Glassdoor UK](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Job/purchasing-assistant-jobs-SRCH_KO0,20.htm)).

**c. Software on each side.** In: Outlook or Gmail. Out: the ERP or inventory tool. Unleashed has a purchase orders API ([Unleashed API purchases](https://apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/Purchases)). I did not check the purchase order APIs of Business Central, Sage 200, SAP Business One or Odoo, so only offer this where you have checked the client's system.

**d. How often and how long (estimate).** 20 to 60 confirmations a day. 5 to 10 minutes each, plus chasing. A vendor claims PO handling drops from 15 to 20 minutes to about 2 with automation ([Leverage AI](https://tryleverage.ai/blog/pf/po-acknowledgement-automation-hybrid-edi-1)); treat that as a sales claim.

**e. Who does it and the cost.** A purchasing assistant or buyer. Indeed UK puts average UK pay at about £25,869 a year (March 2026) ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/purchasing-assistant/salaries)); PayScale says about £21,736 ([PayScale UK](https://www.payscale.com/research/UK/Job=Purchasing_Assistant/Salary)). Worked example: 30 confirmations a day at 8 minutes is 4 hours, about half an assistant, roughly £15,000 a year with employer costs (estimate). The bigger cost is a missed date change that stops production or lets down a customer.

**f. Existing tools, spend evidence, and the gap.** Firms already pay for this. Enterprise tools: [Doxis](https://www.doxis.com/en/solutions/order-confirmation-automation), [SupplyOn](https://www.supplyon.com/en/solutions/logistics-supply-chain/purchase-order-confirmation/), Conexiom's vendor acknowledgements ([ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom)), [tacto](https://www.tacto.ai/en/procurement-glossary/purchase-order-acknowledgment-requirement). Leverage AI sells acknowledgement parsing and supplier follow up to mid market manufacturers on quote based plans ([Leverage AI](https://tryleverage.ai/)). Nanonets published a case study of a Europe focused furniture retailer that automated supplier order confirmations because 20 to 30% of its orders had an error or issue ([Nanonets](https://nanonets.com/blog/supplier-order-management-automation/)). Business Central's built in agents cover supplier invoices and sales orders, not supplier confirmations ([MSDynamicsWorld](https://msdynamicsworld.com/blog-post/business-centrals-ai-agents-run-consumption-billing-heres-what-means-your-2027-budget)). The gap: firms with 50 to 250 staff that are too small for these vendors' sales process. Honest caution: I found vendors selling it and job ads listing it, but no UK small firm spend figure. That is why it is an add on, not a lead offer.

**g. Realistic price.** One third rule on the worked example: about £400 a month. Quote £3,000 to £5,000 build and £350 to £600 a month, and only above 20 confirmations a day.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### European ideas I rejected

1. **Supplier invoice entry.** Business Central now has a Payables Agent ([MSDynamicsWorld](https://msdynamicsworld.com/blog-post/business-centrals-ai-agents-run-consumption-billing-heres-what-means-your-2027-budget)), and Sage users already have AutoEntry and Dext on the Sage Marketplace ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-integration/does-sage-50-have-an-api-yes-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)). Also, e invoicing rules are removing PDF invoices: Belgium since 1 January 2026, Poland's KSeF from 2026, France receiving from 1 September 2026, Germany receiving since 2025 with sending phased in from 2027 ([Marosa VAT](https://marosavat.com/resources/e-invoicing-in-europe-overview-and-dates), [Symtrax](https://blog.symtrax.com/b2b-e-invoicing-european-deadlines-2026-2027/)). Customer purchase orders are not covered by these rules, which is one more reason order intake is the better bet.
2. **Temp agency timesheets into payroll and invoicing.** Pay and bill software already covers it: [Access Pay and Bill](https://www.theaccessgroup.com/en-gb/recruitment/software/pay-and-bill/), [Vincere TimeTemp](https://www.vincere.io/timetemp/), [Flo](https://flo.co.uk/).
3. **Freight quote requests.** A crowded field of dedicated products: [Quotio](https://quotio.eu/), [Info-X](https://infox.com/rate-quote-automation), and lists of 10 to 14 AI tools for forwarders ([FreightMynd](https://freightmynd.com/blog/best-ai-tools-freight-forwarders-2026/)).

## Final top picks

1. **Customer order intake, in both regions. This is your one product.** It has proof on every point: firms hire people for it in both regions, enterprise vendors and agencies charge large sums for it, and small firms on Xero with Unleashed or Cin7, and on Tally, see no clear low fixed price. It fits your skills: Developer A builds the matching engine, approval queue and the Tally, Unleashed or Cin7 connector in Spring Boot, Spring AI and PostgreSQL. Developer B builds the email intake, approval notices and client screens in Node.js or n8n. The Indian version is the same product, so the case study transfers. Know the risk: Logistify and others are in this space, so your price and service must be clearly better for small firms.
2. **Supplier order confirmation check, for medium European firms, as an add on.** It reuses most of the same engine (read a PDF, compare it to a record) and sells to a department head with budget. Offer it to your second or third European client once order intake is running, not before.

What I would not do: chase India for revenue, pitch Sage 50 before you have a Hyperext quote, target Business Central shops, build anything on WhatsApp before your first email based order workflow is live, or offer a menu of workflows. One product, sold well, beats three half built.

## What to do in the next 12 weeks

At 15 to 20 hours a week between you.

1. **Weeks 1 and 2: find and measure the Indian client (about 25 hours).** Visit 10 to 15 manufacturers and distributors in Ludhiana's industrial areas.
