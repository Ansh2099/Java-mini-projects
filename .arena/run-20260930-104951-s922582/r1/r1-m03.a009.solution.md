# One Workflow to Sell: Messy Customer Orders Into the ERP

Research date: 30 September 2026. Written for a two person team in Ludhiana who want to sell one workflow per client, mainly to European SMBs.

## The short answer

Sell one thing first: **customer order intake**. A customer sends an order as an email, a PDF purchase order, a spreadsheet, or a WhatsApp message. Your system reads it, finds the customer, matches their product names to the seller's own item codes, checks price and stock, and puts a **draft** sales order into the seller's ERP. A person approves the unclear ones.

Here is the part you may not like. **This is not an empty market.** In 2026 there are paid tools for it on almost every ERP: Cin7's own add on, Sicon and Zestcode on Sage 200, Logistify on Unleashed, Vistaflow, Proton.ai, StackCube, and several Dutch vendors on Exact Online. That is bad news if you hoped to be first. It is good news for one reason: **it proves businesses pay real money for this today.** Vistaflow's smallest plan is 10,900 USD a year.

So you do not win by being the only one. You win by being **the done for you option at a small firm price, on ERPs where the vendor has not built its own tool, for orders that arrive through mixed channels.**

1. **Europe (where you earn):** small wholesalers and distributors on **Unleashed** first, then **Sage 200** through warm leads only. Do **not** target Cin7 Core or Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central. Both vendors now sell their own order reading feature cheaply.
2. **India (proof of work):** the same workflow for Tally distributors and manufacturers who take orders on **one WhatsApp Business number** and by email. Ludhiana's own industry is full of these firms.
3. **Fallback in both regions:** **supplier price file updates** (E3 and I2). This uses the same matching engine and I found few SMB tools for it.
4. **Both regions:** order intake is the same workflow in both, so an Indian case study with real numbers is a believable story for a European buyer.

## Assumptions

1. "Workflow" means the full path from "order arrives" to "draft order sits in the ERP, checked, with problems flagged." Sending the confirmation back to the customer is included only after a human approves it.
2. "Would pay for" means someone already spends salary or a subscription on it today. Vendor marketing numbers count as weak evidence, and I say when I rely on them.
3. A "cheap tool" means roughly under 100 USD a month, or a feature already inside software the client pays for.
4. Prices in USD use about 1 GBP = 1.3 USD and 1 USD = 85 INR. Treat these as rough.
5. My page fetch tool was blocked for most sites. I opened the Microsoft Learn pages in full. Every other link here I found through web search and read from the search summary. Before you quote any vendor price to a client, open that vendor's page yourself.

## Open questions the task leaves, and how I settled them

1. **How many workflows should we list?** You can only sell and support one well while working part time. So I give one main pick per region, one fallback per region that reuses the same engine, and a list of the ideas I checked and rejected, with reasons.
2. **Should India and Europe get different workflows?** Only if the evidence forces it. It does not. One codebase with two connectors is simpler than two products.
3. **Which software should we target?** Only ERPs that have a usable API **and** where the ERP vendor does not sell its own order reading feature. That rules out Business Central (Sales Order Agent) and Cin7 Core (IDR). It leaves Unleashed and Sage 200, where the competitors are third parties you can undercut on price.
4. **Where does the human approve?** Inside the ERP they already use. Create the order as a draft, quote or on hold, and send a short list of exceptions. Do not build a dashboard until a client asks.
5. **n8n or code?** Developer A's Spring Boot and Spring AI skills fit a small, testable service better than a large visual flow. Use n8n only for glue if it saves time. If you host n8n for paying clients, read its license first.
6. **Is the 1,500 to 4,000 USD plus 400 to 1,500 USD a month guess right?** Partly. The competitor prices below let me check it. See the pricing section.
7. **Is WhatsApp readable by software, and can we still tell which dealer sent the order?** Only if the order goes to a number connected to the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API). Forwarding is a trap: a forwarded message shows the forwarder's number, not the dealer's, so customer matching fails. The fix is Meta's **Coexistence** feature. It links a business's existing WhatsApp Business app number to the Cloud API, so staff keep using the app and every message still arrives with the dealer's own number. See I1 for details.
8. **Who owns the pain?** In small firms, the owner, who sees staff tied up with typing. In medium firms, the customer service or sales operations manager.
9. **Why would a buyer choose two unknown developers over a real product?** Because the products are either priced for bigger firms (Vistaflow from 10,900 USD a year), sold only through ERP partners, or they stop at reading the PDF. You offer setup, per customer code mapping, WhatsApp and email in one place, and a person who fixes things fast, at a lower yearly cost. If a prospect already has a quote from Sicon or Zestcode that they like, let them go.

### What survived, and why

Order intake survived, but not for the reason I first thought. It is not unserved. It survived because:

1. **People are paid to do it today, and tools that do it sell at high prices.** UK job boards list "sales order processor" and "quotes administrator" roles by name. Vistaflow charges 10,900 USD a year at its smallest tier ([Appvizer: Vistaflow pricing](https://www.appvizer.com/operations/order-mgt/vistaflow)). That is the strongest proof of spend in this whole report.
2. **The hard part is matching, not reading.** Turning "10 x blue hex bolt M8 long" or "Sharma ji wala 40 no. dhaaga, 5 peti" into the seller's item code, price and customer account is where cheap parsers stop. Good tools do the matching, but they are priced for bigger firms or tied to one ERP.
3. **It meets your "partly digital" rule.** The order is already digital (email, PDF, WhatsApp) and the ERP is already digital. The only manual part is the person in the middle who retypes.
4. **Small firms are underserved on price, not on features.** A 15 person distributor taking 40 orders a day will not pay 10,900 USD a year to a SaaS vendor that sells through demos. Almost none of these vendors publish prices, which StackCube itself says usually means "the sales process is sized for bigger deals than yours" ([StackCube](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors)).

Then I argued against order intake itself. Three objections are real.

1. **ERP vendors are shipping this themselves.** Microsoft did it for Business Central, and Cin7 did it for Cin7 Core. Sage and Unleashed could follow. That is why you sell a service (mapping, fixes, a person who answers), not "AI reads your PDFs," and why you keep the build small enough that losing one ERP does not kill you.
2. **Third party competitors exist on every ERP I picked.** True. You compete on price, on WhatsApp plus email in one flow, and on hands on setup. You will lose some deals to them. Qualify early.
3. **In India, HublerX already does WhatsApp orders into ERPs.** True, but it says it is best for manufacturers of ₹200 to 2,000 crore on SAP, Oracle, D365 or Tally ([HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/whatsapp-order-management-software), [HublerX guide](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)). The ₹5 to 150 crore Tally distributor is left out. That is your case study market.

## Region 1: India

### Workflow I1 (main pick): WhatsApp and emailed orders into Tally as draft sales orders

**a. Who has it, and size.** Distributors and wholesalers (FMCG, pharma, electrical, hardware, auto parts, yarn and hosiery), and manufacturers selling to dealers. In Ludhiana: hosiery and knitwear, cycle parts, hand tools and auto components. Fits **both** sizes, but the realistic Indian buyer is **small** (owner decides) or the lower end of **medium**.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A retailer or dealer sends an order on WhatsApp. It can be text, a voice note, or a photo of a handwritten list. Bigger buyers email a PDF purchase order.
2. A billing or order clerk reads it, works out which items are meant (often local names or the buyer's own codes), and checks the rate and stock.
3. They type a sales order or sales invoice into Tally, or Busy or Marg.
4. They reply to the dealer, often by calling back if something is unclear.

Industry write ups describe exactly this: orders come as voice notes, photos of handwritten lists and casual messages, and someone types them into the system by hand ([HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026), [Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)).

**After automation.**
1. The distributor's **existing** WhatsApp Business app number, the one dealers already message, is linked to the Cloud API through **Coexistence**. Meta made this available in 2025. Staff keep using the app on their phone as before, and each new message is also sent to your system with the dealer's own number ([Meta for Developers: onboarding Business app users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users/), [360dialog: Coexistence](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/phone-numbers/coexistence), [Chakra: Coexistence availability](https://chakrahq.com/article/whatsapp-coexistence-business-app-register-cloud-api/)). Provider guides say Coexistence is not offered for EU and UK numbers. That does not matter here, because you only use WhatsApp in India.
2. The system transcribes voice notes and reads text and photos. It matches the **customer by the sender's phone number** against the party ledgers in Tally, and the **items** against the Tally stock item list plus a learned alias table for each dealer.
3. It checks the rate against the price list and posts a **sales order voucher** into Tally. Anything uncertain goes to an exceptions list for a clerk. Nothing is invoiced without a person.

**Limits you must check on the first call.**
1. If dealers send orders to **staff members' personal phones**, the design breaks. Forwarding loses the dealer's number. Either the firm moves dealers to the one business number (a change for dealers, which you said you want to avoid) or you walk away. The weak fallback is to let a clerk forward the message, and the system replies with a quick "which dealer?" pick list. That still saves typing, but it adds a step.
2. Provider guides say the API link can drop if the Business app on the phone is not opened for about 7 days ([Whautomate: Coexistence](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence), [MSG91: Coexistence guide](https://msg91.com/guide/unlock-whatsapp-coexistence-use-whatsapp-business-app-and-cloud-api-together)). For a firm that answers dealers every day, this is not a real problem, but monitor it.
3. Phone numbers must be saved on the party ledgers in Tally. If they are not, the first week of setup is filling them in.

**c. Software on each side.**
1. In: WhatsApp Business app linked to the Cloud API through Coexistence, and Gmail or Outlook.
2. Out: TallyPrime. Tally has no cloud API. You talk to it by posting XML over HTTP to port 9000 on the machine running Tally. It must be running, it handles one request at a time, and you must never open that port to the internet. Use a small local connector, or Tally on a cloud server behind a VPN ([TallyHelp: integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/integrate-with-tallyprime/), [AI Accountant: Tally API guide](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/tally-api-integration-guide)).
3. Busy and Marg: I did not confirm a usable API for either. Do not promise them until you test one.

**d. How often and how long.** My estimate, not a measured figure: a small distributor gets 30 to 150 orders a day, and each takes 3 to 8 minutes to read, clarify and type. That is **1.5 to 20 staff hours a day** (30 x 3 minutes at the low end, 150 x 8 minutes at the high end). The range is wide on purpose. **Count this yourself in week one.** It is the number your case study rests on.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Billing executives or order clerks. Ludhiana listings show about ₹14,000 to ₹30,000 a month ([Apna: billing executive jobs in Ludhiana](https://apna.co/jobs/title_billing_executive-jobs-in-ludhiana), [Indeed: billing jobs in Ludhiana](https://in.indeed.com/q-billing-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)).

**Brutal part: when this does not pay in India.** A clerk at ₹15,000 a month works about 8 hours a day.
1. At 30 orders a day and 3 minutes each (1.5 hours), the time saved is worth about ₹2,800 a month. That is less than your fee. **Do not sell here on time saved.**
2. At 60 orders a day and 5 minutes each (5 hours), the time saved is worth about ₹9,400 a month. Now a ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 fee makes sense.
3. **India walk away rule:** under about 50 orders a day, only sell if wrong items, late dispatch or orders lost in chat cost the firm real money, and the owner can tell you how much. For your free case study, pick a firm with 50 or more orders a day, or your numbers will not impress anyone.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** HublerX targets much bigger manufacturers. WhatsApp platforms like Interakt (about ₹2,499 to ₹3,499 a month) do broadcasts, catalogs and chatbots ([ConviDesk: WhatsApp pricing in India](https://www.convideskai.com/blog/whatsapp-automation-pricing-india-2026)). Catalog ordering apps make the retailer change how they order. Tally WhatsApp add ons send documents **out** of Tally. They do not read free text orders **in**. The gap is free text and voice orders into Tally, with item matching, for small firms.

**WhatsApp running cost.** From 1 October 2026, Meta charges for utility messages in India, such as order confirmations, even inside the 24 hour window. That is about ₹0.115 each plus GST ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/meta-to-charge-for-whatsapp-business-utility-messages-in-india/tldr), [ChatMaxima rates](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/india/), [Meta pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). Small, but pass it to the client.

**g. Realistic price (INR).**
1. First 1 or 2 clients: free or ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 setup. In return you get written permission to publish their numbers, a named quote, and a short video.
2. After that: ₹30,000 to ₹75,000 setup and ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 a month, plus WhatsApp and AI usage at cost. Only for firms above the walk away line.
3. Anything above ₹10,000 a month is a hard sell to a small Indian distributor. The number in their head is one clerk's salary.

**h. Sources.** Linked inline above.

### Workflow I2 (fallback, add on only): Principal company price lists into Tally

**a. Who and size.** Distributors of FMCG, pharma, electrical goods and cables, who get rate revisions from the companies they distribute for. **Small and medium.**

**b. Steps today.** The principal company sends a new rate list as a PDF or Excel file, often by email or WhatsApp. A staff member finds each item in Tally (the company's names and codes rarely match the distributor's item names), and updates the price list or price levels. Mistakes mean billing dealers at old rates.

**After automation.** The file is read, each line is matched to the Tally stock item using the same alias table as I1, and a price list update is prepared. A person approves it before it is posted, and big changes are flagged.

**c. Software.** Email or WhatsApp in, TallyPrime out. Tally supports price lists and price levels by item ([TallyHelp: price lists and price levels](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/sales-process/price-lists-and-price-levels-faq/)).

**d. How often and how long.** My estimate: a few files a month per principal, from tens to hundreds of lines each. Count it in the pilot.

**e. Who and cost.** The same billing staff as in I1, at ₹14,000 to ₹30,000 a month.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Cheap Tally add ons already import price lists from Excel, if the file is in their template ([Tally Planet: price list Excel import](https://tallyplanet.com/product/price-list-excel-import/)). The only gap is **matching the principal's names to your item names**. That is too thin to sell on its own.

**g. Price.** ₹1,000 to ₹2,000 a month as an add on to an I1 client. Not a standalone product.

**h. Verdict.** Build it only if an I1 client asks. Its real value is that it mirrors E3, so it gives you a second Indian proof point for free.

## Region 2: Europe (including the UK)

### Workflow E1 (main pick): Emailed customer orders into the ERP as draft sales orders or quotes

**a. Who has it, and size.** B2B wholesalers and distributors (food and drink, building and plumbing supplies, electrical, industrial and MRO parts, packaging, cleaning supplies) and small manufacturers that take orders from trade customers.
1. **Small (15 to 49 staff):** the owner or a director decides. They have one or two people entering orders. **This is your best fit**, because the paid tools are priced above them.
2. **Medium (50 to 250):** the customer service or sales operations manager owns it. These firms are more likely to be approached by Sicon, Vistaflow or Proton, so expect to compete.

The best fit is 20 to 200 orders a day, many different customer formats, and no EDI for most customers.

**b. Steps today.**
1. An order arrives in a shared inbox. It might be email text, a PDF purchase order, an Excel file, or a scan.
2. A sales order processor finds the customer account, reads each line, turns the customer's product codes or descriptions into the seller's own codes, checks price against the customer's price list, and checks stock.
3. They key the sales order into the ERP and send an acknowledgement.
4. Problems (unknown item, wrong price, credit hold) go back and forth by email.

Vendors describe the same pattern ([Vistaflow](https://www.vistaflow.ai/en/blog/wholesalers-and-distributoremail-order-automation-became-vital-in-2026), [Infrrd](https://www.infrrd.ai/blog/order-entry-automation)). Sage users ask about it in Sage's own forums ([Sage 200 community: order automation](https://communityhub.sage.com/gb/sage-200/f/general-discussion/193723/sales-order-purchase-order-automation), [Sage 50 community: sales order automation](https://communityhub.sage.com/gb/sage-50-accounts/f/general-discussion-uk/252855/sage-50-accounts---sales-order-generation---automation-software)).

**Same build, second output: quote requests.** A contractor emails a material list and asks for a price. The steps are the same (read, match items, apply customer pricing) and the output is a quote instead of an order. UK firms hire "quotes administrators" for exactly this ([Reed: Quotes Administrator, Ash Vale](https://www.reed.co.uk/jobs/quotes-administrator/56738280), [Reed: Quotes Administrator, Leicester](https://www.reed.co.uk/jobs/quotes-administrator/56549409)). Offer it as an option in the same project, not as a second product.

**After automation.** The system watches the inbox, reads the order, matches the customer and each line (using the ERP's item list, the customer's past orders, and a learned table of that customer's own codes), checks price and stock, and creates the order as a **draft, quote or on hold** in the ERP. A daily or live exceptions list shows only the orders a human must fix. A human sends the confirmation.

At 60 orders a day and 6 minutes each, that is 6 hours a day, close to one full time person. Measure it in the first week.

Reed shows an average UK sales processor salary of about £28,900 ([Reed average salary](https://www.reed.co.uk/average-salary/average-sales-processor-salary?kd=administration)), with listed roles from £22,300 in Glasgow ([Reed listing](https://www.reed.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor-wfh/54379423)) to £31,500 in Worsley ([Reed listing](https://www.reed.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor-worsley-1300221)). With employer costs, that is roughly £2,300 to £3,300 a month per person. Firms also spend on tools: Vistaflow starts at 10,900 USD a year ([Appvizer](https://www.appvizer.com/operations/order-mgt/vistaflow)).

That gap is narrower than it looked. It will shrink as ERP vendors ship their own features. Move fast, and keep the build small.

**g. Realistic price (checked against the competitor prices above).**

Your guess was 1,500 to 4,000 USD setup and 400 to 1,500 USD a month.

1. **Setup: mostly right.** 2,000 to 4,000 USD for a small firm and 4,000 to 8,000 USD for a medium firm with many customer formats. For your first two European clients, charge 1,000 to 1,500 USD. You have no European case study yet, and buyers will discount an unknown team from abroad.

### Workflow E3 (fallback): Supplier price files into the ERP

This is the same matching engine, pointed at the buying side. It replaces my earlier follow on idea (supplier PO confirmations), which I could not count.

**a. Who and size.** Wholesalers and distributors that buy from many suppliers: building, plumbing and electrical supplies, MRO parts, food and drink. **Both sizes**, but it hurts most in **medium** firms with hundreds of suppliers. In small firms the owner decides; in medium firms the purchasing or commercial manager owns it.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A supplier emails a new price list as Excel or PDF, usually with a date the new prices start.
2. A pricing or purchasing administrator matches each line to the ERP's items (supplier codes and names rarely line up), checks what changed, and works out the new sell prices.
3. They update cost and sell prices in the ERP, often by building an import CSV by hand.
4. Mistakes mean selling below cost until someone notices.

Job descriptions spell this out: "investigating price change files to match SKUs," "conversion and uploading of price changes," "managing new price lists into the company" ([Work From Home Hub: Pricing Administrator](https://www.theworkfromhomehub.co.uk/jobs/pricing-administrator), [Velvet Jobs: pricing administrator duties](https://www.velvetjobs.com/job-descriptions/pricing-administrator)). Sage 200 users ask how to do it in Sage's own forum ([Sage 200 community: supplier price increase](https://communityhub.sage.com/gb/sage-200/f/general-discussion/259797/supplier-price-increase)).

**After automation.** The system reads the file, matches each line to the ERP item, shows what changed (for example "212 lines up, average 6%, 14 lines now sell below cost"), and prepares the update. The pricing person approves, and the prices are written to the ERP on the start date.

**c. Software.** Outlook or Gmail in. Sage 200, Unleashed or Sage 50 out. Sage 200 can already import price changes from a CSV, so you can write through its API or produce a clean file for its own import ([Sage 200 Evolution community: batch price update](https://communityhub.sage.com/za/sage-200-evolution/f/general-discussion/214773/batch-updating-excl-price-and-unit-cost-price-on-inventory-items-when-supplier-provides-updated-price-list)).

**d. How often and how long.** My estimate, not a measured figure: a distributor with 100 active suppliers gets 100 to 300 price files a year, about 2 to 6 a week. Each takes 1 to 4 hours when codes do not match. That is about 4 to 24 hours a week. It is countable: count files, lines and hours per file in the first month.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Pricing administrators, purchasing administrators, or the owner in small firms. General UK administrator pay is in the high £20,000s a year ([Reed: average administrator salary](https://www.reed.co.uk/average-salary/average-administrator-salary)). Bigger firms hire full time for it ([Wickes: Pricing Systems Administrator](https://www.wickescareers.co.uk/job/pricing-systems-administrator-in-watford-hertfordshire-jid-24590)), though Wickes itself is far too big to be your buyer.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** The AI tools I found target SAP users ([CBS Consulting](https://www.cbs-consulting.com/en/ai-supplier-price-update-sap-procurement-prima/), [Duvo](https://blog.duvo.ai/how-to-automate-supplier-price-list-updates-sap-spreadsheets)). Others are generic extractors or parsers that stop before matching ([TableFlow](https://tableflow.com/industries/wholesale), [Apify price list parser](https://apify.com/invincible_nova/supplier-price-list-parser)). Some small ERPs build in bulk price updates ([ERPAG](https://www.erpag.com/news/automating-supplier-price-list-updates)). I found no SMB tool that matches supplier files to Sage 200 or Unleashed items with a margin check. **Weakness:** it happens weekly, not hourly, so the monthly value is lower than E1.

**g. Price.** 1,500 to 3,000 USD setup and 200 to 400 USD a month on its own, or about 150 to 250 USD a month added to an E1 client.

**h. Sources.** Linked inline above.

**When to use E3.** If the first Unleashed or Sage 200 prospect already has an order intake tool, offer E3 instead. It is the same engine, the same buyer and the same ERP connector, and you face far fewer competitors.

## Workflows in both regions: the case study bridge

**Order intake (I1 and E1) is the pair that is truly the same in both.** Price file updates (I2 and E3) are a weaker second pair.

1. **The core is identical.** Messy order in, customer and item matched, price and stock checked, draft order out, human approves exceptions. Only the connector changes (Tally XML versus the Unleashed or Sage 200 REST API).
2. **The Indian version is harder.** Voice notes, Hinglish, handwritten photos and local item names are tougher than a clean PDF purchase order. "It handles WhatsApp voice orders in Punjabi and Hindi" is a strong proof point for a UK buyer whose orders are tidier.
3. **What makes it believable:** numbers, not logos. Publish orders per day, the share of orders that went through with no human edit, the error rate before and after, minutes per order before and after, and a named quote from the owner.
4. **Brutal limit:** some European buyers will still discount an Indian case study. Get your first European client early, even at a low price. Developer A's Finnish client is your best warm lead for an introduction.

## Final top picks

1. **Sell E1 in the UK to small distributors on Unleashed, then Sage 200 through warm leads.** It has named job titles, real salaries, usable APIs, and competitors whose prices prove buyers pay. Avoid Cin7 Core, Business Central and Exact Online, where the vendor or local rivals already cover it.
2. **Build I1 first in Ludhiana, on Tally, as your proof.** Pick one local distributor with 50 or more orders a day, on one WhatsApp Business number, and do it free or cheap in exchange for published numbers.
3. **Keep E3 ready as your fallback.** If a prospect already has order intake covered, sell supplier price file updates on the same engine.

### Things you may not want to hear

1. **You are not first.** At least eight vendors sell order intake. Your edge is price, setup and WhatsApp, not the idea. If that edge does not close deals within your first 10 serious sales calls, switch your lead offer to E3.
2. **Selling is the hard part, not building.** A two person part time team from India with no European case study will not win cold outreach easily. Do not rely on Sage 200 resellers. Many of them sell Sicon. Use warm leads, starting with your Finnish contact, and direct outreach to small Unleashed users.
3. **Pick one ERP and say no to the rest** for the first three clients. Every new connector is weeks of part time work.
4. **Platform risk is real and growing.** Microsoft and Cin7 already built this. Others will follow. Your defence is the service: per client code mapping, fast fixes, and a person who answers.
5. **Indian revenue will be small,** and under 50 orders a day it may not beat hiring a clerk. Treat India as your test lab.
6. **GDPR is not optional in Europe.** You need a data processing agreement, hosting in an EU or UK region, and a clear AI subprocessor list. Your Finnish KYC work should make this familiar.

### Simplest build that works (fits under 50 USD a month)

1. One Spring Boot service (Developer A) with PostgreSQL on a small VPS or a small AWS instance in an EU region.
2. It reads the inbox through Microsoft Graph or the Gmail API. In India only, it also takes WhatsApp webhooks from the client's own number, linked through Coexistence.
3. An LLM call through Spring AI extracts lines. Item matching is plain code: the customer's past orders, an alias table, and fuzzy search on the item list. Only uncertain matches go to the model or a human.
4. The ERP connector writes a draft order. The Tally connector is a tiny local agent. Unleashed and Sage 200 are direct REST calls.
5. Approval happens in the ERP, plus one exceptions email or one simple page (Developer B). No dashboard until a client pays for one.
6. AI and WhatsApp usage is billed to the client at cost. Your fixed cost stays at hosting only.

### Checklist for your first client call

1. How many orders a day, and how many customers send them?
2. What share arrive by email text, PDF, Excel, WhatsApp or phone?
3. In India: do dealers send orders to **one business number**, or to staff members' personal phones?
4. How long does one order take? Time five of them live.
5. Which ERP, which version, and is it cloud or on a local server?
6. Has anyone already offered you an order reading tool (Sicon, Cin7 IDR, Vistaflow, Logistify)? What did they quote?
7. Who fixes wrong orders today, and what did the last mistake cost?
8. Who signs off on spending? (The owner in small firms, the operations head in medium ones.)

**Walk away if:** Europe, fewer than about 20 orders a day; India, fewer than about 50 orders a day with no costly errors; most orders already come through EDI or a web shop; or the client is on Cin7 Core, Business Central or Exact Online.
