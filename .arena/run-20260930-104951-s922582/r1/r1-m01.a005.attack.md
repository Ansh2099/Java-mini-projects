ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The top pick's "gap" is already filled by cheap tools on every ERP it recommends, and the report never checked
Where: E1 f, "The gap: UK and EU SMBs on Sage 200, Exact, Odoo, Unleashed, Cin7 or Xero based stacks, where the US SaaS tools do not connect well" and the E1 table ("Sage 200 ... Good target", "Exact Online, Odoo, Unleashed, Cin7 ... Good targets").
Problem: The task says "If it is already solved by a built in feature or a cheap tool, reject it." The report only checked for a built in feature on Business Central. It did not look for add ons on the systems it tells the user to target, and they exist. The Odoo Apps Store sells AI sale order OCR modules that turn a customer PO (PDF or photo) into a draft quotation with product matching: https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/18.0/dx_sale_digitization, https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/jjro_ai_sales_capture, and Webkul's module at https://webkul.com/blog/odoo-ai-ocr-document-digitization-sale-order/. Logistify sells AI PDF sales order entry for Unleashed (https://logistify.ai/blog/ai-pdf-sales-order-entry-for-unleashed-software). virtualworkforce.ai sells automated order creation for Exact Online (https://virtualworkforce.ai/automated-order-creation-in-exact-online/). The report's own table cites Zestcode already selling "Sage 200 order automation" and still calls Sage 200 a "Good target". So each of the "two European target systems" in the Plan (Sage 200, and Exact Online or Odoo) already has a seller. The report claims a gap there without searching for one. The user would pick targets based on a gap that has not been shown to exist.

The E1 g setup figures are not even marked (estimate), even though Assumption 3 promises that marking. The whole price answer is inference from one salary figure and one SaaS that the report calls irrelevant.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The top India pick's rule 7 compliance rests on a claim the report did not verify
Where: I1 Traps 1, "Meta has offered a 'coexistence' mode that lets a business keep using the phone app on the same number (not verified here). Confirm this for each client before you quote." Compare I1 f, "they ask the retailer to change how they order ... That breaks your rule 7."
Problem: The task's rule 7 says "We do NOT want processes that are fully manual today, because then we would have to convince both the decision maker and the staff to change how they work." Reading WhatsApp messages by software needs the number on the Cloud API. If coexistence does not work for a client, the distributor's staff must stop using the WhatsApp Business app on that number. That is the same kind of change the report uses to reject the competing dealer apps. So whether I1 even qualifies depends on a point the report left as "not verified", and it hands the checking to the user "for each client before you quote". No fallback is given if coexistence fails (for example a second number, or forwarding). The report fails quietly at the exact point that decides if the India pick is valid.

ATTACK 6 [MAJOR] E3 is ACCEPTED even though it fails the report's own acceptance checklist
Where: E3, "suppliers email new price lists ... once to four times a year" for firms "with 20 to 200 suppliers", and "Too lumpy to carry a retainer alone"; checklist item 1, "Does the process run at least 20 times a week? If not, the fee will not stick. ... If any answer is no, walk away".
Problem: Using the report's own numbers, 20 to 200 suppliers times 1 to 4 updates a year is 20 to 800 events a year. That is 0.4 to about 15 a week, below the 20 a week minimum even at the top end. The report tells the user to "walk away" from that, yet labels E3 ACCEPTED and prices it as a 150 to 400 USD monthly retainer. It also admits the work is too lumpy for a retainer. A user following the checklist would reject a workflow the report told them to sell.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] The India price logic contradicts the report's own volume estimate
Where: I1 d, "That is 3 to 30 staff hours a day"; I1 g, "if you save one clerk (15,000 to 25,000 INR), the owner will not pay more than a third of that" leading to "3,000 to 8,000 INR a month".
Problem: 30 staff hours a day is about 4 full time clerks. At the report's own 15,000 to 25,000 INR salary, that is 60,000 to 100,000 INR a month of labour. Pricing is still anchored to "one clerk", so the top of the range is set with no link to the volume. By the report's own method ("Price your monthly fee at 25 to 40% of that value"), a high volume distributor should be quoted 15,000 to 40,000 INR a month, not at most 8,000. A related timing problem: the Plan says UK hours are "13:30 to 22:00 in Ludhiana". UK clocks go back on 25 October 2026, less than 4 weeks after today. After that UK hours are 14:30 to 23:00 IST, which matters for a part time team setting support hours.
