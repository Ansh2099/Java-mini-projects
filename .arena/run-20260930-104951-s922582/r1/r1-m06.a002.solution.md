# Workflow automations SMEs will pay for: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Build one thing: **customer orders that arrive as messages become draft sales orders in the client's ERP or accounting system, and a person clicks approve.**

* **Europe (your paying market):** emailed purchase orders (PDF, Excel, or typed in the email body) become draft sales orders in **Sage 200** (UK) or in Nordic small business systems (**Netvisor, Procountor, Fortnox**). Do **not** target Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central. Microsoft now ships its own Sales Order Agent inside it, so that market is closed to a small team. Exact Online and Odoo are also crowded.
* **India (your case study):** the same engine, with WhatsApp orders (text, photos of handwritten lists) becoming sales orders in TallyPrime for one Ludhiana distributor. Be clear with yourselves: this is **not an Indian product**. Zotok, Karvan and HublerX already sell it in India. You build it once, at cost, to get real before and after numbers.

Three hard truths up front:

1. **The order entry market is being squeezed from both sides.** ERP makers are building it in (Microsoft), and SaaS tools cover the popular ERPs (OrderPilot from €349 a month). Your opening is the ERPs neither has reached yet, plus setup, item matching and custom rules. That window may last one to two years, not five. Build the engine so the ERP connector is a small, swappable part.
2. **Your price guess needs correcting at both ends.** A build fee of $1,500 to $4,000 is right for small firms and in line with UK agency pricing. Medium firms can pay up to about $6,000. Monthly upkeep of $400 to $1,500 is **too high for small firms** ($250 to $500 is realistic) and about right for medium firms ($500 to $1,200).
3. **Your real problem is not the build. It is getting the first European client from Ludhiana with no case study.** The 16 week plan at the end deals with that, and it fits about 20 combined hours a week.

## Assumptions

1. **Exchange rates** (rough working rates, check before you quote): £1 ≈ $1.33, €1 ≈ $1.16, $1 ≈ ₹88. All prices exclude VAT or GST.
2. **"A few weeks of part time work"** means about 100 to 120 hours for one workflow. At 20 combined hours a week, that is 5 to 6 calendar weeks per build. The plan at the end assumes you do **one build at a time**.
3. **"Europe" includes the UK.** Start with the UK (English, Sage 200 common, no structured B2B e-invoicing until April 2029) and Finland (Developer A already has a paying Finnish client, so you have one warm contact).
4. **How I researched.** I used web search and Microsoft Learn. Direct page fetching was blocked for most sites in my setup, so for most links the facts I quote are what the search results showed from that page. The Microsoft Learn pages I read in full. Vendor numbers are vendor claims, not independent studies. Where I have no source, I write **"estimate"** and say how to check it.
5. **Time per order** comes from order entry benchmarks (vendor sourced, see the worked example). You must measure the real number at each client in week one. Your pitch depends on it.

## Part 1. One real example, worked end to end

I solved one concrete case first, then kept only the ideas that behaved like it.

### Worked example: a UK plumbing and heating wholesaler on Sage 200

**The business.** 30 staff (small, by your definition), one branch, sells to installers and small merchants. Runs Sage 200. One order processor, plus sales staff who help at busy times. The owner decides.

**What arrives.** About 50 customer orders a day by email (estimate for this size). Some are PDF purchase orders from the customer's own system. Some are Excel sheets. Many are typed in the email body ("2 x 15mm copper elbow, 10 x 22mm..."). Customers use their own part codes or plain words, not the wholesaler's codes.

**What the person does today.**
1. Opens the email and the attachment.
2. Finds the customer account in Sage 200.
3. For each line, works out which item it means, types code and quantity, checks price and stock.
4. Saves the order and emails the customer a confirmation.

**The count.**
* Published benchmarks for manual order entry: "5 to 8 minutes to process end to end: opening the email, identifying the customer, looking up SKUs, checking inventory, applying pricing, entering the order into the ERP, and sending a confirmation" ([Mirage Metrics](https://miragemetrics.com/blog/true-cost-manual-order-entry/)). Suppliers interviewed by Hobson & Company put the manual purchase order process at 8 to 12 minutes an order ([TradeCentric](https://tradecentric.com/blog/manual-order-processing-cost/)). Both come from vendors that sell automation, so I take the **low end**.
* **Base case: 5 minutes an order.** **Low case: 3.5 minutes** (about 10 to 15 seconds a line for 8 lines, plus reading and confirming).
* 50 orders × 5 minutes = **about 4.2 hours a day**, about 1,040 hours a year over 250 working days.

**The cost.**
* Average UK order processor pay is £26,832 a year ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)). With employer National Insurance and pension, call it about £31,500 (estimate). Over about 1,650 working hours, that is **about £19 an hour**.
* If the processor only reviews a prepared draft (about 1.5 minutes an order, estimate):
  * Base case: saves 3.5 minutes × 50 = 2.9 hours a day, about 730 hours a year, **about £13,900 a year (£1,160 a month).**
  * Low case: saves 2 minutes × 50 = 1.7 hours a day, about 417 hours a year, **about £7,900 a year (£660 a month).**

**What we would charge.** £2,000 build (about $2,660) plus £300 a month (about $400). First year cost to the client: £5,600.
* Base case: the client keeps about £860 a month after our fee, so the build **pays back in about 2.3 months**.
* Low case: about £360 a month after our fee, so **about 5.5 months**.
* These are estimates until you measure the real minutes in week one. Quote the range, not the best case.

**What this example taught me (the rules I then used on every other idea):**
1. **The saving is time, not a head.** Nobody gets fired over 3 hours a day. The owner buys for other reasons: same day dispatch before the courier cut off, fewer wrong items sent, and no backlog when the order person is off sick. Sell those, with the hours as proof.
2. **The hard part is item matching, not reading the PDF.** Any AI model can read the PDF. The value is mapping "22mm elbow" to item code 10457 for this customer and learning from each correction. Tools that only extract text are not a threat. Tools that do the matching are.
3. **The ERP decides if the job is buildable, and if it is sellable.** Sage 200 has a REST API that creates sales orders ([Sage Developer, POST sop_orders](https://developer.sage.com/200-uk/apis/sage-200-professional/2024.01/combined/groups/sop/tags/sop-orders/paths/postsoporder)), so it is buildable. But the ERP must also not already include this feature. Business Central has a clean API too, and Microsoft now ships a built in Sales Order Agent there, so BC is not sellable.
4. **The monthly price is capped by the cheapest alternative on that ERP.** If a ready tool or a built in agent exists for the client's ERP, you cannot charge $1,000 a month. You win where neither exists, or where the client needs custom rules.
5. **Structured e-invoicing kills supplier invoice ideas but not order ideas.** Mandates cover invoices, not customer purchase orders, so orders stay messy.

Every workflow below is checked against these five lessons and your seven rules.

## Part 2. India

**Honest framing.** India is your case study market. At Indian salaries the labour saving is small, and for the main workflow funded Indian tools already exist. Treat paid Indian work as a bonus.

### Workflow I1 (case study build): WhatsApp orders to TallyPrime sales orders

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors that sell to retailers: hosiery and garments, auto parts, bicycle parts, hardware, FMCG sub distributors, pharma stockists. All are common in Ludhiana. Fits **both** sizes: small (owner decides) and medium (sales or accounts head owns it, often with 2 to 4 billing clerks).

**b. Steps today.**
1. Retailers or the firm's own salesmen send orders on WhatsApp: typed lists, photos of a handwritten list, sometimes voice notes, in Hindi, Punjabi or English.
2. A billing clerk reads each chat, finds the party ledger in Tally, and types each item and quantity into a sales order or sales invoice. Stock is checked.
3. The clerk replies on WhatsApp with a confirmation or "item not in stock".
4. Data ends up in TallyPrime (sales order, then invoice and e-way bill).

**c. Software.** In: WhatsApp Business. Out: TallyPrime (sometimes Busy or Marg). Middle: our review screen. That is 2 tools plus our screen.

**d. Frequency and time.** Estimate for a 15 to 25 person distributor: 50 to 100 orders a day, 4 to 6 lines each, about 4 minutes each. At 70 orders that is about 4.7 hours a day. Peaks are much higher in season (Diwali, and October to January for hosiery). **Check in one day** by sitting with the clerk and counting.

**e. Who does it and what it costs (sourced).**
* The job exists and is advertised. Naukri lists 96 billing job vacancies in Ludhiana ([Naukri](https://www.naukri.com/billing-jobs-in-ludhiana)), and Tally operator and billing executive listings ask for "Sale & Purchase Entry" and invoice making ([Naukri Tally jobs](https://www.naukri.com/tally-jobs-in-ludhiana), [SimplyHired](https://www.simplyhired.co.in/search?q=tally&l=ludhiana,+punjab)).
* Pay: Ludhiana billing executive listings show ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 a month, mostly ₹13,000 to ₹18,000 ([Apna](https://apna.co/jobs/title_billing_executive-jobs-in-ludhiana), [WorkIndia listing](https://www.workindia.in/jobs/billing_executive-sector39a-ludhiana-6349089/), [WorkIndia listing 2](https://www.workindia.in/jobs/billing_executive-miller_ganj-ludhiana-6484303/)).
* At ₹16,000 a month and about 208 hours (26 days × 8), that is about ₹77 an hour. Order typing at 4.7 hours a day costs about ₹9,300 a month. If the clerk only reviews drafts (about 1 minute an order), the saving is about 3.5 hours a day, **about ₹7,000 a month**.
* Firms also already pay for tools that do this job (see f). That is the strongest proof of spend.

**The jugaad test, done honestly.** A second clerk costs ₹13,000 to ₹18,000 a month. Automation beats that only when (1) volume would otherwise need a second clerk, especially in season, (2) wrong items cost returns and angry retailers, or (3) the owner wants same day billing and dispatch. A firm with one clerk who is not overloaded will not buy. Do not try to sell to them.

**The honest verdict.** There is **no clear gap** for a plain "WhatsApp order to Tally" product in India. HublerX targets manufacturers with ₹200 to 2,000 crore turnover, which is above most Ludhiana distributors, but Zotok and Karvan aim at exactly the distributors you would visit, and Zotok's entry price is low. By your own rule ("if a cheap tool solves it, reject it"), **I1 fails as an Indian product.** It still passes as a **case study build**, for three reasons:
1. The European buyer never sees Zotok. What they see is your engine reading a messy order, matching it to the item master, and learning from corrections. That is exactly what you will sell in Europe.
2. Some distributors will not have tried these tools, or will run Busy or Marg. Busy has no default third party integration and needs a partner or custom route ([Busy FAQ](https://busy.in/faqs/third-party-services/integration/1/)), so check each one separately.
3. A photo of a handwritten Punjabi order list working is a far stronger demo than a clean PDF.

**Your answer to "why not just buy Zotok?"** is: "Try it. If it works for you, use it. We will build yours free in return for your numbers and a video." If the distributor already uses Zotok or Karvan and is happy, move on to another distributor.

**Technical notes.**
* Tally integration is proven. TallyPrime runs an HTTP XML server (usually port 9000), and open source code imports sales orders through it ([sales order import example](https://github.com/SatyamTechsolvo/tallyerp9import), [Node.js to TallyPrime](https://github.com/JeevanKN/Tally_Integration_with-EC2-Virtual-Window-RDP-), [TallyPrime SDK](https://github.com/labs-infinitum/tallyprime-sdk)).
* Tally usually sits on a Windows PC in the shop. You need a small local agent on that PC that pulls approved orders from your server and posts XML to localhost:9000. Developer A can write this in Java in a few days.
* WhatsApp: Meta's "coexistence" lets one number run both the WhatsApp Business app and the Cloud API, and it is supported in India. The app must be opened at least once every 13 days ([360dialog](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/onboarding/whatsapp-coexistence), [Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence)). So the client keeps its existing number. Service replies inside the 24 hour window are free, and utility templates cost about ₹0.145 each through a provider such as AiSensy, which starts at ₹1,500 a month ([AiSensy pricing](https://aisensy.com/pricing)). Pass these costs to the client.

**g. Realistic price (INR).** Capped by Zotok's entry price, so keep it low:
* Small (5 to 49 staff): ₹20,000 to ₹40,000 setup, ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 a month.
* Medium (50 to 250 staff, 2 or more clerks): ₹60,000 to ₹1,20,000 setup, ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 a month, only where you add something the tools do not (Busy or Marg, several companies in one Tally, custom scheme and discount rules).
* Your first client: free or at cost, in return for real numbers, a named case study and a video testimonial.

**h. Sources.** All linked in e and f above.

### Workflow I2 (add on only, validate first): payment screenshots to Tally receipts

**a. Who has it.** The same distributors. Retailers pay by UPI, NEFT or cheque and send a screenshot or message ("sent 25,000 for bills 1142 and 1150"). Both small and medium.

**b. Steps today.** The accountant matches the bank credit to the party, creates a receipt voucher in Tally, and allocates it against the right bills. Short payments are chased on WhatsApp.

**c. Software.** WhatsApp plus the bank statement in, TallyPrime out. That is 3 tools.

**d. Frequency and time.** Estimate: 30 to 80 receipts a day for a mid sized distributor, 2 to 4 minutes each. Check in one day.

**e. Who and cost.** An accounts clerk in the same pay band as I1 (₹10,000 to ₹20,000 a month in Ludhiana listings, sources in I1 e).

**f. Existing tools and gap.** This is crowded too. Vyapar TaxOne (formerly Suvit) says its AI "will extract data from images, create accurate vouchers and push them directly to Tally" ([Vyapar TaxOne](https://taxone.vyapar.com/gst-reconciliation-feature)). Karvan parses bank SMS and matches payments to orders ([Karvan](https://www.karvan.ai/)). The only possible gap is bill wise allocation from the retailer's own WhatsApp note. **Test TaxOne and Karvan before building.** If either does bill wise receipts, drop this.

**g. Price.** Only as an add on to I1: ₹10,000 to ₹25,000 setup, ₹2,000 to ₹4,000 a month (estimate).

**About the Business Central agent (why I dropped BC).** It watches a Microsoft 365 mailbox, reads the email body and PDF or image attachments, finds the customer by sender email, matches items by code, cross reference or meaning, checks stock, and drafts a quote or order for a person to approve ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent)). ### E-invoicing changes the map (important)

* Germany: all businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices since 1 January 2025; issuing becomes mandatory in 2027 and 2028 ([ecosio](https://ecosio.com/en/blog/germany-e-invoicing-explained/)).
* France: all companies must be able to receive e-invoices from 1 September 2026; SMEs must issue from September 2027 ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/europe/2025/09/france-e-invoicing-e-reporting-mandate-2026-2027.html)).
* UK: mandatory B2B e-invoicing from April 2029 ([ICAS](https://www.icas.com/news-insights-events/news/tax/autumn-budget-2025-e-invoicing-will-go-ahead-from-2029)).

**What this means for you.** Supplier invoices arriving as PDFs will shrink. Do not build a business on reading supplier invoice PDFs (it is also crowded, and Business Central's Payables Agent does it). Customer purchase orders and remittance advices are not covered by these mandates, so they stay messy.

### Workflow E1 (top pick): emailed customer POs to draft sales orders in Sage 200 or Nordic systems

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors (plumbing and heating, electrical, building supplies, industrial and MRO parts, packaging, food service, fasteners) and manufacturers selling to trade customers. **Both** sizes. Small: owner or sales manager decides. Medium: operations or customer service manager owns it. Sage 200 users lean medium; Netvisor, Procountor and Fortnox users lean small.

**b. Steps today.** An order arrives by email as PDF, Excel or typed text. **d. Frequency and time.** 20 to 300 orders a day depending on size (estimate; measure it). About 5 minutes an order in the base case and 3.5 in the low case (worked example, with sources). A 50 order day is about 3 to 4 hours of keying.

**e. Who and cost.** Sales order processors, customer service staff, sometimes sales reps. **The honest gap.**
* On Business Central, Exact Online and Odoo, there is no gap for you. Walk away.
* On **Sage 200**, no cheap self serve tool showed up in my searches. The competitor is a service firm, which proves UK firms pay for this as a service, the same model you want to sell. OrderPilot claims "50+ ERPs", so **check whether it supports Sage 200 before each pitch.**
* On **Netvisor, Procountor and Fortnox**, I found no dedicated tool, but my search was limited, so check again before selling. Orders there will often be in Finnish or Swedish, which modern LLMs handle well, and which Microsoft's agent does not officially support.
* Your edge everywhere: you set it up, tune item matching to their catalogue and each customer's own codes, add their rules (price band, credit hold, minimum order, delivery cut off), and answer the phone.

**g. Realistic price (Europe).**
* Small: $1,500 to $3,000 build, **$250 to $500 a month**.
* Medium: $3,000 to $6,000 build, $500 to $1,200 a month (more volume, more customer formats, more rules).

**h. Sources.** All linked above in this section, in the software table and in the worked example.

**Build outline (about 100 to 120 hours).**
1. Mail hook: Microsoft Graph subscription (or Gmail push) on the orders inbox.
2. Spring Boot service (Developer A): pull the attachment, call an LLM with vision to extract customer, lines and dates as JSON.
3. Matching: a PostgreSQL table of the item master plus a per customer alias table ("22mm elbow" means item 10457 for customer X). Every human correction saves a new alias.
4. Review screen (Developer B, TypeScript): email on the left, draft order on the right, low confidence fields in yellow, one Approve button.
5. On approve: create the sales order through the ERP connector (Tally, Sage 200, Netvisor, Procountor or Fortnox); send the confirmation email.
6. Log counts: orders in, lines matched without edits, corrections, minutes spent. This log is your case study and your renewal argument.

Keep step 5 as a thin adapter. The same engine then serves India and every European ERP, and you can move when a vendor closes a market.

### Workflow E2 (validate before building): remittance advice to cash allocation in Sage 200

**a. Who has it.** Suppliers to big retailers and national accounts: food and drink producers, consumer goods wholesalers, building product suppliers. Mostly **medium**; small only if they sell to big chains.

**b. Steps today.** A big customer pays one lump sum and emails a long remittance (PDF or spreadsheet) listing many invoices, with short payments and deductions. A credit controller matches each line to open invoices, posts the allocation, and raises queries on deductions. Vendor example: a food wholesaler supplying a national grocer "may receive a payment once a fortnight with a 12 page remittance covering 180 odd invoices" ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/remittance-automation-software)).

**c. Software.** In: email. Out: Sage 200. Middle: our review screen. The bank lines are read from the ERP itself (its bank feed or statement import), so that is **2 tools**, or 3 if the client only has a bank CSV. **Remittances that sit on retailer portals are out of scope**, because those portals usually have no public API. If the client downloads them and forwards them to the inbox, that is their step, not ours. We only prepare allocations for a person to approve. We never move money. **Check first** that the Sage 200 API can post the allocation. If it cannot, we prepare a batch that the clerk imports.

**d. Frequency and time.** Estimate: weekly or fortnightly per big customer; 1 to 3 hours per large remittance, plus query chasing. Measure at the client.

**e. Who and cost.** Credit controller. Average UK pay is £27,985 a year ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/credit-controller/salaries)).

**f. Existing tools and gap.** Crowded on Xero (RemitClear, [for Xero](https://remitclear.com/solutions/automated-cash-application-xero)) and on Business Central (a 2026 roundup lists several AR and cash application tools for BC: [Centime](https://www.centime.com/articles/top-7-accounts-receivable-automation-software-solutions-for-microsoft-dynamics-365-business-central-in-2026)). Enterprise suites exist: [Esker](https://www.esker.com/solutions/cash-application/), [HighRadius](https://www.highradius.com/product/automated-cash-allocation-software/). UK services also sell it ([Telic Digital](https://telic.digital/automated-cash-allocation/)). The only possible gap is medium Sage 200 firms too small for enterprise suites. That is narrow, so **only build E2 if an E1 client asks for it.**

**g. Price.** $2,500 to $5,000 build, $400 to $800 a month (estimate). Medium buyers only.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### Workflow E3 (add on only): supplier price lists into ERP and webshop

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors with many suppliers, often with a B2B webshop. Both sizes.

**b. Steps today.** A supplier emails a new price list in Excel or PDF with its own columns. A buyer maps it, recalculates margins, and updates cost and sell prices in the ERP and the webshop. A vendor claims 200 changed products take 2 to 3 business days by hand ([Duvo](https://blog.duvo.ai/how-to-automate-supplier-price-list-updates-sap-spreadsheets)).

**c. Software.** Email in; ERP plus Shopify or WooCommerce out. That is 3 tools.

**d. Frequency and time.** Each supplier changes prices a few times a year, but a firm with 100 suppliers sees updates most weeks (estimate).

**e. Who and cost.** Buyer or purchasing assistant; not sourced.

**f. Existing tools and gap.** Tools exist: [Arovon](https://arovon.com/price-list-automation), [TableFlow](https://tableflow.com/industries/wholesale), ERPAG's own feature ([ERPAG](https://www.erpag.com/news/automating-supplier-price-list-updates)). The gap is only client specific margin rules and posting to their exact systems.

**g. Price.** $1,500 to $3,000 build, $200 to $400 a month (estimate).

**h. Verdict.** Lower value and tools exist. Offer it only as a second workflow to an E1 client.

## Part 5. Final top picks

1. **Build E1 for Sage 200 (UK) and Netvisor, Procountor or Fortnox (Nordics) as your European product.**
   Why: it passes all seven rules; the job is in hundreds of UK job ads; firms already pay SaaS and service firms for it, so the budget exists; these ERPs have usable APIs and no built in agent or cheap tool that I found; orders are not affected by e-invoicing; and it fits your skills (Spring Boot, Postgres and Spring AI for Developer A, the review screen in TypeScript for Developer B). Finland gives you a warm first contact through Developer A's existing client.
   Risk: vendors are moving in. Microsoft already did on Business Central. Check OrderPilot's ERP list before every pitch, keep the ERP connector thin, and sell setup, matching, custom rules and support rather than "AI reads PDFs".

2. **Build I1 (WhatsApp orders to TallyPrime) once, for one Ludhiana distributor, as your case study.**
   Why: the same engine, cheap to run, local and in person, and it gives you real before and after numbers. Do not expect Indian revenue from it. Zotok and Karvan already sell it, so skip any distributor already using them.

3. **Keep E2 (remittance to cash allocation on Sage 200) as an upsell** only if an E1 client asks. It is narrow and crowded elsewhere.

4. **Do not build:** order entry on Business Central, Exact Online or Odoo; supplier invoice capture; statement reconciliation; PO matching in Xero; freight quoting; customs entries; timesheets; GST 2B tools; WhatsApp to Tally as an Indian product.
