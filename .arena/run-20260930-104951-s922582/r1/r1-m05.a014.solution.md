# Workflows to Build and Sell: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Build one thing well: **an "order reader" that turns messy customer orders into draft sales orders in the client's ERP or accounting system, with a person approving each one.**

* In **Europe and the UK** (your paying market), sell it to wholesalers, distributors and small manufacturers whose customers email orders as PDFs, Excel files or plain text. Start with firms on **Unleashed or Cin7 Core** (clean APIs included in the plan). Next come **Sage 200** firms through a Sage reseller, and **Odoo** firms on a plan that allows the API.
* In **India** (proof of work), sell it to firms on **Tally**. There are two routes: exporters and manufacturers who get purchase orders by email (the best case study for Europe), and distributors whose retailers send orders 1:1 to one WhatsApp Business number.
* Your second product, for medium firms in Europe only, is **checking supplier order confirmations against your own purchase orders**. It uses the same reading engine, pointed the other way.

Things we checked and you should **not** build: supplier invoice entry, GST reconciliation, and payment reminders. Cheap tools or free built in features already do these.

Two things that will hurt if you miss them:
1. **Microsoft Business Central now has its own Sales Order Agent**, at about 17 US cents an order. Do not lead with Business Central shops. Only pitch them where that agent has been tried and fails on their orders.
2. **Busy has no public API, and Odoo Online and Sage 200 put their APIs behind paid plans or programmes.** "Which system, and can we write to it?" is the first question on every call.

Your price guess is close for small firms but a bit high on upkeep. See the pricing section.

## Assumptions

1. "Workflow we can sell" means your own rules are met on all 7 points. I dropped anything that failed even one.
2. "Europe" means the UK plus the EU. I lean toward the UK first because it speaks English, uses Sage and Xero with Unleashed or Cin7 a lot, and is 4.5 to 5.5 hours behind Ludhiana. That gap is easy to work with.
3. When I give volumes or times with no source, I label them **(estimate)**. Check every estimate in your first client call with a stopwatch.
4. My research tools could open only some pages. Many facts below come from search results that listed those pages. **Check any price again before you quote it to a client.** Prices change fast in this market.
5. Phone orders do not count. They are fully manual today, and your rule 7 excludes them. We only automate orders that already arrive as text, files or images in a channel we can reach by API.

## How I judged each idea: copy the post office

The US Postal Service had this exact problem decades ago, and solved it.

**How they did it.** Letters arrive with messy addresses. A machine reads most of them. The ones it cannot read get photographed and sent to a Remote Encoding Center, where a person looks at the image and types the address. The typed result is checked against the real address list, and then a barcode is printed so the letter flows on by machine. As reading software got better, it could handle up to 95% of the handwritten addresses that used to need people. Today one center in Salt Lake City does what 55 centers used to do. ([USPS Facts](https://facts.usps.com/remote-encoding-center-rec-deciphering-handwriting/), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/when-usps-cant-decipher-bad-handwriting-address-utah-facility-comes-to-rescue/))

That is your business in one picture:

| Post office | Your workflow |
|---|---|
| Messy letter arrives | Messy order, confirmation or remittance arrives by email or WhatsApp |
| Machine reads the address | AI reads the document |
| Checked against the national address list | Checked against the client's item list, price list, open orders or open invoices |
| Humans key the ones the machine cannot read | A clerk approves or fixes the exceptions |
| Barcode printed, letter moves on | Draft order or update written into the ERP |

**Four lessons to copy:**
1. **Always check against a master list.** Reading is not enough. The postal system matches every reading against real addresses. Every workflow below has a "truth list" in the client's system to check against. If there is no truth list, the idea is weak.
2. **Route by confidence.** Easy items flow through. Unsure items go to a person, with the original image beside the AI's guess.
3. **Keep a manual fallback.** If the machine fails, mail still gets delivered by hand. If your system goes down, orders stay in the inbox and the clerk works the old way. Promise clients this. It kills the fear of "what if it breaks."
4. **Measure the read rate.** The postal service tracks how much the machine handles on its own. You track "share of order lines that needed no fix." That number is your case study.

**Where the copy breaks, and the fix:**
1. **The post office has a clean address list. Small firms have messy item lists.** One product can have three names, and customers use their own names too. *Fix:* sell a paid "clean up" step first. Build a memory of each customer's names for each product, which grows every time the clerk fixes a line.
2. **The post office handles millions of letters. Your client handles 20 to 300 orders a day.** *Fix:* reuse one engine for every client so a custom build stays cheap. Only the connector to their ERP changes.
3. **A wrong letter costs a stamp. A wrong order costs a customer.** *Fix:* a person approves every order at first. Only after weeks of clean results do you let simple repeat orders from trusted customers go through with a lighter check.
4. **The post office owns its machines and its mail slots. You own neither the client's ERP nor their inbox.** This break is bigger than it looks. You can only automate what you can **read** by API and **write** by API. *Fix:* the first two questions on every sales call are "Where do orders land?" and "Which system, which version, cloud or desktop, and which plan?" If there is no reachable inbox or no write route, it is no deal, or a slower build with a file the clerk imports.
5. **The keyers kept jobs.** *Fix:* tell the owner and the clerk that the clerk becomes the approver. That lowers pushback from staff, which matters because you cannot win if the staff fight it.

India is for proof of work. Pick the Indian workflow that looks most like the European one and has the cleanest write route. That is why I2 below comes before I1.

### I2. Purchase orders by email into Tally for Indian manufacturers and exporters (TOP PICK for India: the bridge to Europe)

**a. Who has it.** Manufacturers and exporters who sell to other businesses: knitwear and hosiery, hand tools, bicycle parts, auto parts, machinery parts. **Both small and medium.** Ludhiana has many of these. The best target is a firm that exports to European buyers, because the orders look exactly like the ones you will handle in Europe.

**b. Steps today.** A buyer emails a PO as a PDF or Excel file. A sales or export coordinator reads it, matches the buyer's item codes to their own, checks price and delivery date against the agreed rate, and types a sales order into Tally. Any mismatch goes back to the buyer by email. Local job ads show this task exists as paid work. Ludhiana listings for back office and order processing coordinators include billing, purchase orders, order processing and "mail communications with customers" ([Indeed Ludhiana order processing jobs](https://in.indeed.com/q-order-processing-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)).

**c. Software.** Email (Gmail or Outlook, both with free APIs) in. Tally out, through the port 9000 connector. If the client is on Busy, use the Excel import route above.

**d. Frequency (estimate).** 5 to 40 POs a day. Each can have many lines, so 10 to 30 minutes each. That is 1 to 20 hours a day of coordinator time. Measure it in week one.

**e. Who and cost.** An export sales coordinator. A Ludhiana export firm lists this role at ₹3.6 to 8.4 lakh a year ([Internshala, New Swan, Ludhiana](https://internshala.com/job/detail/export-sales-coordinator-job-in-ludhiana-at-new-swan1763036647)). The India average is about ₹3.2 lakh a year, in a range of about ₹1.7 to 4.9 lakh ([WorldSalaries](https://worldsalaries.com/average-export-sales-coordinator-salary-in-india/)). So one coordinator costs about ₹27,000 to ₹70,000 a month. The bigger cost is errors: a wrong price or date on an export order can mean a chargeback or a lost buyer.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Purchase side tools like Suvit handle bills, not customer orders (see I3). Enterprise order tools such as Conexiom are priced for large firms (see E1). I found no cheap tool aimed at Tally users for inbound customer POs. That is a real gap, though the volume per client is lower than I1.

**g. Price (estimate).** Setup ₹60,000 to ₹1,50,000. Monthly ₹8,000 to ₹15,000, which stays under half of the coordinator's monthly cost (about ₹13,500 to ₹35,000). Exporters earn in euros and dollars, so they pay a little more readily. For your first client, a free or at cost 6 week pilot in return for published numbers is the better deal.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### I1. WhatsApp orders from retailers into Tally (SECOND PICK for India, with a hard qualifying check)

**a. Who has it.** Distributors and wholesalers: FMCG, pharma, electricals, hardware, auto spares, paints. Mostly **small** (5 to 49 staff), where the owner decides. Some **medium** distributors too. WhatsApp is already the default ordering channel for Indian distributors and wholesalers ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/)).

**b. Steps today.**
1. A retailer or field salesman sends a WhatsApp message. It can be text ("Parle G 10 ctn, Tiger 5 ctn"), a photo of a handwritten list, or a voice note.
2. A billing clerk reads it, works out which item codes are meant, and checks stock and the retailer's credit.
3. The clerk types a sales order or invoice into Tally (or Busy or Marg).
4. The clerk sends a confirmation or the invoice PDF back on WhatsApp, often from a personal phone ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Takkada](https://takkada.com/blog/whatsapp-invoice-format-tally/)).

The data ends up in the accounting software. The middle part (read, match, retype) is manual.

**c. Software on each side, and the big limit.**
* **In: WhatsApp.** You can only read messages sent 1:1 to **one registered WhatsApp Business number**, through Meta's Cloud API. The good news: with **Coexistence**, the distributor keeps its existing WhatsApp Business app number and keeps using the app on the phone, while the API gets a copy of 1:1 chats. So retailers who already message that number change nothing ([Meta: onboarding Business app users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users/), [YCloud](https://www.ycloud.com/blog/whatsapp-business-app-coexistence-meta-update), [Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence)). The bad news: **group chats do not sync to the API**, and neither do disappearing or view once messages. The API also **cannot see anyone's personal WhatsApp** ([Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence), [Unipile](https://www.unipile.com/whatsapp-api-a-complete-guide-to-integration/)). Orders that go to a salesman's own phone, or into a beat group, are invisible to you.
* **Out:** Tally by XML on port 9000. Busy by the Excel import file. Marg is skipped (see the table above).

**The qualifying check (do this before you quote).** Ask the owner to count one normal day of orders by where they land: the business number 1:1, a group, a salesman's phone, a phone call. **Only go ahead if most orders (say 70% or more) land 1:1 on one business number, or can be moved there by staff alone.** The staff side fix is small: salesmen forward retailer orders to the business number instead of to the clerk's personal phone. That asks staff, not 200 to 500 retailers, to change one habit. If most orders live in groups, walk away. You would have to change how retailers order, which breaks your rule 7.

**d. How often and how long (estimate).** A distributor serving 200 to 500 shops may get 50 to 300 orders a day. Each takes about 3 to 10 minutes to read, match and type. The crunch comes in the morning, before dispatch vans leave. Measure this on day one.

**e. Who does it and the cost.** Billing clerks or data entry operators. In Ludhiana, jobs with Tally skills list about ₹15,000 to ₹17,000 a month, and some computer operator jobs list ₹18,000 to ₹23,000 ([Glassdoor Ludhiana](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/ludhiana-data-entry-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,8_IC2939907_KO9,19.htm), [Apna Ludhiana](https://apna.co/jobs/computer_data_entry_operator_copa-jobs-in-ludhiana), [Apna data entry](https://apna.co/jobs/data_entry-jobs-in-ludhiana)).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Many tools already send invoices, dispatch updates and payment reminders *out* of Tally or Marg on WhatsApp ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Perimattic Marg connector](https://perimattic.com/products/erpplugs/connectors/marg-whatsapp/)). Others push retailers to order from a WhatsApp catalog or bot ([HublerX list](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026), [Wydely](https://www.wydely.io/whatsapp-automation-wholesale-trading-businesses.html)). **The gap:** the messy free text, photo and voice orders that retailers keep sending to the business number, matched to *this* distributor's own item codes in Tally.

**Honest warning.** One clerk costs ₹15,000 to ₹23,000 a month. If the distributor has one clerk who is not overloaded, just hiring beats you. Only sell where there are **2 or more people typing orders**, or where morning delays and wrong items (returns, lost sales) clearly hurt. And only where the qualifying check above passes.

**WhatsApp running cost.** Meta charges no monthly fee for using the Cloud API directly, without a reseller ([Chatarmin](https://chatarmin.com/en/blog/whatsapp-cloudapi)). From 1 October 2026, replies inside the 24 hour service window stop being free in India. Each business number gets 1,000 free service messages a month, then about ₹0.115 each plus GST ([Authgear](https://www.authgear.com/post/whatsapp-api-pricing/), [Whautomate India rate card](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india), [Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). At 200 confirmations a day (about 5,000 a month), that is roughly ₹460 plus GST a month. The number and the Meta bill belong to the client, so this never touches your budget.

**g. Realistic price (estimate).** It has to cost clearly less than one clerk, so keep the monthly fee **below half of one clerk's salary** (₹7,500 to ₹11,500). Setup ₹40,000 to ₹1,00,000. Monthly ₹5,000 to ₹10,000, with WhatsApp charges paid by the client straight to Meta. Take ₹1,000 to ₹2,000 a month off for Busy clients, because the clerk still does the import click.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### I3. Supplier bill entry into Tally with GSTR 2B checking (REJECT as a product)

**a. Who.** Traders, manufacturers, and CA firms that keep books for them. Both sizes.

**b. Steps.** Supplier bills arrive as PDFs or photos. Someone types them into Tally, then matches them against GSTR 2B to claim input tax credit.

**c. Software.** Email or WhatsApp in, Tally out, GST portal data.

**d, e.** High volume at month end. Done by accountants and junior staff.

**f. Why reject.** It is already covered. **Suvit (now Vyapar TaxOne)** turns purchase and sales PDFs, images and bank statements into Tally entries, and includes GST reconciliation ([Suvit pricing](https://www.suvit.io/pricing), [Suvit data entry](https://www.suvit.io/data-entry-automation-feature)). **TallyPrime itself** now imports GSTR 2B and flags mismatches, with more matching added in release 6.1 ([Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gstr-2a-2b-reconciliation-in-tallyprime/), [Tally at Cloud](https://tallyatcloud.com/article/Automated-GSTR2B-Matching-in-TallyPrime-6.1:-Save-Time-&-Improve-GST-Accuracy/145/0/1)).

**g. Price.** Low, because cheap tools exist. Do not compete here.

### Also rejected for India
* **Payment reminders from Tally outstanding lists.** Many WhatsApp for Tally tools already send reminders and ledger statements ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)). It is also close to a "single task," which you said you do not sell.

## Region 2: Europe and the UK

### E1. Customer orders by email into the ERP as draft sales orders (TOP PICK overall)

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers, distributors, trade suppliers (building, electrical, food service, packaging, industrial parts), and small manufacturers. **Both sizes.** Small firms (5 to 49) with 20 or more emailed orders a day. Medium firms (50 to 250) with a sales office team, where the customer service or operations manager owns the pain.

**b. Steps today.** A customer emails an order: a PDF from their own system, an Excel sheet, or a line of text like "send 30 of the usual." A sales order processor opens it, works out the SKUs, checks that customer's prices, and types the lines into the ERP. Manual entry of one order commonly takes **10 to 15 minutes**, so 20 orders a day is a part time job ([StackCube](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors), [Y Meadows](https://ymeadows.com/en/insights/blog/ai-order-entry-for-distributors/)).

**c. Software on each side, and whether it has usable APIs.** Email in is easy: Microsoft 365 and Gmail both have free APIs. The write side decides the deal. Here is the order to chase them in:

| Rank | System | Usable API? What it costs you to get in | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|

The number of API calls depends on the client's plan, and extra bundles can be bought. Trial accounts can connect ([Unleashed API: Sales Orders](https://apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/SalesOrders), [Unleashed API FAQ](https://apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/Faq), [Unleashed API access](https://support.unleashedsoftware.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402393233689-Unleashed-API-Access)) | **Start here.** No developer programme fee, and cloud only. |

| 3 | **Odoo** | Yes, a documented external API. **But** on Odoo Online, API access needs the **Custom** plan (about $61 per user a month against about $31 on Standard). Odoo.sh and self hosted Odoo are fine. The old XML RPC and JSON RPC endpoints are being phased out for the newer JSON 2 API ([Odin on Odoo API cost](https://www.odin.ist/blog/odoo-external-api-cost.html), [Wavect](https://wavect.io/blog/odoo-erp-api-integration-limits-2026/), [Odoo 19 JSON 2 API docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/developer/reference/external_api.html)) | **Good if they are already on Custom, Odoo.sh or self hosted.** Do not ask a client to upgrade every user just for you. Build on JSON 2 so you are not caught by the phase out. |
| 4 | **Business Central** | Yes, a good API ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/api/dynamics_salesorder_create)) | **Qualify, do not lead.** See below. |
| 5 | **Sage 50** (desktop) | Weak. SData support ended in September 2025. You now need the Sage Developer Programme and something running on the client's Windows PC ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-news/sage-sdata-is-gone-heres-what-that-means-for-your-sage-50-integrations/)) | Same programme gate as Sage 200, plus a desktop connector. Skip for now. |

**Business Central: qualify, do not lead.** Microsoft now ships a **Sales Order Agent** inside Business Central. It reads a shared mailbox, including PDF and image attachments, finds the customer, checks stock, and creates a quote or order ([Sales Order Agent overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent)). A typical order uses about 16.5 Copilot Credits ([Microsoft billing page](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/administration/tenant-admin-center-manage-consumption-billing)), at about $0.01 per credit ([MSDynamicsWorld](https://msdynamicsworld.com/blog-post/business-centrals-ai-agents-run-consumption-billing-heres-what-means-your-2027-budget)). That is about 17 cents an order.

The real problem is not the 17 cents. You sell a monthly retainer against a clerk's salary, not a per order fee. The problem is that the client's Business Central partner will offer the agent first, as a switch they turn on, and the buyer will want to try it before paying you. But the agent has real, documented limits, and they are the same messy cases you sell on:
* It finds customers only by the sender's email address, and it works only with a Microsoft Exchange mailbox ([Sales Order Agent FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/faqs-sales-order-taker-agent)).
* In a public test, it could not handle a split unit of measure ("one bag and five units"). It overcharged by using one unit for the whole quantity, then asked for human help ([Dynamics Communities test](https://dynamicscommunities.com/ug/dynamics-business-central-nav-ug/testing-limits-sales-order-agent-in-business-central/), [Lidd](https://lidd.com/microsoft-dynamics-insights-sales-order-agent/)).

So the rule is: **ask "Have you tried the Sales Order Agent? Where does it fail?"** If they have not tried it, tell them to try it first. It builds trust, and you come back in a month. If it fails on their orders (customers who email from Gmail, orders that say "the usual," mixed pack sizes, a non Exchange mailbox), pitch a narrow build that handles exactly those orders and leaves the rest to the agent. This segment still ranks fourth, because every Business Central deal starts with a free competitor already installed.

**d. How often and how long.** 20 to 300 orders a day depending on size (estimate). 10 to 15 minutes each (sources above).

**e. Who does it and the cost.** "Sales order processor" and "order processor" jobs are common in the UK. Pay is about £21,000 to £27,000 a year ([Jobsite](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor), [Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)). Worked example (estimate): 30 orders a day at 10 minutes is 5 hours a day, about 1,100 hours a year. That is roughly £15,000 to £18,000 of staff time, before counting errors.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* **Conexiom** and **Esker** are built for enterprises. Conexiom deals often run $10,000 to $100,000+ a year with per partner fees ([OrderDrafter comparison](https://orderdrafter.com/alternatives/conexiom-alternative/), [ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom)). Esker's price tends to pay off only above about 2,000 to 3,000 documents a month ([CheckThat on Esker pricing](https://checkthat.ai/brands/esker/pricing)).
* **New small business tools exist**, and you must know them. OrderDrafter costs $399 to $799 a month but targets only Epicor Prophet 21 users ([OrderDrafter](https://orderdrafter.com/)). StackCube targets small distributors ([StackCube](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors)). Most vendors do not publish prices.
* **The gap you fill:** firms on Unleashed, Cin7 Core, Sage 200 and Odoo, who are too small for enterprise tools and have no built in agent. Also firms whose orders come in many forms (email text, Excel, PDF), and firms with odd rules: customer specific prices, pack sizes, "the usual." A custom build that learns *their* customers' naming habits is the product.

**g. Realistic price.** See the pricing section. In short: small firms $2,500 to $4,000 setup plus $300 to $600 a month. Medium firms $5,000 to $9,000 setup plus $700 to $1,500 a month. Add the Sage programme fee and on site setup to Sage 200 quotes.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### E2. Supplier order confirmations checked against your purchase orders (SECOND PICK, medium firms)

**a. Who has it.** Manufacturers and wholesalers that buy many parts from many suppliers. Mostly **medium** (50 to 250), where a purchasing or supply chain lead owns it. Strong in Germany, Austria and the Netherlands, where this job has its own name (Auftragsbestätigungsprüfung, or "AB check").

**b. Steps today.** The buyer sends a PO from the ERP. The supplier replies with an order confirmation as a PDF or email. A purchasing clerk compares it line by line with the PO: part number, quantity, price, delivery date. They update the ERP with the confirmed date and chase any differences. Documents arrive by email or on paper, and each is opened, read and matched by hand ([Handelsblatt feature](https://www.handelsblatt.com/adv/firmen/auftragsbestaetigung-automatisiert-pruefen.html), [edoc](https://edoc.de/blog/digitale-auftragsbestaetigungen-automatisiert-verarbeiten)).

**c. Software.** Email in. ERP out: Business Central (has a purchase order API), Sage 200 (purchase order module, same developer programme gate as E1), Odoo (same plan rule as E1), Unleashed or Cin7 Core (both have purchase order APIs), or a German mid market ERP. **d. How often and how long (estimate).** A firm with 30 active suppliers may get 20 to 100 confirmations a week. A careful check takes a few minutes per document, and much longer when lines differ.

**e. Who and cost.** Purchasing clerks. In Germany a Sachbearbeiter Einkauf earns about €42,000 to €48,000 a year ([Jobted](https://de.jobted.com/gehalt/einkaufssachbearbeiter), [meingehalt.net](https://www.meingehalt.net/gehalt/sachbearbeiter-einkauf.html)). Worked example (estimate): 60 confirmations a week at 8 minutes is 8 hours a week, about 20% of one clerk. In Germany that is about €8,000 to €10,000 a year of time, before counting late deliveries that slip through.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Real firms already pay to solve this, which is good evidence. Perzeptron's OCC.AI checks confirmations against POs in any ERP. This is also where Business Central shops become your customers instead of a dead end.

**g. Realistic price (estimate).** $4,000 to $8,000 setup, $500 to $1,200 a month. Buyers here are operations heads with a budget. Against the worked example above, $500 a month is about 60% of the time saved, so pitch it on late deliveries caught, not only on hours.

## 3. Workflows in BOTH regions: your case study bridge

**Order intake to draft sales order** is the only strong one that appears in both regions.

| | India | Europe |
|---|---|---|
| What arrives | Email PO (I2), or WhatsApp text, photo or voice note sent 1:1 to the business number (I1) | Email PDF, Excel, text (E1) |
| Truth list to check against | Tally item list, rates, credit limit | Unleashed, Cin7, Sage 200 or Odoo item list, customer prices |
| Output | Draft sales order in Tally (or an import file for Busy) | Draft sales order by API |
| Person who approves | Coordinator or billing clerk | Sales order processor |

**How to make the Indian case study believable to a European buyer:**
1. **Best case: an Indian exporter (I2) that gets email POs from European buyers.** Then your case study literally says "we process POs from German and UK buyers." That is the same job as E1, with the same input (email and PDF). This is why I2 now comes before I1.
2. **Use numbers, not stories.** European buyers do not care about WhatsApp. They care about "orders per day, minutes per order before and after, share of lines matched with no fix, errors caught before dispatch." Measure these the same way in both places.
3. **Run in "shadow mode" first.** For two weeks the AI drafts, and the clerk still types as usual. Compare the two. This gives you honest before and after numbers, and it proves safety.
4. **Record a 2 minute screen video** of a real order flowing from inbox to approved draft. That sells better than any PDF.
5. **Show the write step live.** A European buyer's first doubt will be "can you actually write into our system?" Use a free Unleashed or Cin7 trial account to show a draft order landing by API ([Unleashed API FAQ](https://apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/Faq)). That fills the gap a Tally case study leaves.

## 4. Pricing in Europe: confirming and correcting your guess

**What the market says.**
* UK AI and automation agencies quote fixed projects from about £2,000 up. A single well defined workflow often costs £8,000 to £25,000, simple ones £500 to £2,000, and mid range ones £2,000 to £10,000 over 2 to 6 weeks. Small business retainers commonly run about £300 to £900 a month ([ExpertSure](https://www.expertsure.com/uk/ai-tools/ai-tools-costs/), [Northern Codes](https://northerncodes.com/blog/ai-automation-cost-uk-small-business), [Quantel](https://quantel.uk/blog/ai-automation-cost-uk)).
* Freelance n8n and automation builders on Upwork bill about $40 to $100 an hour ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/n8n-experts/), [Ciphernutz](https://ciphernutz.com/blog/hire-n8n-expert-cost-pricing-guide)).
* Order entry software for small distributors sells at about $399 to $799 a month ([OrderDrafter](https://orderdrafter.com/)).

**My verdict on your guess ($1,500 to $4,000 setup, $400 to $1,500 a month):**
* **Setup: right for small firms, too low for medium.** You have no European case study yet, so the first two deals will close near the low end. After that, charge by size.
* **Upkeep: too high for small firms, right for medium.** A 20 person wholesaler will balk at $1,000 a month when a SaaS tool costs $400.

| Buyer | First workflow | Monthly upkeep | Orders included each month | Who signs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small (5 to 49 staff), 20 to 60 orders a day | $2,500 to $4,000 | $300 to $600 | Up to 1,500 (60 a day for 25 days) | Owner or MD |
| Medium (50 to 250 staff), 60 to 300 orders a day | $5,000 to $9,000 | $700 to $1,500 | Up to 7,000 (about 300 a day for 23 days) | Ops, sales office or purchasing head |

Above the cap, charge a flat overage, for example $40 per extra 1,000 orders (estimate: about 5 times your AI cost per order, so you are safe if a model gets pricier). Price the lower end of the medium tier with a lower cap (for example 3,000 orders) if the client sits near 60 orders a day.

**Rules that protect you:**
1. Never go below about $2,000 in Europe. A price that is too cheap looks risky coming from an unknown team abroad.
2. Charge the "clean up the item list" step separately. It is where the hidden work is.
3. Every monthly fee has a volume cap that fits its tier (table above) and a stated overage. AI costs grow with volume.
4. Upkeep must include something real: monitoring, fixing new document layouts, a monthly report showing hours saved. If it is just "hosting," clients will cancel.
5. Pass through gated access costs. Sage developer programme fees, Odoo plan upgrades and extra API bundles are line items in the quote, never your cost.

**The same rule in India.** Keep the monthly fee below half of the monthly salary of the person whose work you save. For a billing clerk (₹15,000 to ₹23,000) that means under ₹7,500 to ₹11,500. For an export coordinator (₹27,000 to ₹70,000) there is more room.

## 5. Build, then break: hostile scenarios and the fixes

I built the plan above, then attacked it. Here is what broke and how I patched it.

**1. "We run Business Central."**
*Breaks:* E1 starts against a free agent their partner will switch on.
*Patch:* ask "Have you tried the Sales Order Agent, and where does it fail?" No trial means they try it first and you book a follow up in a month. Named failures (Gmail senders, "the usual," mixed units, a non Exchange mailbox) mean you pitch a build for just those orders. Otherwise pitch E2, which Business Central does not cover.

**2. "Our Tally or Sage 50 runs on one office PC, with no cloud."**
*Breaks:* no public API to call from your server.
*Patch:* for Tally, install a small connector service on their PC that pulls approved drafts from your server and posts XML to Tally on port 9000 ([Tally XML docs](https://github.com/NoumaanAhamed/tally-prime-api-docs/blob/main/index.md)). Add a week to the quote. If the PC is often off, the drafts wait in a queue and post when it comes back. For Sage 50, skip for now (programme fee plus desktop connector).

**3. "We use Busy." or "We use Marg."**
*Breaks:* no write API. A read only add on does not help.
*Patch:* for Busy, your system writes an approved Excel file in the column layout the clerk mapped once in Busy's Import Vouchers from Excel ([Busy Excel import FAQ](https://busy.in/faqs/how-to-import-excel-or-csv-files-into-busy-answerid-63052/)). The clerk clicks import once per batch. Price it lower. For Marg, say no until they show a working import.

**4. "Most of our orders come in the retailer group, or to our salesmen's phones."**
*Breaks:* the Cloud API cannot see groups or personal numbers ([Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence)). You would see only part of the orders.
*Patch:* run the one day count before quoting. If 70% or more land 1:1 on the business number, or salesmen agree to forward orders there, go ahead. If not, walk away or pitch I2 instead. Never promise to read groups.

**5. "Half our orders are Punjabi voice notes and blurry photos of handwritten lists."**
*Breaks:* the read rate drops, and the clerk fixes more than they save.
*Patch:* launch with typed and printed orders only. Treat voice and handwriting like the post office treats bad handwriting: send them straight to the person, with the AI's best guess beside the image. Add them to automation only when your measured accuracy is good enough. Track read rate by type of input.

**6. "You are in India. Our data cannot go to a US AI service." (GDPR)**
*Breaks:* the European deal stalls in legal review.
*Patch:* host the whole thing on a server in the EU (Hetzner in Germany or Finland, or AWS in Frankfurt). Use an AI model deployment that keeps processing inside the EU. For example, Azure "Data Zone" deployments in an EU country process prompts only inside EU member nations ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-foundry/openai/how-to/deployment-types)). Sign a data processing agreement. Use the EU Standard Contractual Clauses for your own access from India. Keep personal data minimal, since order documents are mostly product lines.

**7. "What happens when you two are asleep, sick, or gone?"**
*Breaks:* a part time team looks like a risk to any operations head.
*Patch:* use the post office rule. If anything fails, orders simply stay in the inbox and the clerk works the old way. Nothing is lost. Give them a one page runbook, alerting to you and to them, and a handover pack (code, setup, credentials) held by the client. Your working day overlaps UK mornings, since Ludhiana is 4.5 hours ahead of UK summer time and 5.5 hours ahead in winter.

**8. "Your ₹60,000 costs more than another clerk."** (India)
*Patch:* only pitch distributors with 2 or more order typists, or a painful morning rush. Keep the monthly fee below half of one clerk's salary. Lead with exporters (I2), where the person saved earns more.

### Your next 90 days (part time)

**Weeks 1 to 2: Build the core and find a pilot.**
* Build the engine: read a document, match lines against an item list using a memory of each customer's naming habits, flag price and quantity problems, and show a review screen. Write to Tally first, and to an Unleashed trial account second.
* Walk into 10 Ludhiana exporters and distributors that use Tally. Ask four questions: "How many orders a day? Where do they land (email, business WhatsApp 1:1, groups, personal phones)? How many people type them? What goes wrong?"

**Weeks 3 to 8: Pilot in India.**
* Two weeks in shadow mode, then four weeks live with the clerk approving.
* Measure everything: orders a day, minutes per order, share of lines with no fix, errors caught.
* Get written permission to publish the numbers and a short screen recording.

**Weeks 6 to 12: Start selling in Europe.**
* **Warm lead first:** ask your Finnish fintech client for two introductions to firms that ship physical goods. A referral beats a hundred cold emails.
* **Go through partners:** Unleashed, Cin7 and Sage 200 resellers in the UK have clients asking for AI, but many lack builders. Offer to be their white label build team. For Sage 200 this also solves the developer programme gate, because many resellers are already members. This is the fastest route to trust for a team based abroad.
* **Cold outreach:** email UK operations and customer service managers at wholesalers, using your Indian numbers, your video, and a live write into an Unleashed or Cin7 demo account.
* **Always qualify with two questions:** "Which system, and which plan?" and "Where do orders land?" Unleashed or Cin7 Core means go. Sage 200 means go through a reseller or add the fee. Odoo means go if they are on Custom, Odoo.sh or self hosted. Business Central means ask about the Sales Order Agent first, or pitch E2.

**The brutal truth:** the idea is not your hard part. Order automation is a known category with funded competitors, and some ERP makers now build it in. Your edge is being cheap, fast, custom to the client's messy data, and aimed at the ERPs the big vendors ignore. Two things decide every deal: whether you can **write** into their system, and whether you can **reach** the channel their orders arrive in. Check both before you quote. Then get **one set of real before and after numbers** in the next 8 weeks. Nothing else matters as much.
