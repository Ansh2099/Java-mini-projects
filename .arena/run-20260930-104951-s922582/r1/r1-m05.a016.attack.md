ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The Busy "REST API" claim is wrong, and it breaks the write step of the top India pick
Where: I1 c. "**Busy** offers REST APIs, and a listing says four endpoints (customers, invoices, products, ledgers) come free with a subscription ([BusyNotify](https://busynotify.in/solutions/custom-apis) ...)". Scenario 2 and Pick 1 then treat Busy as a working "out" system.
Problem: The cited page says the opposite. BusyNotify says "BUSY Accounting Software does not provide a public REST API out of the box". BusyNotify is a paid third party add on that reads the BUSY database. Its four free endpoints are there to "fetch" customers, invoices, products and ledgers. There is no sales order endpoint, and "custom queries for additional data are billed separately based on complexity." I1's output is "a sales order or invoice" typed into Busy. So for a Busy distributor, the one step that makes the workflow end to end (writing the draft order) has no confirmed route. The route it would need is a paid third party subscription plus custom paid work, and neither is counted in the under 50 USD budget. Marg gets only "check before you promise." That leaves Tally as the only confirmed write target for the region's top pick. The user would pitch Busy distributors (named first in I1) and then find they cannot deliver.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] I1's WhatsApp intake cannot see the orders its own "steps today" describe
Where: I1 b. "A retailer or field salesman sends a WhatsApp message ... The clerk sends a confirmation ... often from a personal phone". I1 c. "To automate it, you need the official WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) through a provider."
Problem: The Cloud API only receives messages sent to one registered business number. It cannot read a clerk's or salesman's personal WhatsApp, and it cannot read the retailer groups that distributors commonly use. If the distributor keeps its existing number through Coexistence, group chats are not synced or automated, broadcast lists become read only, and the catalog is switched off ([Unipile groups](https://www.unipile.com/whatsapp-group-api/), [InstantReply](https://www.instantreply.co/blog/whatsapp-coexistence-what-actually-syncs-2026)). Counterexample: a salesman gets "Parle G 10 ctn" on his own phone in a beat group and forwards it to the billing clerk's personal WhatsApp. That is the flow the report itself describes, and the proposed system never sees that order. Fixing it means making all 200 to 500 retailers message one new or migrated business number. The report says "Retailers do not want to change how they order. You do not ask them to." Its own design asks exactly that, and it never says so. Rule 7 (the process must already be partly digital in a form you can reach) is not shown to be met for the main input channel.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Sage 200 is called "Best target" but its paid developer gate is left out, while Sage 50 is marked down for the same gate
Where: E1 c. table. Sage 200: "Yes. REST API ... **Best target.**" Sage 50: "You now need the Sage Developer Programme ... Possible, but slower and riskier." Also "Always qualify ... Sage 200 ... means go."
Problem: Sage's own KB says "there is a cost associated with developing for the Sage 200 API and development with the Sage 200 API requires membership of the Sage 200 API Developer Programme." Credentials come by request form with up to 72 hours to process ([Sage KB FAQ](https://gb-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=200427112454709), [Knit guide](https://www.getknit.dev/blog/deep-dive-developer-guide-to-building-a-sage-200-api-integration)). On premise Sage 200 Professional also needs the Native API (Azure AD tunnelling) or a local IIS setup at the client. So the "best target" has the same paid programme gate the report uses to mark Sage 50 down, plus per site setup. None of this is priced against the under 50 USD a month budget or the build time. The task requires "Name the software these businesses actually use and whether it has usable APIs". For the report's lead European ERP, that answer is incomplete, and the user cannot budget the first build from it.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] "Business Central means walk away" contradicts the report's own gap
Where: E1 "Why avoid Business Central ... You cannot beat that on price ... those are small gaps to build a business on." Section 6: "Business Central means pitch E2 or walk away."
Problem: The report says the product is "firms with odd rules: customer specific prices, pack sizes, 'the usual.' A custom build that learns their customers' naming habits is the product." Those are the cases where the Sales Order Agent is documented to fail. It cannot create new customer or item records. It limits the product lines per document. It needed manual help on split units of measure ("one bag and five units"). It files exact item matches as "alternative items" when the language differs. It needs an Exchange mailbox and matches customers only by sender email ([Dynamics Communities test](https://dynamicscommunities.com/ug/dynamics-business-central-nav-ug/testing-limits-sales-order-agent-in-business-central/), [ERP Software Blog](https://erpsoftwareblog.com/2026/06/business-central-sales-order-agent/)). The price argument also misses the point. The report never sells per order. It sells $300 to $1,500 a month of setup and upkeep against a £21k to £27k clerk, so a 17 cent agent is not the competitor in that pitch. Counterexample: a UK distributor on BC whose customers email "20 of the usual" from a Gmail address, as multi line PDFs. The SOA fails that order on sender matching, "the usual" and line count, and the report tells the user to walk away from exactly that buyer.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The pricing breaks the report's own rules
Where: "Bonus break": "Price the monthly fee below half of one clerk's salary." I1 g. "Monthly ₹5,000 to ₹15,000". I2 g. "Monthly ₹8,000 to ₹20,000". Pricing rule 3: "Put a volume cap in the monthly fee (for example, 'up to 2,000 orders a month')". Medium tier: "60 to 300 orders a day".
Problem: Half of the cited clerk salary (₹15,000 to ₹23,000) is ₹7,500 to ₹11,500. So I1's top price (₹15,000) and all of I2's range above ₹11,500 break the report's own ceiling. The example cap also does not fit the tier it protects. 60 to 300 orders a day over about 22 working days is 1,320 to 6,600 orders a month. A 2,000 order cap is already passed at about 91 orders a day, so most medium clients would breach it in month one. The user copying these numbers into a proposal would contradict themselves.

The user cannot tell whether the first European pilot fits the stated constraint.
