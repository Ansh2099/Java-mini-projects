It "automatically compares every line of your statement with all of your supplier bills", highlights missing invoices, and lets you "email suppliers directly from Lightyear in 2 clicks" with the missing bills filled in. It then auto matches the bills as suppliers send them in (https://www.lightyear.cloud/features/automated-supplier-statement-reconciliation/). It connects to Business Central, NetSuite and Sage Intacct as well as Xero and QBO (https://www.lightyear.cloud/partners/software-partners/business-central-accounts-payable-automation/).

A finance manager who searches "supplier statement reconciliation Xero" will find Dext or Lightyear before the second call. The main recommendation fails the task's own rejection test.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] E1's matching design gives false exceptions on the most common UK statement type
Where: E1 build step 3, "Pull the supplier's ledger for the same period", and step 2, "the rows must add up to the closing balance on the statement ... This one check catches most bad extractions."
Problem: Many suppliers send open-item statements. These list every unpaid invoice no matter how old, and leave out invoices paid this month. Counterexample: a statement for September lists an unpaid invoice from May and one from July. It does not list a June invoice paid on 10 September. Step 3 pulls only September ledger entries. So the May and July invoices come up as "missing invoice" even though both are in Xero, and the September payment comes up as "payment not on statement". Every account then shows false exceptions, and the clerk loses trust in the tool in the first week. The fix is to compare against open items as of the statement date, not the period. The answer never tells the user this, and the step 3 Xero call (`GET /Invoices?where=Type=="ACCPAY"`) does not even filter by supplier contact or status. The closing balance check also breaks for balance-forward statements unless the brought-forward line is extracted as a row. The answer relies on this check as its main guard against bad LLM extraction.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] "Read only" security pitch is not true for the build described
Where: E1 build step 6, "create a draft email to the supplier in Outlook (Graph createMessage, not send)", and step 7, "Read only on the ledger ... This makes security approval much easier." Also Step 6, "read-only ledger access for E1".
Problem: To create drafts in a shared accounts mailbox from a backend service, Microsoft Graph needs the Mail.ReadWrite application permission. That permission gives read and write access to every mailbox in the client's tenant, unless the client's admin limits it with an Exchange application access policy or RBAC for Applications. The answer never mentions this. A European finance or IT manager will see a request for tenant-wide mailbox write access from two unknown offshore developers, not the "easy approval" the answer promises. The user should be told to scope the permission to the one shared mailbox, or ask for delegated access, before the first security call.
