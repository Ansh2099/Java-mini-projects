ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The lead Europe pick's "gap" (non-BC ERPs) is already filled by ready-made products, and a008 never checked
Where: Europe workflow 1, f.2: "Generic parsers and enterprise order automation products exist. I could not open their pricing pages this time, so I do not name prices." And the targeting rule: "Their ERP is not BC, so Sales Order Agent is not an option (Exact Online, Sage 200, SAP Business One, Odoo, Xero with an inventory add-on)."
Problem: The task says "Whether existing software already solves it. If it is already solved by a built in feature or a cheap tool, reject it. We already dropped one idea because existing tools covered it." a008 only checked Microsoft, then built the whole lead offer on "not BC = open gap". A quick search finds products built for exactly those non-BC ERPs: Hyperfox reads order emails, checks customer pricing and stock, and has native connectors for Exact Online, Odoo and BC (https://www.hyperfox.com/integrations, https://www.hyperfox.com/solutions/use-cases/ai-email-order-entry-agent). The Odoo App Store sells an AI module that turns a customer PO PDF into a draft quotation with product matching, using the client's own Claude or OpenAI key (https://apps.odoo.com/apps/modules/19.0/jjro_ai_sales_capture), and there is a second one for Odoo 16 to 19 (https://www.techultrasolutions.com/solutions/ocr-ai-sale-orders). Logistify sells AI PDF sales order entry for Unleashed, the kind of "inventory app" a008 names for its small Xero segment (https://logistify.ai/blog/ai-pdf-sales-order-entry-for-unleashed-software). Ordermind and Y Meadows sell the same thing. So the stated reason to pick this workflow (Microsoft's agent leaves non-BC firms unserved) is false for Odoo, Exact and Unleashed buyers. The user would build straight into the trap they told us they already fell into once. The pitch has to be rebuilt around a gap these products leave, and a008 never names one.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] India workflow 1 is kept even though a cheap tool covers it, and the price quoted is far above that tool
Where: India workflow 1, f.2 and f.3: "SalesOn says 1,100+ Indian distributors ... use it, with orders synced to Tally ... I could not see prices for these products." g: "Setup ₹30,000 to ₹75,000. Monthly ₹4,000 to ₹10,000."
Problem: The task rule is to reject a workflow when a cheap tool solves it. a008 names the tools, says they target "this exact job", and keeps the workflow without checking their price. SalesOn's own pricing page says ₹208 per user per month (yearly), with no setup fee, and syncs orders to Tally Prime (https://saleson.co.in/pricing). A 10 user distributor pays about ₹2,080 a month for SalesOn against a008's ₹30,000+ setup plus ₹4,000 to ₹10,000 a month. Zotok also says it handles unstructured WhatsApp orders into Tally with no code. "If a prospect already uses one of them, walk away" does not fix this. The problem is that a prospect can buy one for less than a008's monthly fee. Even as "proof of work only", the Indian owner will compare prices, so the ₹ price in g is not realistic, and g asks for "Realistic price".

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] The "one twentieth" price comparison is wrong
Where: Part 3, item 4: "₹30,000 to ₹80,000 setup and ₹4,000 to ₹10,000 a month. That is about one twentieth of the European price for the same engine".
Problem: At roughly ₹88 to the US dollar, ₹30,000 to ₹80,000 is about $340 to $910. Against a008's European setup of $1,500 to $4,500 that is about one fifth, not one twentieth. Monthly ₹4,000 to ₹10,000 is about $45 to $115 against $250 to $1,000, so about one fifth to one ninth. The claim makes India look about 4 times less valuable than a008's own numbers show, and that feeds the "India is for proof, not profit" advice.
