ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] The week by week plan builds the connectors before the checks that are supposed to decide whether to build them
Where: Final top picks, pick 1.5: "By week 6, at least 3 of your first 10 calls must confirm 30 or more emailed orders a day. If either check fails, do not build the Unleashed connector." Compare "What to do, week by week" step 3: "Weeks 2 to 5, build: ... Developer A builds the Tally XML connector in Java, then writes the Unleashed connector (or the BC connector)". Also short answer 5 ("If you find no fit in four weeks, skip India") against step 3, which starts the Tally connector in week 2.
Problem: The go/no go gate for Unleashed ends in week 6, but the Unleashed connector is written in weeks 2 to 5. By the time the gate can fail, the work it was meant to prevent is done. The same happens in India: the Tally connector is built from week 2, while the screening that decides whether any Tally client exists runs until week 4. Scenario: the calls in weeks 3 to 6 find only 1 of 10 firms with 30+ emailed orders. The plan says "do not build", but Developer A has already spent the part time weeks on it. For a two person part time team, this is the main cost the plan claims to protect against. The user cannot follow both instructions, and the document does not say which one wins.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The payback check treats fractions of a salary as cash the buyer gets back, and this breaks exactly at the small firm tier it approves
Where: Europe workflow 1, section g, row "Small, 30 to 50 orders a day": "4 hours a day at 30 orders | 8,750 to 11,550 GBP | ... 1.56 to 2.2 times. Pass". Also Assumptions 4: "Automation does not remove the clerk." India table row "Small ... 0.35 clerk".
Problem: At 30 orders a day the task takes half of one processor's day, and the document says automation leaves the clerk in place. So the "saving" is 0.35 of one person's time freed up. No salary goes away. A small firm owner who pays 5,230 to 5,615 GBP in year one gets no drop in payroll. The P rule ("The buyer comes out ahead") is scored as if that time turns into cash. The task asks for "brutal honesty" and wants to know what buyers "would realistically pay". An owner who decides on cash will not accept 8,750 GBP of paper saving when payroll stays the same. The document never says that the small tier only pays off if the freed hours are really used for other work, or if a hire is avoided. So the "Pass" at the bottom of the small range, and the claim in the short answer that the product passes "from 30 orders a day", are overstated. The pass points should be tied to whole people saved or a hire avoided, or this should be flagged as a sales risk.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The Unleashed E2 check skips Unleashed's own built in order channels, and uses a different standard from the one applied to Tally
Where: Europe workflow 1, ERP table, Unleashed row: "Built in feature or rival: None built in that I found" and top pick 1.2 "On Unleashed there is no built in reader and no cheap tool".
Problem: The task says: "Whether existing software already solves it. If it is already solved by a built in feature or a cheap tool, reject it." For Tally, the document counts the built in Excel voucher import as a reason to turn buyers away ("Most orders are Excel files from a few customers in a fixed layout: set up Tally's Excel import templates"). Unleashed has the same kind of feature: a built in sales order import (Unleashed help: "Import Sales Orders", http://help.unleashedsoftware.com/import-sales-orders). It also sells a B2B eCommerce store where trade customers place orders themselves. The document assesses neither one. Scenario: a UK Unleashed wholesaler whose top customers send fixed layout Excel POs, or whose small trade buyers could use the B2B store. By the document's own Tally logic, this firm should be screened out. But the Unleashed plan has no such screening question. The lead market's E2 "yes" is therefore claimed without the check the task asks for, and the screening questions in the week 3 to 6 calls miss this.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] India delivers one unproven workflow and gives no realistic paid price for small firms
Where: India section: "Only one India workflow survived my checks." "This buyer shape is a guess, not a proven fact." Price table: "Small, 30 orders a day ... Free or at cost ... Case study only".
Problem: The task asks for "India: the workflows you found" with a to h for each, and "Say what Indian businesses would realistically pay, in INR", for small and medium buyers. What is delivered: one workflow whose buyer the author says may not exist near Ludhiana. It rests on 25 Indeed "order processing" ads that, as the author admits, "do not say how orders arrive". The small buyer tier has no price at all ("Free"), so the "what would they pay" question is not answered for the size the task says the owner decides. The document's own source (HublerX) says small buyers in India mostly order on WhatsApp by text or voice. That channel is excluded ("Text and voice note orders stay out"), and no other India workflow replaces it. The user ends up with zero confirmed Indian workflows, plus a fallback that says to "skip India". The task's India deliverable is only half met.

ATTACK 5 [MINOR] The Tally connector is not specified well enough to build or quote
Where: India workflow 1, section c.3: "Tally must be running with the company loaded on a PC ... So you need a small connector on the client's PC, or Tally on a cloud server. Developer A can write it in Java in about one week."
Problem: The engine runs in the cloud, but Tally's XML server listens on port 9000 on an office PC behind NAT. The document does not say how the cloud reaches it: an outbound polling agent, a tunnel, or moving the client onto a hosted Tally. It also does not say what happens when the PC is off or the company is not loaded. That is the normal case for a small Indian office after hours, and posts would fail with no alert. "Tally on a cloud server" also means a hosted Tally licence and server cost that the budget and price tables leave out. The user must guess the design, and the "one week" estimate has no basis.
