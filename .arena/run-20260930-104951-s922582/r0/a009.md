# One Workflow to Sell: Messy Customer Orders Into the ERP

Research date: 30 September 2026. Written for a two person team in Ludhiana who want to sell one workflow per client, mainly to European SMBs.

## The short answer

Sell one thing: **customer order intake**. A customer sends an order as an email, a PDF purchase order, a spreadsheet, or a WhatsApp message. Your system reads it, finds the customer, matches their product names to the seller's own item codes, checks price and stock, and puts a **draft** sales order into the seller's ERP or accounting software. A person approves the unclear ones.

1. **Europe (where you earn):** wholesalers, distributors and small manufacturers that run **Sage 200, Sage 50, Unleashed, Cin7 Core** or similar. Stay away from Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central clients, because Microsoft now ships its own Sales Order Agent.
2. **India (proof of work):** the same workflow for distributors and manufacturers on **Tally**, where orders arrive by WhatsApp and emailed POs. Ludhiana's own industry is full of these firms.
3. **Both regions:** it is the same workflow, so an Indian case study with real numbers is a believable story for a European buyer.

Almost everything else I checked is already solved by cheap tools. I list those rejects below so you do not waste a month on them.

## Assumptions

1. "Workflow" means the full path from "order arrives" to "draft order sits in the ERP, checked, with problems flagged." Sending the order confirmation back to the customer is included only after a human approves it.
2. "Would pay for" means someone already spends salary or a subscription on it today. Vendor marketing numbers count as weak evidence, so I say when I rely on them.
3. A "cheap tool" means roughly under 100 USD a month, or a feature already inside software the client pays for.
4. Prices in USD use about 1 GBP = 1.3 USD and 1 USD = 85 INR. Treat these as rough.
5. Most links below were found through web search. My page fetch tool was blocked for most sites, so I could fully open only the Microsoft Learn pages. Before you quote any vendor price to a client, open that vendor's page yourself.

## Open questions the task leaves, and how I settled them

1. **How many workflows should we list?** The task says "the workflows you found." But you can only sell and support one well while working part time. So I list many that I checked, reject most with reasons, and keep one main pick plus one follow on.
2. **Should India and Europe get different workflows?** Only if the evidence forces it. It does not. The same workflow exists in both, and one codebase with two connectors is simpler than two products.
3. **Which software should we target?** Pick the ERPs that have a usable API and do **not** have a built in AI order agent. That rules in Sage 200, Unleashed and Cin7 Core. It rules out Business Central for now.
4. **Where does the human approve?** Simplest answer: inside the ERP they already use. Create the order as a draft or quote, or on hold, and send a short exceptions list. Do not build a separate dashboard until a client asks.
5. **n8n or code?** Developer A's Spring Boot and Spring AI skills fit a small, testable service better than a large visual flow. Use n8n only for glue if it saves time. If you host n8n for paying clients, read its license first.
6. **Is the 1,500 to 4,000 USD plus 400 to 1,500 USD a month guess right?** Partly. See the pricing section.
7. **Is WhatsApp readable by software?** Only through the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API). Orders sent to a staff member's normal phone are not. The simplest fix is for staff to forward order messages to one dedicated business number. They already copy these messages today, so forwarding does not change their habit much.
8. **Who owns the pain?** In small firms, the owner, who sees staff tied up on typing. In medium firms, the customer service or sales operations manager.

The default answer to this task is a long list: supplier invoice capture, invoice chasing, bank reconciliation, GST reconciliation, timesheets, customs forms, lead follow up, support email triage. Every "AI automation agency" website sells that list.

I argued against each item with one test: **is this already sold as a cheap product or built into the software the client already pays for?** Most failed.

### What survived, and why

Order intake survived for three reasons.

1. **The hard part is not reading the document.** It is matching "10 x blue hex bolt M8 long" or "Sharma ji wala 40 no. dhaaga, 5 peti" to the seller's own item code, price list and customer account. Cheap parsers stop at extraction. Enterprise tools do the matching but are built for large companies.
2. **Someone is paid to do it today.** UK job boards list "sales order processor" roles by name. Indian billing executive jobs list sales bill entry in Tally.
3. **It already meets your "partly digital" rule.** The order is already digital (email, PDF, WhatsApp) and the ERP is already digital. The only manual part is the person in the middle who retypes.

I then argued against order intake itself. Two objections are real, and the plan below handles both.

1. **Microsoft already built it for Business Central.** True. Its Sales Order Agent reads emails and PDF or image attachments, finds the customer, matches items, and creates a quote for a human to review ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent)). It is billed per use in Copilot Credits at 0.01 USD each ([Microsoft Learn: pay as you go meters](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-platform/admin/pay-as-you-go-meters)). A typical order uses about 14 to 19 credits ([Dynamics 365 Lab](https://yzhums.com/68484/)), so roughly 0.15 to 0.20 USD per order. You cannot beat that on price. **So do not sell to Business Central users.** Treat this as a warning too: Sage and others will likely ship similar features, so sell a result and a service, not just "AI reads your PDFs."
2. **In India, HublerX already does WhatsApp orders into ERPs.** True, but it says it is best for manufacturers of ₹200 to 2,000 crore on SAP, Oracle, D365 or Tally ([HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/whatsapp-order-management-software), [HublerX guide](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)). The ₹5 to 150 crore Tally distributor is left out. That is your case study market.

## Region 1: India

### Workflow I1: WhatsApp and emailed orders into Tally as draft sales orders

**a. Who has it, and size.** Distributors and wholesalers (FMCG, pharma, electrical, hardware, auto parts, yarn and hosiery), and manufacturers selling to dealers. In Ludhiana: hosiery and knitwear, cycle parts, hand tools and auto components. Fits **both** sizes, but the realistic Indian buyer is **small** (owner decides) or the lower end of **medium**.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A retailer or dealer sends an order on WhatsApp. It can be text, a voice note, or a photo of a handwritten list. Bigger buyers email a PDF purchase order.
2. A billing or order staff member reads it, works out which items are meant (often local names or the buyer's own codes), and checks the rate and stock.
3. They type a sales order or sales invoice into Tally, or Busy or Marg.
4. They reply to the dealer, often by calling back if something is unclear.

Industry write ups describe exactly this: orders come as voice notes, photos of handwritten lists and casual messages, and someone moves them into the system by hand ([HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026), [Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)).

**After automation.** Staff forward the order to one business WhatsApp number, or it arrives by email. The system transcribes and reads it, matches the customer (by phone number or email) and the items (against the Tally stock item list plus a learned alias table), checks the rate against the price list, and posts a **sales order voucher** into Tally. Anything uncertain goes to an exceptions list for staff to fix. Nothing is invoiced without a person.

**c. Software on each side.**
1. In: WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) and Gmail or Outlook.
2. Out: TallyPrime. Tally has no cloud API. You talk to it by posting XML over HTTP to port 9000 on the machine running Tally. It must be running, it handles one request at a time, and you must never expose that port to the internet. Use a small local connector or Tally on a cloud server behind a VPN ([TallyHelp: integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/integrate-with-tallyprime/), [AI Accountant: Tally API guide](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/tally-api-integration-guide)).
3. Busy and Marg: I did not confirm a usable API for either in this research. Do not promise them until you test one.

**d. How often and how long.** My estimate, not a measured figure: a small distributor gets 30 to 150 orders a day, and each takes 3 to 8 minutes to read, clarify and type. That works out to about 2 to 15 staff hours a day. **Count this yourself in week one of any pilot.** It is the number your case study will be built on.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Billing executives or order clerks. Ludhiana listings show about ₹14,000 to ₹30,000 a month ([Apna: billing executive jobs in Ludhiana](https://apna.co/jobs/title_billing_executive-jobs-in-ludhiana), [Indeed: billing jobs in Ludhiana](https://in.indeed.com/q-billing-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)).

**Brutal part:** at ₹15,000 to ₹20,000 a month, hiring one more clerk is cheap. Automation only wins where **speed and errors** hurt: wrong item shipped, late dispatch in peak season, orders lost in chat, or the owner being the only one who knows the item codes. Sell on "no wrong items, same day dispatch, and orders don't depend on one person," not on salary saved.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** HublerX targets much bigger manufacturers. WhatsApp platforms like Interakt (about ₹2,499 to ₹3,499 a month) do broadcasts, catalogs and chatbots ([ConviDesk: WhatsApp pricing in India](https://www.convideskai.com/blog/whatsapp-automation-pricing-india-2026)). Catalog ordering apps make the retailer change how they order, which is a change you have ruled out. Tally WhatsApp add ons send documents **out** of Tally. They do not read free text orders **in**. The gap is free text orders into Tally, with item matching, for small firms.

**WhatsApp running cost.** Messages inside a conversation the customer started are free. From 1 October 2026, Meta charges for utility messages in India, such as order confirmations, even inside the 24 hour window. That is about ₹0.115 each plus GST ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/meta-to-charge-for-whatsapp-business-utility-messages-in-india/tldr), [ChatMaxima rates](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/india/), [Meta pricing page](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing)). That is small, but pass it to the client.

**g. Realistic price (INR).**
1. First 1 or 2 clients: free or ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 setup. In return you get written permission to publish their numbers, a named quote, and a short video.
2. After that: ₹30,000 to ₹75,000 setup and ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 a month, plus WhatsApp and AI usage at cost.
3. Anything above ₹10,000 a month is a hard sell to a small Indian distributor. The benchmark in their head is one clerk's salary.

**h. Sources.** Linked inline above.

**Why I did not list more Indian workflows.** Every other common Indian back office idea I checked (GST reconciliation, purchase entry, bank entry, WhatsApp invoice sending) is already covered cheaply by Tally or by Tally add ons. India is your proof market, so a second Indian workflow adds work without adding European revenue.

## Region 2: Europe (including the UK)

### Workflow E1 (main pick): Emailed customer orders into the ERP as draft sales orders

**a. Who has it, and size.** B2B wholesalers and distributors (food and drink, building and plumbing supplies, electrical, industrial and MRO parts, packaging, cleaning supplies) and small manufacturers that take orders from trade customers. It fits **both** sizes:
1. **Small (15 to 49 staff):** the owner or a director decides. They have one or two people entering orders.
2. **Medium (50 to 250):** the customer service or sales operations manager owns it. They have a team of order processors.

The best fit is 20 to 200 orders a day, many different customer formats, and no EDI for most customers.

**b. Steps today.**
1. An order arrives in a shared inbox. It might be email text, a PDF purchase order, an Excel file, or a scan.
2. A sales order processor finds the customer account, reads each line, translates the customer's product codes or descriptions into the seller's own codes, checks price against the customer's price list, and checks stock.
3. They key the sales order into the ERP and send an acknowledgement.
4. Problems (unknown item, wrong price, credit hold) go back and forth by email.

Vendors describe the same pattern: orders by email as PDFs, Excel files and body text, keyed by hand ([Vistaflow](https://www.vistaflow.ai/en/blog/wholesalers-and-distributoremail-order-automation-became-vital-in-2026), [Infrrd](https://www.infrrd.ai/blog/order-entry-automation)).

**After automation.** The system watches the inbox, reads the order, matches the customer and each line (using the ERP's item list, the customer's past orders, and a learned table of that customer's own codes), checks price and stock, and creates the order as a **draft, quote or on hold** in the ERP. A daily or live exceptions list shows only the orders a human must fix. A human sends the confirmation.

**c. Software on each side, and APIs.**

| Side | Software | Usable API? |
|---|---|---|
| In | Microsoft 365 Outlook or Google Workspace Gmail | Yes (Microsoft Graph, Gmail API) |
| Out | **Sage 200** (UK mid market) | Yes. REST with OAuth 2.0, and it can create sales orders ([Sage Developer: POST sales order](https://developer.sage.com/200-uk/apis/sage-200-professional/2024.01/combined/groups/sop/tags/sop-orders/paths/postsoporder)) |
| Out | **Sage 50** (UK small firms) | Not built in. It is a desktop app. It needs a paid bridge like Hyperext ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-integration/does-sage-50-have-an-api-yes-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)). Possible, but more fragile. |
| Out | **Unleashed** (inventory, common with Xero users) | Yes. Sales orders can be created by API ([Unleashed API](https://apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/SalesOrders)) |
| Out | **Cin7 Core** (inventory, common with Xero users) | Yes. Sale POST endpoint, with some limits ([Cin7 Core: Sale POST](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/9034512161423-Sale-POST)) |
| Out | **Business Central** | Yes, but Microsoft's own Sales Order Agent competes at about 0.15 to 0.20 USD per order. **Avoid.** |
| Out | Odoo, Exact Online (Netherlands and Belgium), Visma, weclapp | Widely used and known to have APIs, but I did not verify them in this research. Check before you sell. |

**Simplest choice:** start with **one** ERP. Sage 200 is the strongest UK pick: real REST API, many mid sized distributors, and no built in agent that I found. Unleashed and Cin7 Core are good second choices for smaller firms on Xero.

**d. How often and how long.** Vendors claim 10 to 15 minutes of manual entry per order ([Infrrd](https://www.infrrd.ai/blog/order-entry-automation)). That is a vendor number, so treat it as the top of the range. My honest estimate is 3 to 5 minutes for a short repeat order and 15 minutes or more for a long PDF PO with unfamiliar codes. At 60 orders a day and 6 minutes each, that is 6 hours a day, close to one full time person. Measure it in the first week.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Sales order processors or customer service staff. Reed shows an average UK sales processor salary of about £28,900 ([Reed average salary](https://www.reed.co.uk/average-salary/average-sales-processor-salary?kd=administration)), with listed roles from £22,300 in Glasgow ([Reed listing](https://www.reed.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor-wfh/54379423)) to £31,500 in Worsley ([Reed listing](https://www.reed.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor-worsley-1300221)). Add employer costs and that is roughly £2,300 to £3,300 a month per person. This is real money already being spent.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. **Conexiom, Esker and similar:** they do the full job, but for larger firms on Oracle, SAP, Dynamics 365, Epicor and Infor ([Capterra: Conexiom](https://www.capterra.com/p/205134/Conexiom/)). They are sold through sales teams with implementation projects.
2. **Parseur, Docparser, Lido:** cheap extraction (Parseur 39 to 399 USD a month, [Parseur pricing](https://parseur.com/pricing)). They do not match items to the seller's codes, check prices, or write a clean order into Sage 200.
3. **Business Central Sales Order Agent:** covers BC users only.
4. **The gap:** a 10 to 250 person distributor on Sage 200, Sage 50, Unleashed or Cin7, whose customers each send orders in their own format with their own codes. Too small for Conexiom, and too messy for a generic parser.

**g. Realistic price (my estimate, checked against the benchmarks above).**

Your guess was 1,500 to 4,000 USD setup and 400 to 1,500 USD a month.

1. **Setup: mostly right.** 2,000 to 4,000 USD for a small firm and 4,000 to 8,000 USD for a medium firm with several customer formats. For your first two European clients, charge 1,000 to 1,500 USD. You have no European case study yet, and you are an unknown team from abroad. Buyers will discount you for that risk whether you like it or not.
2. **Monthly: too high for small firms, fine for medium.** Small firms compare you to SaaS prices: Chaser at £199 a month, Dext at a few hundred a month for practices, Parseur at 39 to 399 USD. Expect **250 to 500 USD a month** for small firms and **600 to 1,500 USD a month** for medium firms, plus AI usage at cost.
3. **The anchor to use when selling:** "This costs less than a quarter of one order processor, and it doesn't make typing mistakes when you're busy." At £2,300 or more a month per person, 400 to 800 USD a month is an easy yes if the time saved is real.

**h. Sources.** Linked inline above.

### Workflow E2 (follow on, medium firms only): Supplier PO confirmations and delivery date chasing

This is the same engine pointed at the buying side, so it is a second sale to an E1 client, not a new product.

**a. Who and size.** Medium manufacturers and distributors with a purchasing team. **Medium** only, because small firms rarely have enough open POs for it to hurt.

**b. Steps today.** A buyer sends a PO from the ERP. Suppliers reply by email or PDF with confirmations that change dates, quantities or prices. The buyer checks each one against the PO, updates the expected date in the ERP, and chases suppliers who never replied.

**After automation:** replies are read and matched to the PO. Matching confirmations update the expected date. Price or quantity changes and late dates go to the buyer. Reminders for missing confirmations are drafted for the buyer to send.

**c. Software.** Outlook or Gmail, and the same ERPs as E1. Sage 200 exposes purchase orders by API ([Knit: Sage 200 guide](https://www.getknit.dev/blog/deep-dive-developer-guide-to-building-a-sage-200-api-integration)).

**d and e. Volume and cost.** Buyers or purchasing assistants. I found no independent timing data, only vendor claims ([Leverage: PO acknowledgements](https://tryleverage.ai/blog/pf/po-acknowledgement-automation-hybrid-edi-1), [Leverage: manual PO follow ups](https://tryleverage.ai/blog/pf/how-to-end-manual-po-follow-ups-for-mid-market-distributors-2)). Count it at the client.

**f. Existing tools.** Leverage and similar tools aim at US mid market firms. Enterprise procurement suites also do it. There is less competition in UK SMBs on Sage, but the evidence is weaker than for E1.

**g. Price.** 1,500 to 3,000 USD setup as an add on, and 300 to 600 USD a month.

**Why it is second, not first:** the pain is spread across a team, and the cost shows up as late deliveries, not as one clear salary. That makes it harder to sell than E1.

## Workflows in both regions: the case study bridge

**Order intake (I1 and E1) is the only one that is truly the same in both.** Here is why an Indian case study can help you sell in Europe.

1. **The core is identical.** Messy order in, customer and item matched, price and stock checked, draft order out, human approves exceptions. Only the connector changes (Tally XML versus the Sage 200 REST API).
2. **The Indian version is harder.** Voice notes, Hinglish, handwritten photos and local item names are tougher than a clean PDF purchase order. "It handles WhatsApp voice orders in Punjabi and Hindi" is a strong proof point for a UK buyer whose orders are tidier.
3. **What makes it believable:** numbers, not logos. Publish orders per day, the share of orders that went through with no human edit, the error rate before and after, minutes per order before and after, and a named quote from the owner. A European buyer will not know the Indian company, but they will understand those numbers.
4. **Brutal limit:** some European buyers will still discount an Indian case study. Get your first European client as early as you can, even at a low price. Developer A's Finnish fintech client is your best warm lead for an introduction.

## Final top picks

1. **Sell E1 in the UK to distributors on Sage 200, then Unleashed and Cin7 Core.** It has named job titles, real salaries, usable APIs, and a gap between cheap parsers and enterprise tools. Avoid Business Central.
2. **Build I1 first in Ludhiana, on Tally, as your proof.** Do one or two local distributors or manufacturers for free or cheap, in exchange for published numbers. It is the same engine, and the local market is next door to you.
3. **Upsell E2 later** to medium E1 clients. It is the same code on the buying side.

### Things you may not want to hear

1. **Selling is the hard part, not building.** A two person part time team from India with no European case study will not win cold outreach easily. Your best channels are ERP resellers and partners (Sage 200 and Unleashed or Cin7 partners have clients asking for this) and your existing Finnish contact.
2. **Pick one ERP and say no to the rest** for the first three clients. Every new connector is weeks of part time work.
3. **Platform risk is real.** Microsoft already did this for Business Central. Others can follow. Your defence is the service: per client item matching, alias tables, fast fixes, and a person who answers.
4. **Indian revenue will be small.** Treat India as your test lab and your source of case studies.
5. **GDPR is not optional in Europe.** You need a data processing agreement, hosting in an EU or UK region, and a clear AI subprocessor list. Your Finnish KYC work should make this familiar.

### Simplest build that works (fits under 50 USD a month)

1. One Spring Boot service (Developer A) with PostgreSQL on a small VPS or a small AWS instance in an EU region.
2. It reads the inbox through Microsoft Graph or the Gmail API. The WhatsApp Cloud API is for India only.
3. An LLM call through Spring AI extracts lines. The item matching is plain code: the customer's past orders, an alias table, and fuzzy search on the item list. Only the uncertain matches go to the model or to a human.
4. The ERP connector writes a draft order. The Tally connector is a tiny local agent. Sage 200, Unleashed and Cin7 are direct REST calls.
5. Approval happens in the ERP, plus one exceptions email or one simple page (Developer B). Do not build a dashboard until a client pays for one.
6. AI and WhatsApp usage costs are billed to the client at cost. Your fixed cost stays at hosting only.

### Checklist for your first client call

1. How many orders a day, and how many customers send them?
2. What share arrive by email text, PDF, Excel, WhatsApp or phone?
3. How long does one order take? Time five of them live.
4. Which ERP, which version, and is it cloud or on a local server?
5. Who fixes wrong orders today, and what did the last mistake cost?
6. Who signs off on spending? (The owner in small firms, the operations head in medium ones.)

If a prospect gets fewer than about 20 orders a day, or most of their orders already come through EDI or a web shop, walk away. The saving will be too small to justify the monthly fee.
