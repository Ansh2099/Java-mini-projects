# Workflows SMBs Will Pay For: India and Europe (Sept 2026)

## The short answer

Build one product and sell it in both regions: **"Order inbox to ERP."** A customer order arrives by email (PDF, Excel, or plain text) or by WhatsApp. Today a person reads it, finds each item in the system, checks price and stock, and types a sales order. You automate the reading, the matching, the checks, and the draft order. A person approves anything odd.

Why this one wins:

1. It is a real, paid job today. UK firms hire "sales order processors" at about £26,800 a year on average ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)).
2. The tools that do it well are priced for big firms. Rossum starts around 18,000 USD a year ([Floowed](https://www.floowed.com/insights/rossum-pricing), [Rossum pricing](https://rossum.ai/pricing/)). Conexiom is aimed at midsize and enterprise makers and distributors ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/205134/Conexiom/)). The cheap tools, like Parseur at 39 to 399 USD a month, pull text out of documents. They do not match a customer's part codes to your SKUs, check prices, or write the order ([Parseur pricing](https://parseur.com/pricing)).
3. It is the same job in Ludhiana and in Leeds. Indian distributors get orders as WhatsApp messages, photos, and voice notes, and staff retype them into Tally ([Sunray](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)). So your Indian pilot becomes a real case study for European buyers.
4. Europe's new e-invoicing rules make invoices structured, but they do not touch purchase orders. The messy PDFs move to the order desk. That is where you want to be (details in Europe section).

Your second product, later: **"Remittance to cash allocation."** A customer pays several invoices in one lump and emails a remittance PDF. Someone reads it and allocates the money invoice by invoice. There is already a product for this on Xero and QuickBooks ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/multi-invoice-payment-matching-xero)), so you would only sell it to firms on Sage 200, Business Central, or Tally.

What I tell you not to build: supplier invoice capture, payment reminders, GST reconciliation, and bank statement entry. Cheap or free tools already do them. Details below.

## Assumptions

1. "Would pay for" means the buyer gets back at least twice what they pay within a year, in saved staff time or fewer errors. If the math does not show that, I rejected the idea.
2. You want the fewest products that can pay you. So I rank a narrow list, not a long menu.
3. Money conversion used for rough math: 1 GBP is about 1.3 USD, 1 EUR is about 1.1 USD, 1 USD is about 88 INR. Check the day's rate before you quote.
4. **About my sources.** Every link below is a page I found in search. My tool could open only a few pages in full (the TallyPrime API notes on GitHub). For the rest I used the search summaries of those pages. Prices change often. Open the pricing pages yourself before you quote a competitor's price to a client.
5. Where I give a volume or time per task without a link, it is my estimate. It is labeled "estimate." You measure the real number in the first discovery call (questions at the end).

## How I weighed it: three experts, one answer

I ran every idea past three views.

1. **The buyer** (an operations head at a 60 person UK wholesaler). Asks: does one person own this pain, and will the boss sign a check for it?
2. **The market analyst.** Asks: does a free feature or a cheap tool already do this? If yes, kill it.
3. **The delivery lead.** Asks: can two part timers build it in a few weeks, keep it running on under 50 USD a month, and not get paged every night?

Where they disagreed, and how I settled it:

| Question | Buyer said | Analyst said | Delivery lead said | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supplier invoice capture? | "Everyone hates it" | Xero includes capture for free; Dext and many others fight here | Easy to build | **Reject.** Free and cheap tools own it. |
| Accountancy firms (UK MTD, Indian CAs)? | Easy to find, many firms | Dext, Hubdoc, Vyapar TaxOne at about ₹10,000 a year | Many small clients, lots of support | **Reject as a main offer.** |
| Small or medium in Europe? | Medium has budget | Medium has fewer cheap options | Medium means real ERPs and security questions | **Lower medium (20 to 150 staff)** on an ERP with a real API. |
| WhatsApp orders in India? | Huge volume | Order apps exist | WhatsApp API costs and Tally is desktop only | **Yes, but only for firms with 2 or more order clerks.** |
| One product or several? | Offer what each client asks | Focus beats breadth | You are two part timers | **One product, two ERP connectors to start.** |

## Region 1: India

### The honest picture first

Indian labor is cheap. A Tally accounts assistant in Ludhiana earns about ₹12,000 to ₹25,000 a month ([Indeed Ludhiana](https://in.indeed.com/q-account-assistant-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html), [Indeed Ludhiana Tally jobs](https://in.indeed.com/q-accountant-tally,-data-entry-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)). A data entry operator in Punjab earns about ₹9,000 to ₹20,000 ([Glassdoor Punjab](https://www.glassdoor.com/Job/punjab-data-entry-operator-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,6_IS4951_KO7,26.htm)). So in India you do not win on salary alone. You win when:

1. Volume already needs two or more clerks, or jumps in season.
2. Mistakes cost real money: wrong item shipped, missed order, return freight.
3. Speed matters: same day dispatch, dealer waiting for confirmation.
4. The owner wants to stop depending on one key person who "knows all the codes."

Treat most Indian work as proof of work. Charge something, even a small amount, because a paying client makes a stronger case study.

### India workflow 1: WhatsApp and email orders into Tally or Busy (recommended)

**a. Who has it.** Distributors and wholesalers (hardware, auto parts, electricals, FMCG, pharma distribution, paints), and manufacturers who sell to dealers. Ludhiana is full of these: auto parts, hand tools, bicycle parts, hosiery. Fits **medium** best (50 to 250 staff, a real order desk). **Small** firms (5 to 49) have the problem but rarely the budget, unless volume is high.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A dealer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp: text, a photo of a handwritten list, a voice note, or an Excel file. Some come by email.
2. An order clerk reads it and works out which item the dealer means ("10 box 6mm bolt" means which stock item?).
3. The clerk checks stock and the dealer's rate, and asks back on WhatsApp when something is unclear.
4. The clerk types a sales order or sales invoice in Tally or Busy.
5. The clerk tells dispatch and replies to the dealer.

WhatsApp is the order channel for most Indian distributors, and orders arrive as voice notes, photos, and casual messages that get retyped ([Sunray](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/what-is-whatsapp-order-management), [CampaignHQ](https://blog.campaignhq.co/whatsapp-for-manufacturing-india)).

**c. Software on each side.** In: WhatsApp Business, Gmail. Out: TallyPrime (most common), Busy, Marg (pharma), sometimes Zoho Books.

TallyPrime can take orders by API. It runs a small HTTP server on port 9000 on the client's PC and accepts XML to create vouchers ([Tally XML integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/), [TallyPrime API notes on GitHub](https://github.com/NoumaanAhamed/tally-prime-api-docs/blob/main/index.md)). TallyPrime 7.0, released December 2025, added JSON data exchange too ([Tally release notes 7.0](https://help.tallysolutions.com/release-notes-tallyprime-7-0/)). The catch: Tally lives on a desktop in the client's office. You need a small connector program on that PC that pulls approved orders from your cloud and pushes them into Tally. Developer B can write it in Node.

WhatsApp catch: you cannot read messages from the normal WhatsApp Business phone app by code. The client must connect the order number to the WhatsApp Business API through a provider (Interakt, AiSensy, Gupshup, WATI and others). Budget roughly ₹1,000 to ₹3,000 a month for the provider (estimate; check current plans and Meta's per message rules). Tell the client this up front; it is the most common deal breaker.

**d. How often, how long.** Estimate: a medium distributor gets 50 to 300 orders a day, 2 to 8 minutes each, more for photos and voice notes. Measure it in discovery.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Order clerks or billing staff at ₹12,000 to ₹25,000 a month each (sources above). A firm with 3 clerks spends about ₹50,000 to ₹75,000 a month on this desk.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Order taking apps (for example [RetailCore](https://www.retailcore.in/sales-order/mobile-ordering-system-for-distributors-and-traders-in-india)) and WhatsApp catalogue bots ([Fast2SMS guide](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)) exist. Their gap: they make the dealer change habits, use an app or tap through a bot menu. In my judgment many small dealers will not do that; ask in discovery how many dealers use the firm's app today. Your version lets dealers keep sending free text, photos, and voice notes, and does the understanding on your side. That fits your rule that the process must already be part digital.

**g. Realistic price.** Medium: ₹60,000 to ₹1,50,000 setup, plus ₹6,000 to ₹15,000 a month. Small: ₹20,000 to ₹50,000 setup plus ₹3,000 to ₹5,000 a month, or a discounted pilot for a case study. Brutal truth: if the firm has one clerk at ₹15,000 a month, they will not pay ₹10,000 a month to you. Walk away from those or treat them as free pilots.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### India workflow 2: Dealer payments into bill wise allocation in Tally (secondary)

**a. Who has it.** The same distributors and manufacturers. Medium mostly.

**b. Steps today.** A dealer pays by NEFT, UPI, or cheque, often one payment for several bills, sometimes short paid. The dealer says which bills on WhatsApp or not at all. An accountant opens the bank statement, reads the message, and allocates the receipt against bills in Tally (bill wise details), then follows up on short payments.

**c. Software.** Bank statement (PDF or Excel, or Tally's banking features), WhatsApp, TallyPrime.

**d. How often, how long.** Estimate: 20 to 150 receipts a day at a medium distributor, 2 to 10 minutes each when bills are unclear.

**e. Who does it.** An accountant at about ₹18,000 to ₹25,000 a month ([Indeed Ludhiana](https://in.indeed.com/q-account-assistant-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)).

**f. Existing tools and gap.** CredFlow costs ₹4,999 to ₹19,999 a year and focuses on reminders and receivables views from Tally ([Techjockey CredFlow](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/credflow), [CredFlow pricing](https://credfloat.in/pricing)). TallyPrime 7.0 added PrimeBanking payments and reconciliation help ([Tally release notes 7.0](https://help.tallysolutions.com/release-notes-tallyprime-7-0/)). What I could not find in either: reading a dealer's WhatsApp message and proposing which bills a lump payment covers. Check a live demo of both before you build, because this gap may be smaller than it looks.

**g. Price.** ₹30,000 to ₹80,000 setup, ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 a month.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

**Why secondary:** it only makes sense for firms with many dealers and messy payments, and its main value to you is as the Indian twin of the European remittance workflow.

## Region 2: Europe and the UK

### Context that shapes everything

1. **Supplier invoices are becoming structured data.** Belgium made B2B e-invoicing over Peppol mandatory from 1 January 2026. Poland's KSeF became mandatory for large firms in February 2026 and most others in April 2026. France requires every business to be able to receive e-invoices from 1 September 2026. Germany requires issuing for firms above 800,000 EUR from 1 January 2027 ([Finbite](https://finbite.eu/en/e-invoicing-mandates-europe-2026/), [OpenText](https://blogs.opentext.com/e-invoicing-europe-2026-lessons-from-poland-belgium-greece-france-and-germany/)). Result: invoice OCR is a shrinking market. Purchase orders and remittances are not covered by these rules, so they stay as PDFs and emails.
2. **Xero includes invoice and receipt capture free** in its plans ([Xero](https://www.xero.com/us/accounting-software/capture-data-with-hubdoc/), [Xero Central](https://central.xero.com/0/article/Hubdoc-in-Xero)). Do not compete with free.

### Europe workflow 1: Emailed purchase orders into sales orders (top pick)

**a. Who has it.** Wholesale distributors (building supplies, industrial parts, food service, packaging, electrical, medical consumables), manufacturers who sell to trade customers, and 3PLs that take client orders by email. **Medium** fits best. **Small** firms in the 20 to 49 range fit if they have a dedicated order person.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A customer emails a PO: a PDF from their ERP, an Excel file, or lines typed in the email.
2. An order processor opens it, maps the customer's part numbers to their own SKUs, checks price against the agreed price list, checks stock and delivery date.
3. They type the sales order into the ERP.
4. They email an order confirmation and chase anything wrong (old price, unknown part, missing delivery address).

Vendors in this space describe the same flow: read the email and attachment, extract lines, match to the item master, and queue an ERP ready order for human review ([Y Meadows](https://ymeadows.com/en/insights/blog/ai-order-entry-for-distributors/), [Logistify](https://www.logistify.ai/blog/ai-order-entry-software), [Lido](https://www.lido.app/blog/best-sales-order-automation-software)). Job ads for the role list exactly this work: checking orders, preparing order documents, working in an ERP and a shared inbox ([Prime Appointments](https://prime-appointments.co.uk/sales-order-processor), [Indeed UK jobs](https://uk.indeed.com/q-sales-order-processor-jobs.html)).

**c. Software on each side.** In: Outlook or Microsoft 365 (most common in UK and EU B2B), Gmail. Out, with API status:

| ERP | Who uses it | Usable API for sales orders? |
|---|---|---|
| Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central | Lower medium, very common in UK and EU | Yes, REST API with a documented create salesOrders call ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/api/dynamics_salesorder_create)) |
| Sage 200 | UK medium | Yes, Sage 200 API exposes sales orders ([Sage KB](https://gb-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=200427112342684), [Sage developer forum](https://developer-community.sage.com/topic/19805-basic-sales-order-flow-with-apis/)) |
| Unleashed, Cin7, Odoo, Exact Online | Small and medium wholesalers | Yes, public APIs (from my knowledge; confirm per client) |
| Sage 50 (UK desktop) | Small | Hard. Desktop, limited API. Avoid at first. |
| Xero, QuickBooks Online | Small | No true sales orders in Xero. Usually paired with an inventory app above. Target that app instead. |
| Epicor (Kinetic, BisTrack) | Medium | Epicor now sells its own order entry automation ([Epicor Kinetic](https://www.epicor.com/en-uk/products/enterprise-resource-planning-erp/kinetic/sales-management/sales-order-automation/), [Epicor BisTrack](https://www.epicor.com/en-uk/products/enterprise-resource-planning-erp/bistrack/order-entry-automation/)). **Skip Epicor shops.** |

**d. How often, how long.** Estimate: 30 to 300 emailed orders a day at a 20 to 150 person distributor; 3 to 15 minutes each depending on the number of lines and how clean the PDF is. One vendor claims order reps spend up to 40 percent of their time on data entry ([Y Meadows](https://ymeadows.com/en/insights/blog/ai-order-entry-for-distributors/)); treat vendor numbers as marketing until you measure.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Sales order processors or customer service reps. UK average about £26,800 a year, typical range £26,000 to £29,000 ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)). With employer costs, call it £31,000 to £34,000 per person (estimate). Example: 100 orders a day at 6 minutes each is 10 hours a day, a bit more than one full person. At 7.5 working hours a day that is about 1.3 people, or roughly £40,000 to £45,000 a year.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**

| Tool | What it does | Gap for your buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Conexiom | Full order automation with validation | Built for midsize and enterprise, big ERPs ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/205134/Conexiom/), [Conexiom](https://conexiom.com/solutions)) |
| Rossum | Document AI with email intake | Starter about 18,000 USD a year, quote only ([Floowed](https://www.floowed.com/insights/rossum-pricing)) |
| Parseur, Docparser | Extract fields from emails and PDFs | 39 to 399 USD a month, but no SKU matching, price checks, or ERP order writing ([Parseur](https://parseur.com/pricing)) |
| Epicor built in | Order automation for Epicor users | Epicor only |
| Y Meadows, Logistify, Lido and others | AI order entry | New and growing. Some target your exact buyer. This is your real competition. |

Your honest gap: a 50 person distributor on Business Central or Sage 200 cannot justify 18,000 USD a year, and a 39 USD extractor still leaves the hard part (matching and checks) to a person. You sit in between, with setup done for them and a person they can call. **Brutal honesty:** the gap is closing. New AI order entry startups appeared in 2025 and 2026. Your edge is service and a fixed price for one ERP, not technology. Move in the next 6 to 12 months or the gap fills.

**g. Realistic price.** See the pricing section. Short version: 3,000 to 7,500 USD setup, 400 to 1,200 USD a month.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### Europe workflow 2: Remittance advice into cash allocation (second product)

**a. Who has it.** B2B firms that invoice many trade customers: wholesalers, manufacturers, logistics firms, contract cleaners, staffing firms. **Medium** mostly; small firms rarely have enough volume.

**b. Steps today.** A customer pays one amount for many invoices and emails a remittance PDF. A credit controller opens it, reads each invoice number, finds it in the ledger, checks the amount, and allocates the payment line by line. Short payments and deductions go to a query list. In Xero there is no built in way to read a remittance PDF and split a lump payment across invoices ([RemitClear on Xero](https://remitclear.com/blog/how-to-reconcile-remittance-advice-in-xero), [RemitClear multi invoice](https://remitclear.com/solutions/multi-invoice-payment-matching-xero)).

**c. Software.** In: Outlook inbox, bank feed. Out: Sage 200, Business Central, Exact, Xero, QuickBooks.

**d. How often, how long.** Estimate: 20 to 200 remittances a week, 5 to 20 minutes for multi invoice ones. RemitClear itself says automation pays off above about 10 to 20 remittances a week ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/multi-invoice-payment-matching-xero)).

**e. Who does it.** Credit controllers or sales ledger clerks at about £27,000 to £31,000 a year outside London, £36,750 to £45,000 in London ([Robert Walters](https://www.robertwalters.co.uk/our-services/salary-survey/credit-controller-salaries.html), [Robert Half](https://www.roberthalf.com/gb/en/job-details/credit-controller)). Remittance chasing and cash allocation are listed duties in these roles.

**f. Existing tools and gap.** RemitClear covers Xero and QuickBooks with quote based pricing ([RemitClear pricing](https://remitclear.com/pricing)). So **do not sell this to Xero or QuickBooks firms.** Sell it only to Sage 200 and Business Central users, where I found no cheap equivalent. Verify that before you build: ask two Sage or BC resellers what their clients use.

**g. Price.** 2,500 to 5,000 USD setup, 300 to 800 USD a month.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

**Rules check:** you propose allocations, a person approves. You never move money. This fits your limits.

## Workflows in both regions (your case study bridge)

| Workflow | India version | Europe version | How strong is the bridge? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Order inbox to ERP** | WhatsApp and email orders into Tally or Busy | Emailed PDF and Excel POs into Business Central or Sage 200 | **Strong.** Same hard part: understand the order, match items, check price, send exceptions to a person. The numbers you measure in Ludhiana (orders a day, minutes saved, match rate, errors caught) are exactly what a UK operations head asks for. |
| **Payment to allocation** | Dealer payments allocated bill wise in Tally | Remittance PDFs allocated in Sage 200 or BC | **Medium.** Same logic, but Indian payments often have no remittance at all, so the Indian case is messier and less comparable. |

How to write the case study so a European buyer trusts it: show before and after numbers, the match rate on the first pass, the share of orders a person still had to fix, and a short screen video of the review screen. Do not hide that it was an Indian distributor. Say "Punjab based auto parts distributor, 140 orders a day, Tally." A UK buyer cares that the matching works on messy real orders, and Indian orders are messier than UK ones. That is a selling point.

## Pricing in Europe: your guess, corrected

Your guess: 1,500 to 4,000 USD for a first workflow, 400 to 1,500 USD a month.

| Buyer | First 1 to 2 clients (no case study) | After 2 case studies | Monthly upkeep |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small (20 to 49 staff) | 1,500 to 3,000 USD | 3,000 to 4,500 USD | 300 to 600 USD |
| Medium (50 to 250 staff) | 3,000 to 5,000 USD | 5,000 to 7,500 USD | 600 to 1,200 USD |

My corrections:

1. **Setup:** your range is right for small firms and for your first clients. It is too low for medium firms once you have proof. An ERP integration with matching rules is worth more than 4,000 USD to a firm spending £40,000 a year on the task.
2. **Monthly:** 1,500 USD a month is too high for one workflow at this size. Buyers will compare you to Parseur at 39 to 399 USD a month. Justify the monthly fee with things a tool does not give: you fix broken matches, update rules when a customer changes their PO layout, and report the numbers each month. 600 to 1,200 USD is the realistic ceiling for medium.
3. **The math that sells it:** 100 orders a day costs about £40,000 to £45,000 a year of staff time (estimate above). If you cut 60 percent of it, they save about £24,000 to £27,000 (about 31,000 to 35,000 USD) a year. Year one at 5,000 USD setup plus 800 USD a month is 14,600 USD. They get about 2 times back. That is a real yes.
4. **Charge LLM and API costs at cost** to the client, or have them use their own keys. This keeps you under your 50 USD a month budget.

## Final top picks

### Pick 1: Order inbox to ERP. Do this now.

**Reasons:** a paid role exists in both regions; enterprise tools are too expensive and cheap tools stop at extraction; one owner (order desk lead or customer service manager); 2 to 3 tools (inbox, ERP, your review screen); easy to count (orders a day, minutes per order); a person approves every exception.

**Who to target first:**
1. India pilot: a Ludhiana distributor or manufacturer with 2 or more order clerks on TallyPrime. Charge ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 for the pilot. 4 to 6 weeks.
2. Europe: UK and Irish distributors with 20 to 150 staff on **Business Central** first (best API, largest pool), then **Sage 200**. Two connectors only for the first year.

**What you build (split to your skills):**
1. Developer A (Spring Boot, PostgreSQL, Spring AI): inbox reader (Microsoft Graph for Outlook, Gmail API), LLM extraction into a fixed order schema, item matching (customer part number table, then fuzzy text, then vector search with pgvector), rule checks (price against price list, minimum quantity, unknown item, credit hold), ERP writers for Business Central and Sage 200.
2. Developer B (Node, TypeScript, n8n): review screen where the order desk approves or fixes each order, the confirmation email draft, the Tally desktop connector and WhatsApp API hook for India, and glue flows in n8n.
3. Hosting: one small EU region server running n8n, Postgres and the Spring app. Estimate about 10 to 25 USD a month. EU hosting matters: medium EU buyers will ask where data sits and want a data processing agreement under GDPR.

**How to sell in Europe from Ludhiana:** go through Business Central and Sage partners (resellers). They have the clients and lack AI builders. Offer them a referral fee or white label. The trade off: you give up 15 to 30 percent of revenue and some client contact, but you skip cold outreach, which is slow for an unknown two person team in India.

### Pick 2: Remittance to cash allocation. Add after 2 paying order clients.

**Reasons:** same buyers, same ERPs, same tech (read PDF, match to open items, propose, approve). You can sell it as a second workflow to the same client, which is the cheapest sale you will ever make. **Only for Sage 200 and Business Central users**, because RemitClear already serves Xero and QuickBooks.

### Trade offs you are accepting

| Choice | What you get | What you give up |
|---|---|---|
| One product, not a menu | Faster build, sharper case studies, reusable code | Clients who want something else. You will say no a lot. |
| Distributors and manufacturers only | Buyers who all speak the same language (SKUs, POs, price lists) | Services firms, clinics, agencies, and accountants |
| Business Central and Sage 200 first | Real APIs, medium budgets | Xero only shops, Sage 50 desktop firms, Epicor shops |
| Lower medium in Europe | Budgets of 5,000 USD and more | Longer sales cycles (expect 1 to 3 months) and security questions |
| India as pilot ground | Fast, cheap proof on messy real data | Little Indian income. Paid Indian work only from firms with 2 or more clerks. |
| Selling through ERP partners | Warm leads | 15 to 30 percent of revenue and some control |

### What could make me wrong

1. AI order entry startups may drop to small business prices within a year. Watch Y Meadows, Logistify and Lido pricing every quarter.
2. Business Central and Sage may ship their own order reading features, like Epicor already did. If they do, move to the remittance workflow or to ERPs they do not cover.
3. Indian firms may refuse the WhatsApp API setup. Then start the Indian pilot with emailed orders only.

## Discovery call: measure before you quote

Ask these in the first call and fill the numbers into the price math:

1. How many orders (or remittances) a day, and in the busiest week?
2. How many people touch them, and for how many hours a day?
3. What share come as PDF, Excel, email text, WhatsApp, or photos?
4. Do customers use your SKUs or their own part numbers?
5. How many orders last month had an error (wrong item, wrong price, missed)? What did one error cost?
6. Which ERP and version? Cloud or on premise? Who holds admin access?
7. Who signs off on spending, and who owns the order desk?

If the answers show fewer than about 30 orders a day in Europe, or only one clerk in India, the savings will not cover your price. Say so and walk away.
