# Workflows worth selling: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Build one engine and sell one workflow: **turning customer orders that arrive by email (PDF, Excel, or plain text) into a checked draft sales order in the client's ERP, which a person approves.**

1. Sell it in Europe to wholesalers, distributors, and small manufacturers that run Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central, Exact Online, or Sage 200.
2. Build it first for one Indian supplier that runs Tally, so you have a real case study with real numbers.
3. Later, sell the mirror workflow (checking supplier order confirmations against your purchase orders) to the same European clients.

Everything else we checked is either already sold cheaply by a tool, or too small to be a workflow. Details and sources follow.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK plus EU countries. Most evidence below is from the UK, with some from the Netherlands and Belgium (Exact Online).
2. Currency: I use about $1.30 per £1 for rough conversions. Check the rate on the day you quote.
3. Many numbers come from vendor blogs and job boards. Vendors inflate pain to sell tools. I say where a number is a vendor claim and where it is my own estimate. Your first 5 discovery calls must replace my estimates with the client's real counts.
4. My web search worked, but direct page fetching was blocked in my session. So every link below is a page I found through search and read in the search results. Open each one before you quote it to a client.

## How I chose (the argument in three steps)

**Thesis: sell supplier invoice (accounts payable) automation.** It is the most common back office pain in every small business. Every firm gets bills. The steps are clear. The APIs (Xero, QuickBooks, Business Central) are good.

**Antithesis: that market is already solved and cheap.** Dext captures bills and pushes them into accounting software from about £24 to £30 a month for 5 users and 250 documents ([BookkeeperTools](https://bookkeepertools.uk/dext-pricing-uk-2026/)). Dext also sells supplier statement reconciliation ([Dext](https://dext.com/en/business/products/supplier-statements-reconciliation)), and so does Yooz ([Yooz](https://www.getyooz.com/en-gb/yooz-vendor-statements)). In India, Suvit (now Vyapar TaxOne) turns purchase and sales PDFs and bank statements into Tally entries and does GST reconciliation, from about ₹10,000 ([Techjockey](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/suvit), [Suvit pricing](https://www.suvit.io/pricing)). You cannot beat a £25 tool with a $3,000 service.

**Synthesis: go to the other side of the same document problem.** Bills coming IN are standard and a cheap tool handles them. Orders coming IN from customers are not standard. Each customer uses its own product codes, units, and price deals. Every order has to be matched to the seller's own item list and price list inside the seller's own ERP. That custom matching is exactly why SMB tools are thin here and enterprise tools are expensive (Conexiom about $10,000 to $100,000+ a year per [ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom); Rossum Starter $18,000 a year per [Floowed](https://www.floowed.com/insights/rossum-pricing)). Custom matching per client is what a service business does well. So: keep the "documents into software" idea, but point it at sales orders.

# Region 1: India

**Brutal truth first.** A billing executive with Tally skills in Ludhiana earns about ₹12,000 to ₹22,000 a month ([Indeed Ludhiana billing jobs](https://in.indeed.com/q-billing-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html), [PlacementIndia](https://www.placementindia.com/job-search/billing-executive-jobs-in-ludhiana.htm)). Order apps with Tally sync cost about ₹3,999 per user per year ([SoftwareSuggest on Bizmitra](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/bizmitra-erp)). So Indian owners will not pay European prices, and they will compare you to one more junior hire. Treat India as your lab and your case study. Take paid work when it comes, but do not plan your income on it.

## I2. Emailed purchase orders from bigger buyers into Tally (the case study)

**a. Who has it.** Small manufacturers and suppliers that sell to larger companies: auto and bicycle parts makers, hosiery and garment units, packaging and hand tool makers. Ludhiana is full of them. Mostly **small** (owner decides), some **medium** (sales coordinator or accounts head owns it).

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: a buyer emails a PO as a PDF or Excel file.
2. A staff member opens it, finds the customer in Tally, and reads each line.
3. They map the buyer's part code or description to their own stock item, check the rate against the agreed rate, and note the delivery date.
4. They type a sales order (or go straight to a sales invoice later) into Tally.
5. End: order is in Tally and the owner or sales head knows what to make and ship.

**c. Software.** Intake: Gmail or Outlook. Destination: TallyPrime (sometimes Busy). Tally takes data through its XML over HTTP interface on port 9000; you post "Import" requests to write vouchers, and masters (ledgers, stock items) must exist first ([TallyHelp XML Integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/), [AI Accountant Tally API guide](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/tally-api-integration-guide)). Tally runs on the client's own PC, so you need a small local connector (a Node script on that PC) that pulls approved orders from your server and posts them to Tally on localhost. That is one extra moving part. Accept it.

**d. How often and how long.** My estimate: 5 to 40 POs a day, 5 to 20 minutes each depending on line count. Industry guides report 4 minutes for a simple order up to 60 to 90 minutes for a 100 line PO (vendor figures, not India specific: [Infrrd](https://www.infrrd.ai/blog/order-entry-automation), [Hyperfox](https://www.hyperfox.com/insights/the-real-cost-of-manual-order-entry)). Count this in discovery.

**e. Who does it and the cost.** A billing or accounts executive at ₹12,000 to ₹22,000 a month (sources above). Often the owner checks rates himself, which is the real cost: his time and wrong rates.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Indian order tools (Bizmitra, Rupyz, Takkada, Nexxio) are built around their own app, catalogue, or salesman flow ([Bizmitra](https://bizmitra.io/), [Rupyz](https://rupyz.com/blog/order-management-system-with-whatsapp-tally-integration/), [Takkada](https://takkada.com/), [Nexxio](https://nexxio.in/dealer-connect.html)). A big buyer will not use its supplier's app. It sends its own PDF. Suvit reads purchase and sales PDFs into Tally ([Suvit data entry](https://www.suvit.io/data-entry-automation-feature)), but it is sold to CA firms for accounting entries, not for matching a buyer's part codes to your stock items and checking agreed rates. That matching and checking is the gap. Honest caveat: I found no hard Indian data on how common this is. Prove it in 10 calls with Ludhiana suppliers before you build.

**g. Realistic price.** ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 setup, ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 a month. For your first one, charge little or nothing in return for: written permission to name them, before and after numbers, and a short video of the owner.

**h. Sources.** Listed inline above.

## I1. WhatsApp orders from retailers into Tally (paid Indian work only)

**a. Who has it.** FMCG, pharma, hardware, and electrical distributors and wholesalers. Mostly **small**.

**b. Steps today.** Retailers and salesmen send orders on WhatsApp as text, voice notes, and photos of handwritten lists. Staff read them, check stock, and retype them into Tally, then send the bill back on WhatsApp ([Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)).

**c. Software.** WhatsApp Business (app or API) and TallyPrime or Busy. To read messages automatically, orders must reach a WhatsApp Business Platform number through a provider; Indian providers list pricing from ₹0.20 per message ([Chat Mitra](https://chatmitra.com/pricing/), [Meta pricing](https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/platform-pricing/)). Tally side as in I2.

**d. How often and how long.** My estimate: 30 to 150 orders a day, 2 to 6 minutes each. Vendors claim 60 to 80% less processing time after automation (vendor claim, [HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)).

**e. Who and cost.** One or two billing executives at ₹12,000 to ₹22,000 a month.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** This space is crowded and cheap: Bizmitra (₹3,999 per user per year), Rupyz, Takkada, Nexxio, plus Tally WhatsApp senders like Fast2SMS (links above). Their gap is that they want the retailer or salesman to order through a catalogue. Voice notes and handwritten photos from retailers who will not change are still retyped. That gap is real but small, and the buyer is price sensitive.

**g. Realistic price.** ₹20,000 to ₹40,000 setup, ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 a month. Only take it if they pay.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

# Region 2: Europe (including the UK)

## E1. Emailed customer orders into the ERP (your main product)

**a. Who has it.** B2B wholesalers and distributors (food and drink, building and electrical supplies, industrial parts, packaging, cleaning and janitorial) and small manufacturers selling to trade customers. **Both sizes.** Small: the owner or MD buys, because 1 to 3 people spend their day keying orders. Medium: the customer service or sales operations manager owns it.

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: a customer emails an order: PDF, Excel, or lines typed in the email body.
2. The order processor finds the customer in the ERP.
3. For each line, they translate the customer's code or description to their own SKU and unit.
4. They check price against the customer's price list and check stock or lead time.
5. They key the sales order line by line.
6. End: sales order in the ERP and a confirmation email to the customer.

UK job ads describe exactly this: processing orders received by phone, email, and website into Sage, and entering sales orders following customer specific instructions ([Jobsite sales order processor jobs](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor), [Totaljobs](https://www.totaljobs.com/jobs/sales-order-processor), [DWP Find a Job listing](https://findajob.dwp.gov.uk/details/16605000)).

**c. Software on each side and APIs.**

| Side | Software | API status |
|---|---|---|
| Intake | Microsoft 365 Outlook or Gmail | Both have standard mail APIs. |
| ERP | Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central | Good. REST API v2.0 lets you create salesOrders and salesOrderLines with OAuth ([Microsoft Learn: salesOrders](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/api/dynamics_salesorder_create), [salesOrderLines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/api/dynamics_salesorderline_create)). Best first target. |
| ERP | Exact Online (NL, BE, growing in UK and DE) | Good. REST API supports sales orders ([Exact knowledge base](https://support.exactonline.com/community/s/article/All-All-DNO-Content-rest-api-business-cases-rest-bsncs-slsord)); for volume, the XML API is recommended for higher throughput and quotas ([Invantive forum](https://forums.invantive.com/t/create-an-exact-online-sales-order-with-details-through-rest-calls/230)). |
| ERP | Sage 200 (UK) | Usable. REST API covers sales orders ([Sage developer portal](https://developer.sage.com/200-uk/apis/sage-200-professional/2024.01/combined/groups/sop/tags/sop-orders/paths/postsoporder)), but you must join the Sage 200 API Developer Programme ([Sage KB](https://gb-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=200427112454709)). | Accounting | Xero | API is fine. Since March 2026 Xero charges developers by tier; the Starter tier is free but capped at 5 connections ([Xero Developer pricing](https://developer.xero.com/pricing), [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/xero-shifts-to-tiered-pricing-model-for-developers)). Fine for your first 5 clients. Before you quote a Xero client, check where their orders actually live (Xero itself or a separate stock tool), because that tool is then your target. |

**d. How often and how long.** Vendor and benchmark figures: about 4 minutes for a simple order, 8 to 30 minutes typical, 60 to 90 minutes for a 100 line PO; a vendor puts the cost at €25 to €100 per manually processed order ([Infrrd](https://www.infrrd.ai/blog/order-entry-automation), [Hyperfox](https://www.hyperfox.com/insights/the-real-cost-of-manual-order-entry), [Ziiware](https://www.ziiware.com/blog/manual-order-entry-wholesale-distribution/)). My estimate for your buyers: 20 to 150 orders a day. **Qualify rule:** only sell where at least half of one person's day goes on keying orders.

**e. Who does it and the cost.** Sales order processors earn about £20,000 to £27,000 a year ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries), [PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/UK/Job=Order_Processor/Salary)). Live ads show £13.50 an hour in St Helens and £28,000 to £29,000 in Wellingborough (found via the job board links above).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**

| Tool | Price found | Why it does not close the deal for a small firm |
|---|---|---|
| Conexiom | about $10,000 to $100,000+ a year, multi year ([ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom)) | Priced for enterprise. |
| Rossum | Starter $18,000 a year, 1 year minimum ([Floowed](https://www.floowed.com/insights/rossum-pricing)) | Extraction platform; you still build the ERP link and matching. |
| OrderSync | $500 to $2,500 a month ([OrderSync vs Conexiom](https://ordersync.io/ordersync-vs/conexiom)) | Closest rival. Real competition for medium firms. |
| StackCube | Volume based plans; I could not confirm the numbers ([StackCube pricing](https://www.stackcube.io/pricing)) | New SMB entrant. Check its ERP list before each pitch. |
| Nanonets | about $0.30 per page, Starter $100 a month ([DocuOCR](https://docuocr.com/nanonets-pricing)) | Extraction only. No SKU matching, no price check, no ERP order. |

**The gap:** cheap tools stop at "read the PDF". Expensive tools do the full job but cost $6,000 to $30,000+ a year. Nobody cheap does "match this customer's lines to my items and prices in my Business Central, then let my processor approve". That is your product. Be honest with yourself: OrderSync and StackCube are closing this gap. Your edge is doing the per client setup (customer code tables, price rules) as a service.

**g. Realistic price.** Your guess of $1,500 to $4,000 for the first workflow is **right for the build.** UK agencies quote £1,000 to £5,000 for a single automation on a £550 day rate ([Axivon](https://www.axivon-ai.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-cost-uk)). Your upkeep guess of $400 to $1,500 a month is **too high at the top for small firms.** UK small business retainers sit around £300 to £900 a month ([AI Workforce](https://www.aiworkforce.co.uk/blogs/ai-automation-pricing-uk-small-businesses-2026)), and Axivon quotes £300 to £997.

Charge this:

| Buyer | Build | Monthly (includes hosting, AI usage, fixes, new customer formats) |
|---|---|---|
| Small (5 to 49 staff) | $2,500 to $3,500 | $400 to $700 |
| Medium (50 to 250 staff) | $3,500 to $6,000 | $800 to $1,500 |

Sanity check for a small client: one processor at £24,000. If you remove half the keying, that frees about £12,000 of time a year. Your year one price at $3,000 plus $550 a month is about $9,600, or roughly £7,400. That works, but only above the qualify rule in (d). Below it, the owner is right to say no.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

## E2. Supplier order confirmations checked against purchase orders (second sale)

**a. Who has it.** Manufacturers and distributors that buy a lot of stock. Mostly **medium**; the purchasing or supply chain manager owns it. Some larger **small** firms.

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: the firm sends a PO; the supplier replies with an order confirmation, usually a PDF by email.
2. A purchasing assistant compares price, quantity, and delivery date with the PO.
3. They update the delivery date in the ERP and flag differences.
4. They chase suppliers who never confirmed.
5. End: every open PO has a confirmed date and price, or a flagged exception.

UK ads list exactly these duties: checking order confirmations, updating the system with delivery dates, chasing suppliers ([Jobsite purchasing assistant jobs](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/purchasing-assistant), [DWP listing](https://findajob.dwp.gov.uk/details/16396322), [Lucy Walker Recruitment](https://www.lucywalkerrecruitment.com/job/purchasing-assistant-2/)). A vendor claims up to half of mid market POs never get a formal confirmation, and that email with a PDF is the normal channel (vendor claim, [Leverage](https://tryleverage.ai/blog/pf/po-acknowledgement-automation-hybrid-edi-1)).

**c. Software.** Outlook or Gmail, plus Business Central, Exact Online, or Sage 200 (same APIs as E1, on the purchase side).

**d. How often and how long.** My estimate: 20 to 100 confirmations a day for a medium firm, 3 to 10 minutes each, plus chasing. Measure it.

**e. Who and cost.** Purchasing assistants at about £17,000 to £29,000 (ads above).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Netfira, Doxis (formerly Klippa), DocBits, Hyperfox, and Leverage all do this ([Netfira](https://netfira.com/solutions/process-purchase-order-confirmations-automatically/), [Doxis](https://www.doxis.com/en/solutions/order-confirmation-automation), [DocBits](https://docbits.com/en/order-confirmation-automation/), [Hyperfox](https://www.hyperfox.com/solutions/use-cases/ai-order-confirmation-agent)). None publish SMB prices that I found, and they lead with SAP and large ERPs. The gap is smaller than E1. That is why it is the second sale, not the first.

**g. Realistic price.** $2,000 to $3,500 build for an existing E1 client (you reuse the engine), $400 to $900 a month.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

# Final top picks

1. **E1: emailed customer orders into Business Central (then Exact, then Sage 200).** Clear paid pain (named job role, £20,000 to £27,000 salaries), a real price gap between $0.30 per page readers and $10,000+ a year platforms, and clean APIs. It fits your skills: Developer A's Spring Boot and fintech review flows match "draft, check, approve". Start with Business Central because its API is the cleanest and its clients already pay for software.
2. **I2 in Ludhiana as the case study, before or alongside the first European sale.** Same engine, a Tally connector instead of Business Central. Cheap or free in exchange for named results.
3. **E2 as the second workflow for the same European client.** Easier to sell to someone who already trusts you than to a stranger.

**Not recommended:** supplier bill entry, statement reconciliation, GST reconciliation, payment reminders. Cheap tools already do them (sources in the matrix).

## What to do next, in order

1. **Weeks 1 to 2: 10 calls.** 5 Ludhiana suppliers that get emailed POs, 5 UK wholesalers or distributors on Business Central. Ask: orders per day, minutes per order, who keys them, what goes wrong. Walk away from anyone under the qualify rule.
2. **Also call 3 Business Central partners (resellers) in the UK.** They know which of their clients drown in emailed orders, and they do not want to build this themselves.
3. **Weeks 3 to 6: build for the Indian pilot.** Parts, and nothing more:
   1. Mail inbox watcher (one mailbox per client).
   2. Extraction with an LLM into a fixed JSON order schema.
   3. Matching against the client's customer list, item list, and a customer code table you maintain.
   4. Checks: unknown item, price differs from list, unusual quantity.
   5. One review screen where the processor edits and approves.
   6. One connector: Tally XML (India) or Business Central API (Europe).
4. **Weeks 7 to 8: run the pilot for 4 weeks, collect the numbers above, and write the case study.**
5. **Then sell E1 in the UK** at the prices in the E1 pricing table.

**Budget check.** Keep one client's running cost (server, database, AI calls) small enough that your total stays under $50 a month until you have revenue. Measure the real AI cost per order in the pilot, then build it into the monthly fee. You never touch money or approve anything: the client's processor approves every order.

**What would change this advice.** If your 5 UK calls show that most prospects already use OrderSync or StackCube, or keep fewer than 20 orders a day, drop E1 and move to E2 for medium firms.
