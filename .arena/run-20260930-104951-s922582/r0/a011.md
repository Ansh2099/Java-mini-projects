# Workflow Automations to Sell: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Sell one workflow first: **turning customer orders that arrive as messy messages into clean sales orders in the client's system, with a human approving anything odd.** In Europe that means purchase orders sent by email (PDF, Excel or plain text) and keyed into inventory or ERP software like Unleashed, Cin7 Core, Sage 200 or Odoo. In India it means orders sent on WhatsApp (text, voice notes, photos of handwritten lists) and punched into Tally or Busy. It is the same engine in both places, so your Indian client becomes a real case study for European buyers. Real companies pay full time salaries to do this job today, big vendors prove the need but price out small firms, and it sits on the revenue side, so owners care. Your second product is the mirror image: checking supplier order confirmations against your client's purchase orders. Do not build supplier invoice capture, freight quoting or mortgage paperwork. Cheap tools or new laws already cover those. Your price guess is about right for the build, but too high for monthly upkeep with small firms.

The rest of this document backs that up. First the facts everything else stands on. Then the workflows. Then the detail.

---

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK plus EU countries where English works for sales (UK, Ireland, Netherlands, Nordics first). German and French markets need local language sales, so I treat them as later.
2. Prices in Europe are in USD so you can compare with your own guess. INR for India.
3. Time per task and volume numbers marked **(estimate)** are my own reasoning, not sourced. Check them in your first three sales calls.
4. My page fetch tool was blocked during this research. Every number below comes from search results that summarised the linked pages. The links are real and I found them, but open the key ones yourself before you quote a number to a client.
5. Many sources are vendor blogs selling a fix. I use them as proof that the problem exists and that people pay for it, not as neutral statistics.

## Pass 1: The facts nobody can argue with

I rebuilt the answer from these facts only.

1. **You have about 15 to 20 hours a week between you and under $50 a month.** So the workflow must reuse one engine many times. Every new client should be setup, not a new build.
2. **Europe pays, India proves.** So the best workflow exists in both places in almost the same shape.
3. **Your sweet spot is "unstructured thing arrives, a person retypes it into software that has an API."** LLMs are now good at the reading part. The value you add is the checking and the matching to the client's own data.
4. **If a built in feature or a tool under about $100 a month does it, you lose.** So look for where current tools are either enterprise priced or locked to one ERP.
5. **Owners pay faster for money coming in than for money going out.** A faster, correct order means a happier customer and cash sooner. Cost saving on back office work is a weaker story.
6. **Selling is harder than building for you.** You are two part timers in Ludhiana selling to strangers in Europe. A case study with real numbers is your only trust signal at the start.

Order intake passes all six. Supplier invoice capture fails fact 4 (Dext, ApprovalMax, and Europe's new e-invoicing laws). Freight quoting fails fact 4 (several vertical SaaS tools). That is why the ranking below looks the way it does.

## Pass 3: The detail

### Region 1: India

**The honest truth about India first.** A clerk who punches orders into Tally costs about ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month. Indeed puts an average "distributor" role at about ₹22,715 a month, and Tally operator averages range from about ₹16,000 to ₹38,670 depending on the site ([Indeed](https://in.indeed.com/career/distributor/salaries), [Internshala](https://internshala.com/jobs/tally-jobs/), [Jooble](https://in.jooble.org/salary/tally-operator)). You cannot charge more than a fraction of that. Automation only wins where there is a rush (all orders land between 9 and 11 am), where wrong items cost real money, or where orders arrive at night and the business wants to dispatch first thing. For a distributor doing under about 30 orders a day, a clerk is cheaper. Say that out loud to prospects. It builds trust.

#### I1. WhatsApp order to Tally or Busy sales order

**a. Who has it.** Distributors and wholesalers of FMCG, pharma, hardware, electricals, auto parts and cycle parts (Ludhiana has plenty). Also manufacturers who take dealer orders. Mostly **small** (owner decides). Medium FMCG distributors have it too, but they get pushed onto brand DMS apps.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A retailer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp. It can be text like "bhai 200 amul 1L, 50 dahi 400g", a voice note listing 15 items by local names, or a photo of a handwritten list. Vendor blogs describe exactly this pattern ([Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/what-is-whatsapp-order-management)).
2. A staff member reads it and works out which Tally stock item each line means.
3. They check stock and the party's outstanding balance.
4. They "punch" a sales order or invoice into Tally or Busy. Job posts in India literally call this "order punching" ([Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/q-order-punching-jobs.html)).
5. They reply to confirm and tell the godown.

**c. Software.** WhatsApp Business app on one side. Since Meta's coexistence feature, the same number can stay on the phone app and also feed the Cloud API, and this works in India ([360dialog docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/phone-numbers/coexistence), [Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence)). Tally Prime on the other side. Tally takes XML over HTTP on port 9000, but only while Tally is open on a local PC with the company loaded ([Tally XML integration guide on Appycodes](https://appycodes.dev/blog/tally-integration-tdl-guide-2026/), [community API docs](https://noumaanahamed.github.io/tally-prime-api-docs/)). So you need a tiny local agent on the client's PC. Developer A can write it in Java in a day or two. A Google Sheet holds the "local name to Tally item" map.

**d. Volume and time (estimate).** 30 to 300 orders a day for a typical distributor. 2 to 6 minutes per order, longer for voice notes and photos.

**e. Who does it and cost.** An order punching clerk or Tally operator at ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month. Often the owner's family member does it at night. Vendor blogs claim 12% to 18% error rates from retyping. Treat that as marketing until you measure it yourself.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* Brand DMS tools like Bizom and FieldAssist cost roughly ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 per user per month and are built for FMCG brands with sales teams using an app ([SalesPort comparison](https://sortstring.com/blogs/fieldassist-vs-bizom-vs-beatroute-vs-salesport-2026-matrix), [SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/bizom/pricing)).
* WhatsApp ordering products push Tally sync, but most work by making the retailer order from a catalogue or app ([Nexxio Dealer Connect](https://nexxio.in/dealer-connect.html), [Takkada](https://takkada.com/blog/whatsapp-invoice-format-tally/), [PrecisionTech](https://precisiontech.in/apps/tally/tally-integration/)).
* **The gap:** retailers will not change how they order. Nobody cheap reads free text, Hinglish or Punjabi voice notes and handwritten photos and maps them to *this* distributor's own Tally item names with a human check. Brutal note: this space is getting crowded fast. Your edge is setup and the item mapping, not the idea.

**g. Realistic price.** ₹25,000 to ₹60,000 setup and ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 a month. For your first one or two clients, go near free in exchange for the right to publish numbers and a video testimonial. That is the real payment.

**h. Sources.** Linked inline above.

#### I2. Customer PO email to Tally sales order for manufacturers

**a. Who has it.** Component makers and exporters who sell to bigger companies (auto parts, fasteners, hosiery, cycle parts). **Both** sizes. This is the Indian version that looks most like the European workflow.

**b. Steps today.** A customer emails a PDF purchase order. A sales coordinator checks each line against the agreed rate list, enters a sales order in Tally, and emails back if a rate or item code is wrong. The same "order punching" job covers phone, WhatsApp and email orders ([Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/q-order-punching-jobs.html)).

**c. Software.** Gmail or Outlook, then Tally Prime (same XML route as I1). Some use Zoho Books, which has a normal cloud API.

**d. Volume and time (estimate).** 10 to 80 POs a week. 10 to 20 minutes each because lines are many and codes differ.

**e. Who and cost.** A sales coordinator at ₹18,000 to ₹35,000 a month (estimate, in line with the Tally role ranges above).

**f. Tools and gap.** Tally integrators build custom TDL jobs, priced per requirement ([IndiaMART listings](https://dir.indiamart.com/impcat/tally-customization-services.html)). None read the PDF and check the rate contract for you.

**g. Price.** ₹30,000 to ₹75,000 setup, ₹4,000 to ₹10,000 a month.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

### Region 2: Europe (including the UK)

**Price check on your guess.** UK agency guides put simple workflows from about £500, mid range builds at £3,000 to £10,000, and monthly support at £200 to £800 ([AI Workforce UK](https://www.aiworkforce.co.uk/blogs/ai-automation-pricing-uk-small-businesses-2026)). Another survey puts single workflows at $400 to $1,200 and workflows with an AI step at $1,500 to $4,500 ([Lets Viz](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/ai-automation-agency-pricing-2026-what-buyers-pay)). One UK agency sells fixed sprints from £4,500 ([Axivon](https://www.axivon-ai.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-cost-uk)).

**My correction:**
* **Build: $1,500 to $4,000 is right.** Small firms: $1,500 to $3,000. Medium firms: $3,000 to $6,000.
* **Upkeep: $400 to $1,500 a month is too high for small firms.** Small: $250 to $600. Medium: $600 to $1,500, only if you promise response times and they run high volume.
* **The hard ceiling:** SaaS order tools charge about $399 to $799 a month ([OrderDrafter pricing via search](https://orderdrafter.com/alternatives/conexiom-alternative/)). If your monthly fee is above that, a buyer will compare.

#### E1. Email purchase order to sales order in the ERP

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors (food and drink, packaging, industrial supplies, electrical, building products) and manufacturers selling to trade customers. **Both** sizes. Small: owner plus one to three order clerks. Medium: a customer service or sales operations head owns it.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A customer emails a PO as a PDF, an Excel file, or text in the email.
2. A clerk finds the customer account and matches the customer's product codes to the firm's own SKUs.
3. They check prices against the price list, and check stock and credit.
4. They key the order into the ERP line by line.
5. They send a confirmation, and chase the customer when something does not match.

Vendors say customer service reps spend 20% to 40% of their time on manual order handling, and that a manual B2B order costs $30 to $80 to process ([Conexiom blog](https://conexiom.com/blog/the-real-cost-of-manual-order-entry-in-b2b-operations), [Bizowie](https://bizowie.com/how-distributors-can-automate-80-of-manual-order-entry)). Those are sellers' numbers, but the job ads below prove firms pay people to do it.

**c. Software and APIs.**

| System | Who uses it | API for creating sales orders? |
|---|---|---|
| Unleashed | UK, NZ, AU wholesalers | Yes, REST SalesOrders endpoint ([docs](https://apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/SalesOrders)) |
| Cin7 Core | Product businesses | Yes, REST with API key ([docs](https://dearinventory.docs.apiary.io/)) |
| Sage 200 | UK medium firms | Yes, sales order endpoints ([Sage community](https://developer-community.sage.com/topic/19805-basic-sales-order-flow-with-apis/)) |
| Sage 50 desktop | Many UK small firms | **No native REST API.** Needs a connector on the same PC or network ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-integration/does-sage-50-have-an-api-yes-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)). Avoid at first. |
| Xero | Small firms without an ERP | Yes. Free Starter tier covers up to 5 connected orgs ([Truto on Xero 2026 pricing](https://truto.one/blog/xero-api-pricing-changes-2026-costs-tiers-and-how-to-minimize-egress/), [Xero pricing](https://developer.xero.com/pricing)) |
| Business Central | Medium firms | Yes, **but** Microsoft now ships a Sales Order Agent. Skip these clients. |
| Email | Everyone | Microsoft 365 or Gmail |

**d. Volume and time (estimate).** 20 to 300 orders a day. 3 to 15 minutes each depending on line count. A firm with 60 orders a day at 8 minutes spends 8 hours a day, so one full time person.

**e. Who does it and cost.** UK "sales order processor" jobs are common: Totaljobs listed about 754 of them ([Totaljobs](https://www.totaljobs.com/jobs/sales-order-processor)). Pay is about £21,000 to £29,000, with an order processor average of £26,832 as of May 2026 ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries), [Prime Appointments](https://prime-appointments.co.uk/sales-order-processor)). With employer costs that is roughly £30,000 to £36,000 a year per person (estimate).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* **Conexiom** is the big name. Deployments run about $10,000 to $100,000+ a year with per trading partner setup. Reviewers say the ROI does not clear for a 5 to 50 person distributor ([ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom), [Ordermatic comparison](https://ordermatic.co/blog/conexiom-alternatives)).
* **Business Central Sales Order Agent** reads emails and PDF attachments and drafts quotes or orders. It costs Copilot credits at $0.01 each, about 16.5 credits per request at typical usage ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent), [Dynamics 365 Lab](https://yzhums.com/68484/)). This kills the idea for BC users. Do not target them.
* **New SMB tools** exist but are tied to certain ERPs: OrderDrafter is for Epicor Prophet 21 at $399 to $799 a month ([OrderDrafter](https://orderdrafter.com/)). OrderPier targets Business Central, QuickBooks and webhooks ([OrderPier](https://orderpier.com/)). Workist targets SAP, Sage and Dynamics with quote only pricing ([Workist](https://www.workist.com/en/order-management-software)). Sage X3 has an add on for firms over 250 orders a month ([X3CloudDocs](https://www.x3clouddocs.com/solutions-modules/sales-order-automation/)).
* **The gap:** small and medium firms on Unleashed, Cin7 Core, Sage 200, Odoo or Xero with messy customer specific rules ("this customer's code 4471 is our SKU AB-12, and they always get the trade price"). A service that sets up the mapping and the checks fits them better than a US SaaS.
* **Brutal note:** this is a hot category. Expect a cheap SaaS for your target ERP within 12 to 24 months. Win now as a service, pick one ERP, and be ready to turn your engine into a product.

**g. Realistic price.** Small: $2,000 to $3,000 build, $300 to $600 a month. Medium: $3,500 to $6,000 build, $700 to $1,500 a month. Sell it as "less than a quarter of one order clerk."

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

#### E2. Supplier order confirmation check

**a. Who has it.** The same wholesalers and manufacturers, on the buying side. Mostly **medium** (a purchasing team exists). Small firms have it when they import a lot.

**b. Steps today.** The firm sends a PO to a supplier. The supplier replies with a confirmation PDF that may change the price, quantity or delivery date. A purchasing assistant compares it to the PO, updates the ETA in the system, and chases suppliers who have not confirmed. UK purchasing assistant job ads list exactly these duties: chasing confirmations and dispatch dates and updating ETAs and prices in the system ([Point Recruitment](https://pointrecruitment.co.uk/job-listing/purchasing-assistant), [Potential Recruitment](https://www.potentialrecruitment.com/role/purchasing-assistant/)).

**c. Software.** Outlook or Gmail, then the purchase order module of Unleashed, Cin7 Core, Sage 200 or Odoo. Same APIs as E1.

**d. Volume and time (estimate).** 20 to 150 POs a week. 5 to 10 minutes to check each confirmation, plus chasing.

**e. Who and cost.** Purchasing assistants earn about £25,000 to £29,000 (same job ad sources).

**f. Tools and gap.** Didero and Tacto do this for mid sized to large manufacturers on SAP, Oracle and Dynamics ([Procurement Magazine on Didero](https://procurementmag.com/news/how-didero-is-putting-procurement-on-autopilot), [Tacto on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10023900/Tacto/)). Conexiom handles vendor acknowledgements too, at enterprise prices. Nothing cheap exists for a 60 person wholesaler on Unleashed.

**g. Price.** $1,500 to $3,000 build (you reuse E1's engine), $300 to $800 a month. Best sold as the second workflow to an E1 client.

#### E3. Remittance advice to payment allocation (conditional)

A customer pays one lump sum for 40 invoices and emails a remittance PDF. Someone reads it and allocates the payment invoice by invoice. That pain is real ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/cash-application-software)). But RemitClear already does it for Xero and QuickBooks Online ([Xero App Store](https://apps.xero.com/us/app/remitclear)). **Only sell this to firms on Sage 200, Odoo or other ERPs RemitClear does not cover.** You do not move money here. You only suggest the allocation and a person approves it. Price like E2.

#### Europe: rules you must follow to get paid

1. **GDPR.** Sign a data processing agreement with each client. Host in the EU (AWS Frankfurt or Ireland, which Developer A knows). Use an LLM API with EU data residency or a no training setting, and say so in writing.
2. **Avoid Business Central and Sage 50 desktop clients at first.** One has a built in agent. The other has no cloud API.
3. **Time zones work.** UK 9 am is 1:30 pm in Ludhiana. You can cover the UK working day in your evening.

## Final top picks

**1. E1, email PO to sales order, for UK wholesalers on Unleashed or Cin7 Core.** Why: proven spend (hundreds of open order processor jobs at about £27,000), enterprise tools too expensive for this size, clean REST APIs, and it touches revenue. Pick one ERP so your second client is setup, not a rebuild. Unleashed is a good start because it is built for wholesalers and distributors, connects to Xero, and has a documented SalesOrders API ([Xero App Store](https://apps.xero.com/uk/app/unleashed-software)).

**2. I1 or I2 in Ludhiana first, as the proof.** Why: you can walk into the business, watch the clerk, and get real numbers in weeks. Do I2 if you can find a manufacturer with email POs, because it looks most like the European sale. Do I1 if a distributor friend will say yes tomorrow. Speed beats perfection here.

**3. E2, supplier confirmation checks, as the upsell.** Why: same engine, same buyer company, same ERP connector. It turns one client into two workflows without new sales effort.

**What I would not do:** supplier invoice capture, GST reconciliation, freight quoting, or anything on Business Central. Details below.

**The hard truth:** your bottleneck is selling, not building. Both of you can build E1 in a month. Finding the first European buyer will take longer. Start outreach in week 1, not after the build.

### Plan you can start today

| Week | Do this |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ask 5 Ludhiana distributors or manufacturers to let you time their order punching for one morning. Start a list of 50 UK wholesalers on Unleashed or Cin7 (their partner pages and Xero App Store reviews name customers). |
| 2 to 5 | Build the engine once: email or WhatsApp in, LLM extraction, SKU map in a Sheet, review screen, write to Tally (local Java agent) and to Unleashed (REST). Developer A owns connectors and the backend. Developer B owns n8n flows and the review screen. |
| 6 to 8 | Run it live at the Indian client. Measure orders per day, % auto matched, minutes saved, errors caught. Record a 2 minute video. |
| 8 onward | Email and LinkedIn the UK list with the numbers. Offer a fixed price pilot: $1,500 for 30 days on their real orders, credited to the full build. |

**Budget check (under $50 a month):** n8n self hosted on a small VPS or n8n Cloud Starter at about $20 a month ([n8n pricing summary](https://www.softomatesolutions.com/blog/n8n-review-2026/)); LLM calls for a few thousand orders cost a few dollars; Xero Starter API tier is free for up to 5 orgs; WhatsApp coexistence keeps messages sent from the phone app free ([Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence)). Unleashed and Cin7 APIs come with the client's own subscription.
