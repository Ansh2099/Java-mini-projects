# Workflow Automations SMBs Will Pay For: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

1. **Build one thing: "customer order arrives, sales order is ready to approve."** A customer sends an order by email, PDF, Excel or WhatsApp. Today a person reads it, finds the right products, checks prices and retypes it into the accounting or ERP system. You automate the reading, matching and checking. A person approves the exceptions. This is the strongest workflow in **both** regions, so an Indian case study carries straight over to European buyers.
2. **Sell it in Europe to distributors and wholesalers that do NOT run Microsoft Business Central.** Microsoft now ships its own Sales Order Agent inside Business Central. It costs about 0.17 USD per order. You cannot beat that.
3. **Stay away from supplier invoice entry and bank reconciliation.** In 2026 Xero, QuickBooks and Business Central all added AI agents that do this work. E-invoicing laws across Europe are also turning invoices into clean data. That market is closing.
4. **Your price guess is about right for medium firms and too high for small ones.** A first workflow at 1,500 to 4,000 USD is fine. Monthly upkeep of 400 to 1,500 USD is fine for medium firms. For small firms, plan on 300 to 700 USD a month, because a US tool already sells order entry for 399 USD a month.
5. **India will not pay much.** A Tally accountant in Ludhiana costs 15,000 to 25,000 INR a month. Use India for 1 or 2 low price case studies, then move on.

The rest of this report shows the evidence, the rejected ideas, and how to build this so it still works for you in six months.

## Assumptions

1. "Pay for" means pay you, a two person outside team, not buy a SaaS tool.
2. "Europe" means the UK plus EU countries where English sales calls work (Ireland, Netherlands, Belgium, Nordics, Germany at a push).
3. Time and volume figures marked **(estimate)** are my working numbers, not measured facts. I could not find public studies that measure them for SMBs. You should measure them in the first week with every client (method below). Those measured numbers become your case study.
4. My web page fetches were blocked in this research session. So every link below is one I found in search results, and the facts I quote come from those search results. Claims marked **(not verified here)** come from general knowledge. Check them before you put them in a sales deck.

## The system you are selling into

Before looking at single workflows, look at the forces that decide which ones survive. This matters more than any one idea, because you are part time and you cannot afford to rebuild in a year.

**Force 1: Accounting software is eating bookkeeping tasks.**
* Xero's JAX agent now does smart document capture, automatic bank reconciliation, bill screening before payment, and chasing clients for missing documents ([Accounting Today, Xerocon 2026](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/xerocon-2026-jax-ai-improvements-focused-on-automating-unbillable-admin-work); [Xero, Xerocon London](https://www.xero.com/us/media-releases/xero-announces-new-ai-innovations-xerocon-london/)).
* QuickBooks rolled out a team of AI agents to UK customers, including an Accounting Agent for bookkeeping and categorisation ([Intuit press release](https://investors.intuit.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1281/intuits-all-in-one-platform-introduces-a-virtual-team-of-ai-agents-to-help-uk-businesses-increase-efficiency-and-growth)).
* Business Central has a Payables Agent for vendor invoices and a Sales Order Agent for customer orders ([Microsoft Learn, billing](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/administration/tenant-admin-center-manage-consumption-billing)).

**What this means for you:** anything that lives inside the ledger (bills, bank lines, reminders) is becoming a free or cheap built in feature. Work that sits **between** the customer or supplier and the business's operations system is safer. The ledger vendors are slower to go there, except Microsoft.

**Force 2: E-invoicing laws turn invoices into data, but not orders.**
* Belgium: structured B2B e-invoices mandatory since 1 January 2026. Poland (KSeF): large firms February 2026, most others April 2026, micro firms January 2027. France: all firms must receive e-invoices from 1 September 2026, SMEs must send from September 2027 ([Scanman](https://scanman.com/2025/09/23/latest-e-invoicing-mandate-belgium-poland/); [SPS Commerce guide](https://www.spscommerce.com/community/articles/e-invoicing-mandates-in-europe-the-2026-business-guide)).
* Germany: all firms must receive e-invoices since January 2025. Sending is required from 2027 (turnover over 800,000 EUR) and 2028 (everyone else) ([ADVISORI](https://www.advisori.de/en/blog/e-invoicing-mandate-germany); [DiKAS](https://dikas.de/en/blog/e-rechnung-2027-fristen-ausnahmen/)).
* UK: mandatory B2B e-invoicing from 1 April 2029 over Peppol, announced in the November 2025 Budget, with a detailed roadmap expected at the November 2026 Budget ([EDICOM](https://edicomgroup.com/blog/united-kingdom-electronic-invoicing-b2b-b2g); [Tradeshift](https://tradeshift.com/resources/compliance/uk-e-invoicing-mandate-2029/)).

**What this means for you:** "read the supplier's PDF invoice" has a shelf life of 1 to 3 years in most of Europe. Purchase orders, order confirmations, price lists and WhatsApp messages are **not** covered by these laws. They will stay messy. Build on the messy documents that stay messy.

**Force 3: API costs and access change under you.**
* Xero moved developers to paid tiers on 2 March 2026. Starter is free for 5 connections. Core is 35 AUD a month for 50 connections, with metered data egress ([Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/xero-shifts-to-tiered-pricing-model-for-developers); [Truto](https://truto.one/blog/xero-api-pricing-changes-2026-costs-tiers-and-how-to-minimize-egress/); [Xero Developer pricing](https://developer.xero.com/pricing)).
* Sage 50 (UK desktop) has no modern REST API. SData is free but unsupported, and ODBC is read only ([Chift](https://www.chift.eu/tools/sage-50-accounts-uk); [Apideck](https://www.apideck.com/accounting-software/sage-50-uk)).

**What this means for you:** put third party API fees in your contract as a pass through cost. Refuse clients whose core system has no write API, unless a supported import route exists.

**Force 4: Your own capacity is the tightest limit.** Two part time developers can support maybe 6 to 10 live clients **(estimate)** if every client runs the same engine, and maybe 3 if every client is custom. Every new ERP connector you write is something you must fix forever. So the best workflow is one where client number 5 reuses 80% of client number 1.

## How to measure any workflow in week one

Use this with every prospect. It proves the problem, sets the price, and gives you the case study numbers.

1. Count: ask for last month's count (orders, confirmations, price files). Most systems can export this.
2. Time: have the person who does the work log start and stop times for 20 items over 3 days.
3. Errors: ask how many credit notes, wrong deliveries, or re-issued documents came from keying mistakes last quarter.
4. Cost: salary plus about 20 to 30% overhead in the UK/EU **(estimate)**, or plain monthly salary in India.

Value per month = count × minutes saved × cost per minute, plus avoided error costs. Price your monthly fee at 25 to 40% of that value.

## Region 1: India

### I1. WhatsApp and phone orders into Tally or Busy (ACCEPTED, top India pick)

**a. Who has it:** distributors and wholesalers (FMCG, pharma, hardware, auto parts, electrical, textiles), and manufacturers that sell through dealers. Fits **small** (owner run distributor, 10 to 40 staff) and **medium** (regional distributor with a billing team). Ludhiana has plenty of these.

**b. Steps today:**
1. Retailers send orders as WhatsApp text, voice notes, or photos of handwritten lists, often late at night ([Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/); [HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/what-is-whatsapp-order-management)).
2. A billing clerk reads each message, works out which products the retailer means ("10 of the usual soap, 2 box of the blue one"), checks stock and the retailer's rate, and types a sales order or invoice into Tally or Busy.
3. The clerk messages back to confirm or to ask about missing items. Dispatch gets a printout.

**c. Software:** WhatsApp Business app on a phone (input). TallyPrime or Busy (output). TallyPrime can accept vouchers posted as XML over HTTP on port 9000 when the Tally server option is on ([Tally XML Integration help](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/); [community API docs](https://noumaanahamed.github.io/tally-prime-api-docs/)).

**d. Volume and time (estimate):** 50 to 300 orders a day for a mid sized distributor. 3 to 8 minutes per order, longer for photos and voice notes. That is 3 to 30 staff hours a day.

**e. Who does it and cost:** billing clerks and Tally accountants. In Ludhiana these jobs pay about 15,000 to 25,000 INR a month, and data entry roles about 16,000 INR ([Indeed Ludhiana Tally/data entry](https://in.indeed.com/q-accountant-tally,-data-entry-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html); [Jobhai data entry Ludhiana](https://www.jobhai.com/data-entry-jobs-in-ludhiana-sccty); [Glassdoor Ludhiana accountant jobs](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/ludhiana-accountant-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,8_IC2939907_KO9,19.htm)).

**f. Existing tools and the gap:**
* Dealer and distributor apps and WhatsApp catalog bots exist (for example [Nexxio Dealer Connect](https://nexxio.in/dealer-connect.html); vendors listed by [HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)). The gap: they ask the **retailer** to change how they order (use an app, tap a menu). Many retailers will not. That breaks your rule 7.
* Tally WhatsApp plugins, such as the free Fast2SMS plugin, send invoices, confirmations and reminders **out** of Tally ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)). They do not read a messy incoming order and turn it into a voucher.
* Your gap: accept orders exactly as retailers already send them, match them to the item master and party rates, and put a draft order in Tally for the clerk to approve.

**g. Realistic price:** 25,000 to 60,000 INR setup, 3,000 to 8,000 INR a month **(estimate)**. The anchor is salary: if you save one clerk (15,000 to 25,000 INR), the owner will not pay more than a third of that. The stronger sales argument is speed (same day dispatch) and fewer wrong items, not headcount.

**h. Sources:** listed inline above.

**Traps to plan for:**
1. To read WhatsApp messages by software, the distributor must connect the number to the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API). Meta has offered a "coexistence" mode that lets a business keep using the phone app on the same number **(not verified here)**. Confirm this for each client before you quote.
2. Tally usually runs on a PC in the office. You need a small connector on that PC or on the local network to reach port 9000. Budget time for this and for power cuts and PCs that are switched off.
3. Item masters in Tally are often messy (duplicates, odd names). Cleaning them is part of the setup, and you should charge for it.

### I2. Export shipment document pack (ACCEPTED, conditional, local to Ludhiana)

**a. Who has it:** small and medium exporters (hosiery and knitwear, bicycle parts, hand tools, auto parts). Fits **both** sizes. Ludhiana is full of them.

**b. Steps today:** the buyer's purchase order and the factory's packing data (usually Excel) arrive. A documentation executive builds the commercial invoice, packing list, and shipping bill details for the customs agent (CHA), and checks that quantities, weights, values and HS codes match across every document. Job ads in Ludhiana describe exactly this work: preparing invoice, packing list, shipping bill and bill of lading, and coordinating with forwarders and CHAs ([ABP Empower job listing](https://www.abpempower.in/job-openings-for-export-import-documentation-executive-ludhiana-1299973.htm); [PlacementIndia listing](https://www.placementindia.com/job-detail/export-documentation-executive-in-penguin-consultants-at-ludhiana-1413013.htm)).

**c. Software:** Excel and email in, Tally (export invoice) plus PDFs for the CHA out.

**d. Volume and time (estimate):** 5 to 40 shipments a month, 1 to 3 hours each. A mismatch between documents can hold cargo at customs, which costs far more than the staff time.

**e. Who does it and cost:** export documentation executives. One Ludhiana listing pays 30,000 to 35,000 INR a month, others 50,000 to 60,000 INR ([ABP Empower](https://www.abpempower.in/job-openings-for-export-import-documentation-executive-ludhiana-1299973.htm); [Indeed export jobs Ludhiana](https://in.indeed.com/q-export-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)).

**f. Existing tools and the gap:** Tally makes export invoices, and export ERPs exist. The gap is the cross check: "does the invoice match the packing list match the PO match what the CHA filed?" This is where costly errors happen.

**g. Realistic price:** 40,000 to 80,000 INR setup, 5,000 to 12,000 INR a month **(estimate)**.

**Honest verdict:** the evidence is job ads, not studies. It is a real local problem, but it transfers poorly to Europe (UK customs automation is already a crowded software market, see rejected list). Do this only if a warm contact in Ludhiana asks for it and pays.

### I3. Matching non UPI receipts (NEFT, RTGS, cheque) to invoices in Tally (WEAK, only as an add on to I1)

Distributors with many retailers struggle to match payments to open bills and post receipts ([Takkada, ledger reconciliation](https://takkada.com/blog/ledger-reconciliation-tally-distributor/)). But TallyPrime now auto links UPI QR payments to invoices ([Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/business-guides/upi-qr-payments-auto-link-receipts-tallyprime/)), and payment link tools auto post receipts ([Takkada](https://takkada.com/blog/payment-link-tally-integration/)). What is left (NEFT and cheques with no reference) is too small to sell alone. Offer it to an I1 client as an extra.

## Region 2: Europe (including the UK)

### E1. Customer order email or PDF into the ERP as a sales order (ACCEPTED, top pick overall)

**a. Who has it:** wholesalers and distributors (building supplies, electrical, food service, industrial and MRO parts, packaging, beauty and pet supplies), and small manufacturers that sell to trade customers. Fits **small** (owner decides, 10 to 49 staff) and **medium** (a customer service or operations manager owns it).

**b. Steps today:**
1. Customers email orders as PDFs, Excel sheets, or plain text ("30 of the usual plus 2 cases of the white ones").
2. A sales order processor opens each one, finds the matching SKUs (customers often use their own codes), checks prices against the customer's price agreement, checks stock, and types every line into the ERP ([StackCube 2026 guide](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors); [Tiraverse](https://tiraverse.co.uk/blog/true-cost-manual-order-processing-wholesale-distribution/)).
3. They email a confirmation or a question back.

One vendor estimate: EDI covers only about 20% of trading partners. The other 80% send unstructured documents that are keyed by hand ([StackCube](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors)). Treat this as a vendor claim, not a study.

**c. Software:** Outlook or Gmail in. Out: Sage 200, Sage 50, Xero or QuickBooks with an inventory add on, Exact Online (strong in NL and BE), Odoo, Unleashed, Cin7, Business Central.

| System | Write API? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Business Central | Yes, but Microsoft's own Sales Order Agent reads order emails and PDFs and creates quotes and orders ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent)) | **Avoid** for this workflow |
| Sage 200 | Yes (not verified here); partners already sell Sage 200 order automation ([Zestcode](https://zestcode.co.uk/services/sage-200-order-automation/)) | Good target |
| Sage 50 (UK) | No modern REST API; SData unsupported; CSV import cannot be undone ([Chift](https://www.chift.eu/tools/sage-50-accounts-uk)) | Only via a unified API vendor, or avoid |
| Xero (+ inventory add on) | Yes; paid tiers since March 2026 ([Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/xero-shifts-to-tiered-pricing-model-for-developers)) | Good, pass API cost through |
| Exact Online, Odoo, Unleashed, Cin7, QuickBooks Online | Yes, public REST or RPC APIs (not verified here) | Good targets |

**d. Volume and time (estimate):** 30 to 150 emailed orders a day for a small distributor. 3 to 10 minutes each depending on line count. That is 2 to 25 staff hours a day. One UK case claims document handling fell from 17 hours to under 1 at a national distributor ([Tiraverse](https://tiraverse.co.uk/blog/true-cost-manual-order-processing-wholesale-distribution/)).

**e. Who does it and cost:** sales order processors and customer service staff. UK pay is about 21,000 GBP (Jobsite and PayScale style figures) up to about 26,800 GBP (Indeed average) a year before employer costs ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries); [PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/UK/Job=Order_Processor/Salary); [Jobsite live vacancies](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor)). Job ads list "processing orders via email" and keeping Sage 200 sales orders accurate as core duties ([The Recruitment Co](https://therecruitmentco.uk/job/sales-order-processing-with-sage/)). This is proof that firms pay salaries for exactly this task today.

**f. Existing tools and the gap:**
* Enterprise: Conexiom deployments run about 10,000 to 100,000+ USD a year with per trading partner fees ([OrderDrafter comparison](https://orderdrafter.com/alternatives/conexiom-alternative/); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/205134/Conexiom/)). Too expensive for SMBs.
* SMB SaaS: OrderDrafter charges 399 USD a month (about 300 orders) and 799 USD a month (about 1,000 orders), aimed at 5 to 50 person distributors, but built around the US ERP Epicor Prophet 21 ([OrderDrafter](https://orderdrafter.com/)). Canals and Proton also sell AI order entry to distributors ([Canals](https://www.canals.ai/products/distributor-order-entry); [Proton](https://www.proton.ai/order-and-quote-entry)).
* Built in: Business Central's Sales Order Agent. By Microsoft's own example it uses about 16.5 Copilot Credits per request ([Microsoft Learn billing](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/administration/tenant-admin-center-manage-consumption-billing)), and Microsoft's own example uses a pay as you go rate of 0.01 USD per credit ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/cost-management-billing/reservations/copilot-credit-p3)). That is about 0.17 USD per order.
* **The gap:** UK and EU SMBs on Sage 200, Exact, Odoo, Unleashed, Cin7 or Xero based stacks, where the US SaaS tools do not connect well, and where the business wants someone to set up SKU mapping, customer price rules and exception rules for them. Your advantage is "done for you and fitted to your ERP," not the AI itself.

**g. Realistic price:**
* Small: 2,000 to 3,500 USD setup, 300 to 700 USD a month.
* Medium: 3,500 to 6,000 USD setup, 700 to 1,500 USD a month.
* Why: a saved half of one order processor is worth roughly 1,000 to 1,500 USD a month in the UK **(estimate from the salaries above)**. And the SaaS ceiling is 399 to 799 USD a month. You can charge above SaaS only if you connect to an ERP they cannot, or you handle their messy customers better.

### E2. Supplier order confirmation vs purchase order check (ACCEPTED, medium firms, best second product)

**a. Who has it:** manufacturers and distributors that raise many purchase orders. Mostly **medium**. A purchasing or supply chain lead owns it.

**b. Steps today:** the business sends a PO. The supplier replies with an order confirmation (PDF or email) that may change the price, quantity or delivery date. A buyer compares it line by line with the PO, updates the expected date in the ERP, and chases suppliers who never confirmed. Missed date changes cause stock outs and production delays ([DocBits](https://docbits.com/en/order-confirmation-automation/); [Nanonets case](https://nanonets.com/blog/supplier-order-management-automation/)).

**c. Software:** email in; Sage 200, Exact, Odoo, Business Central or similar out.

**d. Volume and time (estimate):** 200 to 2,000 POs a month, 3 to 10 minutes per confirmation, plus chasing.

**e. Who does it and cost:** buyers and purchasing administrators (UK salary data not gathered here). Vendors claim 70+ hours a week saved at mid sized firms ([DocBits](https://docbits.com/en/best-purchase-order-automation-software-2026/)). Treat as a vendor claim.

**f. Existing tools and the gap:** DocBits, Doxis and Zimply exist, but target mid to large firms. Zimply names 2,500 to 5,000 PO pages a month as its fit ([Zimply](https://zimply.ai/en/zimply-purchase-order-matching-ai-assistant/); [Doxis](https://www.doxis.com/en/solutions/order-confirmation-automation)). Below that volume there is a gap. E-invoicing laws do not cover order confirmations, so this stays messy.

**g. Realistic price:** 3,000 to 5,000 USD setup, 600 to 1,200 USD a month **(estimate)**.

**Why it is the best second product:** it uses the same engine as E1 (read document, match to SKUs and an open document, flag differences, human approves) and the same ERP connector, just in the other direction. You sell it to an E1 client you already have.

### E3. Supplier price list updates (ACCEPTED with weak evidence, add on)

**a. Who has it:** wholesalers, retailers and web shops with 20 to 200 suppliers. **Both** sizes.

**b. Steps today:** suppliers email new price lists (Excel or PDF, each in its own layout) once to four times a year. Someone maps them to internal SKUs, updates cost prices, checks margins, and updates selling prices in the ERP or web shop ([ERPAG](https://www.erpag.com/news/automating-supplier-price-list-updates); [Duvo](https://blog.duvo.ai/how-to-automate-supplier-price-list-updates-sap-spreadsheets)).

**c. Software:** email with Excel or PDF in; the ERP (Sage 200, Exact, Odoo, Unleashed) and often Shopify or WooCommerce out. That is 2 to 3 tools, so it still qualifies.

**d. Volume and time:** a vendor claims 20 to 30 hours a week for retail procurement teams ([Duvo](https://blog.duvo.ai/how-to-automate-supplier-price-list-updates-sap-spreadsheets)). This is marketing, not a study. The work is lumpy: heavy in January and at supplier price rises.

**e. Who does it and cost:** a purchasing administrator or the owner, in bursts. In small firms it is often the owner **(estimate)**, so updates get done late and margins leak until they are.

**f. Existing tools and the gap:** Arovon, PriceSoft and ERPAG's own feature cover parts of it ([Arovon](https://arovon.com/price-list-automation); [PriceSoft](https://pricesoft.com/)).

**g. Realistic price:** sell it per batch or as an add on to E1, around 150 to 400 USD a month **(estimate)**. Too lumpy to carry a retainer alone.

## Final top picks

### Pick 1: Order intake (I1 in India, then E1 in Europe)

**Why:**
1. Proven spending: firms hire people with this exact job title in the UK, and job ads name the task.
2. Clear owner, clear start and end, 2 tools (email or WhatsApp plus the ERP), countable per order.
3. Already half digital. Nobody has to change how customers order.
4. Long shelf life. E-invoicing laws do not touch orders. Only Microsoft has built this into the ledger, and you avoid Microsoft shops.
5. One engine serves both regions.

**Plan:**
1. Months 1 to 2: build the engine and do one Indian distributor on Tally at a low price, in exchange for a named case study and measured numbers.
2. Months 2 to 4: pick **two** European target systems only (I suggest Sage 200 for the UK and Exact Online or Odoo for NL and BE). Get 2 paying clients.
3. Month 4 onward: offer E2 (order confirmations) to those clients as the second workflow.

### Pick 2: Supplier order confirmation check (E2), as the upsell

Same engine, same connector, same buyer's company. Higher price because medium firms own it.

### Not a pick: anything around supplier invoices, bank lines or reminders

Even if a prospect asks. It is being given away free inside Xero, QuickBooks and Business Central, and e-invoicing will shrink it further. If you take that work, you sign up to maintain something that will be worth zero within two years.

## Building it so it holds up in six months

These choices decide whether client number 8 is easy or kills you.

1. **One engine, many adapters.** Split the system into four parts that do not know about each other:
   * Inputs: email inbox, WhatsApp webhook, later Peppol order messages.
   * Reader: turns any document into one standard "order draft" format (customer, lines, quantities, prices, dates).
   * Matcher and rules: match customer SKUs to yours, check price agreements and stock, score confidence.
   * Writers: one per ERP (Tally XML, Sage 200, Exact, Odoo, Xero).
   A new client should need only configuration and, at most, one new writer. Developer A's Spring Boot, PostgreSQL and Spring AI stack fits this well. Keep n8n for glue and alerts, not for core logic, because large n8n flows get hard to test and hand over.
2. **The approval screen is the product.** Show the original message beside the draft order, highlight low confidence lines, and learn from each correction (save the approved SKU mapping per customer). Over time, auto approval rises and the client sees it in a monthly report.
3. **Measure everything from day one.** Log each order: received time, draft time, approved time, lines changed by the human. This gives you the case study, the renewal argument, and early warning when accuracy drops.
4. **Keep the AI model swappable.** Call it through one interface so you can change model or provider when prices change. Pass LLM and API costs through to the client at cost plus a margin, written into the contract.
5. **Data protection for European clients.** Host client data in an EU or UK cloud region. You will still be accessing it from India, which counts as a transfer. India has no EU adequacy decision, so you will need a data processing agreement with Standard Contractual Clauses (and the UK addendum or IDTA for UK clients) **(not verified here; check with a GDPR adviser before your first contract)**. Buyers will ask. Have the documents ready.
6. **Fit your 50 USD budget.** One small cloud server, PostgreSQL on the same box at first, and the client pays their own API and model usage. Do not pay for per client SaaS seats yourself.
7. **Write the upkeep scope down.** Monthly fee covers: monitoring, fixing breakages from ERP or API changes, adding up to N new customer formats a month, and a monthly report. Anything else is extra. Without this, "upkeep" becomes unlimited free work.
8. **Time zones help you.** UK office hours (9:00 to 17:30 BST) are 13:30 to 22:00 in Ludhiana. You can cover the UK working day part time.

## Checklist before you say yes to a client

1. Does the process run at least 20 times a week? If not, the fee will not stick.
2. Does one named person own it and feel the pain?
3. Does the target system have a supported write API (not Sage 50 desktop, not Business Central for order intake)?
4. Is the data already digital (email, PDF, Excel, WhatsApp)?
5. Will the client let you measure time per item for a week?
6. Can a human approve exceptions in under a minute each?

If any answer is no, walk away or fix it first.
