# Workflow automations SMEs will pay for: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Build one thing first: **customer orders that arrive as messages get turned into draft sales orders in the ERP or accounting system, and a person clicks approve.**

* In Europe (start with the UK), that is emailed purchase orders (PDF, Excel, or typed in the email) turned into draft sales orders in Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central or Sage 200.
* In India, it is the same job with WhatsApp orders (text, photos of handwritten lists) turned into sales orders in TallyPrime. Do this for one Ludhiana distributor as your case study.

It is the only idea in this research that passes every one of your seven rules, has clear proof that companies pay for it today, and is not already solved cheaply for the systems small firms use. Your second option is remittance advice to cash allocation for medium European suppliers. Most other ideas I checked are already covered by cheap tools, so I rejected them. The list is below so you do not waste time on them.

Two hard truths up front:

1. Your European price guess is right for the build fee but **too high for monthly upkeep at small firms.** Ready made order entry tools already sell for about €349 to $799 a month. A 20 person wholesaler will not pay you $1,000 a month for the same job. See the pricing section.
2. Your real problem is not the build. It is getting the first European client from Ludhiana with no case study. The plan at the end deals with that.

## Assumptions

1. **Exchange rates** (rough working rates, check before you quote): £1 ≈ $1.33, €1 ≈ $1.16, $1 ≈ ₹88. All prices exclude VAT or GST.
2. **"A few weeks of part time work"** means 3 to 6 calendar weeks with both of you putting in about 20 hours a week combined.
3. **"Europe" includes the UK.** I recommend the UK as your first European market because it is English speaking, it does not force structured e-invoicing until April 2029, and Sage and Business Central are common there.
4. **How I researched.** I used web search. Direct page fetching was blocked in my research setup, so every link below is one that search returned to me, and the facts I quote from it are what the search showed. I also used Microsoft Learn and GitHub search directly. Where I had no source (mainly Indian salaries and some time per task figures), I say **"estimate"** and tell you how to check it in under an hour. Vendor numbers are vendor claims, not independent studies.
5. **Time per task numbers** are worked out from one sourced figure (about 24 to 36 seconds to key one line item, see Workflow E1) plus my estimate for reading and checking. You must measure the real number at each client in week one. Your pitch depends on it.

## Part 1. One real example, worked end to end

I solved one concrete case first, then only kept ideas that behaved like it.

### Worked example: a UK plumbing and heating wholesaler

**The business.** 30 staff, one branch, sells to installers and small merchants. Runs Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central. One order processor, plus sales staff who help at busy times.

**What arrives.** About 50 customer orders a day by email. Some are PDF purchase orders from the customer's own system. Some are Excel sheets. Many are typed in the email body ("2 x 15mm copper elbow, 10 x 22mm..."). The customer uses their own part codes or plain descriptions, not the wholesaler's codes.

**What the person does today.**
1. Opens the email and the attachment.
2. Finds the customer account in Business Central.
3. For each line, works out which of the wholesaler's items it means, types code and quantity, checks the price and stock.
4. Saves the order and emails the customer a confirmation.

**The count.**
* Keying line items: a customs software vendor states that keying a 50 line invoice by hand takes 20 to 30 minutes (iCustoms blog: [document automation workflow](https://www.icustoms.ai/blogs/logistics-customs-document-automation-workflow/), [C88 auto fill](https://www.icustoms.ai/blogs/c88-form-automation/)). It is a vendor claim, so treat it as a rough guide. That is about 24 to 36 seconds a line.
* An 8 line order is then about 3 to 5 minutes of typing, plus about 2 minutes to read, find the customer and confirm (estimate). Call it **6 minutes an order**.
* 50 orders × 6 minutes = **5 hours a day**, about 1,250 hours a year.

**The cost.**
* Average UK order processor pay is £26,832 a year ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)). With employer National Insurance and pension, call it about £31,500 (estimate).
* At roughly 1,650 working hours a year, that is about **£19 an hour**, so the typing costs about **£23,700 a year**.
* If the person only reviews a prepared draft (about 1.5 minutes an order), they save about 3.75 hours a day, worth about **£17,800 a year**.

**What we would charge.** £2,400 build plus £350 a month upkeep. First year cost £6,600. Payback on the build is about 2 months.

**What this example taught me (the rules I then used on every other idea):**
1. **The saving is time, not a head.** Nobody gets fired at 5 hours a day. The owner buys it for other reasons: same day dispatch before the courier cut off, fewer wrong items sent, and no backlog when the order person is off sick. Sell those, with the hours as proof.
2. **The hard part is item matching, not reading the PDF.** Any AI model can read the PDF. The value is mapping "22mm elbow" to item code 10457 for this customer, and learning from each correction. Tools that only extract text are not a threat. Tools that do the matching are.
3. **The ERP decides if the job is buildable.** Business Central has a clean REST API that creates sales orders ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/api/dynamics_salesorder_create)). Sage 50 has no native REST API ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-integration/does-sage-50-have-an-api-yes-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)). The same workflow is a 4 week job on one and a painful one on the other.
4. **The monthly price is capped by SaaS.** If a ready tool exists at $400 a month for the client's ERP, you cannot charge $1,000. You win where no ready tool supports the client's ERP or rules.
5. **Structured e-invoicing kills supplier invoice ideas but not order ideas.** Mandates cover invoices, not customer purchase orders, so orders stay messy.

Every workflow below is checked against these five lessons and your seven rules.

## Part 2. India

**Honest framing.** India is your case study market. The labour saving at Indian salaries is small, so the prices here are low. Treat paid Indian work as a bonus.

### Workflow I1 (recommended): WhatsApp orders to TallyPrime sales orders

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors that sell to retailers: hosiery and garments, auto parts, bicycle parts, hardware, FMCG sub distributors, pharma stockists. All of these are common in Ludhiana. Fits **both**: small (owner decides) and medium (sales or accounts head owns it, often with 2 to 4 billing clerks).

**b. Steps today.**
1. Retailers or the firm's own salesmen send orders on WhatsApp: typed lists, photos of a handwritten list, sometimes voice notes, in Hindi, Punjabi or English.
2. A billing clerk reads each chat, finds the party ledger in Tally, and types each item and quantity into a sales order or sales invoice. Stock is checked.
3. The clerk replies on WhatsApp with a confirmation or "item not in stock".
4. Data ends up in TallyPrime (sales order, then invoice and e-way bill).

**c. Software.** In: WhatsApp Business (app today; the WhatsApp Business Platform Cloud API for automation). Out: TallyPrime (sometimes Busy or Marg). Middle: our review screen. That is 2 tools plus our screen.

**d. Frequency and time.** Estimate for a 15 to 25 person distributor: 50 to 100 orders a day, 4 to 6 lines each, about 4 minutes each. At 70 orders that is about 4.7 hours a day. Peaks are much higher in season (Diwali, and October to January for hosiery).

**e. Who does it and cost.** A billing or data entry clerk. Salary estimate: ₹15,000 to ₹20,000 a month in Ludhiana (unverified; check Naukri and Indeed India for "billing executive Tally Ludhiana"). At ₹18,000 a month that is about ₹87 an hour, so the order typing costs about ₹10,600 a month. Automation would save about ₹7,500 to ₹8,000 a month.

**Brutal point:** that saving alone does not justify much. It beats "hire another clerk" only when (1) volume spikes in season and extra clerks make mistakes, (2) wrong items cost returns and angry retailers, or (3) the owner wants orders billed and dispatched the same day. Pitch those.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* Order booking apps for Tally and sales force apps exist (examples I know of: Biz Analyst style Tally apps, Bizom, FieldAssist; I did not check their current prices in this research). They all need the retailer or salesman to **use a new app**. Your rule 7 says we avoid that. Retailers will keep sending WhatsApp, so the gap is real: turn the WhatsApp message they already send into a Tally draft.
* TallyPrime has built in WhatsApp sending of invoices, but that does not read incoming orders (I did not re-verify Tally's feature list in this research).
* Tally integration is proven. TallyPrime runs an HTTP XML server (usually port 9000), and open source code imports sales orders and creates sales invoices through it ([sales order import example](https://github.com/SatyamTechsolvo/tallyerp9import), [Node.js REST to TallyPrime](https://github.com/JeevanKN/Tally_Integration_with-EC2-Virtual-Window-RDP-), [TallyPrime SDK](https://github.com/labs-infinitum/tallyprime-sdk), [a scan to Tally connector](https://github.com/usmansharjeel619/scan-to-tally)).
* **Technical catch:** Tally usually sits on a Windows PC in the shop, not in the cloud. You need a small local agent on that PC that pulls approved orders from your server and posts XML to localhost:9000. Developer A can write this in Java in a few days.
* **WhatsApp catch:** reading messages automatically needs the order number on the WhatsApp Business Platform. Check Meta's current rules before you promise anything: whether the client can keep using the normal WhatsApp Business app on the same number, and the per message charges. My understanding is that replies inside the 24 hour customer service window are free, but I did not verify this in this research. The simplest safe option: the client gets a separate "orders" number on the API.

**g. Realistic price (INR, estimate).**
* Small (5 to 49 staff): ₹30,000 to ₹60,000 setup, ₹4,000 to ₹8,000 a month.
* Medium (50 to 250 staff, 2 or more clerks): ₹75,000 to ₹1,50,000 setup, ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 a month.
* For your first client, do it at cost or free in return for real numbers, a named case study and a video testimonial. That is worth more to you than the fee.

**h. Sources.** Tally XML integration code linked above. Indian salary and volume figures are estimates; check them with the client in week one.

### Workflow I2 (second, validate first): payment screenshots to Tally receipts

**a. Who has it.** The same distributors. Retailers pay by UPI, NEFT or cheque and send a screenshot or message ("sent 25,000 for bills 1142 and 1150"). Both small and medium.

**b. Steps today.** The accountant matches the bank credit to the party, creates a receipt voucher in Tally, and allocates it against the right bills. Short payments are chased on WhatsApp.

**c. Software.** WhatsApp plus bank statement in, TallyPrime out.

**d. Frequency and time.** Estimate: 30 to 80 receipts a day for a mid sized distributor, 2 to 4 minutes each.

**e. Who and cost.** Accounts clerk, same salary band as I1 (estimate).

**f. Existing tools and gap.** AI tools already turn documents into Tally vouchers: Vyapar TaxOne (formerly Suvit) says its AI "will extract data from images, create accurate vouchers and push them directly to Tally" ([Vyapar TaxOne](https://taxone.vyapar.com/gst-reconciliation-feature)). The possible gap is the WhatsApp side (reading the retailer's message and bill numbers) and bill wise allocation. **Before you build this, test TaxOne yourself.** If it already handles bill wise receipts from images, drop this idea.

**g. Price.** Sell it as an add on to I1: ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 setup, ₹2,000 to ₹5,000 a month (estimate).

**h. Sources.** Vyapar TaxOne link above. Rest is estimate.

### E-invoicing changes the map (important)

* Germany: all businesses must be able to receive structured e-invoices since 1 January 2025; issuing becomes mandatory in 2027 and 2028 ([ecosio](https://ecosio.com/en/blog/germany-e-invoicing-explained/)).
* France: all companies must be able to receive e-invoices from 1 September 2026; SMEs must issue from September 2027 ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/europe/2025/09/france-e-invoicing-e-reporting-mandate-2026-2027.html)).
* UK: mandatory B2B e-invoicing from April 2029 ([ICAS](https://www.icas.com/news-insights-events/news/tax/autumn-budget-2025-e-invoicing-will-go-ahead-from-2029)).

**What this means for you:** supplier invoices arriving as PDFs will shrink, first in Germany and France. Do not build a business on reading supplier invoice PDFs. Customer purchase orders and remittance advices are not covered by these mandates, so those stay messy. The UK has the longest window of messy invoices, but invoice capture there is already a crowded, cheap market anyway (see rejected list).

### Workflow E1 (top pick): emailed customer POs to draft sales orders in the ERP

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors (plumbing and heating, electrical, building supplies, industrial and MRO parts, packaging, food service, fasteners) and manufacturers that sell to trade customers. **Both** sizes. Small: owner or sales manager decides. Medium: operations or customer service manager owns it.

**b. Steps today.** Order arrives by email as PDF, Excel or typed text. A sales order processor finds the customer, maps each line to an item code, checks price and stock, keys the order into the ERP, and sends a confirmation. Job ads describe exactly this: "check, record, track, and validate customer orders in the system (ERP SAGE)" ([Totaljobs](https://www.totaljobs.com/jobs/sales-order-processor)).

**c. Software.** In: Outlook or Gmail. Out: Business Central or Sage 200 first (good APIs). Middle: our review screen. Sage 50 only if the client accepts a CSV import step.

**d. Frequency and time.** 20 to 300 orders a day depending on size. About 6 minutes for an 8 line order (worked example). A 50 order day is about 5 hours of keying.

**e. Who and cost.** Sales order processors, customer service staff, sometimes sales reps. Average UK order processor pay £26,832 a year ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)). Totaljobs listed 754 sales order processor jobs when searched ([Totaljobs](https://www.totaljobs.com/jobs/sales-order-processor)). This is real, paid, recurring work.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** This market is active, which proves demand, but it leaves gaps for small firms:

| Tool | Price seen | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conexiom | About $10k to $100k+ a year, with per trading partner fees and multi week setup (figure from competitor comparison pages) | Enterprise, too expensive for SMEs ([OrderPier comparison](https://orderpier.com/compare/conexiom-alternative), [Ordermatic comparison](https://ordermatic.co/blog/conexiom-alternatives)) |
| OrderDrafter | $399 a month (about 300 orders), $799 a month (about 1,000 orders) | Built for Epicor Prophet 21, a US ERP ([OrderDrafter](https://orderdrafter.com/)) |
| OrderPilot | From €349 a month (Starter) | ERP coverage not checked in this research ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10041707/OrderPilot/)) |
| OrderPier | Self serve, has a free plan | Business Central, QuickBooks, or a webhook ([OrderPier](https://orderpier.com/)) |

**The honest gap:**
* Business Central is now covered by at least one self serve tool (OrderPier). So for a plain BC client, you compete on service: you set it up, you tune the item matching to their catalogue, you add their rules (minimum order, price checks, credit hold warnings, delivery cut off), and you answer the phone. Some owners pay for that; some will just use the tool. **Before every sales call, check whether OrderPier or OrderPilot supports that client's ERP.**
* Your clearest opening is ERPs and setups these tools do not cover well: Sage 200, Sage 50 with import, and firms that need messy text orders matched to their own item codes with custom checks.

**g. Realistic price (Europe).**
* Small: $1,500 to $3,000 build, **$250 to $500 a month**.
* Medium: $3,000 to $6,000 build, $500 to $1,200 a month (higher volume, more rules, more customers' formats).
* Why: SaaS for the same job sits at €349 to $799 a month including software. Your upkeep must be at or below that for small firms. Medium firms pay more for custom rules and a named person.

**h. Sources.** All linked above in this section and in the worked example.

**Build outline (3 to 5 weeks part time).**
1. Mail hook: Microsoft Graph subscription (or Gmail push) on the orders inbox.
2. Spring Boot service (Developer A): pull the attachment, call an LLM with vision to extract customer, lines and dates as JSON.
3. Matching: PostgreSQL table of item master plus a per customer alias table ("22mm elbow" means item 10457 for customer X). Every human correction saves a new alias.
4. Review screen (Developer B, TypeScript): email on the left, draft order on the right, low confidence fields in yellow, one Approve button.
5. On approve: create the sales order via the ERP API; send the confirmation email.
6. Log counts: orders in, auto matched lines, corrections, minutes spent. This log is your case study and your renewal argument.

### Workflow E2 (second pick): remittance advice to cash allocation

**a. Who has it.** Suppliers to big retailers and national accounts: food and drink producers, consumer goods wholesalers, building product suppliers. Mostly **medium**; small only if they sell to big chains.

**b. Steps today.** A big customer pays one lump sum and sends a long remittance (PDF, email, or portal download) listing many invoices, with short payments and deductions. A credit controller matches each line to open invoices in the ERP, posts the allocation, and raises queries on deductions. Example from a vendor: a food wholesaler that supplies a national grocer "may receive a payment once a fortnight with a 12 page remittance covering 180 odd invoices, with some short paid on delivery shortages and offsets by promotional rebates" ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/remittance-automation-software)).

**c. Software.** In: email or retailer portal plus bank feed. Out: Business Central or Sage 200. Middle: our review screen. We only post ledger allocations for a person to approve; we never move money.

**d. Frequency and time.** Estimate: per big customer weekly or fortnightly; 1 to 3 hours per large remittance, plus query chasing. Measure at the client.

**e. Who and cost.** Credit controller or accounts assistant. I did not get a sourced UK salary for this role in this research; treat it as similar to the order processor band and check job ads.

**f. Existing tools and gap.** RemitClear sells cash application automation for Xero users ([RemitClear for Xero](https://remitclear.com/solutions/automated-cash-application-xero)). **So reject this for Xero clients.** The gap is medium firms on Business Central or Sage 200 who are too small for enterprise cash application suites. I did not find a cheap tool for that combination, but my search was limited, so check again before you sell.

**g. Price.** $2,500 to $5,000 build, $400 to $800 a month (estimate). Medium buyers only.

**h. Sources.** RemitClear links above.

### Workflow E3 (third, weak): supplier price lists into ERP and webshop

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors with many suppliers, often with a B2B webshop. Both sizes.

**b. Steps today.** A supplier emails a new price list in Excel or PDF with its own columns. A buyer maps it, recalculates margins, updates cost and sell prices in the ERP and the webshop. A vendor claims 200 changed products take 2 to 3 business days by hand ([Duvo](https://blog.duvo.ai/how-to-automate-supplier-price-list-updates-sap-spreadsheets)).

**c. Software.** Email in; ERP plus Shopify or WooCommerce out.

**d. Frequency and time.** Each supplier changes prices a few times a year, but a firm with 100 suppliers sees updates most weeks (estimate).

**e. Who and cost.** Buyer or purchasing assistant; unsourced.

**f. Existing tools and gap.** Several tools exist already: [Arovon](https://arovon.com/price-list-automation), [TableFlow](https://tableflow.com/industries/wholesale), and ERPAG's own feature ([ERPAG](https://www.erpag.com/news/automating-supplier-price-list-updates)). The gap is only the client specific margin rules and posting to their exact systems.

**g. Price.** $1,500 to $3,000 build, $200 to $400 a month (estimate).

**h. Verdict.** Lower value because it happens less often per supplier, and tools exist. Offer it only as a second workflow to an E1 client.

## Part 5. Final top picks

1. **Build E1 (emailed POs to Business Central or Sage 200 draft orders) as your European product.**
   Why: passes all seven rules; the job is advertised in hundreds of UK job ads; firms already pay SaaS €349 to $799 a month for it, so the budget exists; Business Central has a clean API; orders are not affected by e-invoicing mandates; it plays to Developer A (Spring Boot, Postgres, Spring AI) and Developer B (review screen in TypeScript).
   Risk: self serve tools are moving in. Your edge is setup, item matching for each client, custom checks, and support. Check the client's ERP against OrderPier and OrderPilot before each pitch.

2. **Build I1 (WhatsApp orders to TallyPrime) first, for one Ludhiana distributor, as the case study.**
   Why: same engine, costs you almost nothing, local and in person, and gives you real before and after numbers in 4 to 6 weeks. Do not expect it to pay well.

3. **Keep E2 (remittance to cash allocation, Business Central or Sage 200) as your second product** for medium clients once E1 is live.

4. **Do not build** supplier invoice capture, statement reconciliation, PO matching in Xero, freight quoting, customs entries, timesheets, or GST 2B tools. All are crowded or cheap already.
