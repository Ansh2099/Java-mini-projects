# Workflows to Build and Sell: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Build one thing well: **an "order reader" that turns messy customer orders into draft sales orders in the client's accounting or ERP system, with a person approving each one.**

* In **India**, sell it to distributors whose retailers send orders on WhatsApp, and to manufacturers who get purchase orders by email. Busy, Tally and Marg are on the other side.
* In **Europe and the UK**, sell it to wholesalers, distributors and small manufacturers whose customers email orders as PDFs, Excel files or plain text. Sage 200, Xero with Unleashed or Cin7, and Odoo are on the other side.
* Your second product, for medium firms in Europe only, is **checking supplier order confirmations against your own purchase orders**. It uses the same reading engine, pointed the other way.

Things we checked and you should **not** build: supplier invoice entry, GST reconciliation, and payment reminders. Cheap tools or free built in features already do these. The biggest surprise: **Microsoft Business Central now has its own Sales Order Agent** at about 17 US cents per order. So skip Business Central shops for order entry.

Your price guess is close for small firms but a bit high on upkeep. See the pricing section.

## Assumptions

1. "Workflow we can sell" means one of your own rules is met on all 7 points. I dropped anything that failed even one.
2. "Europe" means the UK plus the EU. I lean toward the UK first because it speaks English, uses Sage and Xero a lot, and has a 4.5 to 5.5 hour time gap with Ludhiana. That gap is easy to work with.
3. When I give volumes or times with no source, I label them **(estimate)**. Check every estimate in your first client call with a stopwatch.
4. My research tools could open only some pages. Many facts below come from search results that listed those pages. **Check any price again before you quote it to a client.** Prices change fast in this market.
5. Phone orders do not count. They are fully manual today, and your rule 7 excludes them. We only automate orders that already arrive as text, files or images.

## How I judged each idea: copy the post office

The US Postal Service had this exact problem decades ago, and solved it.

**How they did it.** Letters arrive with messy addresses. A machine reads most of them. The ones it cannot read get photographed and sent to a Remote Encoding Center, where a person looks at the image and types the address. The typed result is checked against the real address list, and then a barcode is printed so the letter flows on by machine. As reading software got better, it could handle up to 95% of the handwritten addresses that used to need people. Today one center in Salt Lake City does what 55 centers used to do. ([USPS Facts](https://facts.usps.com/remote-encoding-center-rec-deciphering-handwriting/), [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/when-usps-cant-decipher-bad-handwriting-address-utah-facility-comes-to-rescue/))

That is your business in one picture:

| Post office | Your workflow |
|---|---|
| Messy letter arrives | Messy order, confirmation or remittance arrives by email or WhatsApp |
| Machine reads the address | AI reads the document |
| Checked against the national address list | Checked against the client's item list, price list, open orders or open invoices |
| Humans key the ones the machine cannot read | A clerk approves or fixes the exceptions |
| Barcode printed, letter moves on | Draft order or update written into the ERP by API |

**Four lessons to copy:**
1. **Always check against a master list.** Reading is not enough. The postal system matches every reading against real addresses. Every workflow below has a "truth list" in the client's system to check against. If there is no truth list, the idea is weak.
2. **Route by confidence.** Easy items flow through. Unsure items go to a person, with the original image beside the AI's guess.
3. **Keep a manual fallback.** If the machine fails, mail still gets delivered by hand. If your system goes down, orders stay in the inbox and the clerk works the old way. Promise clients this. It kills the fear of "what if it breaks."
4. **Measure the read rate.** The postal service tracks how much the machine handles on its own. You track "share of order lines that needed no fix." That number is your case study.

**Where the copy breaks, and the fix:**
1. **The post office has a clean address list. Small firms have messy item lists.** One product can have three names, and customers use their own names too. *Fix:* sell a paid "clean up" step first. Build a memory of each customer's names for each product, which grows every time the clerk fixes a line.
2. **The post office handles millions of letters. Your client handles 20 to 300 orders a day.** *Fix:* reuse one engine for every client so a custom build stays cheap. Only the connector to their ERP changes.
3. **A wrong letter costs a stamp. A wrong order costs a customer.** *Fix:* a person approves every order at first. Only after weeks of clean results do you let simple repeat orders from trusted customers go through with a lighter check.
4. **The post office owns its machines. You do not own the client's ERP.** *Fix:* the first question on every sales call is "which system, which version, cloud or desktop?" No usable API means no deal, or a slower build with a small connector on their PC.
5. **The keyers kept jobs.** *Fix:* tell the owner and the clerk that the clerk becomes the approver. That lowers pushback from staff, which matters because you cannot win if the staff fight it.

## Region 1: India

### I1. WhatsApp orders from retailers into Busy, Tally or Marg (TOP PICK for India)

**a. Who has it.** Distributors and wholesalers: FMCG, pharma, electricals, hardware, auto spares, paints. Mostly **small** (5 to 49 staff), where the owner decides. Some **medium** distributors too. WhatsApp is already the default ordering channel for Indian distributors and wholesalers ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/)).

**b. Steps today.**
1. A retailer or field salesman sends a WhatsApp message. It can be text ("Parle G 10 ctn, Tiger 5 ctn"), a photo of a handwritten list, or a voice note.
2. A billing clerk reads it, works out which item codes are meant, and checks stock and the retailer's credit.
3. The clerk types a sales order or invoice into Busy, Marg or Tally.
4. The clerk sends a confirmation or the invoice PDF back on WhatsApp, often from a personal phone ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Takkada](https://takkada.com/blog/whatsapp-invoice-format-tally/)).

The data ends up in the accounting software. The middle part (read, match, retype) is manual.

**c. Software on each side.**
* In: WhatsApp Business. To automate it, you need the official WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) through a provider.
* Out: **Tally** takes XML over HTTP on port 9000, which can import vouchers such as sales orders, with the rule that ledgers and stock items must already exist ([Tally help](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/), [TallyPrime XML docs on GitHub](https://github.com/NoumaanAhamed/tally-prime-api-docs/blob/main/index.md)). Tally runs on the client's PC, so you need a small connector running there. **Busy** offers REST APIs, and a listing says four endpoints (customers, invoices, products, ledgers) come free with a subscription ([BusyNotify](https://busynotify.in/solutions/custom-apis), [Busy integration FAQ](https://busy.in/faqs/third-party-services/integration/1/)). **Marg** mostly has third party connectors; check before you promise.

**d. How often and how long (estimate).** A distributor serving 200 to 500 shops may get 50 to 300 orders a day. Each takes about 3 to 10 minutes to read, match and type. The crunch comes in the morning, before dispatch vans leave. Measure this on day one.

**e. Who does it and the cost.** Billing clerks or data entry operators. In Ludhiana, jobs with Tally skills list about ₹15,000 to ₹17,000 a month, and some computer operator jobs list ₹18,000 to ₹23,000 ([Glassdoor Ludhiana](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/ludhiana-data-entry-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,8_IC2939907_KO9,19.htm), [Apna Ludhiana](https://apna.co/jobs/computer_data_entry_operator_copa-jobs-in-ludhiana), [Apna data entry](https://apna.co/jobs/data_entry-jobs-in-ludhiana)).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Many tools already send invoices, dispatch updates and payment reminders *out* of Tally or Marg on WhatsApp ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Perimattic Marg connector](https://perimattic.com/products/erpplugs/connectors/marg-whatsapp/)). Others push retailers to order from a WhatsApp catalog or bot ([HublerX list](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026), [Wydely](https://www.wydely.io/whatsapp-automation-wholesale-trading-businesses.html)). **The gap:** the messy free text, photo and voice orders that retailers keep sending, and matching them to *this* distributor's own item codes in Busy or Tally. Retailers do not want to change how they order. You do not ask them to.

**Honest warning.** One clerk costs ₹15,000 to ₹23,000 a month. If the distributor has one clerk who is not overloaded, just hiring beats you. Only sell where there are **2 or more people typing orders**, or where morning delays and wrong items (returns, lost sales) clearly hurt.

**WhatsApp running cost.** In India, Meta charges per message: about ₹0.115 for a utility message (such as an order confirmation) and ₹0.86 for marketing, plus 18% GST. From 1 October 2026, utility messages sent inside the 24 hour reply window are no longer free ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing), [MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026), [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/meta-to-charge-for-whatsapp-business-utility-messages-in-india/tldr)). At 200 confirmations a day, that is roughly ₹700 a month. Small.

**g. Realistic price (estimate).** It has to cost clearly less than one clerk. Setup ₹40,000 to ₹1,20,000. Monthly ₹5,000 to ₹15,000, plus WhatsApp charges passed through at cost. For your **first one or two clients, do it at cost or free for a 6 week pilot**, in return for written permission to publish real numbers and a video testimonial. That case study is worth more to you than the fee.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### I2. Purchase orders by email into Tally or Busy for Indian manufacturers and exporters (the bridge to Europe)

**a. Who has it.** Manufacturers and exporters who sell to other businesses: knitwear and hosiery, hand tools, bicycle parts, auto parts, machinery parts. **Both small and medium.** Ludhiana has many of these. The best target is a firm that exports to European buyers, because the orders look exactly like the ones you will handle in Europe.

**b. Steps today.** A buyer emails a PO as a PDF or Excel file. A sales coordinator reads it, matches the buyer's item codes to their own, checks price and delivery date against the agreed rate, and types a sales order into Tally or Busy. Any mismatch goes back to the buyer by email.

**c. Software.** Email (Gmail or Outlook) in. Tally or Busy out (same APIs as I1).

**d. Frequency (estimate).** 5 to 40 POs a day. Each can have many lines, so 10 to 30 minutes each.

**e. Who and cost.** A sales coordinator or export executive. The pay is higher than a clerk's, so errors on price or dates cost more than the salary.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Purchase side tools like Suvit handle bills, not customer orders (see I3). Enterprise order tools such as Conexiom are priced for large firms (see E1). I found no cheap tool aimed at Tally users for inbound customer POs. That is a gap, though the volume per client is lower than I1.

**g. Price (estimate).** Setup ₹60,000 to ₹1,50,000. Monthly ₹8,000 to ₹20,000. Exporters earn in euros and dollars, so they pay a little more readily.

**h. Sources.** Tally and Busy links in I1. Market links in E1.

### I3. Supplier bill entry into Tally with GSTR 2B checking (REJECT as a product)

**a. Who.** Traders, manufacturers, and CA firms that keep books for them. Both sizes.

**b. Steps.** Supplier bills arrive as PDFs or photos. Someone types them into Tally, then matches them against GSTR 2B to claim input tax credit.

**c. Software.** Email or WhatsApp in, Tally out, GST portal data.

**d, e.** High volume at month end. Done by accountants and junior staff.

**f. Why reject.** It is already covered. **Suvit (now Vyapar TaxOne)** turns purchase and sales PDFs, images and bank statements into Tally entries, and includes GST reconciliation ([Suvit pricing](https://www.suvit.io/pricing), [Suvit data entry](https://www.suvit.io/data-entry-automation-feature)). **TallyPrime itself** now imports GSTR 2B and flags mismatches, with more matching added in release 6.1 ([Tally Solutions](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/gstr-2a-2b-reconciliation-in-tallyprime/), [Tally at Cloud](https://tallyatcloud.com/article/Automated-GSTR2B-Matching-in-TallyPrime-6.1:-Save-Time-&-Improve-GST-Accuracy/145/0/1)).

**g. Price.** Low, because cheap tools exist. Do not compete here.

### Also rejected for India
* **Payment reminders from Tally outstanding lists.** Many WhatsApp for Tally tools already send reminders and ledger statements ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)). It is also close to a "single task," which you said you do not sell.

## Region 2: Europe and the UK

### E1. Customer orders by email into the ERP as draft sales orders (TOP PICK overall)

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers, distributors, trade suppliers (building, electrical, food service, packaging, industrial parts), and small manufacturers. **Both sizes.** Small firms (5 to 49) with 20 or more emailed orders a day. Medium firms (50 to 250) with a sales office team, where the customer service or operations manager owns the pain.

**b. Steps today.** A customer emails an order: a PDF from their own system, an Excel sheet, or a line of text like "send 30 of the usual." A sales order processor opens it, works out the SKUs, checks that customer's prices, and types the lines into the ERP. Manual entry of one order commonly takes **10 to 15 minutes**, so 20 orders a day is a part time job ([StackCube](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors), [Y Meadows](https://ymeadows.com/en/insights/blog/ai-order-entry-for-distributors/)).

**c. Software on each side, and whether it has usable APIs.**

| System | Usable API? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Sage 200** (UK) | Yes. REST API with sales order endpoints and OAuth ([Sage Developer: Sage 200 sales orders](https://developer.sage.com/200-uk/apis/sage-200-professional/2024.01/combined/groups/sop/tags/sop-orders/paths/postsoporder)) | **Best target.** Common in UK firms, and no built in order agent. |
| **Xero** with Unleashed or Cin7 | Yes. From March 2026 Xero moved apps to paid tiers, but the Starter tier is free up to 5 connections ([Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/xero-shifts-to-tiered-pricing-model-for-developers), [Apideck](https://www.apideck.com/blog/xero-api-pricing-and-the-app-partner-program), [Xero pricing](https://developer.xero.com/pricing)) | Good for small firms. Orders usually live in Unleashed or Cin7, so connect there. |
| **Odoo** | Yes, a well known external API (check the version) | Good, common with growing EU firms ([ERP guide for UK SMEs](https://opsmavix.com/blog/best-erp-software-small-business-uk/)). |
| **Sage 50** (desktop) | Weak. SData support ended in September 2025. You now need the Sage Developer Programme and something running on the client's Windows PC ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-news/sage-sdata-is-gone-heres-what-that-means-for-your-sage-50-integrations/)) | Possible, but slower and riskier. Charge more or skip. |
| **Business Central** | Yes, good API ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/api/dynamics_salesorder_create)) | **Avoid for this workflow.** See below. |

**Why avoid Business Central.** Microsoft now ships a **Sales Order Agent** inside Business Central. It reads a shared mailbox, including PDF and image attachments, finds the customer, checks stock, and creates a quote or order ([Sales Order Agent overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent)). A typical order uses about 16.5 Copilot Credits ([Microsoft billing page](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/administration/tenant-admin-center-manage-consumption-billing)), at about $0.01 per credit ([MSDynamicsWorld](https://msdynamicsworld.com/blog-post/business-centrals-ai-agents-run-consumption-billing-heres-what-means-your-2027-budget)). That is about 17 cents an order. You cannot beat that on price. Its known limits are real: it finds customers only by the sender's email address, it only works with a Microsoft Exchange mailbox, and some languages are not fully supported ([Sales Order Agent FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/faqs-sales-order-taker-agent)). But those are small gaps to build a business on.

**d. How often and how long.** 20 to 300 orders a day depending on size (estimate). 10 to 15 minutes each (sources above).

**e. Who does it and the cost.** "Sales order processor" and "order processor" jobs are common in the UK. Pay is about £21,000 to £27,000 a year ([Jobsite](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor), [Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)). Worked example (estimate): 30 orders a day at 10 minutes is 5 hours a day, about 1,100 hours a year. That is roughly £15,000 to £18,000 of staff time, before counting errors.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* **Conexiom** and **Esker** are built for enterprises. Conexiom deals often run $10,000 to $100,000+ a year with per partner fees ([OrderDrafter comparison](https://orderdrafter.com/alternatives/conexiom-alternative/), [ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom)). Esker's price tends to pay off only above about 2,000 to 3,000 documents a month ([CheckThat on Esker pricing](https://checkthat.ai/brands/esker/pricing)).
* **New small business tools exist**, and you must know them. OrderDrafter costs $399 to $799 a month but targets only Epicor Prophet 21 users ([OrderDrafter](https://orderdrafter.com/)). StackCube targets small distributors ([StackCube](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors)). Most vendors do not publish prices.
* **The gap you fill:** firms on Sage 200, Xero with Unleashed or Cin7, Odoo, or older ERPs. Also firms whose orders come from many channels (email text, Excel, PDF), and firms with odd rules: customer specific prices, pack sizes, "the usual." A custom build that learns *their* customers' naming habits is the product.

**g. Realistic price.** See the pricing section. In short: small firms $2,500 to $4,000 setup plus $300 to $600 a month. Medium firms $5,000 to $9,000 setup plus $700 to $1,500 a month.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### E2. Supplier order confirmations checked against your purchase orders (SECOND PICK, medium firms)

**a. Who has it.** Manufacturers and wholesalers that buy many parts from many suppliers. Mostly **medium** (50 to 250), where a purchasing or supply chain lead owns it. Strong in Germany, Austria and the Netherlands, where this job has its own name (Auftragsbestätigungsprüfung, or "AB check").

**b. Steps today.** The buyer sends a PO from the ERP. The supplier replies with an order confirmation as a PDF or email. A purchasing clerk compares it line by line with the PO: part number, quantity, price, delivery date. They update the ERP with the confirmed date and chase any differences. Documents arrive by email or on paper, and each is opened, read and matched by hand ([Handelsblatt feature](https://www.handelsblatt.com/adv/firmen/auftragsbestaetigung-automatisiert-pruefen.html), [edoc](https://edoc.de/blog/digitale-auftragsbestaetigungen-automatisiert-verarbeiten)).

**c. Software.** Email in. ERP out: Business Central (has a purchase order API), Sage 200 (purchase order module), Odoo, or a German mid market ERP. Confirm that the ERP API can **update** the promised date on a PO line before you quote.

**d. How often and how long (estimate).** A firm with 30 active suppliers may get 20 to 100 confirmations a week. A careful check takes a few minutes per document, and much longer when lines differ.

**e. Who and cost.** Purchasing clerks. Job ads list "verification of order confirmations" as a core task ([LISTEC job ad](https://www.listec-gmbh.com/en/job-offers/order-processing-purchasing-clerk-m-f-d)).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Real firms already pay to solve this, which is good evidence. Perzeptron's OCC.AI checks confirmations against POs in any ERP. One electronics maker, esw, cut its manual checking effort by over 80% ([Mittelstand Cafe](https://www.mittelstandcafe.de/perzeptron-automatisiert-ab-kontrolle-bei-esw-pr-faufwand-sinkt-mit-occ-ai-um-ber-80-prozent-2252271.html), [Perzeptron](https://scm-supply-chain-management.de/auftragsbestaetigung-automatisieren-digitalisieren/)). SER sells the same for SAP users ([ECMGuide](https://www.ecmguide.de/news/ser-automatisiert-pruefung-der-auftragsbestaetigung-in-sap-24940/)). These vendors are German, sell to larger firms, and do not publish prices. I found no built in Business Central agent for this as of September 2026 (the Payables Agent handles invoices, and the Sales Order Agent handles sales). **The gap:** smaller firms on Sage 200, Business Central or Odoo, especially in the UK and Nordics.

**g. Realistic price (estimate).** $4,000 to $8,000 setup, $500 to $1,200 a month. Buyers here are operations heads with a budget.

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

**Honest warning.** You will not find many Indian clients for this, so your case study must come from E1 or a European pilot. Build it second.

### E3. Remittance advice to cash allocation (NICHE, medium firms not on Xero)

**a. Who.** B2B firms whose big customers pay many invoices in one bank transfer and send a separate remittance email. **Medium** firms mainly.

**b. Steps.** The remittance arrives as a PDF, Excel file or email body. A credit controller reads it, finds each invoice in the ledger, and allocates the payment line by line. It can take AR teams hours every day ([Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/cash-application-in-2026-how-to-automate-matching-payments-to-invoices/), [RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/cash-application-software)).

**c. Software.** Email in. Accounting system out (Sage 200, Business Central, Xero).

**d, e (estimate).** 20 to 200 remittances a month. A credit controller or AR clerk.

**f. Existing tools and gap.** **RemitClear already does this for Xero and QuickBooks** ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/xero-remittance-matching), [pricing](https://remitclear.com/pricing)). Enterprise tools cover Sage Intacct and bigger systems. The only gap is mid sized firms on Sage 200 or similar. **Verdict:** a real but narrow niche. Keep it as a later add on, not a lead product.

**g. Price (estimate).** $2,500 to $5,000 setup, $300 to $700 a month.

## 3. Workflows in BOTH regions: your case study bridge

**Order intake to draft sales order** is the only strong one that appears in both regions.

| | India | Europe |
|---|---|---|
| What arrives | WhatsApp text, photo, voice note (I1), or email PO (I2) | Email PDF, Excel, text (E1) |
| Truth list to check against | Busy or Tally item list, rates, credit limit | Sage 200, Unleashed, Odoo item list, customer prices |
| Output | Draft sales order or invoice | Draft sales order |
| Person who approves | Billing clerk | Sales order processor |

**How to make the Indian case study believable to a European buyer:**
1. **Use numbers, not stories.** European buyers do not care about WhatsApp. They care about "orders per day, minutes per order before and after, share of lines matched with no fix, errors caught before dispatch." Measure these the same way in both places.
2. **Best case: an Indian exporter (I2) that gets email POs from European buyers.** Then your case study literally says "we process POs from German and UK buyers." That is the same job as E1.
3. **Run in "shadow mode" first.** For two weeks the AI drafts, and the clerk still types as usual. Compare the two. This gives you honest before and after numbers, and it proves safety.
4. **Record a 2 minute screen video** of a real order flowing from inbox to approved draft. That sells better than any PDF.

## 4. Pricing in Europe: confirming and correcting your guess

**What the market says.**
* UK AI and automation agencies quote fixed projects from about £2,000 up. A single well defined workflow often costs £8,000 to £25,000, simple ones £500 to £2,000, and mid range ones £2,000 to £10,000 over 2 to 6 weeks. Small business retainers commonly run about £300 to £900 a month ([ExpertSure](https://www.expertsure.com/uk/ai-tools/ai-tools-costs/), [Northern Codes](https://northerncodes.com/blog/ai-automation-cost-uk-small-business), [Quantel](https://quantel.uk/blog/ai-automation-cost-uk)).
* Freelance n8n and automation builders on Upwork bill about $40 to $100 an hour ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/hire/n8n-experts/), [Ciphernutz](https://ciphernutz.com/blog/hire-n8n-expert-cost-pricing-guide)).
* Order entry software for small distributors sells at about $399 to $799 a month ([OrderDrafter](https://orderdrafter.com/)).

**My verdict on your guess ($1,500 to $4,000 setup, $400 to $1,500 a month):**
* **Setup: right for small firms, too low for medium.** You have no European case study yet, so the first two deals will close near the low end. After that, charge by size.
* **Upkeep: too high for small firms, right for medium.** A 20 person wholesaler will balk at $1,000 a month when a SaaS tool costs $400.

| Buyer | First workflow | Monthly upkeep | Who signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small (5 to 49 staff), 20 to 60 orders a day | $2,500 to $4,000 | $300 to $600 | Owner or MD |
| Medium (50 to 250 staff), 60 to 300 orders a day | $5,000 to $9,000 | $700 to $1,500 | Ops, sales office or purchasing head |

**Rules that protect you:**
1. Never go below about $2,000 in Europe. A price that is too cheap looks risky coming from an unknown team abroad.
2. Charge the "clean up the item list" step separately. It is where the hidden work is.
3. Put a volume cap in the monthly fee (for example, "up to 2,000 orders a month"). AI costs grow with volume.
4. Upkeep must include something real: monitoring, fixing new document layouts, a monthly report showing hours saved. If it is just "hosting," clients will cancel.

## 5. Build, then break: five hostile scenarios and the fixes

I built the plan above, then attacked it. Here is what broke and how I patched it.

**1. "We run Business Central."**
*Breaks:* E1 dies. Microsoft's own agent costs about 17 cents an order.
*Patch:* ask "which ERP?" in the first two minutes. For Business Central firms, offer E2 (supplier confirmations) instead, or walk away.

**2. "Our Tally or Sage 50 runs on one office PC, with no cloud."**
*Breaks:* no public API to call from your server.
*Patch:* install a small connector service on their PC that pulls drafts from your server and posts XML to Tally on port 9000 ([Tally XML docs](https://github.com/NoumaanAhamed/tally-prime-api-docs/blob/main/index.md)). For Sage 50, only go ahead through the Sage Developer Programme route ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-news/sage-sdata-is-gone-heres-what-that-means-for-your-sage-50-integrations/)). Add a week to the quote. If the PC is often off, fall back to a file the clerk imports with one click.

**3. "Half our orders are Punjabi voice notes and blurry photos of handwritten lists."**
*Breaks:* the read rate drops, and the clerk fixes more than they save.
*Patch:* launch with typed and printed orders only. Treat voice and handwriting like the post office treats bad handwriting: send them straight to the person, with the AI's best guess beside the image. Add them to automation only when your measured accuracy is good enough. Track read rate by type of input.

**4. "You are in India. Our data cannot go to a US AI service." (GDPR)**
*Breaks:* the European deal stalls in legal review.
*Patch:* host the whole thing in an EU region (Dev A knows AWS, so use an EU region such as Frankfurt). Use an AI model deployment that keeps processing inside the EU. For example, Azure "Data Zone" deployments in an EU country process prompts only inside EU member nations ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-foundry/openai/how-to/deployment-types)). Sign a data processing agreement. Use the EU Standard Contractual Clauses for your own access from India. Keep personal data minimal, since order documents are mostly product lines.

**5. "What happens when you two are asleep, sick, or gone?"**
*Breaks:* a part time team looks like a risk to any operations head.
*Patch:* use the post office rule. If anything fails, orders simply stay in the inbox and the clerk works the old way. Nothing is lost. Give them a one page runbook, alerting to you and to them, and a handover pack (code, setup, credentials) held by the client. Your working day overlaps UK mornings, since Ludhiana is 4.5 hours ahead of UK summer time and 5.5 hours ahead in winter.

**Bonus break: "Your ₹60,000 costs more than another clerk."** (India)
*Patch:* only pitch distributors with 2 or more order typists, or a painful morning rush. Price the monthly fee below half of one clerk's salary.

## 6. Final top picks, and what to do next

### Pick 1: Order reader into the ERP (E1, proven first in India through I1 or I2)
**Why:**
* It passes all 7 of your rules. One process, one owner, 2 to 3 tools (inbox, ERP, and maybe a review screen), human approval, countable, and already half digital.
* The money is proven. Enterprises pay Conexiom and Esker big sums, and new small tools charge $400 to $800 a month. So buyers already understand the category.
* The gap is clear: Sage 200, Xero with Unleashed or Cin7, and Odoo users, who are too small for enterprise tools and not covered by a built in agent.
* It fits your skills. Dev A builds the engine and ERP connectors in Spring Boot with Spring AI. Dev B builds the review screen and the WhatsApp and email intake in TypeScript or n8n.
* The same engine works in both regions, so your Indian work becomes a real case study.

### Pick 2: Supplier confirmation checker (E2)
**Why:** medium firms already pay German vendors for this, and small and mid ERPs lack it, including Business Central for now. Build it after Pick 1, reusing the same engine.

### What NOT to spend time on
AP invoice capture, GST reconciliation, payment reminders, and haulage TMS work. Each one is solved cheaply or blocked by closed systems.

### Your next 90 days (part time, under $50 a month)

**Weeks 1 to 2: Build the core and find a pilot.**
* Build the engine: read a document, match lines against an item list using a memory of each customer's naming habits, flag price and quantity problems, and show a review screen.
* Run it on one self hosted n8n or Spring Boot box. A small server costs about €5 a month, and self hosted n8n is free ([Softomate](https://www.softomatesolutions.com/blog/n8n-review-2026/), [Hand On Web](https://www.handonweb.com/blog/n8n-pricing-uk-2026)). AI model costs at pilot volume should be a few dollars a month (estimate). You stay well under $50.
* Walk into 10 Ludhiana distributors and exporters that use Busy or Tally. Ask three questions: "How many orders a day? How many people type them? What goes wrong?"

**Weeks 3 to 8: Pilot in India.**
* Two weeks in shadow mode, then four weeks live with the clerk approving.
* Measure everything: orders a day, minutes per order, share of lines with no fix, errors caught.
* Get written permission to publish the numbers and a short screen recording.

**Weeks 6 to 12: Start selling in Europe.**
* **Warm lead first:** ask your Finnish fintech client for two introductions to firms that ship physical goods. A referral beats a hundred cold emails.
* **Go through partners:** Sage 200 and Odoo resellers in the UK have clients asking for AI, but many lack builders. Offer to be their white label build team. This is the fastest route to trust for a team based abroad.
* **Cold outreach:** email UK operations and customer service managers at wholesalers, using your Indian numbers and video.
* **Always qualify with one question:** "Which ERP do you use?" Business Central means pitch E2 or walk away. Sage 200, Unleashed, Cin7 or Odoo means go.

**The brutal truth:** the idea is not your hard part. Order automation is a known category with funded competitors. Your edge is being cheap, fast, custom to the client's messy data, and aimed at the ERPs the big vendors ignore. That edge only turns into sales once you have **one set of real before and after numbers.** Get those numbers in the next 8 weeks, and nothing else matters as much.
