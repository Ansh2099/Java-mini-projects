# Workflows Worth Selling: India and Europe (30 September 2026)

## The short answer

1. **Build one thing first: "customer order arrives, draft sales order is ready to approve."** Orders come in by email (Europe) or WhatsApp and email (India). Today a person reads each one and types it into the ERP or Tally. We read it, match every line to the right product code, create a *draft* order, and flag anything odd. A human clicks approve. This is the only idea that passed every filter in both regions, and an Indian distributor gives you a real case study for a UK wholesaler.
2. **Second product for Europe: "supplier order confirmation arrives, checked against our purchase order."** Less crowded, less loud pain, sold to medium manufacturers. Good upsell to the same buyers.
3. **Do not sell supplier invoice entry, remittance matching, or Tally purchase bill entry.** Cheap tools already do them. Details below.
4. **Your Europe price guess is right for the build and too high for upkeep.** Plan on 2,000 to 4,000 USD to build and 250 to 700 USD a month to run. You only reach 1,000+ USD a month when the client has high volume.
5. **Hard truth:** order entry automation is a busy market in 2026. Many funded vendors sell it. You will not win on features. You win on three things they are bad at: small clients, European and Indian software they do not support, and hands on setup. If you cannot get 5 real sales calls with distributors in your first 60 days, stop and rethink.

---

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK first, then the Netherlands, Ireland and the Nordics, because English works for sales there. Germany and France are harder to sell to from India.
2. "Few weeks of part time work" means about 60 to 100 hours of build time for two people.
3. "Under 50 USD a month" covers our own tools only. Each client pays its own hosting and AI usage inside the monthly fee.
4. Where I give time per task without a source, I say it is an estimate. Every workflow includes a way to count it at the client before you quote.
5. How I got the facts: I used web search and cite only links it returned. Many vendor pages blocked direct opening, so vendor numbers (like "85% faster") are the vendors' own claims. Treat them as marketing, not proof.

## Step 1: The box we have to fit in

I started from your limits, not from ideas. The tightest limit decides everything.

| Limit | What it forces |
|---|---|
| 2 part time developers | One client build at a time. Nothing that needs 24/7 support. |
| Under 50 USD a month | Self hosted n8n or a small Spring Boot service on one cheap server. No paid platform seats. Xero's free Starter API tier allows only 5 connected organisations ([Truto](https://truto.one/blog/xero-api-pricing-changes-2026-costs-tiers-and-how-to-minimize-egress/), [Xero Developer](https://developer.xero.com/pricing)). |
| India time zone, Europe clients | UK 9:00 is 13:30 or 14:30 in Ludhiana. Fine for meetings. But if our system breaks at 16:00 UK time, you are asleep. **So the business must keep working when our system is down.** |
| Human approves, we never move money | Output must be a *draft* (draft order, flagged list). Nothing is posted as final. No payments. |
| No legal, medical, lending decisions | Rules out insurance claims, clinic work, KYC approval, visa work. |
| 2 to 3 tools, one owner | Inbox plus ERP, maybe a sheet. Nothing that crosses departments. |
| Already partly digital | Data must already arrive as email, PDF, Excel or WhatsApp. We do not ask anyone to change how they send it. |
| Sold from India to Europe | Buyers will ask "what happens to our data" and "who else have you done this for". We need GDPR answers and a case study. |

**The limit that drives the design is the time zone plus "never in the critical path".** Every workflow here is built so that the original email or WhatsApp message still reaches the human exactly as today. We only add a draft next to it. If our server dies, the staff member just types the order like last week. Nothing is lost. This one rule kills a lot of ideas (live chat bots, payroll deadlines, anything with a same day cut off that only we can do).

## Step 2: The edge cases that kill these projects

I hunted the failure modes first, then picked workflows that survive them.

| Failure mode | Real example | How the design survives it |
|---|---|---|
| ERP has no usable API | Sage 50 is a desktop product. Its free Sdata API covers only a small part of the features, and third party apps are limited ([Sage Community](https://communityhub.sage.com/gb/sage-50-accounts/f/announcements/269682/sage-50-cloud-accounts---frequently-asked-questions---third-party-integration-and-apps), [Sage Developer Community](https://developer-community.sage.com/topic/495-api-for-sage-50/)). | Check the ERP on the first call. No API, no deal. Or quote extra for an on site connector like HyperAccounts ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-rest-apis/sage-50-accounts-api/)). |
| Software sits on one office PC | Tally only listens on a local port (default 9000) on the machine running it ([TallyHelp](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/)). | Never open that port to the internet. Run a tiny agent on the client PC that *pulls* approved drafts from our server and posts them to Tally locally. If the PC is off, drafts wait in a queue. |
| AI misreads a number | "10" read as "100". A 5 line PDF becomes 4 lines. | Hard checks, not trust: line totals must add up to the order total; quantity is compared with that customer's past orders; unknown product code goes to exceptions. Any failed check means a human looks. |
| Customer uses their own product names | "Blue widget 5L" or "Lux 100 wala" instead of your SKU. | A mapping table per customer that learns from every human correction. Week 1 has more exceptions. That is expected and written into the contract. |
| Same order sent twice | Customer resends "in case you missed it". | Duplicate check on customer PO number plus lines plus date. |
| Order changes and cancellations | "Please change PO 4471 qty to 20." | Version 1 does **not** apply changes. It flags them as "amendment, human action". Later versions draft the change. |
| Low volume | 30 orders a month. | Y Meadows gives 50 orders a month free ([Software Finder](https://softwarefinder.com/artificial-intelligence/y-meadows)). Below about 300 orders a month in Europe, do not sell. |
| IT blocks mailbox access | Microsoft 365 admin must approve API access. | Fallback: client sets a forwarding rule to our intake address. Works on day 1. |
| Personal data and GDPR | EU buyers ask where data goes. | B2B orders carry little personal data (a buyer's name and email). Host in an EU AWS region, sign a DPA, keep documents 90 days. This is one reason I rejected HR and timesheet workflows. |
| WhatsApp rules change | Meta changes pricing often. One 2026 India pricing guide says charges for service messages inside the 24 hour window start from 1 October 2026 ([MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026), [ChatMaxima](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/india/)). Verify before quoting. | Price WhatsApp usage as pass through cost, not inside a fixed fee. Utility messages were about ₹0.115 each in 2026 per the same guides. |

## Step 3: How I filtered ideas

Every idea had to pass all of these, in order. It dies at the first "no".

1. Does a named role do it by hand today, with the data already digital?
2. Can we count it (how many, how long)?
3. Only 2 to 3 tools, and do they have APIs we can reach?
4. Does a cheap tool or built in feature already do it? If yes, reject.
5. Can the business keep running if we are down?
6. Can we build a working first version in 60 to 100 hours?
7. Will they pay more than it costs us to support?

## Part 1: India

### Price reality in India

A Tally data entry person costs about ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 a month ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/india-data-entry-tally-salary-SRCH_IL.0,5_IN115_KO6,22.htm)). The average data entry clerk earns about ₹22,249 a month ([Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/career/data-entry-clerk/salaries)). So an automation that costs ₹15,000 a month will lose to "hire one more boy". It only wins when:

1. Volume is high enough that it replaces one or more people, or
2. Speed matters (orders at 7 pm must be packed by 9 am), or
3. Errors are costly (wrong item shipped, wrong rate given to a dealer).

### India workflow 1 (strong): Dealer order on WhatsApp or email to draft sales order in Tally

**a. Who has it.** Distributors and wholesalers: FMCG, pharma trade distribution (trade only, no medical decisions), electrical, hardware, auto parts, cycle parts, hosiery and textile wholesale (Ludhiana has plenty). Fits **medium** best (50 to 250 staff, often 1 to 3 order entry people). Small firms with 100+ orders a day also fit. Small firms with 20 orders a day do not; a clerk is cheaper.

**b. Steps today.**
1. Dealer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp: typed list, Hinglish text, photo of a handwritten list, sometimes a voice note. Bigger buyers email a PDF or Excel.
2. One person at the distributor reads every message and types it into Tally or the ERP. Vendor articles on WhatsApp ordering describe exactly this ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Rupyz](https://rupyz.com/blog/order-management-system-with-whatsapp-tally-integration/)).
3. They look up the item code, check the dealer's rate and scheme, and create the sales order.
4. Godown picks from the order. Invoice follows.

**c. Software.** In: WhatsApp Business (usually the phone app, not the API), Gmail. Out: TallyPrime mostly, also Busy and Marg. Tally accepts vouchers through its XML over HTTP interface ([TallyHelp](https://help.tallysolutions.com/integrate-with-tallyprime/), [community docs](https://noumaanahamed.github.io/tally-prime-api-docs/)). Busy and Marg: I did not confirm a usable API. **Start with Tally clients only.**

**d. How often, how long.** Medium distributors get dozens to hundreds of orders a day. A vendor article in the same search claims mid market firms run manual processing teams of 10 to 15 people with 12 to 18% error rates ([Hublerx](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026), [Wydely](https://www.wydely.io/whatsapp-automation-wholesale-trading-businesses.html)); I could not open them to confirm which one. Treat it as vendor marketing. My estimate: 3 to 10 minutes per order, more for photos and long lists. **Count it at the client:** export one week of WhatsApp chats and match against that week's Tally sales orders.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Order entry or billing staff at ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 a month each ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/india-tally-operator-and-data-entry-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,5_IN115_KO6,35.htm)). The hidden cost is the wrong item shipped and the late evening backlog.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* Order apps like Rupyz sync orders to Tally ([Rupyz](https://rupyz.com/blog/order-management-system-with-whatsapp-tally-integration/)), and many WhatsApp tools push catalog orders ([Wydely](https://www.wydely.io/whatsapp-automation-wholesale-trading-businesses.html), [Sunray](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/)). **The gap:** most make the dealer order through an app, a catalog, or a fixed format. Dealers refuse. That is exactly the "change how people work" problem you want to avoid.
* Tools like Fast2SMS send invoices *out* of Tally on WhatsApp ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-automation-auto-send/)). That is the other direction. It does not read messy orders coming *in*.
* **Honest risk:** AI players like Hublerx are now aiming at free text WhatsApp orders. The gap is closing. Move fast or accept this as a case study project, not a long term Indian business.

**g. Realistic price.** Setup ₹30,000 to ₹75,000. Monthly ₹5,000 to ₹12,000 plus WhatsApp and AI usage at cost. For your first 1 or 2 clients, charge only setup at cost in exchange for a named case study, real numbers, and a video testimonial. That case study is worth more than the fee.

**h. Sources.** Linked inline above.

**Edge cases specific to India.**
* **Voice notes and photos.** Version 1 handles typed text and PDF/Excel. Photos go to exceptions. Voice notes come in version 3.
* **WhatsApp access.** Most distributors use the WhatsApp Business phone app. Moving the number to the API is a big ask. Smallest safe start: order staff forward order chats to one dedicated number or email that we read. The dealer changes nothing.
* **Product nicknames.** Build the nickname table from the last 3 months of Tally sales plus the chat history before go live.
* **Schemes and credit limits.** We never approve credit. We only flag "dealer over limit" if Tally shows it.

**Smallest version that works (build in this order, keep it working at each step).**
1. Shadow mode, 2 weeks: read forwarded orders, write the parsed order to a Google Sheet. Staff keep typing as normal. Compare. This gives you the accuracy number for the case study with zero risk.
2. Draft orders for the top 20 dealers into Tally through the local agent. Human approves in Tally.
3. All dealers, plus exceptions list (unknown item, rate mismatch, duplicate).
4. Photos, then voice notes, then amendments.

### India workflow 2 (conditional): Freight rate request to draft quote, for small forwarders

**a. Who.** Freight forwarders and customs brokers serving exporters (Ludhiana exporters use ICDs and Delhi forwarders). **Small** mostly.

**b. Steps today.** Exporter emails or WhatsApps "rate for 1x40HC Ludhiana to Hamburg". Staff read it, check carrier rate sheets in Excel or ask agents, add margin, and type a quote email.

**c. Software.** Email, WhatsApp, Excel rate sheets. Some use a freight system.

**d. How often, how long.** Vendors claim 2 to 4 hours per request, cut by 85% ([41 Labs](https://41labs.ai/blog/ai-freight-forwarding-quote-automation)). That is surely high for simple lanes. My estimate for simple sea freight: 15 to 45 minutes each, 10 to 50 a day.

**e. Who and cost.** Pricing or sales executives, often ₹25,000 to ₹45,000 a month (my estimate, no source).

**f. Tools and gap.** Crowded: FreightMynd, Wisor, Cargofive, Info-X, Shipthis all sell AI quoting ([FreightMynd](https://freightmynd.com/blog/best-ai-freight-quoting-software-2026/), [Wisor](https://wisor.ai/solution/freight-email-automation/), [Cargofive](https://cargofive.com/ai-powered-freight-quoting/), [Info-X](https://infox.com/rate-quote-automation), [Shipthis](https://www.shipthis.co/solutions/freight-quote-management-software)). Gap: small forwarders whose rates live in messy Excel that these tools do not ingest well.

**g. Price.** ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 setup, ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 a month.

**Verdict.** Only do this if a forwarder you know asks for it. Rates change weekly, so the "data" is unstable. That is a support burden two part time people will feel.

### Rejected in India (so you do not waste time)

| Idea | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Purchase bill entry into Tally | Vyapar TaxOne (formerly Suvit) already reads PDFs, Excel and scans, maps ledgers and syncs to Tally, from about ₹10,000 ([TaxOne pricing](https://taxone.vyapar.com/pricing), [Techjockey](https://www.techjockey.com/detail/suvit)). AI Accountant and Accubrai do the same ([AI Accountant](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/purchase-bill-entry-in-tally), [Accubrai](https://accubrai.in/automatic-invoice-entry-tally)). |
| Sending invoices and reminders on WhatsApp from Tally | A single task, and cheap tools do it ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-automation-auto-send/)). |
| Bank statement to Tally | Covered by TaxOne ([Suvit](https://www.suvit.io/data-entry-automation-feature)). |

## Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

### Price check: your guess, confirmed and corrected

What UK buyers see from other sellers in 2026:
* Simple workflows start around £500. Mid range builds run £3,000 to £10,000. Monthly support usually runs £200 to £800. (These three figures came from one search across these UK pricing guides, and I could not open them to see which guide said which: [AI Workforce](https://www.aiworkforce.co.uk/blogs/ai-automation-pricing-uk-small-businesses-2026), [Axivon](https://www.axivon-ai.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-cost-uk), [Optimised](https://getoptimised.co.uk/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-automate-a-small-business).)
* UK automation freelancers and agencies charge £50 to £95 an hour, median £65 to £70 ([AutomationHire](https://automationhire.co.uk/how-much-does-workflow-automation-cost-uk)).
* Order entry vendors pitch against a manual cost of €12 to €28 per order ([Graip](https://graip.ai/wholesale-distribution)).

**My verdict:**
* **Build fee: 1,500 to 4,000 USD is right.** Charge about 1,500 to 2,500 USD for your first two European clients (you have no EU case study and you are remote). Move to 3,000 to 5,000 USD once you have one.
* **Upkeep: 400 to 1,500 USD a month is too high for most.** Realistic is 250 to 700 USD a month. Tie it to volume: for example, a base fee that covers up to 1,000 orders a month, then a per order fee. That is how buyers already think, because vendors price per order.
* **Selling angle that works:** "It costs less than 1 day a week of your order clerk." At a UK clerk rate this is easy to show once you have counted their volume.

### Europe workflow 1 (top pick): Customer purchase order email to draft sales order in the ERP

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors (food service, building supplies, electrical, industrial parts, packaging, beauty and pet products), and manufacturers who sell to trade customers. **Medium** is the sweet spot: a customer service or sales order team of 2 to 8 people, owned by the operations or customer service manager. **Small** fits only with high volume (the owner will ask "why not just the clerk").

**b. Steps today.**
1. Customer emails a PO as PDF, Excel, or plain text in the email body ([Y Meadows](https://ymeadows.com/en/insights/blog/ai-order-entry-for-distributors/)).
2. Staff open it, find the customer in the ERP, and match each line to a product code, often from the customer's own part numbers.
3. They check prices against the price list, check stock, and key the sales order.
4. They send an order confirmation.

**c. Software.** In: Outlook or Gmail. Out: Business Central, Sage 200, Exact Online, Odoo, Unleashed, Cin7, or QuickBooks and Xero for smaller firms. Approval happens inside the ERP as a draft or quote status. That keeps it to 2 tools.

**d. How often, how long.** A medium distributor gets hundreds to thousands of emailed orders a month. Vendors put the manual cost at €12 to €28 per order ([Graip](https://graip.ai/wholesale-distribution)) and each order error at 250 USD or more ([Y Meadows](https://use.ymeadows.com/cost_of_errors?_bhlid=demo-link)). My estimate: 5 to 15 minutes of keying per order, plus fixing errors later. **Count it:** pull 4 weeks of the orders inbox and 4 weeks of sales orders created by user. Time 20 orders with a stopwatch.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Sales order processors, customer service coordinators, sales administrators. The same work shows up in the Sage manuals for sales order processing ([Sage 200 help](https://desktophelp.sage.co.uk/sage200/professional/Content/SOP/The%20Sales%20Order%20Processing%20module.htm)). I could not pull live UK salary data this time. Ask the client for the loaded cost on the discovery call rather than guessing.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* **This market is crowded.** Canals, Y Meadows, Graip, WizCommerce, Endeavor and others sell AI order entry ([WizCommerce list](https://wizcommerce.com/blog/top-ai-order-entry-automation-software/), [Oro](https://oroinc.com/b2b-ecommerce/blog/sales-order-automation/), [Canals](https://www.canals.ai/products/distributor-order-entry), [Endeavor](https://www.endeavor.ai/sales-order-automation)).
* **The gap, honestly stated:**
  1. Many are built for US ERPs and mid market budgets and use custom quotes. A 60 person UK distributor on Business Central, Exact or Unleashed is not their priority.
  2. The hard part is not reading the PDF. It is the customer part number to our SKU mapping, units (case vs each), price list rules, and the ERP write. That needs hands on setup per client. That is a service, which is what you sell.
  3. Y Meadows' free 50 orders a month covers tiny users, so do not target them ([Software Finder](https://softwarefinder.com/artificial-intelligence/y-meadows)).
* **If a client already pays for one of these tools and is happy, walk away.**

**g. Realistic price.** First client 1,500 to 2,500 USD build. After a case study: 3,000 to 5,000 USD. Monthly 300 to 700 USD including hosting and AI usage, with a per order fee above an agreed volume.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

**Edge cases this build must handle on day 1:** multi page PDFs; Excel orders; orders in the email body; two POs in one email; customer resends the same PO; new delivery address (flag, never create); price differs from price list (flag); unknown product (flag); German, Dutch or French text (the AI reads it fine; the approval screen stays in English). Amendments and cancellations are flagged, not applied, in version 1.

**Smallest version that works.**
1. Week 1 to 2: shadow mode. Parse orders from a forwarded inbox into a sheet. Staff keep working. Measure accuracy per field.
2. Week 3 to 4: draft orders in the ERP for the top 10 customers by volume.
3. Week 5 to 6: all customers, exceptions list, duplicate check, daily summary email to the owner of the process.
4. Later, paid as a change: amendments, order confirmations back to the customer.

### Europe workflow 2 (good second product): Supplier order confirmation checked against our purchase order

**a. Who.** Manufacturers and distributors that raise many purchase orders: engineering, food production, furniture, electrical wholesale. **Medium** mostly; the purchasing manager owns it. **Small** only if they raise 20+ POs a week.

**b. Steps today.** Buyer sends a PO from the ERP. Supplier replies with an order confirmation PDF. It lands in one person's inbox and often gets checked late or not at all ([Cflow](https://www.cflowapps.com/purchase-order-acknowledgement/)). A careful buyer compares price, quantity and delivery date line by line and updates the ERP.

**c. Software.** Outlook plus Business Central, Sage 200, Exact or Odoo. Output: confirmed dates written back to the PO (or a draft update) and a mismatch list for the buyer.

**d. How often, how long.** Tens to hundreds of confirmations a week. My estimate: 5 to 10 minutes each when done properly. **Count it:** POs raised per month, and how many confirmations sit unread in the inbox.

**e. Who and cost.** Buyers and purchasing assistants. The real cost is not their time. It is paying a price increase nobody noticed and a late delivery nobody saw coming.

**f. Existing tools and gap.** SER, Doxis and free-com automate this, but they are document management suites for bigger firms ([SER](https://www.sergroup.com/en/solutions/order-confirmation-automation.html), [Doxis](https://www.doxis.com/en/solutions/order-confirmation-automation), [free-com](https://free-com.eu/en/products/order-confirmations/)). I found no cheap SMB tool that does this for Business Central or Exact users. That is a real gap.

**g. Price.** 2,000 to 3,500 USD build, 250 to 500 USD a month.

**Edge case that kills the deal:** if nobody checks confirmations today, the process is "fully manual or skipped", which you said you do not want. **Only sell where a buyer already checks them and complains about it.** Ask: "Who checked the last supplier price increase, and how did you find it?"

**Why it is a strong second product:** same buyers, same ERP connection, same parsing engine as workflow 1. Your second sale to the same client is cheap to build.

### Europe workflow 3 (conditional): Freight rate request to draft quote

Same as India workflow 2, for small UK and Dutch forwarders. The market is crowded with AI quoting vendors (sources in India workflow 2). Rates change weekly, which means constant upkeep. **Only take it if a forwarder comes to you.** Price: 2,500 to 4,000 USD build, 400 to 800 USD a month.

## Part 4: Final picks and plan

### Pick 1: Order to draft sales order (India first, then Europe)
**Why:** It passed every filter. Data already arrives digitally. One owner. Two tools. Draft output keeps a human in control. The business keeps running if we fail. It is countable per order. Same engine sells in both regions, so your Indian proof directly helps European sales. Developer A takes the ERP write, matching logic and AWS; Developer B takes intake, n8n plumbing and the approval sheet.

### Pick 2: Supplier confirmation check (Europe only, upsell)
**Why:** Real gap between enterprise suites and nothing. Reuses Pick 1's parsing and ERP connector. Sell it to Pick 1 clients who are medium manufacturers.

### Not picked
Freight quoting (crowded, unstable data), all accounts payable and remittance ideas (cheap tools exist), timesheets (deadline plus personal data), Tally purchase entry (TaxOne).

### Your 90 day plan

1. **Days 1 to 15.** Find 2 Indian distributors on Tally with at least 80 orders a day. Ludhiana is full of them. Run shadow mode free. Record every number.
2. **Days 15 to 45.** Go live with draft orders at the first one. Get a written quote and screen recording.
3. **Days 30 to 60.** In parallel, contact UK and Dutch distributors on Business Central, Exact or Unleashed. Ask one question: "How many emailed orders a month, and how many people key them?" Aim for 5 real calls.
4. **Days 60 to 90.** Close one European client at 1,500 to 2,500 USD plus 300 to 500 USD a month.

### Stop signs (be honest with yourselves)
* No European sales call in 60 days: the problem is reach, not the product. Fix sales before building more.
* Shadow mode accuracy below 85% of lines correct after 2 weeks of mapping fixes: the client's data is too messy. Do not go live.
* Any client whose ERP has no API: quote the connector cost or say no.
* More than 4 hours a week of support per client: raise the monthly fee or narrow the scope.

### Staying under 50 USD a month
One small cloud server running n8n community edition and your Java or TypeScript service, a domain, and pay per use AI calls during pilots. Xero's free API tier covers your first 5 Xero clients. Each client's hosting, AI usage and WhatsApp fees go into their monthly fee from day 1, so growth never breaks the budget. (Server and AI costs are my estimates; check current prices when you sign up.)
