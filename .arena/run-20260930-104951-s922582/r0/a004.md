# Workflow automations worth selling in India and Europe

Research date: 30 September 2026. Written for a two person, part time team in Ludhiana.

## The short answer

1. **Build one product first: "order inbox to ERP."** Customer orders arrive by email, PDF, Excel or WhatsApp. Today a person reads each one and types it into the accounting or ERP system. You automate the reading, the matching of products and prices, and the checks. A staff member approves each draft order with one click. This is the only idea I found that is strong in both regions, has clear proof of pain and spend, is not fully solved at small business prices, and fits your skills exactly.
2. **Get proof in India, earn in Europe.** Do it first for a Ludhiana distributor or manufacturer on Tally or Busy (WhatsApp and email orders). Then sell the same engine to UK and EU wholesalers and manufacturers on Business Central, Sage 200, Exact Online, Xero or Nordic systems.
3. **Second product, only for medium European manufacturers:** supplier order confirmations checked against purchase orders. Big pain, proven by funded startups, but a longer sale.
4. **Do not sell** supplier invoice entry, payment reminders, temp staff timesheets, freight quoting, or customs entries. Cheap or specialist tools already cover them, or the risk is wrong for you. Details and sources are below.
5. **Your price guess is half right.** 1,500 to 4,000 USD for a first workflow holds for small firms. Your upkeep guess of 400 to 1,500 USD a month is too high for small firms (plan on 250 to 600) and fair for medium firms.
6. **The hard part is selling, not building.** New small business AI order entry tools appeared in 2025 and 2026. Your edge is doing the messy setup work (product code mapping, customer quirks, the client's exact ERP) that a self serve tool leaves to the customer. Pick one ERP and one trade and go deep.

## Where you should be in 12 months, and the path back to today

I started from the finished result and worked backwards, so each step has a reason to exist.

**The finish line (September 2027):**
* 3 to 4 European clients pay a monthly fee for "order inbox to ERP," each at 400 to 1,200 USD a month. That is roughly 2,000 to 4,000 USD of monthly revenue.
* 2 Indian clients have run the workflow for 3+ months. You have before and after numbers: minutes per order, orders per day, share of orders approved with no edits, and errors caught.
* You have one repeatable build: an extraction and checking engine, plus connectors for Tally and for one or two European ERPs.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK, the EU, and nearby countries like Norway and Switzerland. I give extra weight to the UK, the Netherlands, the Nordics and Germany, because their SMEs use cloud ERPs with public APIs and English works for sales in most of them.
2. A workflow "qualifies" only if it passes all 7 of your tests and both checks (proof of spend, and no cheap tool already solving it). I list rejected ideas too, with the reason, so you do not waste time on them.
3. **How the sources were gathered.** I found every link through web search in September 2026. Opening full pages was blocked in my research setup, so the figures come from the search result text for each page. Treat vendor numbers (time saved, error rates) as marketing claims. Before you put any number in a pitch, open the page and check it.
4. Where I give a number without a source (for example orders per day at a typical distributor), I label it as an estimate. You must confirm it in your first discovery calls.
5. Prices exclude VAT and GST.

## Part 1: India

**The honest picture.** Indian owners will compare you to a Tally operator. Those salaries run about ₹12,000 to ₹16,000 a month at entry level and ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 with 1 to 3 years of experience ([Glassdoor job listings](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/india-tally-operator-and-data-entry-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,5_IN115_KO6,35.htm), [Jooble Tally salary](https://in.jooble.org/salary/tally), [Jooble Tally operator salary](https://in.jooble.org/salary/tally-operator)). So your monthly fee must be well below one salary, or you must win on speed and errors. Orders are the place where speed and errors matter most, because a wrong order means wrong goods dispatched, returns, and an angry dealer. That is why orders beat invoices in India.

### India workflow 1: Dealer and customer orders from WhatsApp and email into Tally or Busy

**a. Who has it, and size.** Distributors and wholesalers (FMCG, pharma, hardware, electrical, auto parts, paints), and manufacturers that sell to dealers. In Ludhiana this fits hosiery and garment makers, bicycle and auto parts makers, and hand tool and fastener makers. **Fits both sizes.** Small firms (5 to 49) feel it once order volume passes about 30 a day. Medium firms (50 to 250) often have a whole order desk.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A dealer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp: a text list, a photo of a handwritten list, a voice note, or a PDF. Some come by email.
2. A staff member reads it, works out which product each line means ("blue 10mm, 5 box"), checks stock and the dealer's price list, and types a sales order into Tally or Busy.
3. They reply on WhatsApp to confirm, and pass it to dispatch.
4. The data ends up as a sales order and later a sales invoice in Tally or Busy.

Industry writing describes exactly this: orders arrive "as voice notes, photos of handwritten lists, and casual messages at odd hours," and someone then transfers them into a system, checks stock, confirms with the client and updates dispatch ([HublerX: what is WhatsApp order management](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/what-is-whatsapp-order-management), [Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/), [Wydely](https://www.wydely.io/whatsapp-automation-wholesale-trading-businesses.html)).

**c. Software on each side.**
* In: WhatsApp (Business app today; the Cloud API if you automate), Gmail or Outlook.
* Out: TallyPrime, Busy, or Marg for pharma.
* TallyPrime can be integrated. It runs an HTTP server (default port 9000) that accepts XML to create vouchers, and it returns a log of what was created or rejected ([Tally XML interface](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/DeveloperReference/integration-capabilities/using_XML_interface.htm), [Tally XML request and response formats](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/DeveloperReference/integration-capabilities/case_study_1.htm), [Integration with TallyPrime](https://help.tallysolutions.com/integrate-with-tallyprime/)). Tally usually runs on a PC in the office, so you need a small local connector (a Java or Node service on that PC) that pulls approved orders from your cloud and posts them to Tally.
* Busy: I did not confirm a public API for Busy in this research. Plan on its import features or a local connector, and check before you promise it.

**d. How often and how long.** Estimate, to confirm in discovery: a small distributor gets 30 to 150 order messages a day; a medium one gets several hundred. Vendors say manual handling at mid market firms takes "a manual processing team of 10 to 15 people" with a 12 to 18 percent error rate from retyping ([HublerX top WhatsApp order tools 2026](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)). Treat those as vendor claims. A simple WhatsApp order with 5 to 15 lines usually takes a few minutes to type; photos and voice notes take longer. Measure this yourself on day one of a pilot.

**e. Who does it, and the cost.** An order desk clerk or Tally operator, and sometimes the owner's family member after hours. Cost is ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 a month per person (sources above). The hidden costs are late orders (orders sent at night wait till morning) and wrong dispatches.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* **HublerX** reads text, audio and photo orders and posts to SAP, Oracle, D365 and Tally. It targets mid market manufacturers with ₹200 to ₹2,000 crore turnover ([HublerX search result](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026), [HublerX order management](https://www.hublerx.ai/order-management-software)). No public price. **Gap:** firms below that size.
* **Bizom, FieldAssist and similar sales force apps** make salesmen or retailers order in an app. They are priced per user (Bizom is reported at 60 USD per user a month, or ₹8,000 to ₹15,000) ([Software Finder on Bizom](https://softwarefinder.com/field-service/bizom), [Sortstring comparison](https://sortstring.com/blogs/fieldassist-vs-bizom-vs-beatroute-vs-salesport-2026-matrix)). **Gap:** they need dealers to change habits. Your rule 7 says avoid that. Many dealers will keep sending WhatsApp messages anyway.
* **Fast2SMS Tally plugin and similar** send outgoing invoices, confirmations and reminders from Tally to WhatsApp ([Fast2SMS for distributors](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)). **Gap:** outgoing only. They do not read messy incoming orders.
* **Rupyz, Nexxio** offer order apps and dealer portals with Tally links ([Rupyz](https://rupyz.com/blog/order-management-system-with-whatsapp-tally-integration/), [Nexxio Dealer Connect](https://nexxio.in/dealer-connect.html)). **Gap:** again, dealers must use a portal or a structured catalogue.
* **The real gap you fill:** accept orders in whatever form dealers already send, match them to the item master, check price and stock, and put a draft in Tally for a human to approve. No change for the dealer.

**g. Realistic price in India.**
* Small (5 to 49): ₹25,000 to ₹50,000 setup, then ₹5,000 to ₹10,000 a month. This is a third to a half of one operator's salary.
* Medium (50 to 250): ₹75,000 to ₹1,50,000 setup, then ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 a month. Firms already paying per user for a DMS will see this as cheap.
* Pass WhatsApp message costs through. 2026 India rates are reported at about ₹0.115 per utility message and ₹0.86 per marketing message, plus 18 percent GST and the provider's markup. One 2026 guide also reports that from 1 October 2026 Meta charges service replies at the utility rate, which touches order confirmations ([MyOperator India pricing 2026](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026), [ChatMaxima India rates](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/india/), [AiSensy pricing](https://aisensy.com/pricing)). Check Meta's own rate card before you quote.
* **For your first 1 or 2 clients:** charge only your running costs, in return for a written case study, permission to share numbers, and a reference call with a European prospect.

**h. Sources.** Listed inline above.

**Technical warning.** Reading messages from the normal WhatsApp Business phone app automatically is not allowed. Do not use unofficial WhatsApp Web scrapers; the client's number can be banned. Use the official Cloud API through a provider. Ask your provider whether the client can keep their current number on both the app and the API at the same time (I did not verify this in my research). If not, start with a separate "orders" number or with email orders.

### India workflow 2: Enquiry (RFQ) to quotation for small manufacturers

**a. Who has it, and size.** Job shops and component makers: fasteners, forgings, castings, machined parts, hand tools, textile machinery parts, packaging. Very common in Ludhiana. **Mostly small**, some medium.

**b. Steps today.** A buyer emails or WhatsApps an enquiry with a part list, sizes, quantities and sometimes a drawing. The owner or a sales engineer looks up past quotes in Excel or Tally, applies raw material rates and margins, types a quotation in Excel or Word, and sends it. Many enquiries never get a reply fast enough.

**c. Software.** Email and WhatsApp in; Excel price sheets or Tally item masters; a quotation template out (Excel, Word or Tally quotation voucher).

**d. How often and how long.** Estimate: 5 to 40 enquiries a week at a small maker, 20 to 90 minutes each depending on how many lines and how much lookup. Confirm in discovery.

**e. Who does it, and the cost.** Usually the owner or a senior salesperson, which is the most expensive time in the firm. Slow replies lose orders. This is a case where speed matters more than salary.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** HublerX markets "RFQ to order automation" for manufacturers ([HublerX manufacturing operations](https://www.hublerx.ai/manufacturing-operations-software)), which shows the pain is real, but it aims at larger firms. Generic CRMs store quotes but do not read enquiries or fill in prices. **Gap:** a draft quote built from the firm's own price sheet and past quotes, sent only after the owner approves.

**g. Realistic price.** ₹20,000 to ₹40,000 setup, ₹4,000 to ₹8,000 a month. Harder to sell than workflow 1 because the value is "more orders won," which owners believe less than "fewer hours spent."

**h. Sources.** Above. **Confidence: medium.** The evidence of spend is weaker than for workflow 1. Use it as an add on for a workflow 1 client who also makes goods.

### India workflow 3: Supplier dispatch and delivery date follow up for manufacturers

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers that buy many parts and raw materials. **Medium mostly.**

**b. Steps today.** Purchase staff send POs from Tally or Excel, then chase each supplier on WhatsApp and phone for confirmation and dispatch dates, and update a tracking sheet.

**c. Software.** Tally or Busy (POs), Excel (tracking), WhatsApp and email.

**d, e.** Estimate: tens of open POs a week per buyer, several follow ups each. Done by a purchase executive.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** The same problem is being solved in Europe by funded startups (see Europe workflow 2). I found no Indian evidence or Indian SME tool in my searches.

**g. Price.** ₹30,000 to ₹60,000 setup, ₹8,000 to ₹15,000 a month, only for medium firms.

**h. Sources.** None India specific. **Confidence: low.** I include it because it mirrors a strong European workflow. Validate it in local visits before you build anything.

### Rejected in India, and why

* **Purchase bill entry into Tally, with GST checks.** Vyapar TaxOne (formerly Suvit) already covers data entry automation, client document collection and follow ups, and GST reconciliation, with Tally integration and WhatsApp included. It is reported at about ₹5 to ₹10 per invoice ([Suvit / Vyapar TaxOne pricing](https://www.suvit.io/pricing), [GeekChamp review](https://geekchamp.com/suvit-pricing-reviews-2026/), [Suvit on AI GST reconciliation](https://www.suvit.io/post/ai-gst-reconciliation-tally-automation)). Other tools compete on flat pricing ([TrulyInvoice](https://www.trulyinvoice.com/vs/suvit-alternative)). You cannot beat ₹5 per invoice. Reject.
* **Payment reminders and ledger confirmations from Tally.** Free or cheap Tally WhatsApp plugins already send reminders, receipts and ledger confirmations ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Takkada](https://takkada.com/blog/whatsapp-invoice-format-tally/)). Reject. This is also a single task, not a workflow.
* **CA firm document chasing.** Included in Vyapar TaxOne (source above). Reject.

## Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

**The honest picture.** Europe pays well for labour saved, but it is a more crowded market. Big invoice and order tools are strong at the top end. Rossum's cheapest plan starts at about 18,000 USD a year and Coupa bought it in May 2026 ([Floowed on Rossum pricing](https://www.floowed.com/insights/rossum-pricing), [JustPrompt Rossum review](https://justprompt.io/review/rossum-review)). That leaves room below them. But small business AI order tools now exist too, so your edge must be service and fit, not the AI itself.

### Europe workflow 1: Emailed customer orders into the ERP (the top pick)

**a. Who has it, and size.** Wholesale distributors (building and electrical supplies, food service, packaging, industrial parts, beauty and pharmacy wholesale), and manufacturers selling to trade customers. **Both sizes**, best at 15 to 250 staff. Small firms: the owner or sales manager decides. Medium firms: the head of customer service or operations owns it.

**b. Steps today.**
1. A customer emails a purchase order as a PDF, an Excel file, or text in the email body. Each customer uses their own format and their own product codes.
2. A sales order processor opens it, finds the customer, translates the customer's codes into the firm's codes, checks prices, stock and delivery dates, and types it into the ERP.
3. They send an order confirmation.
4. The data ends up as a sales order in the ERP.

In: Outlook or Gmail (both have mature APIs; you already know this).

**d. How often and how long.** One SMB vendor says a single PO can take 15 to 20 minutes to enter by hand ([StackCube guide](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors)). Estimate: a small distributor gets 20 to 60 emailed orders a day; a medium one gets 100 to 500. At 40 orders a day and 15 minutes each, that is 10 hours of typing a day, which is more than one full time person. Confirm the real numbers in discovery.

**e. Who does it, and the cost.** Sales order processors, customer service reps, or sales admins. In the UK, order processor pay is reported at about £26,800 a year on average ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)), with other sources giving £21,000 to £29,000 ([Talent.com](https://uk.talent.com/salary?job=sales+order+processor), [Prime Appointments](https://prime-appointments.co.uk/sales-order-processor), [PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/UK/Job=Order_Processor/Salary)). Totaljobs listed 754 sales order processor jobs in August 2026 ([Totaljobs](https://www.totaljobs.com/jobs/sales-order-processor), [Jobsite](https://www.jobsite.co.uk/jobs/sales-order-processor)). That is strong proof that firms pay people to do this today. Add employer costs and the true cost is higher.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* **Conexiom:** built for manufacturers and distributors, turns documents into ERP orders ([Conexiom](https://conexiom.com/), [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/205134/Conexiom/)). Aimed at larger firms; price on request.
* **Rossum:** starts at about 18,000 USD a year (sources above).
* **Turian:** AI agent for sales order entry and quotes, used by German mid market firms, price on request ([Turian sales order automation](https://www.turian.ai/sales-order-automation), [OMR reviews](https://omr.com/en/reviews/product/turian-ai)).
* **Newer SMB tools:** StackCube, Lido, Ordermind and others now pitch AI order entry to small distributors ([StackCube](https://blog.stackcube.io/ai-order-entry-software-for-distributors), [Lido comparison](https://www.lido.app/blog/best-sales-order-automation-software), [Ordermind](https://ordermind.ai/blog/ai-order-management-automate-order-entry-from-email-pdf-and-excel)). **This is your real competition. Be honest with yourselves about it.**
* **The gap you can still own:** self serve tools export a file or push to the big ERPs. They leave the hard 20 percent to the customer: mapping each customer's product codes, special price lists, units ("box of 50" versus "each"), split deliveries, and the local ERP setup. A done for you service that owns that mapping and keeps it tuned every month is what a 20 person distributor without an IT team will pay for. That is also exactly what justifies a monthly fee.

**g. Realistic price.**
* Small (15 to 49 staff): 2,000 to 3,500 USD setup, 300 to 600 USD a month.
* Medium (50 to 250 staff): 4,000 to 8,000 USD setup, 800 to 1,500 USD a month, or a per order fee once volume is high.
* Anchor the price on labour saved: if you save half of one order processor (about £13,000 a year), a fee of 500 USD a month pays back fast.

**h. Sources.** Listed inline above.

### Europe workflow 2: Supplier order confirmations checked against purchase orders

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers and wholesalers that buy a lot of parts: machinery, electronics assembly, furniture, food production. **Medium only.** Small firms do not have enough POs to care.

**b. Steps today.** The firm sends POs from the ERP. Suppliers reply with order confirmations by email in their own formats, sometimes with changed prices, quantities, or dates. A buyer reads each one, compares it with the PO, updates the dates in the ERP, and chases suppliers who have not confirmed.

**c. Software.** Outlook or Gmail; the ERP (Business Central, Sage 200, Exact Online and others above; same APIs).

**d. How often and how long.** One case study says confirmation tracking used to take three to four days and now takes less than an hour, and that missing or wrong dates in the ERP stopped planning systems from building reliable schedules ([Nordoon customer story](https://www.nordoon.ai/customer-stories/processing-order-confirmations-ai-agents)). Estimate for a 100 person manufacturer: 50 to 300 POs a month, 5 to 15 minutes each to check, plus chasing.

**e. Who does it, and the cost.** Buyers and purchasing assistants. The bigger cost is production delays from wrong dates.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Turian and Nordoon sell AI agents for exactly this, with human approval ([Turian order confirmations](https://www.turian.ai/use-cases/order-confirmations), [Turian purchase order confirmation agent](https://www.turian.ai/use-cases/ai-agent-for-purchase-order-confirmation), [Nordoon](https://www.nordoon.ai/customer-stories/processing-order-confirmations-ai-agents)). Prices are on request, which usually means enterprise pricing. **Gap:** firms at the lower end of medium, and ERPs these startups do not support. The fact that funded startups exist is proof of the pain, not a reason to avoid it.

**g. Realistic price.** 4,000 to 8,000 USD setup, 800 to 1,500 USD a month.

**h. Sources.** Above. **Why it is second, not first:** the buyer is a purchasing head in a medium firm, the sale takes months, and German firms will want German language support and often on premise ERPs. Sell it later to clients who already trust you with order entry.

### Europe workflow 3: Quote requests to draft quotation (inside sales)

**a. Who has it, and size.** Distributors and manufacturers where customers email "please quote" lists. **Both sizes.**

**b. Steps today.** A request arrives with a list of items. An inside sales rep looks up prices, stock and lead times in the ERP, builds a quote, and sends it.

**c. Software.** Email plus the ERP (same APIs as workflow 1; most of them also expose quotes).

**d, e.** Estimate: 10 to 100 requests a week, 15 to 60 minutes each. Done by inside sales staff, often the same people as order entry.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Turian covers quote generation for mid market firms ([Turian](https://www.turian.ai/)). CPQ tools exist but need clean product rules. **Gap:** the same as workflow 1. It is a natural second workflow for an order entry client, since the engine (read, match products, look up prices) is the same.

**g. Realistic price.** 2,500 to 5,000 USD setup, 400 to 900 USD a month.

**h. Sources.** Above. Confidence: medium.

### Rejected or watch list in Europe, and why

* **Supplier invoice capture into Xero, QuickBooks or Sage.** Dext's business plan is reported at about £24 to £30 a month ([Dext pricing](https://dext.com/uk/business/pricing), [BookkeeperTools](https://bookkeepertools.uk/dext-pricing-uk-2026/), [Receiptflow](https://receiptflow.co/blog/dext-pricing-uk-accounting-practices-2026)). This is the idea you already dropped, and you were right. Reject.

## Part 3: Workflows that appear in both regions, and how to make the case study land

| Workflow | India version | Europe version | Case study strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer orders into the ERP | WhatsApp and email orders into Tally or Busy | Emailed PDF and Excel orders into Business Central, Sage 200, Exact, Xero | **Strong.** The core is identical: read, match products, check prices, human approves, post to ERP. |
| Quote requests to draft quote | Enquiries to Excel or Tally quotations | Quote requests to ERP quotes | Medium. Good add on. |
| Supplier confirmations and follow up | WhatsApp chasing of suppliers | Email confirmations checked against POs | Weak for now. No Indian evidence found. |
| Supplier invoice entry | Tally with GST | Xero, Sage | Do not use. Both regions already have cheap tools. |

**How to make the Indian case study believable to a European buyer:**
1. **Pick an Indian client that also receives orders by email with PDF or Excel attachments**, not only WhatsApp. A Ludhiana manufacturer that supplies OEMs or exporters is ideal. European buyers must see their own situation in it.
2. **Measure before you switch it on.** Log one week of manual handling: orders per day, minutes per order, number of lines, errors found later. Then report the same numbers after 6 weeks.
3. **Report the numbers European buyers care about:** share of orders approved with no edits, minutes per order, time from email to order in the system, errors caught before dispatch. Do not lead with rupees saved; lead with minutes and accuracy.
4. **Show the approval screen and the audit trail.** European buyers (and GDPR) care that a human approves and that every change is logged.
5. **Build a Business Central sandbox demo** with the same engine, so the European buyer sees their own ERP, not Tally.

## Part 4: Final top picks, ranked

### Pick 1: "Order inbox to ERP" (India for proof, Europe for revenue)

**Why it wins:**
* It passes every one of your 7 rules. One owner (the order desk lead), one process (order arrives to order approved), 2 to 3 tools (email or WhatsApp, the ERP, your approval screen), and very countable (orders per day times minutes each).
* Proof of spend is strong in both regions: hundreds of UK job listings for order processors, and whole order desks in Indian distribution.
* Big tools start at about 18,000 USD a year, which is out of reach for small firms. Self serve SMB tools leave the mapping work to the customer.
* It matches your skills: Developer A builds the engine and ERP connectors in Spring Boot with Postgres (you already did KYC document flows, which is the same pattern). Developer B builds the intake in n8n and the approval screen in TypeScript.
* It is the same product in both regions, so the Indian case study directly sells the European version.

**The honest risks:**
* Competition is growing fast. You win on service and fit, not on AI. Specialise in one ERP (I suggest Business Central or Sage 200 for the UK) and one trade (for example building and electrical wholesale).
* Product matching is the hard part. Budget most of your build time for it: customer product codes, synonyms, units, and a "learn from corrections" table in Postgres.
* European buyers will ask about data protection. Host in an EU or UK cloud region, sign a data processing agreement, and never train models on client data.

### Pick 2: Quote requests to draft quotation (add on to Pick 1)

Same engine, same buyer, same ERP connection. Sell it as the second workflow to happy Pick 1 clients in both regions. It raises revenue per client without a new sale.

### Pick 3: Supplier order confirmations (Europe, medium firms, later)

Clear pain and proven by funded startups, but a longer sale to a purchasing head. Start this in month 9 or later, for a client who already trusts you.

### What not to do

* Do not build invoice capture, payment reminders, timesheet tools, freight quoting, or customs tools. Reasons and sources are above.
* Do not sell "general AI automation." Sell one named workflow with a number attached: "We turn your emailed orders into approved Business Central orders. Clients approve most drafts with no edits."
* Do not chase many ERPs at once. Every new ERP is weeks of work.

## Practical checklist

**Discovery questions (ask every prospect, to prove the workflow is countable):**
1. How many orders arrive a day, and through which channels?
2. How many lines does a typical order have?
3. How long does one order take to enter? Can we time five?
4. Who does it, and how many people?
5. How many orders had mistakes last month, and what did a mistake cost?
6. Which ERP and version? Cloud or on premise? Who holds admin access?
7. Do customers use your product codes or their own?

**Running costs under 50 USD a month:**
* One small cloud server for n8n (self hosted, free software), your Spring Boot service, and Postgres.
* LLM calls paid per use, passed through to clients once they pay.
* Xero's Starter API tier is free for up to 5 connections ([Xero developer pricing](https://developer.xero.com/pricing)). Business Central and Exact Online sandboxes cost you nothing to test on if the client or a partner gives access (check the terms for each).
* WhatsApp messages in India are paid per message and should be billed to the client.

**Pilot rules:**
* Draft only. Nothing is posted to the ERP without a human click, until the client asks for auto approval of simple orders.
* Every draft shows the original message next to the extracted lines, with low confidence fields highlighted.
* Keep a log of every correction; it is your training data and your case study.
