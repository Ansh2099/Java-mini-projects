# Workflow Automations SMBs Will Pay For: India and Europe (30 September 2026)

## The short answer

Build one engine and sell it in both regions: **turning messy incoming orders into draft orders in the client's own system, checked line by line, with a human approving anything odd.**

* **Europe (paid):** customer purchase orders that arrive by email as PDF or Excel, typed into the ERP by an order desk. Second product for medium firms: checking supplier order confirmations against your own purchase orders.
* **India (proof of work):** retailer orders that arrive on WhatsApp as text, photos, or voice notes, typed into Tally, Busy, or Marg by billing staff. Also B2B manufacturers and exporters who get buyer POs by email.

Why this and not invoices: across Europe, supplier invoices are being forced into structured e-invoice formats by law (Germany, Belgium, Poland, France). Invoice capture is also already cheap (Hubdoc comes with Xero; Dext starts around £24 a month). No law covers **purchase orders, order confirmations, or WhatsApp orders**. Those stay messy, and messy is where you get paid.

The hard truth: this space is filling up with funded startups. Your edge is not the AI. Your edge is setup work that software companies skip: each customer's own item codes, odd ERPs, local languages, and a person who answers the phone. Price and contract for that.

## How to read this document

1. **Sources.** Every link below came up in live web searches run on 30 September 2026. The network blocked opening most pages in full, so many claims rest on the search result text for that page. Vendor blogs are marketing. Treat their numbers as a starting point, not proof. Where it matters, I say how strong the evidence is.
2. **Assumption labels.** Anything marked **(Assumption)** is my judgment and was not checked against a source. Check it before you quote it to a client.
3. **The one rule that protects you.** Before you quote any ROI to a client, count their real volume: open their inbox or WhatsApp, count the last 30 days of orders, and time 10 of them. Every number in this document gets replaced by theirs.

## Assumptions I made

1. "Would pay for" means paying you as a service (build fee plus monthly upkeep), not buying a SaaS license from you.
2. "Europe" means the EU plus the UK, and English speaking buyers first (UK, Ireland, Netherlands, Nordics), because you can sell and support them without a local language. Germany is the biggest prize but needs German.
3. A "few weeks of part time work" means about 60 to 120 total hours for the first build.
4. "Evidence of spend" can be salaries, job ads, or paid tools that exist for the same task.
5. Your 50 USD a month budget covers your own tools only. Once a client pays, their running costs (hosting, AI calls, API fees) go into their monthly fee.

---

## The filter every idea went through

An idea passed only if it met all of these:

1. One process, clear start and end.
2. Two to three tools at most.
3. One owner who feels the pain.
4. A human approves exceptions.
5. Buildable in a few weeks part time.
6. Countable (how often, how long).
7. Already part digital: the data exists in email, PDF, Excel, WhatsApp, or software, and a person retypes it.
8. **Not already solved by a built in feature or a cheap tool.** If a tool under about 50 USD a month does it, it failed.

# Part 1: India

## India pick 1: WhatsApp orders from retailers into Tally, Busy, or Marg

**Verdict: good for proof of work. Only sell it where volume is high or mistakes are expensive.**

**a. Who has it.** Distributors and wholesalers of FMCG goods, pharma, hardware and sanitary goods, electricals, and auto spare parts. Fits **small** (owner run, 10 to 40 staff) and **medium** distributors (50+ staff, a billing or sales ops head owns it). WhatsApp is already the default order channel for Indian distributors ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/), [Sunray Datalinks](https://sunraydatalinks.com/blog/whatsapp-order-management-system-for-distributors-wholesalers/)).

**b. Steps today.**
1. A retailer or field salesman sends an order on WhatsApp. It may be typed text in Hinglish, a photo of a handwritten list, or a voice note, often at odd hours ([LineNow](https://www.linenow.co/blog/guides/whatsapp-supplier-ordering-smb)).
2. A billing clerk reads it, works out which item "Surf 1kg bada" means in the item master, checks stock and that retailer's rate or scheme.
3. The clerk types a sales order or invoice into Tally, Busy, or Marg.
4. Someone confirms back on WhatsApp and passes it to dispatch.
Data ends up in the accounting software. The WhatsApp chat is the only record of what was asked for.

**c. Software on each side.**
* In: WhatsApp Business app. Meta's "coexistence" now lets the same number run on the app and the Cloud API at once, and India is supported, so the client keeps their number and chat history ([MSG91](https://msg91.com/guide/unlock-whatsapp-coexistence-use-whatsapp-business-app-and-cloud-api-together), [Meta docs](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users/)).
* Out: **TallyPrime** accepts XML over HTTP on port 9000 on the local machine; it can create vouchers but has no webhooks and runs on a local PC ([Tally XML integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/), [AI Accountant guide](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/tally-api-integration-guide)). **Busy** says it has no default third party integration; you need a channel partner or add on ([Busy FAQ](https://busy.in/faqs/third-party-services/integration/1/), [BusyNotify](https://busynotify.in/solutions/custom-apis)). **Marg** has an API gateway covering order details, bills, stock and outstandings ([Marg API](https://margcompusoft.com/Package/Packages.aspx)).
* Best first targets: **Tally or Marg users.** Avoid Busy until you have a working add on route.

**d. How often and how long.** (Assumption) A mid size distributor gets 50 to 300 order messages a day, and each takes 3 to 6 minutes to decode and type, more for photos. Some sources claim WhatsApp order automation cuts processing time by 60 to 80 percent ([Fast2SMS](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-for-distributors/)); that is a vendor claim.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Billing clerks or data entry staff. In Ludhiana, data entry operators with Tally skills are offered ₹15,000 to ₹17,000 a month, and assistant accountants ₹18,000 to ₹25,000 ([Indeed Ludhiana, Tally data entry](https://in.indeed.com/q-accountant-tally,-data-entry-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html), [Indeed Ludhiana, assistant accountant](https://in.indeed.com/q-assistant-accountant-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)).

Worked example (Assumption on volume): 150 orders a day × 4 minutes = 10 hours a day, so about 1.25 clerks, roughly ₹20,000 to ₹25,000 a month in salary. **Brutal honesty:** at 50 orders a day, this is less than one clerk's salary. The owner will hire a ₹15,000 clerk and he will be right. Automation wins only when:
* volume is high (100+ orders a day) or has sharp peaks before the dispatch cutoff,
* wrong items or wrong rates cost real money (pharma batches, scheme mistakes, returns),
* the business depends on one clerk who knows all the item nicknames, and that clerk can leave.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* Order apps like Rupyz, Nexxio Dealer Connect, and HublerX give retailers a catalog to order from and sync to Tally ([Rupyz](https://rupyz.com/blog/order-management-system-with-whatsapp-tally-integration/), [Nexxio](https://nexxio.in/dealer-connect.html), [HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)). **Gap:** they need the retailer to change how they order. Many kirana owners will keep sending a photo of a list. That is a "fully manual behavior change" problem, which you said you avoid; the order apps have it, you do not.
* Outbound WhatsApp from Tally (invoices, reminders, ledgers) is solved: TallyPrime with WhatsApp costs ₹1,300 plus GST, and the Fast2SMS plugin is free plus ₹0.25 per message ([TallyHelp price list](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tallyprime-with-whatsapp-price-list/), [Fast2SMS cost](https://www.fast2sms.com/help/tally-whatsapp-cost-calculator/)). **Do not sell outbound messaging.**
* Your gap: **read the messy inbound order as it arrives today**, match every line to the item master and that retailer's rate, create a draft, and hold anything unclear for a human.

**g. Realistic price.** ₹25,000 to ₹60,000 setup, plus ₹3,000 to ₹8,000 a month. (Assumption, anchored to these facts: Tally customisation runs from ₹2,000 to ₹50,000+ per job ([IndiaMART listing](https://dir.indiamart.com/impcat/tally-customization-services.html)); a TallyPrime Silver license is ₹22,500 one time with ₹4,500 a year renewal ([Markit Solutions](https://www.markitsolutions.in/blogs/tally-renewal-charges-2026-guide)).) Expect pushback above ₹5,000 a month from small owners. For your first 2 or 3 clients, charge at cost in exchange for a written case study with real numbers.

Running cost watch: from **1 October 2026 (tomorrow)**, Meta starts charging for service and utility messages in the open 24 hour window in India, about ₹0.115 each after a free allowance ([MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026), [WatEase](https://watease.com/blog/whatsapp-business-platform-pricing-india)). Small money, but build it into the monthly fee. Sources disagree on the free allowance; check Meta's page before quoting ([WhatsApp pricing](https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/platform-pricing/)).

**h. Sources.** Listed inline above and in the source list at the end.

## India pick 2: Buyer purchase orders into sales orders for B2B manufacturers and exporters

**Verdict: the best Indian case study for European buyers, because it is the same workflow.**

**a. Who has it.** Auto component, fastener, hand tool, bicycle part, and hosiery makers that sell to OEMs, distributors, or foreign buyers. Ludhiana has over 1.5 lakh registered MSMEs across hosiery, engineering goods, bicycles, auto components and hand tools ([SME Communities](https://smecommunities.com/ludhiana-punjabs-industrial-powerhouse-driving-indias-next-sme-revolution/)); the bicycle industry alone is about ₹3,500 crore of exports ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/ludhiana/cycle-industry-seeks-revival-amid-export-challenges)). Fits **both**: small (owner and one clerk) and medium (sales coordination or export team).

**b. Steps today.**
1. A buyer emails a PO as PDF or Excel. Foreign buyers use their own item codes.
2. A sales coordinator or export documentation executive matches buyer codes to internal items, checks price against the agreed rate list, and checks delivery date.
3. They type a sales order in Tally or Zoho Books and add a row to the order tracking sheet in Excel or Google Sheets.
4. For exports, the same data is retyped again into a proforma invoice and later the packing list.

**c. Software.** In: Gmail or Outlook with PDF or Excel attachments. Out: TallyPrime (XML, as above) or Zoho Books, plus an order register sheet. That is 3 tools.

**d. How often and how long.** (Assumption) 5 to 40 POs a week for a small maker, more for a medium one. 15 to 45 minutes each when there are many lines, because buyer codes must be looked up. Measure this at the client.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** A sales coordinator or export documentation executive. A Ludhiana posting offers ₹30,000 to ₹35,000 a month for 3 to 5 years of documentation experience ([ABP Empower](https://www.abpempower.in/job-openings-for-export-import-documentation-executive-ludhiana-1299973.htm)); there are many such listings ([Indeed Ludhiana export jobs](https://in.indeed.com/Export-Import-jobs-in-Ludhiana,-Punjab)). This role is pricier than a billing clerk, and a wrong price or date on a foreign order costs far more than the salary. That is why automation can beat "hire another person" here.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Export document software (ExpoMaster, EximDoc, Export EMS, Eximly) already generates proformas, invoices, and packing lists, some at a one time cost ([ExpoMaster](https://expomaster.in/), [EximDoc](https://eximdoc.in/), [Export EMS](https://www.visualexportems.com/), [Eximly](https://www.eximly.in/blog/best-export-import-software-india-comparison)). **So do not sell document generation.** The gap is the step before it: **reading the buyer's PO** and turning it into clean order data with code mapping and price and date checks. None of those tools claim to do that in the search results I saw.

**g. Realistic price.** ₹30,000 to ₹75,000 setup, ₹4,000 to ₹10,000 a month. (Assumption.) Exporters earn in foreign currency and are used to paying for documentation software, so they pay more than domestic distributors.

# Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

## Europe pick 1: Customer PO emails into draft sales orders

**Verdict: your lead product. Real pain, real spend, tools exist but are priced for big firms or built for US ERPs.**

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors (industrial, electrical, packaging, food service, building supplies) and small manufacturers selling B2B. Fits **both**: small (owner or office manager feels it) and medium (customer service or order desk lead owns it).

**b. Steps today.** Orders arrive as email attachments, spreadsheets, and PDFs in each customer's own format. Someone opens each one, identifies the customer, looks up item codes, checks price and stock, and keys it into the ERP line by line, then sends a confirmation ([ChannelFlex](https://channelflex.com/blog/sales-order-automation-for-distributors-and-manufacturers/), [Y Meadows](https://ymeadows.com/en/insights/blog/ai-order-entry-for-distributors/), [Hyperfox](https://www.hyperfox.com/insights/the-real-cost-of-manual-order-entry)). Data ends up in the ERP as a sales order.

**c. Software on each side and API status.**
* In: Outlook or Gmail (both have good APIs).
* Out, the ERP. This decides whether you can build it:

| ERP | Where common | Can you create sales orders by API? |
|---|---|---|
| Exact Online | Netherlands, Belgium | Yes. REST SalesOrders endpoint, JSON, returns 201 on create ([Exact KB](https://support.exactonline.com/community/s/article/All-All-DNO-Content-rest-api-business-cases-rest-bsncs-slsord)) |
| Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central | UK and EU mid market | Yes. Standard v2.0 API ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/api/dynamics_purchaseorderline_update)) |
| Odoo | Across EU | Yes. XML-RPC and JSON-RPC ([Knit guide](https://getknit.dev/blog/odoo-api-integration-guide-in-depth/)) |
| weclapp | Germany | Yes. REST API with 150+ entities ([weclapp on GitHub](https://github.com/api-evangelist/weclapp)) |
| Unleashed (with Xero) | UK, wholesalers | Yes. Full API on every plan ([WhiteBox](https://whitebox.sg/unleashed-vs-cin7/)) |
| Sage 50 (UK desktop) | UK small firms | **Hard.** No REST API; writes need the Sage SDK or a paid third party connector ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-integration/), [Sage City](https://communityhub.sage.com/gb/sage-50-accounts/f/general-discussion-uk/177497/importing-of-sales-orders-is-theoretically-possible)) |
| Xero alone | UK | (Assumption) Xero has no true sales order object; you would create quotes or draft invoices. Xero's new developer pricing gives a free Starter tier capped at 5 connections, then Core at about 35 AUD a month for 50 ([Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/xero-shifts-to-tiered-pricing-model-for-developers), [Databuzz](https://www.databuzz.com.au/changes-to-the-xero-developer-pricing/)) |

**Rule:** qualify on the ERP first. Say no to Sage 50 desktop clients until you have one paid project that can absorb a connector cost.

**d. How often and how long.** Industry figures: 5 to 8 minutes per order end to end, 8 to 30 minutes per order by IOFM and APQC benchmarks, depending on complexity; error rates of 1 to 4 percent of order lines ([Hyperfox](https://www.hyperfox.com/insights/the-real-cost-of-manual-order-entry), [Ordana](https://ordana.ai/faq/cost-of-manual-order-entry), [appse ai](https://appse.ai/blog/reduce-manual-order-entry-errors-erp)). These come from vendors; measure your own.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Order processors or customer service staff. UK average order processor salary is £26,832 a year (Indeed, May 2026), with other sources at £20,000 to £27,000 ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries), [PayScale](https://www.payscale.com/research/UK/Job=Order_Processor/Salary)).

Worked example: 40 POs a day × 8 minutes = 5.3 hours a day, about 0.7 of a full time person. At £26,832 plus about 20 percent employer costs (Assumption), that is about £22,500 a year. If you remove 60 percent of the keying, the saving is about £13,500 a year, or £1,100 a month. That supports a £400 to £700 monthly fee. Below about 15 POs a day, the saving is too small for a medium price; sell only to small firms at the low end.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* **Conexiom:** typical deployments $10,000 to $100,000+ a year, multi year contracts, and reviewers say ROI does not clear for 5 to 50 person distributors ([ERP Research](https://www.erpresearch.com/erp-add-ons/distribution/conexiom), [Software Finder](https://softwarefinder.com/sales-tools/conexiom)).
* **OrderDrafter:** $399 a month for about 300 orders, $799 for about 1,000, but built for Epicor Prophet 21, a US ERP ([OrderDrafter](https://orderdrafter.com/)).
* **German players:** Workist, Esker, TCG Process DPS Order, Konfuzio, smartextract, Algonaut ORDERFLOW ([Workist](https://www.workist.com/blog/wie-ki-edi-sinnvoll-ergaenzt-auftragserfassung), [TCG Process](https://www.tcgprocess.de/solution/kundenbestellungen/), [Industry Press](https://industry-press.com/orderflow-automatisiert-erp-auftragserfassung-im-grosshandel/)). Pricing not public in what I found.
* **Gap:** small and medium firms on Exact, Business Central, Odoo, weclapp, or Unleashed who want someone to set it up for them, map their customers' item codes, and fix it when a customer changes their PO layout. Products sell software. You sell a working workflow.
* **Warning:** this gap is closing. Expect ERP vendors and startups to cover more of it within 12 to 24 months (Assumption). Keep contracts short, recover your build cost in the setup fee, and keep adding value through upkeep (new customer formats, monthly accuracy report).

**g. Realistic price.** Small: $1,500 to $2,500 build, $300 to $500 a month. Medium: $3,000 to $5,000 build, $600 to $1,200 a month tied to order volume tiers (copy OrderDrafter's per volume model so the buyer can compare).

## Europe pick 2: Supplier order confirmations checked against purchase orders

**Verdict: strong second product for medium firms. Less crowded below SAP level. Needs German or Dutch if you go to those markets.**

**a. Who has it.** Manufacturers and distributors with a purchasing team buying from many suppliers. Mainly **medium** (a purchasing lead owns it). Small firms with one buyer have it too, but the volume is lower.

**b. Steps today.** The ERP sends a PO to the supplier. The supplier replies with an order confirmation PDF (Auftragsbestätigung in German). A buyer opens it and compares every line: item, quantity, price, delivery date. They update the expected date in the ERP and chase the supplier if anything differs. Many POs never get a confirmation at all, so buyers also chase silence ([Pexon](https://pexon-consulting.de/blog/auftragsbestaetigung-automatisieren/), [Leverage](https://tryleverage.ai/blog/pf/po-acknowledgement-automation-hybrid-edi-1)).

**c. Software.** In: email. Out: the ERP's purchase order lines (Business Central API supports updating purchase order lines ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/dev-itpro/api-reference/v2.0/api/dynamics_purchaseorderline_update)); weclapp has a Purchase Order API ([APIs.io](https://apis.io/providers/weclapp/)); Odoo via RPC). Optional third tool: a Teams or email alert for exceptions.

**d. How often and how long.** A German case: buyers spent about 15 minutes per confirmation, and one firm (esw) spent 25 to 30 hours a week on it, cut to under 5 hours after automation ([Mittelstand Cafe](https://www.mittelstandcafe.de/perzeptron-automatisiert-ab-kontrolle-bei-esw-pr-faufwand-sinkt-mit-occ-ai-um-ber-80-prozent-2252271.html), [SCM](https://scm-supply-chain-management.de/presse/prufaufwand-sinkt-mit-occ-ai-um-uber-80-prozent/)). A US vendor says chasing suppliers takes 20 to 30 percent of buyer time at mid market manufacturers and up to half of POs never get a confirmation ([Leverage](https://tryleverage.ai/blog/pf/reduce-supplier-expediting-time)). Both are vendor sources.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Buyers and purchasing assistants. (Assumption) Their salaries are higher than order processors, so the value per hour saved is higher. Get a salary figure for the client's country before quoting.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** OCC.AI (Perzeptron), Doxis, DocBits, TCG DPS Order Response, and SAP focused add ons ([Doxis](https://www.doxis.com/en/solutions/order-confirmation-automation), [DocBits](https://docbits.com/en/order-confirmation-automation/), [henrichsen4s](https://www.henrichsen4s.de/sap-loesungen/einkauf/auftragsbestaetigung)). Leverage serves US mid market. **Gap:** medium firms on Business Central, Odoo, weclapp, or Exact that are too small for an enterprise document platform.

**g. Realistic price.** $3,000 to $5,000 build, $600 to $1,200 a month. (Assumption.)

## Europe: real but not recommended as a lead

* **Freight forwarder quote requests** (email RFQ, look up rates in Excel, draft a quote). The pain is real; one case cut quote time from 4.5 hours to 40 minutes ([Wisor](https://wisor.ai/how-to-speed-up-freight-quotes/)). But it is packed with funded products: Wisor, Cargofive, FreightMynd, Pallet, Info-X, Quotable ([FreightMynd list](https://freightmynd.com/blog/best-ai-freight-quoting-software-2026/), [Cargofive](https://cargofive.com/ai-powered-freight-quoting/)). You also need freight domain knowledge you do not have. Take it only if a forwarder comes to you.
* **UK accountants and Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.** It started 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords over £50,000, falling to £30,000 in 2027 and £20,000 in 2028 ([Bishop Fleming](https://www.bishopfleming.co.uk/insights/making-tax-digital-income-tax-april-2026)). The first quarterly deadline (7 August 2026) exposed messy client records and late submissions ([AccountingWEB](https://www.accountingweb.co.uk/community/industry-insights/mtd-itsa-7-august-2026-deadline-first-quarterly-update-checklist-for)). But bookkeeping capture is already covered by Xero, Dext, and bridging software, and the rest is chasing clients. **Watch it for 2027**, when the client count grows. Not a pick today.

# Part 3: Workflows that appear in both regions

| Workflow | India version | Europe version | Does the Indian case study convince a European buyer? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Customer PO to draft sales order** | Manufacturer or exporter gets buyer POs by email, keys into Tally or Zoho | Distributor gets customer POs by email, keys into Exact, BC, Odoo | **Yes, strongly.** Same inputs (PDF and Excel POs, often from European buyers), same checks, same metrics. Only the ERP changes. |
| **Messy order to draft sales order** | Retailer WhatsApp text, photo, voice | Email body orders, photos of order pads, WhatsApp in some trades | **Partly.** Shows you can handle ugly inputs and item nicknames. European buyers will care about the email PDF version more. |
| **Supplier price list updates** (bolt on) | Company price lists and schemes arrive as PDF or Excel on WhatsApp; staff update rates in Marg or Busy ([Marg help](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/rate-and-discount-master/4694/1/How-to-Update-Item-price)) | Suppliers send new price lists, sometimes every two weeks, in many Excel formats ([ERP Software Blog](https://erpsoftwareblog.com/2024/04/case-how-to-effortlessly-import-supplier-excel-files-in-bc-without-manipulation/)) | **Yes, as an add on.** Lower frequency, so weak as a first sale, but it reuses the same item mapping engine. # Where this plan breaks, and the fix

I attacked the plan with five hostile cases. Each one broke something. Here is the patch for each.

**1. The AI reads "10" as "100", or maps to the wrong item.**
What breaks: a wrong order ships and the client blames you.
Fix: the system never posts a final order. It creates a **draft only**. Every line is checked against the item master, that customer's price, and a normal quantity range for that customer. Any unknown item code, price difference, or odd quantity puts the whole order on **hold** with the reason shown next to the original PDF text. The PO total must match the sum of lines or it holds. If it cannot decide, it asks; it never guesses.

**2. The target system is down (Tally PC switched off, ERP token expired, API limit reached).**
What breaks: orders silently fail to post and nobody notices until a customer calls.
Fix: every incoming order gets an ID and sits in a queue until the ERP confirms it. If posting fails, the owner gets an alert within 15 minutes. Every evening, the system sends a count: "orders received 42, drafted 42, posted 40, on hold 2". A mismatch is loud by design. If the automation stops, the client falls back to typing by hand, the same as today. Nothing is lost.

**3. The buyer says "a clerk is cheaper" (India) or "our ERP will do this next year" (Europe).**
What breaks: the sale, or worse, you build it and they cancel.
Fix: qualify before building. India: at least 100 orders a day or a clear cost of errors. Europe: at least 15 POs a day or one person spending half their day on it. Price below a third of the measured saving. Use 3 to 6 month contracts, not long lock ins, and recover the build cost in the setup fee.

**4. GDPR stops a European client from sending data to India.**
What breaks: the deal dies in legal review. India has no EU adequacy decision, so transfers need Standard Contractual Clauses and a transfer risk assessment ([EDPB guide](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/international-data-transfers_en), [DPDPA.com](https://www.dpdpa.com/blogs/india_eu_data_adequacy_dpdpa_gdpr.html)).
Fix: host each European client's workflow in an EU cloud region, use an AI provider that offers EU processing and a data processing agreement, sign a DPA with SCCs, and keep client data off your laptops. Put this on one page and hand it over before they ask.

**5. A customer changes their PO layout, or a new customer sends a format never seen before.**
What breaks: extraction quality drops quietly over weeks.
Fix: track the share of lines the human corrects, per customer, every week. If it rises above a set level (for example 5 percent), alert yourselves and fix it. This monthly accuracy report is also what justifies the upkeep fee.

Two more risks you cannot fully patch, so plan around them:
* **Part time support.** India is 4.5 hours ahead of the UK in summer and 3.5 hours ahead of Central Europe. You will cover their mornings well and their afternoons badly. Promise next business day fixes, not live support, and rely on the safe fallback in point 2.
* **Weak evidence.** Most time and error numbers here come from vendors selling automation. That is why the rule at the top matters: measure the client's own numbers before you promise anything.
