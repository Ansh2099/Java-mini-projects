# Workflow automations to sell: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Sell one kind of workflow in two versions. The first version handles **orders coming in**. The second handles **order confirmations from suppliers**. Both are messy messages that a person reads and retypes into the business system today. Both have a clear start and end, touch 2 or 3 tools, have one owner, and are easy to count.

1. **Lead offer in Europe: "Customer order email to draft sales order."** A PDF, Excel or plain email order arrives. Our agent reads it, matches the customer and every item to the ERP, builds a draft sales order and flags problems. A person approves it. Target firms whose ERP has **no** built in order agent, or whose orders are too big for the one that exists.
2. **Proof of work in India: "WhatsApp order to Tally sales order"** for distributors and wholesalers in and around Ludhiana. This is the same engine with a different input (WhatsApp text, voice notes, photos) and a different output (Tally). It gives you real numbers to show European buyers.
3. **Second offer in Europe, mainly medium firms: "Supplier order confirmation check and chasing."** The supplier replies to a purchase order with a confirmation PDF. Our agent compares it line by line with the PO, flags changed prices, quantities and dates, drafts the update, and drafts chasers for suppliers who have not confirmed. The buyer approves.

Here is the hard truth behind these picks. Microsoft now ships AI agents inside Dynamics 365 Business Central that do email order intake (Sales Order Agent) and overhead invoice entry (Payables Agent). They cost cents per document. Invoice capture is also being squeezed by e-invoicing laws. So stay away from generic "AI invoice processing". Also do not pitch order intake to a Business Central shop with simple orders. Your openings are in the gaps those tools leave, and the gaps are listed below.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the EU plus the UK, Switzerland and Norway. I treat the UK, the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany and the Nordics (Finland first, because Developer A already has a Finnish client) as the first targets. English works in these markets, and so do the Microsoft agents, which cover English, German, Dutch, Finnish and more. So the language gap is small and you must win on fit, not on language.
2. "A few weeks of part time work" means 3 to 6 weeks for two people working about 15 hours a week each.
3. Where I give a salary, volume or time per task without a link, it is a stated **estimate** for you to check on the first sales call. I label these clearly. I did not invent sources.
4. **Research limits, stated plainly.** My web search budget ran out after 6 searches. Most websites (job boards, salary sites, vendor pricing pages, government pages) were blocked for page fetch in my session. Microsoft Learn and GitHub worked, so most hard facts below come from primary Microsoft, Tally and GitHub documentation. Some India sources were only seen as search results, not opened. The Sources section marks which is which. The section "What I could not verify" lists what to check before you spend money.

## How I judged each idea

A workflow made the list only if it passed all of these tests:

1. It meets your 7 rules: one process with a clear start and end, 2 or 3 tools, one owner, a human approves exceptions, a few weeks to build, countable, and already partly digital.
2. Some business already spends money on it: staff time, a paid tool, or a big vendor building a product for it.
3. **No built in feature or cheap tool already covers it for that buyer.** When one does, I rejected the idea or narrowed the target buyer.
4. It does not move money or make legal, medical or lending decisions.
5. It survives the e-invoicing laws now rolling out across Europe. Those laws turn invoices into structured data and kill PDF reading for invoices. They do **not** cover customer orders or supplier order confirmations. That is a big reason my picks sit on the order side, not the invoice side.

## Part 1: India

### India workflow 1: WhatsApp order to Tally sales order (distributors and wholesalers)

**a. Who has it, and size.** FMCG, pharma, hardware, auto parts, electrical and textile distributors and wholesalers who take retailer orders on WhatsApp and phone and bill in Tally (also Busy or Marg). Fits **both** sizes. Small (5 to 49 staff): the owner decides, and 1 or 2 billing staff key orders. Medium (50 to 250): the sales operations or billing head owns it, and vendor blogs describe order desks of 10 to 15 people at the larger end.

**b. The steps today.**
1. A retailer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp. It may be a text list, a voice note, a photo of a handwritten list, or a forwarded PDF. Local item names, pack sizes ("5 peti", "2 dozen") and mixed Hindi, Punjabi and English are normal.
2. A billing person reads it, works out which items and which pack sizes are meant, checks the rate and stock, and keys a sales order or invoice into Tally.
3. They reply on WhatsApp to confirm, often after a delay, and send the invoice PDF.
4. The data ends up in Tally. The WhatsApp chat is the only record of what was actually asked for.

Vendor blogs found in my search describe this exact pattern. Orders from calls and WhatsApp are keyed into Tally by hand, the process is "error-prone and hours late", and at mid-market firms "hundreds of messages per day" lead to manual teams of 10 to 15 people. These are vendors selling a fix, so treat their numbers as a claim, not a study.

**c. Software on each side.** In: WhatsApp Business app, or the WhatsApp Business API through a provider. Out: TallyPrime. Tally accepts data through XML over HTTP (TallyPrime must be running with its HTTP server on, for example port 9000, and a company loaded). Since TallyPrime 7.0 it also accepts native JSON. Tally is a desktop program, so you need a small connector on the client's PC or server that talks to Tally locally. That is 2 tools plus your approval screen.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** A small distributor: 30 to 150 orders a day. A medium one: 200 to 1,000 or more. Keying one order of 5 to 20 lines takes roughly 2 to 6 minutes, plus back and forth when an item is unclear. Example: 100 orders a day at 4 minutes each is about 6.7 hours a day, close to one full time person. **Measure this on day one.** Count a week of WhatsApp orders and time 20 of them being keyed.

**e. Who does it and what it costs (estimate).** Billing or data entry staff. In Punjab I assume ₹12,000 to ₹22,000 a month per person. That is my estimate. I could not open job boards, so check Naukri or Indeed for "billing executive Ludhiana" before you quote. Here is the brutal part. Cutting one clerk saves only about ₹15,000 a month, so **labor savings alone will not sell this in India.** Sell speed (confirm and dispatch within the hour), fewer wrong items and returns, and staff who stop being the bottleneck in peak season.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. TallyPrime (Release 4.0 and later) can **send** invoices, orders, reports and payment reminder letters on WhatsApp. It does **not** read free text orders and turn them into vouchers. Bulk and automatic sending needs extra WhatsApp API services or add-ons.
2. Paid products already target this exact job. SalesOn says 1,100+ Indian distributors, FMCG firms and manufacturers use it, with orders synced to Tally. Zotok says its AI handles unstructured WhatsApp orders and connects to Tally with no code. HublerX says it handles unstructured WhatsApp messages with ERP integration including Tally. Interakt lists a Tally integration.
3. **So this is not an empty market.** The gap you can honestly claim is narrow. It is a custom fit to one distributor's messy item names, their Busy or Marg setup, their approval rules, and voice or photo orders, delivered as a service by people 20 minutes away. I could not see prices for these products. If a prospect already uses one of them, walk away. This workflow is on the list mainly because it is **the best proof of work for the European order intake offer**, not because India is a gold mine.

**g. Realistic price in India (estimate).** Setup ₹30,000 to ₹75,000. Monthly ₹4,000 to ₹10,000, plus WhatsApp API message costs passed through at cost. For your first 1 or 2 clients, charge a token amount (for example ₹10,000 setup) in exchange for a written case study, permission to share the numbers, and a video testimonial.

**h. Sources.** Tally XML integration prerequisites, Tally XML integration, TallyPrime 7.0 JSON integration, TallyPrime with WhatsApp, the Tally help page on sharing documents via WhatsApp, the Interakt and Tally post, the Fast2SMS, HublerX and Sunray Datalinks blogs, and the SalesOn, Zotok and HublerX product pages. Links are in the Sources section.

### India workflow 2: Supplier PO confirmation and dispatch follow up (manufacturers)

This one is weaker. I include it only as the Indian twin of Europe workflow 2.

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers that buy many inputs from many suppliers: bicycle and bicycle parts, auto components, hosiery and knitwear, hand tools, machinery (all big in Ludhiana). Mostly **medium**, because small firms have too few POs to justify it.

**b. The steps today.** A purchase order is made in Tally (or Excel) and sent to the supplier as a PDF on WhatsApp or email. The supplier replies on WhatsApp with a confirmation, a changed rate, a part quantity or a new dispatch date. A purchase executive reads it, updates an Excel tracker (and sometimes the Tally order due date), and phones or messages suppliers who have not confirmed or are late. Data ends up in Excel and Tally.

**c. Software.** WhatsApp or Gmail in, Tally or Google Sheets out. 2 or 3 tools.

**d. Frequency and time (estimate).** 20 to 100 open POs a week at a medium plant, 3 to 5 minutes per confirmation to check and record, plus daily chasing.

**e. Who does it and cost (estimate).** A purchase executive at about ₹18,000 to ₹35,000 a month. They do not do this full time. The real cost of doing it badly is a stopped production line because a late supplier was not chased in time.

**f. Existing tools and gap.** I found no tool built for this for Tally users, but my search was cut short, so treat this as unverified. Microsoft built an agent for exactly this job, but only in its large company ERP (see Europe workflow 2). That shows the process is real. It says nothing about Indian demand.

**g. Price (estimate).** Setup ₹40,000 to ₹80,000. Monthly ₹5,000 to ₹10,000. Honestly, many Indian owners will say "my purchase boy does this". Pitch it only to plants that have lost production days to late materials.

**h. Sources.** Microsoft Procurement Agent documentation (the process description), Tally integration pages. No India specific demand source. **Validate before you build.**

## Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

### What Microsoft already covers (read this before pitching anything)

This is the fact most likely to kill a sale, so here it is first. Dynamics 365 Business Central (BC) is one of the most common ERPs for European firms with 20 to 250 staff.

1. **Sales Order Agent** watches a shared Exchange mailbox. It reads the email body and PDF or image attachments, finds the customer by email address, checks stock, and creates a sales quote and then an order. A human reviews each step. Microsoft's own cost example is 1,650 Copilot Credits for 100 order emails a month. At the $0.01 per credit rate Microsoft uses in its examples, that is about **$16.50 a month.** It is supported in English, German, Dutch, French, Finnish, Danish, Swedish, Norwegian, Italian and Spanish.
2. **Its documented limits are your opening:** at most **15 item lines** per document (more "might result in lower-quality output"). Item lines only. **No variant codes** (size, colour). It will not create new customers or items. It matches customers **only by the sender's email address**. It reads only an Exchange shared inbox. It will not change prices or discounts. It works only with sales quotes and orders.
3. **Payables Agent** turns emailed PDF invoices into draft purchase invoices. It focuses on overhead costs. Microsoft's example is 6,500 credits for 100 three-line invoices, which is **about $65 a month.** Better purchase order matching is planned but was "moved to the next release wave" in August 2026.
4. **Procurement Agent (supplier communications)**, which reads supplier confirmations and chases late POs, exists only in **Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management**, Microsoft's large company ERP, and is a "production-ready preview". I did not find an equivalent in BC's 2026 release wave 2 feature list.
5. **E-invoicing laws:** Belgium B2B e-invoicing from 1 January 2026. Poland KSeF from 1 February 2026 for large taxpayers and 1 April 2026 for all. France from 1 September 2026. BC has supported German XRechnung and ZUGFeRD since January 2025. Germany has also required all businesses to be able to *receive* e-invoices since 1 January 2025. I know that last fact but could not open a source to link it, so confirm it. The result: invoice PDF reading is a shrinking market in these countries.

### Europe workflow 1: Customer order email to draft sales order

**a. Who has it, and size.** B2B wholesalers and distributors (industrial supplies, packaging, food service, building materials, spare parts, electrical) and make-to-stock manufacturers who get orders by email as PDF purchase orders, Excel sheets or plain text. Fits **both** sizes:
* Small (10 to 49 staff): the owner or office manager. Usually Xero or QuickBooks plus an inventory app, or Exact Online.
* Medium (50 to 250): the customer service or sales operations manager, with 2 to 6 order entry staff. Usually BC, Exact Online, Sage 200, SAP Business One or Odoo.

**Target only buyers with a real gap:**
1. Their ERP is **not** BC, so Sales Order Agent is not an option (Exact Online, Sage 200, SAP Business One, Odoo, Xero with an inventory add-on).
2. They are on BC, but orders often break the agent's limits: more than 15 lines, item variants, new ship-to addresses, customer part numbers, or orders sent from many different email addresses.

**b. The steps today.**
1. An order arrives in a shared mailbox (orders@...). It may be a PDF from the customer's ERP, an Excel sheet, or a typed email.
2. A clerk opens it and finds the customer in the ERP. They translate the customer's part numbers or descriptions to their own item codes, often with a cheat sheet in Excel. They check price, stock and delivery date, and type each line into a new sales order.
3. They email an order confirmation back.
4. The data ends up in the ERP sales order. Mismatches (wrong price, unknown item, discontinued item) are sorted out by phone or email.

**c. Software and APIs.**

| Tool | Usable API? | What I verified |
|---|---|---|
| Microsoft 365 / Outlook (input) | Yes | Microsoft Graph can send change notifications for new messages. For shared mailboxes you need the application permission Mail.Read. |
| Business Central (output) | Yes | API v2.0: POST salesOrders and salesOrderLines. |
| Exact Online (output) | Yes | Community PHP client covers SalesOrder, PurchaseOrder and PurchaseInvoice, generated from Exact's API reference. |
| Xero (output) | Yes, official SDKs (Java SDK covers Accounting, UK Payroll and more) | Xero has no full sales order module of its own. Firms with orders usually add an inventory app, whose API I did not verify. |
| Odoo (output) | **Only on "Custom" plans** | Odoo's docs say the external API is not available on the One App Free or Standard plans. Check the plan before you quote. |
| Sage 200, SAP Business One, weclapp, Xentral | Not verified in this research | Check before pitching. |
| Gmail (input) | Yes (Gmail API) | Not re-verified here. |

That is 2 tools (mailbox and ERP) plus your approval screen. You can add a third (Teams or Slack) for alerts.

**What you build.** Mailbox webhook, then document text extraction (PDF, Excel, email body), then an LLM that pulls out customer, lines, quantities, dates and PO number. Next comes **a matching layer**: a customer part number to item code table that learns from every human correction, plus price and stock checks through the ERP API. Then a draft order goes to an approval screen showing the original document side by side. Nothing posts until a human clicks approve. The matching table and the correction loop are where you beat generic document parsers. Developer A builds the core in Spring Boot with Spring AI and PostgreSQL. Developer B builds the approval screen in TypeScript.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** Small: 10 to 40 orders a day. Medium: 50 to 300. Keying a 10 to 30 line order takes about 4 to 10 minutes, more when part numbers need translating. Example: 60 orders a day at 7 minutes each is 7 hours a day, about one full time person. **Measure it:** export one month of the orders@ mailbox (count) and time 20 orders being keyed.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Sales order processors, customer service reps or the office manager. I could not open UK or EU salary sources in this session. Use the client's own number. As a planning **assumption only**, a fully loaded cost of £28,000 to £35,000 a year in the UK (more in the Nordics, Germany and the Netherlands) means one full time order clerk costs about £2,300 to £2,900 a month.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. BC Sales Order Agent: cheap and good for simple BC orders. The gap is the limits listed above.
2. Generic parsers and enterprise order automation products exist. I could not open their pricing pages this time, so I do not name prices. Enterprise tools usually need a big contract. Generic parsers pull out fields but do not own the customer part number mapping, the ERP checks and the approval screen.
3. EDI solves this for large customers only. The long tail of small customers still sends PDFs and emails.
4. E-invoicing laws do **not** cover purchase orders, so this pain is not going away.

**g. Realistic price.** See the pricing check below. In short: setup $2,000 to $4,000 (small closer to $2,000, medium with ERP matching closer to $4,000). Upkeep $250 to $600 a month for small firms and $500 to $1,000 for medium, tied to volume.

**h. Sources.** Microsoft Learn: Sales Order Agent overview, FAQ, setup, the language table, the consumption billing page and the Copilot Credit pre-purchase page. BC API salesOrders create. Graph Outlook change notifications and shared folder access. The Xero Java SDK and picqer Exact Online client on GitHub. The Odoo external API docs on GitHub.

### Europe workflow 2: Supplier order confirmation check and PO chasing

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers, wholesalers and importers that place many purchase orders with many suppliers: engineering, food production, furniture, electrical and industrial distribution. Mainly **medium** (50 to 250 staff, 1 to 5 buyers). A small importer with 30+ open POs a week can also fit. The owner is the purchasing manager or head buyer.

**b. The steps today.** The buyer sends a PO from the ERP as a PDF by email. The supplier replies with an order confirmation. It may confirm, change the price, change the quantity, split the delivery, or move the date. The buyer opens it, finds the PO, compares line by line, and updates the expected receipt date (and flags the price change). Unconfirmed or late POs get chased by email. The data ends up in the ERP PO lines. Price changes that nobody noticed turn into invoice disputes later.

Microsoft describes this pain in its own documentation: "Purchasing personnel spend a large part of their day on manual tasks that are related to communicating with vendors, following up with them, and maintaining changes that are related to purchase orders," and this communication "is often unstructured and conducted via email, even for companies that implement electronic data interchange (EDI)."

**c. Software and APIs.** Mailbox (Graph or Gmail) and the ERP. In BC the purchaseOrderLine resource has an expectedReceiptDate field and supports PATCH. Exact Online has PurchaseOrder entities. That is 2 tools plus your approval screen.

**What you build.** Classify supplier emails (confirmation, change request, delay notice, other). Extract lines. Match them to the open PO. Show a diff (old against new: price, quantity, date). The buyer approves, then you PATCH the PO line. On a schedule, draft chaser emails for POs that are unconfirmed after N days or overdue, and the buyer sends them. Price changes above a threshold always go to a human.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** 30 to 200 POs a week at a medium firm. 3 to 8 minutes per confirmation to compare and update, plus chasing. Example: 100 confirmations a week at 5 minutes is about 8 hours a week, plus 2 to 4 hours of chasing. **Measure it:** count one month of supplier replies in the buyers' mailboxes and time 20 of them.

**e. Who does it and cost.** Buyers and purchasing assistants. They are paid more than order clerks (use the client's numbers). The bigger cost is late materials and missed price increases. Ask the client for one real example of each. That story sells better than the hours.

**f. Existing tools and gap.** Microsoft's Procurement Agent does this, but only in D365 Supply Chain Management (a large company ERP) and in preview. It also does not yet support split deliveries or site changes. I found no BC equivalent in the 2026 wave 2 feature list. EDI covers large suppliers only. I could not check specialist European tools this time. Ask each prospect what they have tried.

**g. Realistic price.** Setup $2,500 to $4,500 (the matching and diff screen is more work). Upkeep $400 to $900 a month. It sells well as a **second workflow** to a client who already trusts you from workflow 1, because the approval screen and ERP connector are reused.

**h. Sources.** Microsoft Learn: Procurement Agent overview, supplier communications, follow up. BC update 29.0 feature list. BC purchaseOrderLine resource and API. Graph mail notifications.

## Part 3: Pricing check for Europe (confirm or correct)

**Your guess:** $1,500 to $4,000 for the first workflow, $400 to $1,500 a month for upkeep.

**My verdict: the setup range is right. The upkeep range is too high at the top for small firms.**

1. **Setup $1,500 to $4,000: confirmed, with a tilt.** Price small firms at $1,500 to $2,500 and medium firms at $3,000 to $4,500. The work (ERP connector, matching table, approval screen) is a few weeks for you, and one full time order clerk costs a UK firm roughly £2,300+ a month (assumption above). A payback of 2 to 4 months is an easy yes for an owner.
2. **Upkeep $400 to $1,500: correct it to $250 to $600 for small and $500 to $1,000 for medium.** Why:
   * Microsoft's built in agent is about $16.50 per 100 orders. A buyer who has seen that will laugh at $1,500 a month for a similar job. Your upkeep must be justified by **fit and service**: new customer formats, mapping upkeep, monitoring, fixes within a day, and a monthly report of orders processed and minutes saved.
   * Worked example (assumptions marked). A clerk costs £2,500 a month (assumed). The workflow saves 60% of their order time, which is about £1,500 a month in value. Upkeep of £400 leaves the client clearly ahead. Upkeep of £1,200 leaves them almost nothing, and they will cancel in month 3.
   * $1,000 to $1,500 a month is only realistic for medium firms with high volume (300+ documents a day) or for two workflows bundled together.
3. **Tie price to volume.** Include up to N documents a month, then charge per extra 100. It is fair, it scales, and it covers your LLM costs.
4. **India comparison:** ₹30,000 to ₹80,000 setup and ₹4,000 to ₹10,000 a month. That is about one twentieth of the European price for the same engine, which is why India is for proof, not profit.

I could not open agency price pages or freelancer rate pages to cross-check these numbers. Test them. Quote the middle of the range to your first 5 European prospects. If nobody pushes back, you are too cheap.

**How to make the Indian case study believable to a European buyer:**
1. Publish numbers, not adjectives: orders processed, % of lines matched with no human edit, minutes per order before and after, wrong item rate before and after, and time from order to confirmation.

3. Show the human approval screen and the audit log. European buyers care about control, GDPR and "the AI cannot post without us".
4. Get a named, recorded testimonial from the Indian owner, and permission to show a screen recording with customer names blurred.

## Part 5: Final top picks, with reasons

**Pick 1. Europe: customer order email to draft sales order, for non-BC ERPs or BC firms with big or variant-heavy orders.**
* Why: it is partly digital (email plus ERP), countable (orders times minutes), owned by one person, 2 tools, and approved by a human. E-invoicing laws do not touch it. Microsoft's own agent proves the demand but leaves documented gaps (15 lines, no variants, email-only customer matching, BC only).
* Fit to your team: Developer A's Spring Boot, PostgreSQL and fintech background (KYC style document checks, audit trails) is exactly the matching and approval core. Developer B builds the screen and the mail connectors.
* Where to start: your Finnish client. Ask for 3 introductions to wholesalers or manufacturers that are **not** on BC. Then the UK and the Netherlands, where English works.

**Pick 2. India: WhatsApp order to Tally, as proof of work only.**
* Why: the fastest path to a real case study with real volume, 20 minutes from your desk, in the same engine as Pick 1.
* Be honest with yourselves: competing products exist (SalesOn, Zotok, HublerX) and labor is cheap. Do 1 or 2 clients at a token price for the case study, then stop selling in India unless someone pays full price.

**Pick 3. Europe: supplier confirmation check and chasing, as the second workflow for the same clients.**
* Why: medium firms feel it, Microsoft only covers it in its large company ERP, and it reuses your connector and approval screen, so your margin improves.

**Do not build:** generic AI invoice processing, payment reminders, or anything for BC users with simple orders. All three are covered by built in or cheap tools, which is the same trap that killed your earlier idea.

### Practical rules that protect you

1. **n8n licence.** The n8n Sustainable Use License says you may use it "only for your own internal business purposes", and may provide it to others only "free of charge for non-commercial purposes". Do not host one n8n instance and charge clients for workflows on it. Either install n8n in the **client's own** account (their internal use) or write the product in your own Spring Boot and Node code. Email license@n8n.io before you sell anything that runs on n8n.
2. **GDPR.** EU and UK client data processed by you in India is a transfer out of the EEA or UK. India is not on the European Commission's adequacy list (check the Commission page linked from the Microsoft guide in Sources). So sign a Data Processing Agreement with **Standard Contractual Clauses** (plus the UK addendum for UK clients). Keep data in an EU region, such as AWS Frankfurt or Ireland. Keep a written list of subprocessors, including your LLM provider.
3. **Budget under $50 a month.** One small EU cloud VM or container, one PostgreSQL database, and a pay-as-you-go LLM API. With small models, the cost per order document should be around 1 US cent or less. This is my estimate, so check current model prices and log real token use from day one. Charge a client's LLM costs to that client once they pay.
4. **Tally connector.** Tally must be running on the client's machine with the HTTP server on. Plan for a small Windows service on their PC that pulls approved orders from your cloud and posts them to Tally locally. Test with TallyPrime 7.0 JSON if the client is on 7.0, and fall back to XML if not.
5. **Constraints.** Nothing here moves money or makes legal, medical or lending decisions. Keep "human approves before anything posts or sends" as a hard rule in the code, not just a setting.

### First two weeks: exact steps

1. Days 1 to 3: visit 5 Ludhiana distributors. Ask for a week of WhatsApp orders (forwarded) and time 20 entries. Pick the one with the most orders and the messiest item names.
2. Days 4 to 5: Developer A builds the extractor and matcher against their Tally item master (exported as XML or JSON). Developer B builds the approval screen.
3. Week 2: run in shadow mode. The clerk keys as usual while the system drafts. Compare them. Track the match rate and minutes.
4. In parallel, send the Finnish client a one page brief and ask for 3 introductions to non-BC wholesalers. On each call, ask: orders per day? minutes each? which ERP and plan? more than 15 lines? variants? who owns it?
