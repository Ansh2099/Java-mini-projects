# Workflows SMBs Will Pay For: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

1. **Your best product is "customer order arrives by email, becomes a draft sales order in the ERP, a person approves the exceptions."** It passes all seven of your rules. UK and Irish firms pay full time salaries to do it today, and hundreds of job ads say so.
2. **But this space is crowded, and the big software makers now build it in.** Business Central has a Sales Order Agent. TallyPrime 7.1 has "Docs by Ira", which turns PDFs into Sales Order vouchers, and TallyPrime 4.0 and later imports Excel vouchers. Cin7 Core has a PDF order reader. Paid services already exist for Sage 200 (Zestcode) and Unleashed (Logistify). You will not find an empty gap. You win on price, speed of setup, and service for firms with 30 to 150 orders a day.
3. **In the UK and Ireland, aim first at wholesalers on Unleashed, but test that choice before you build for it.** Unleashed has no built in AI order reader and a documented API. It does have two built in order tools, a CSV sales order import and a paid B2B store, so you must screen out firms those already serve (see the Unleashed row in the ERP table). It is not free to build on for months, though. The free trial lasts 14 days. The free sandbox is a copy of a paying customer's live account, so you only get one once a client signs. I have not found how many UK firms run Unleashed, and most job ad proof names Sage. So **no Unleashed code gets written until two gates pass**: 60 named Unleashed prospects by the end of week 2, and 3 of your first 10 calls confirming 30 or more emailed orders a day by the end of week 4. Until then you only build the parts that work for any ERP. If a gate fails, switch the target ERP. The engine stays the same.
4. **Do not build for Sage 200 yet, even though its users feel this pain.** Developing on the Sage 200 API needs Sage's developer programme, which costs about 260 GBP a month, and that fee never stops, even after a client signs. That is five times your whole budget. Zestcode also already sells the full service on Sage 200. Reach Sage 200 firms later, through a Sage partner who already pays for that access, or once a paying client's fee covers it.
5. **Build it first for an Indian B2B distributor on Tally, as a cheap or free case study.** Pick one whose business customers email PDF or Excel POs in their own item codes and changing layouts. That is where Tally's own tools are weak, and it looks like a UK wholesaler's inbox. I have not proven that many Ludhiana firms fit this shape, so check it with 10 short visits or calls first. If the POs arrive as PDF or Excel files on WhatsApp instead, you can still take them. The Tally connector is only written after a firm passes that screen. If you find no fit in four weeks, skip India and use a UK founding client as the case study. Realistic Indian prices: about ₹5,000 setup plus ₹1,250 a month for a small firm, and ₹30,000 setup plus ₹3,000 a month for a medium one.
6. **Supplier order confirmation checking is no longer a top pick.** When I checked the Business Central app store, two cheap add ons already cover much of it. Continia Document Capture reads supplier order confirmations and updates the purchase order, and Abakion's Purchase Order Management tracks what was asked versus confirmed for about DKK 120 a user a month. It is only worth selling to a firm that has neither and handles 36 or more confirmations a day.
7. **Your price guess is about right. Price by order volume, and check the buyer's payback every time.** Charge 2,000 to 4,000 USD to build plus 400 to 700 USD a month for small firms, and 4,000 to 6,500 USD plus 700 to 1,500 USD a month for medium firms. Below 30 orders a day, the buyer does not get their money back fast enough. Do not sell there. **And be honest about small firms:** the clerk stays, so saved hours only become cash if the owner avoids a hire, cuts overtime or temps, or gets their own time back. At 30 to 50 orders a day, sell only when one of those is true. From about 100 orders a day (1.2 people freed), the saving is real cash.
8. **Almost every accounting idea is dead.** Supplier bill entry, bill to PO matching, GST matching, bank reconciliation and supplier statement checks are all sold as cheap tools or built in features in both regions.
9. **Continental Europe has the same problem but already has local vendors.** The Netherlands, Germany and Finland each have several local language tools selling AI order entry. Start with the UK and Ireland, where an English speaking team can compete.

The hard truth: AI order entry is crowded in 2026, and the ERP makers are moving in. You will not win on technology. You win because you set it up for the client, you handle their messy cases, and you cost less than the rivals for mid volume firms. India will not pay you much. Treat it as marketing. Two things in this plan are still guesses, and you must test them cheaply before you spend weeks building: how many UK and Irish Unleashed users get 30 or more emailed orders a day, and whether a Ludhiana firm with emailed POs exists near you. The week by week plan puts each ERP connector after the check that decides it, so a failed check costs you calls, not code. Move fast, because this window may close in one or two years.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK and Ireland as the first market. I also checked the Netherlands, Germany and Finland for demand and competition. The results are in "Europe beyond the UK and Ireland".
2. "One workflow per client" means one workflow per deal. A second workflow for the same client is a new deal.
3. Where a number has no source, I mark it as **my estimate**. Check those before you quote them to a client.
4. **Time saved.** Automation does not remove the clerk. The clerk still reviews drafts and fixes flagged ones. I count about **70% of the clerk's order entry time as saved** (my estimate; vendors claim 80 to 90%, but they are selling). All payback math uses this. **Freed time is not cash.** Payroll only drops if the firm avoids a hire, cuts overtime or temp staff, or the owner is the one doing the typing today. Below about one full person freed, the payback numbers are only real when one of those is true, so the P check below demands it for small firms.
5. **About the sources.** In this session, full page fetching was blocked for most websites. I read Microsoft Learn pages in full and a GitHub guide to the TallyPrime XML API directly. Everything else I read through search result summaries. Vendor numbers such as "minutes per order" come from vendors who want to sell you something. Treat them as rough.
6. Money conversions are rough: about 1.3 USD per GBP, and about 7.45 DKK per EUR. A working day is 8 hours, and a working year is about 220 days.
7. Some pages were blocked for this revision too (Abakion, Continia docs, AppSource, Wikipedia). Where I rely on them, I say the fact came from search result summaries.

## India

A word first. In India, a clerk costs about ₹14,000 to ₹21,000 a month (sources below). So automation only beats hiring when volume is high, when errors cost real money (wrong rates, wrong items, goods sent back), or when speed matters (same day dispatch). Even then, the fee is small. Be honest with yourselves: **India pays for proof, not for your salaries.**

Only one India workflow survived my checks. I tested five others and rejected them. They are listed after it, each with the tool that already does it. That includes the biggest Indian channel, small shops ordering by WhatsApp text or voice note: Zenxos, Zotok and Rupyz already sell that from ₹1,499 a month, so your rules say reject it. I would rather give you one workflow that is honestly open than pad the list with ones a cheap tool already owns.

### India workflow 1: B2B customer orders by email into Tally as sales orders

**Rule check:** R1 yes (order arrives, sales order saved and confirmed). R2 yes (Gmail or Outlook, TallyPrime). R3 yes (order clerk or sales coordinator). R4 yes (the clerk approves each draft at first, then only flagged ones once the numbers prove it works). R5 yes, about 4 weeks plus about 2 part time weeks for the Tally connector and its PC agent. R6 yes. R7 yes, because version 1 reads the inbox the firm already uses. E1 yes. E2 **only for the buyers named in the gap below.** L yes. B yes, Tally's XML interface is free to use. P **yes for medium firms at the standard price; yes for small firms only at a low price and only if they pass the cash test (section g).**

**a. Who has it, and what size**
B2B distributors and makers who sell to **many mid sized business customers**, each with its own purchasing system. Best examples: industrial supply, bearings, fasteners, hardware and packaging distributors, and hosiery or knitwear makers selling to retail chains and big wholesalers.
1. **Paying fit: medium** (50 to 250 staff) with 100 or more orders a day, or any firm where a wrong rate or wrong item costs real money.
2. **Small** (10 to 49 staff) with 30 or more orders a day. They pay little (about ₹5,000 setup plus ₹1,250 a month, see g), but they give you real numbers. Best if the owner types orders himself at night, or is about to hire a second clerk.

**Who does not fit, and why:**
1. Auto and cycle part makers that supply big OEMs. Their large buyers often send POs through the buyer's own supplier portal or ERP, not as varied emailed PDFs (**my judgment**; confirm on calls). Their smaller trade customers may still fit.
2. Firms whose buyers are small shops ordering on WhatsApp by text or voice note. Indian vendors describe this as the normal way small retailers order, and cheap tools such as Zenxos already serve it ([HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026), [Zenxos pricing](https://www.zenxos.com/pricing)).
3. Pharma stockists. Many run Marg (**my judgment**, not checked), and I did not check Marg's integration route. Leave them out of version 1.

**This buyer shape is a guess, not a proven fact.** I found no source that counts Ludhiana firms receiving 30 or more emailed POs a day. What I did find: Ludhiana has a steady flow of order processing and Tally jobs (25 order processing vacancies on Indeed when I checked in September 2026), and local job ads ask for "sales order", "purchase order" and Tally work ([Indeed Ludhiana order processing](https://in.indeed.com/q-order-processing-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html), [Indeed Ludhiana Tally jobs](https://in.indeed.com/q-tally-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)). That proves the job exists. It does not prove the channel is email. So qualify before you build:

| Screening question (ask 10 firms in 2 weeks) | Pass |
|---|---|
| How do business customers send orders? | Mostly PDF or Excel files, by email **or as files on WhatsApp** |
| How many a day? | 30 or more |
| Whose item codes are on the PO? | The customer's, not yours |
| How many different PO layouts? | 5 or more customers with their own layout |
| Which software? | TallyPrime |

If orders come as PDF or Excel files on WhatsApp, that still works. Take the files through the WhatsApp coexistence route in section c, which lets the firm keep its WhatsApp Business app. Once you have the file, the engine is the same. Text and voice note orders stay out.

**b. Steps today**
1. A PO arrives as a PDF or Excel file by email from a business customer, in the customer's own item codes and names.
2. The clerk works out which customer it is. Then the clerk matches the customer's item codes to the firm's own stock items in Tally.
3. The clerk checks the rate against the price list or the last rate. Then the clerk checks stock and the customer's unpaid balance.
4. The clerk types a Sales Order voucher into Tally and sends a confirmation.
5. Problems (unknown item, low stock, rate dispute) go to the owner by phone or WhatsApp.

The data ends up in Tally.

**What you automate:** The system watches the order inbox. The AI reads each PO, including Excel files and multi page PDFs. It matches the customer, then maps each line to a Tally stock item using a mapping table it learns from the clerk's past fixes. It checks each rate against the price list and flags any gap above a set percent. It shows the customer's unpaid balance next to their credit limit, **for the human to judge**. The clerk approves or fixes the draft on one screen. Only then does the system post the Sales Order to Tally. You never approve credit. The human does.

**c. Software on each side**
1. **In (version 1):** Gmail or Outlook, through their standard APIs. Email only.
2. **In (version 2, or version 1 if your case study client gets its PO files on WhatsApp):** WhatsApp. Meta's "coexistence" mode now lets a business keep using the WhatsApp Business app and connect the same number to the Cloud API. Messages sent from the app are copied to the API's webhook, and India is supported ([Meta for Developers: onboard WhatsApp Business app users](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users/), [360dialog: coexistence](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/phone-numbers/coexistence), [Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence)). Meta's own Cloud API has no platform fee, or a provider such as 360dialog charges 49 EUR a month per number ([360dialog pricing](https://360dialog.com/pricing)). Meta bills per message the business sends, and it is changing how service messages are priced from 1 October 2026 ([Meta: pricing updates for service and utility messages](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/non-template-messages)). Put these costs on the client's account, not yours. WhatsApp orders are also often voice notes and photos, which are harder to read. That is why they wait for version 2.
3. **Out:** TallyPrime. Tally takes XML over HTTP on port 9000. It can create vouchers and masters. There is no JSON, and Tally must be running with the company loaded on a PC ([GitHub TallyPrime XML API guide](https://github.com/NoumaanAhamed/tally-prime-api-docs/blob/main/index.md), [TallyHelp XML integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/xml-integration/)). Tally's port 9000 sits on an office PC behind the office router, so your cloud engine cannot reach it directly. Use this design:
   1. **A small agent on the Tally PC** (Developer A, Java, run as a Windows service). It only makes outbound HTTPS calls, so no router changes, no open ports and no tunnel.
   2. Every minute it asks your cloud engine "any approved orders?" It posts each one to Tally at localhost:9000 as a Sales Order voucher, reads Tally's reply, and reports success or the error back.
   3. It also reads the ledger (customer) and stock item lists from Tally once a day, so the engine matches against the real masters.
   4. **When Tally is closed or the company is not loaded** (the normal case after hours), the post fails. The order stays in the queue marked "waiting for Tally" and is retried. Nothing is lost. If an approved order has waited more than 2 working hours, or the agent has not checked in for 30 minutes during office hours, the clerk gets an email or WhatsApp alert. Each morning the clerk sees a list of what posted overnight and what is still waiting.
   5. Each post carries the order's own ID, and the agent checks for that ID before posting again, so a retry never makes a double order.
   6. Hosted Tally on a cloud server is not part of the plan. It adds a server and licence cost. Only use it if the client already runs Tally that way.

   **Estimate:** about 2 part time weeks. Week 1: the XML for a Sales Order voucher and the master list reads, tested on TallyPrime's free Educational mode (**my assumption**: check it allows XML posts on your install). Week 2: the agent, the queue, retries and alerts. This is my estimate from the XML guide above, not a measured number.
4. Busy and Marg: I did not check their APIs. Start with Tally only, and do not pitch firms on Busy or Marg (this is why pharma stockists are out).

**d. How often and how long**
1. **Volume:** 30 to 150 orders a day for the firms above (**my estimate**, not sourced; the screening calls must confirm it).
2. **Time per order:** Vendor blogs say 5 to 8 minutes for a simple order and 10 to 15 minutes for a harder one. A 100 line order can take 60 to 90 minutes ([Mirage Metrics](https://miragemetrics.com/blog/true-cost-manual-order-entry/), [Ziiware](https://www.ziiware.com/blog/manual-order-entry-wholesale-distribution/), [Bizowie](https://bizowie.com/how-distributors-can-automate-80-of-manual-order-entry)).
3. **Example:** 60 orders a day at 8 minutes each is 8 hours. That is one full time person.

**e. Who does it and what it costs**
1. A sales coordinator, order clerk or billing clerk. Indeed puts the average sales coordinator pay in India at about ₹20,810 a month ([Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/career/sales-coordinator/salaries)). Coordinator jobs in Ludhiana list about ₹14,000 to ₹20,000 a month ([Glassdoor Ludhiana coordinator jobs](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/ludhiana-coordinator-jobs-SRCH_IL.0,8_IC2939907_KO9,20.htm)).
2. Firms in Ludhiana still hire for this: Indeed showed 22 order processing jobs there in July 2026 and 25 in September 2026 ([Indeed Ludhiana](https://in.indeed.com/q-order-processing-l-ludhiana,-punjab-jobs.html)). These ads do not say how orders arrive.
3. One vendor says midsize Indian firms keep teams of 10 to 15 people for order entry and that 12 to 18% of retyped orders have errors ([HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)). It sells a fix, so treat these as upper bounds.

**f. Existing tools and the gap**
1. **TallyPrime "Docs by Ira" (built in, release 7.1 and later).** It turns PDF and image documents into vouchers, including Sales Orders. It matches stock items by exact part number first, then by exact name. Each PDF must hold one document. You upload the files. Each licence gets 100 free pages, then paid recharge packs ([TallyHelp: Docs by Ira](https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs-by-ira/), [TallyHelp: Docs by Ira FAQ](https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs-by-ira-faq/)).
2. **TallyPrime Excel import (built in, release 4.0 and later).** It imports Sales and other vouchers from Excel. You save a mapping template that ties each column to a Tally field. The ledgers and items must already exist in Tally ([TallyHelp: Release 4.0 notes](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime/release-notes-tally-prime/release-notes-tallyprime-4/), [TallyHelp: Import Data FAQ](https://help.tallysolutions.com/import-data-faq/), [TallyHelp: how to import data](https://help.tallysolutions.com/import-data-in-tally/)).
3. **Zenxos** from ₹1,499 a month turns retailer WhatsApp orders into orders and syncs to Tally ([Zenxos pricing](https://www.zenxos.com/pricing)). Zotok and Rupyz are similar ([Zotok](https://www.zotok.ai/), [Rupyz](https://rupyz.com/blog/order-management-system-with-whatsapp-tally-integration/)).
4. **Logistify** sells a managed AI order entry service that lists TallyPrime among its ERPs. I could not find its prices ([Logistify](https://logistify.ai/)).
5. **HublerX** targets firms with ₹200 to ₹2,000 crore revenue and takes 6 to 10 weeks to set up ([HublerX](https://www.hublerx.ai/resources/blog/top-whatsapp-order-management-software-manufacturers-india-2026)).
6. **The gap, stated honestly:** Tally's two built in tools work when documents already use the firm's own item codes or names and come in a steady layout. Docs by Ira needs manual upload and one clean document per PDF. The Excel import needs a saved template per layout and exact master names. Neither watches the inbox, learns a customer's own part numbers, or flags rate and credit problems. **So your buyer is a firm whose business customers send PDF or Excel POs in their own codes and in many different layouts.** Send prospects elsewhere when:
   1. Their POs already use the firm's own codes: try Docs by Ira first.
   2. Most orders are Excel files from a few customers in a fixed layout: set up Tally's Excel import templates. Their Tally partner can do this.
   3. Their buyers are small shops ordering from a catalog: Zenxos.

**The cash test, in plain words.** At 30 orders a day you free about a third of one person. The clerk stays, so payroll does not drop by itself. A small firm owner who judges by cash will only pay if one of these is true, so ask before you quote:
1. "Are you about to hire someone to help with orders?" A hire avoided is worth a full salary, about 25,000 GBP or more, and the price passes easily.

### Europe workflow 2 (conditional, not a top pick): Supplier order confirmations checked against purchase orders

**a. Who has it, and what size:** Manufacturers and distributors that buy from many suppliers: engineering, electronics assembly, food production, technical wholesale. Mainly **medium**, and only the busier end of medium. Small firms rarely have enough POs.

**b. Steps today**
1. The buyer sends a PO from the ERP.
2. The supplier replies with an order confirmation as a PDF or email. It may change the price, quantity or delivery date.
3. A purchasing administrator compares it line by line with the PO.
4. The administrator types the confirmed dates into the ERP.
5. The administrator chases suppliers who never confirmed.
6. The administrator tells the buyer about any changes.

The data ends up in the purchase order in the ERP.

**What you automate:** Read the confirmation. Pull the PO through the API. Compare every line. Update the promised dates once the buyer approves. List every price or quantity change for the buyer. Draft chaser emails for missing confirmations, which a human sends.

**c. Software:** Outlook or Gmail. 3. **The number that matters:** hours a day the administrator spends reading, comparing and keying confirmations. Ask for it, then check it by counting one week of confirmations.

## Final top picks

**1. Email PO to sales order for UK and Irish wholesalers, aimed first at Unleashed users.** Start selling here, once the two gates below pass.
1. The pain and its cost are proven: hundreds of UK job ads and Irish ads for the task, UK salaries of about 21,000 to 29,000 GBP, and paid tools from 500 USD a month to six figures a year. Be clear that most of those ads name Sage. They prove the task, not how many firms run any one ERP.
2. On Unleashed there is no built in AI reader and no cheap tool that reads customers' own PO files, the API is documented, and its users already pay about 380 USD a month or more for Unleashed itself. Its CSV import and B2B store do serve some firms. Screen those out with the questions in Europe workflow 1.
3. It fits your budget, but not the way a free sandbox would. You test in one 14 day trial, demo with a recorded video, and build on the client's own account and sandbox after they sign. At no stage do you pay a developer fee. Sage 200, by contrast, needs about 260 GBP a month for as long as you build on it.
4. It passes every rule. The payback is real cash for medium firms, and for small firms from 30 orders a day only when they pass the cash test.
5. Weaknesses: Logistify already sells here, and the number of UK and Irish Unleashed users with 30 or more emailed orders a day is unknown. **Two gates, both before any Unleashed code:** Gate 1, by the end of week 2, name at least 60 UK or Irish firms that run Unleashed and sell to businesses (clues: job ads that name Unleashed, Unleashed's customer stories, Unleashed partners' case studies). Gate 2, by the end of week 4, at least 3 of your first 10 calls must confirm 30 or more emailed orders a day in customers' own PO files, not through the B2B store or the import template. If either gate fails, the Unleashed connector is never started. Point the same engine at pick 3 instead.

**2. The same workflow for an Indian B2B distributor on Tally, as a free or cheap case study.** Do this first in time, but only after the screening calls find a firm that fits. The Tally connector starts the week a firm passes, not before.
1. It proves the engine on real, messy orders and gives you measured before and after numbers.
2. Weaknesses: the buyer shape is not proven in Ludhiana, the Tally connector does not carry over to Europe, and Indian firms pay little. Even a medium firm at 100 orders a day is worth about ₹66,000 in year one at the standard price. Treat it as marketing. If no firm fits within four weeks, use the fallback in the bridge section.

**3. Email PO to sales order for UK firms on Business Central whose orders the built in agent cannot handle.** Your backup target if Unleashed fails either gate, or a second market once pick 1 works.
1. Microsoft's own FAQ lists the gaps: no Excel POs, at most 15 item lines, customer found only by sender email, variant codes left empty, weaker on cryptic item names, and Exchange shared mailboxes only.
2. The 30 day trial is free, so it fits your budget.
3. Weakness: a narrow buyer, and Microsoft may close these gaps in any release. Ask on every call: what share of your POs are Excel files or longer than 15 lines?

**Supplier confirmation checking (Europe workflow 2) is not a top pick.** Continia Document Capture and Abakion's app cover much of it cheaply on Business Central. Offer it only as a second deal to a client who has neither, with 3.6 or more hours a day of this work.

**Later, not now:** Sage 200, for both workflows. The pain is real and the users are many, but the developer programme costs about 260 GBP a month and Zestcode is already there. Approach two or three Sage 200 business partners with your first case study and offer to build under their programme access. If one signs a client, price the build to cover the access cost.

**Not a top pick:** workflow 1 on Business Central for normal PDF orders, Cin7 or Odoo; supplier bill matching in India; every accounting data entry idea; and continental Europe for now. Built in features, cheap tools or local vendors own them.

**What to do, week by week**
The rule: **each ERP connector is built only after the check that decides it has passed.** Before that, you only build parts that work for any ERP, so a failed check costs calls, not code.

1. **Weeks 1 and 2, India screening (Developer A):** call or visit 10 Ludhiana distributors on Tally and use the screening table in India workflow 1. Offer the build free to the first one that passes, in return for numbers and a reference.
2. **Weeks 1 and 2, UK Gate 1 (Developer B):** list UK and Irish firms that run Unleashed and sell to businesses. Stop at 60 names or at the end of week 2. Fewer than 60 means Unleashed is out and pick 3 (Business Central) becomes your UK target.
3. **Weeks 1 to 5, shared engine only (Developer B):** the email intake, AI extraction to a fixed JSON schema, the code mapping table, and the review screen in TypeScript, with n8n for the glue. Test it on sample POs you make in 10 layouts. None of this depends on the ERP, so it is never wasted.
4. **Weeks 3 and 4, UK Gate 2 (both):** book 10 discovery calls from the Gate 1 list. Ask: orders a day, minutes per order, who does it, what share of POs are customers' own files versus the B2B store or the import template, what share are Excel or long PDFs, whether they plan to hire or pay overtime (the cash test), and what another vendor quoted. Pass means 3 of 10 confirm 30 or more emailed own format orders a day. Keep booking calls after week 4 to find your first client.
5. **Week 3 onward, Tally connector (Developer A), only if a Ludhiana firm passed step 1:** about 2 part time weeks for the XML and the PC agent (India workflow 1, section c). If no firm passed by week 2, widen the search as the bridge section says. If still none by week 4, the Tally connector is never built and India is skipped.
6. **Week 5 onward, the UK connector (Developer A), only after Gate 2:** Unleashed if both gates passed, Business Central if not. Build it against the published API docs and a fake test server. About 2 part time weeks.
7. **Week 7 or 8, one real test window:** start the 14 day Unleashed trial (or the 30 day BC trial) only when the connector passes against the fake server. Run your sample POs through it, fix what breaks, and record the 3 minute demo video before the trial ends.
8. **If Gate 2 fails for both Unleashed and BC:** stop building ERP code for Europe. Keep calling with the demo from the Indian case study (or the sample POs) and build the connector for whichever ERP your first paying client runs, as long as its API passes check B.
9. **Costs:** one small cloud server and pay per use calls to a small AI model, well under 50 USD a month. Do not buy an Unleashed plan or the Sage developer programme. Put each client's AI, hosting and WhatsApp costs on their own account once they pay.
