ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] I2 misses Tally's own built in PDF to Sales Order feature, the exact "already solved" check the task demands
Where: I2 section f, "Tools and gap": lists Logistify, Karvan, Zotok and TDL integrators, then states "The gap: a cheap, local setup that reads the PDF, checks it against *this* customer's rate contract, and posts to Tally."
Problem: The task says "Whether existing software already solves it. If it is already solved by a built in feature or a cheap tool, reject it." TallyPrime ships "Docs by Ira" (release 7.1 and later). It turns PDF and image documents into draft vouchers, and Sales Order is one of the supported voucher types. A human reviews each draft before anything posts, and each licence gets 100 free pages ([TallyHelp: Docs by Ira](https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs-by-ira/), [Tally Solutions: Docs by Ira](https://tallysolutions.com/tally/docs-by-ira-ai-invoice-processing/)). So most of the stated gap, "reads the PDF ... and posts to Tally", already exists inside the software every I2 prospect runs, and it is free to start. I2 never mentions it. A Ludhiana owner who has seen the Ira demo would end the sales call there. The real remaining gap (watching the inbox, Excel POs, the customer's own item codes, rate contract checks) is never named. So I2 and the I2 to E2 case study bridge rest on an unchecked competitor.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] I1 fails the India rule that automation must clearly beat hiring, by the solution's own numbers
Where: I1 d "1 to 3 hours a day of one person", e "A purchase executive at ₹12,000 to ₹30,000 a month ... you save hours, not a salary", g "₹20,000 to ₹50,000 setup and ₹3,000 to ₹7,000 a month".
Problem: The task says for India: "A workflow only makes sense where automation clearly beats simply hiring another person, or where speed and errors matter more than salary." Run the solution's own numbers (8 hour days, 26 days a month). One hour a day of a ₹12,000 executive is worth about ₹1,500 a month. Three hours a day of a ₹30,000 executive is worth about ₹11,250. The fee is ₹3,000 to ₹7,000 a month, plus setup of ₹20,000 to ₹50,000. Spread the setup over 12 months and the total is about ₹4,700 to ₹11,200 a month. That is more than the labour saved across almost the whole range. The only other reason the solution gives is "a production line stopped because steel did not arrive", and it has no source and no number for it. I1 is still labelled "Both" sizes with a paid price. It does not show that I1 clears the rule the task set for India.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] E1 is labelled "Both" sizes, but at small firm volume the fee is more than the work it saves
Where: E1 a "Both sizes, leaning medium ... Small firms (20 to 49 staff) that import a lot have it too", d "20 to 150 POs a week. 5 to 10 minutes to check each confirmation", e "£25,000 to £29,000 a year", g "Small: $1,500 to $2,500 build, $250 to $500 a month".
Problem: The task asks "For each workflow, say whether it fits small, medium, or both". The answer must hold up for each size. At the low end, 20 POs a week at 5 to 10 minutes is 100 to 200 minutes a week, about 7 to 14 hours a month. At £25,000 to £29,000 a year (about £13 to £15 an hour), that is about £90 to £210 a month, or roughly $120 to $280. The small firm fee is $250 to $500 a month plus a $1,500 to $2,500 build. That is about $4,500 to $8,500 in year one, against about $1,450 to $3,400 of time saved. Chasing time is never given a number, so it cannot close the gap. The "small" price tier is sold to buyers who lose money on it, and the solution does not say at what PO volume E1 starts to pay. The user needs that number to qualify prospects.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] I1 needs a new WhatsApp number and new behaviour from suppliers and staff, which breaks rule 7
Where: I1 c "use a new dedicated 'purchase desk' number on Meta's Cloud API ... suppliers reply 1:1 to that number ... Suppliers who still reply to the buyer's own phone get forwarded by the buyer."
Problem: Rule 7 says: "We do NOT want processes that are fully manual today, because then we would have to convince both the decision maker and the staff to change how they work." I1's own step 3 says supplier replies arrive today on the purchase executive's existing WhatsApp. The design moves that conversation to a new bot number. Every supplier has to change who they message, and the buyer has to forward anything that still arrives on their own phone. That forwarding is new manual work that did not exist before. This is exactly the change management the rule is meant to avoid. It is also a practical risk: small suppliers who have dealt with one person for years will keep messaging that person, so the forwarding load will not go away. The solution rejects coexistence only because of group chat limits, and it never checks whether coexistence on the buyer's current number would avoid this problem for 1:1 supplier chats.
