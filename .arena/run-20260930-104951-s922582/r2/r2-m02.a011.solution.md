# Workflow Automations to Sell: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

In Europe, sell **supplier order confirmation checking and chasing** first. Your client emails a purchase order (PO) to a supplier. The supplier's reply (a PDF or an email) often changes the price, the quantity or the date, or never comes at all. Today a buyer reads every reply, compares it line by line, updates the ETA in the system and chases the silent suppliers. You automate the reading, the comparing and the chasing, and the buyer approves each change. Firms pay purchasing assistants to do this. The tools that do it (Leverage, Didero, Tacto, Conexiom) are built for mid-market and enterprise ERPs, not for a 40 person UK wholesaler on Unleashed or Sage 200. Only sell it to firms that send **50 or more POs a week**. Below about 30 a week, the fee costs more than the time it saves. In India, build the same engine for a medium Ludhiana manufacturer whose purchase executive chases suppliers on WhatsApp and email, running on the buyer's **existing** WhatsApp number. Be honest with yourself: at Indian salaries this barely pays, so do the first one near free. It is your case study, not your income. Your second product is **customer PO email to sales order**, but **only for Sage 200 firms**. On Cin7 Core, Odoo, Business Central and Unleashed, a built in feature or a cheap add-on already does it. In India, the same idea is dead: Tally's own Docs by Ira turns PDF POs into draft sales orders for ₹200 per 100 pages. Do not build WhatsApp order taking for Indian distributors either: Karvan, Zotok and Mulltiply already sell it. Your price guesses for Europe are right, but what one client will pay is capped by the hours you save them, so qualify on volume first.

The rest of this document backs that up. First the facts everything stands on. Then the workflows. Then the detail.

## Assumptions

1. **Your first European market is the UK and Ireland.** They share English, the same ERPs (Sage 200, Unleashed, Cin7) and one sales pitch. The Netherlands and the Nordics come second, because firms there run different ERPs (Exact Online, Visma, Fortnox) that need their own connector. I list those ERPs and their APIs in the Europe section so you are ready. German and French markets need local language sales, so they come later still.
2. Prices in Europe are in USD so you can compare with your own guess. Prices in India are in INR.
3. Numbers marked **(estimate)** are my own reasoning, not sourced. Check them in your first three sales calls.
4. Most vendor and help pages blocked my page fetch tool. Most numbers below come from search results that summarised the linked pages. The links are real and I found them, but open the key ones yourself before you quote a number to a client.
5. Many sources are vendor blogs selling a fix. I use them as proof that the problem exists and that people pay for it, not as neutral statistics.

## Pass 1: The facts nobody can argue with

1. **You have about 15 to 20 hours a week between you and under $50 a month.** So one engine must serve many clients. Every new client should be setup, not a new build.
2. **Europe pays, India proves.** So the best workflow exists in both places in almost the same shape.
3. **Your sweet spot is "a document or message arrives, a person reads it, compares it to data in the system, and types or chases."** LLMs are now good at the reading. The value you add is the matching against the client's own records and the human approval step.
4. **If a built in feature or a tool under about $100 a month does it, you lose.** So look where current tools are enterprise priced or tied to other ERPs.
5. **ERP vendors and small SaaS firms now ship AI document readers every few months.** Cin7 has one for sales orders. Business Central has an order agent. Odoo's app store has several. Tally has Docs by Ira. So before every sales call, check the prospect's ERP app store and help centre for a built in reader. That check is part of your sales process from now on.
6. **Selling is harder than building for you.** Two part timers in Ludhiana selling to strangers in Europe have one trust signal at the start: a case study with real numbers.

7. **The fee must be smaller than the time it saves.** So every workflow needs a minimum volume, and you only sell to firms above it. Measure volume on the first call.

Supplier confirmation checking passes all seven, above about 30 to 50 POs a week. Customer order intake passes only on ERPs without a reader (Sage 200 is the clear one), so it fails fact 5 in India, where Tally has one. WhatsApp distributor ordering fails fact 4 in India. Supplier invoice capture fails fact 4 everywhere.

## Pass 3: The detail

### Region 1: India

**The honest truth about India first.** Indian staff are cheap. A purchase executive earns about ₹12,000 to ₹30,000 a month ([Glassdoor India purchase executive jobs](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/purchase-executive-jobs-SRCH_IC2930179_KO0,18.htm), [Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/q-purchase-executive-l-delhi,-delhi-jobs.html)). A Tally operator earns about ₹16,000 to ₹38,670 depending on the source ([Internshala](https://internshala.com/jobs/tally-jobs/), [Jooble](https://in.jooble.org/salary/tally-operator)). You cannot charge more than a slice of that. Here is the math for one hour a day of work, over 26 working days of 8 hours: at ₹12,000 a month it is worth about ₹1,500 a month, and at ₹30,000 about ₹3,750. So a ₹5,000 monthly fee only beats hiring when you save **two or more hours a day of a well paid person**, or when a mistake costs real money (a production line that waits for steel). I found no source that puts a rupee number on that second case, so you must get it from the client: "How many times last quarter did production wait for material, and what did it cost?" Treat India as your proof, and take paid work there only when the numbers above say yes.

#### I1. Supplier PO follow-up for manufacturers (case study for E1)

**a. Who has it.** Manufacturers that buy raw material and parts from many suppliers: auto parts, fasteners, cycle parts, hosiery and knitwear, machine tools. Ludhiana is full of them. **Medium** (50 to 250 staff) is the only size where it can pay: a purchase head with one to three executives and 100 or more open POs. **Small** (20 to 49 staff) firms have the same problem, but the owner or one purchase person spends too little time on it for a fee to beat their salary. Use a small firm only as a free case study if it is the first to say yes.

**b. Steps today.**
1. The purchase executive raises a PO in Tally or their ERP, or logs it in an Excel "pending order" sheet, and sends it by email or WhatsApp.
2. They follow up with the supplier for order acceptance, dispatch date, and transport details.
3. Replies come back as WhatsApp texts, voice notes, photos of a challan, or emails.
4. They update the pending order sheet by hand and warn production or stores when material will be late.

Indian job ads list exactly these duties: "follow up with suppliers for order acceptance and delivery as per due date", "updating the pending indent/order sheet in Excel", and coordinating with vendors for "order confirmation, dispatch status, and timely delivery" ([Glassdoor India](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Job/purchase-executive-jobs-SRCH_IC2930179_KO0,18.htm), [JobLeads: Purchase Executive, Ludhiana](https://www.jobleads.com/in/job/purchase-executive--ludhiana--e8bb7d21367162aab7b71c0c7b0e6c410), [Zebronics purchase executive](https://jobs.zebronics.com/job/01K5K9507B3W3WHN/purchase-executive)).

**c. Software.** Tally Prime purchase orders or an Excel sheet on one side. WhatsApp and Gmail on the other.

**WhatsApp: keep the buyer's current number. Do not ask suppliers to message a new one.** Suppliers have messaged the same person for years and will keep doing it, so a new number would only create forwarding work. Use **WhatsApp coexistence** instead. It puts the WhatsApp Business app and the Cloud API on the same number. It works for Indian +91 numbers, and 1:1 chats sync to the API, with up to 180 days of history. Group chats do not sync ([Whautomate: coexistence](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence), [InstantReply](https://www.instantreply.co/blog/whatsapp-coexistence-what-actually-syncs-2026)). That limit does not hurt I1, because supplier follow-up is 1:1. The buyer keeps using the app on their phone as before. Your engine reads the same chats in the background and sends the chasers from the same number.

Two honest limits. (1) Coexistence needs the WhatsApp **Business** app. If the buyer uses personal WhatsApp on a company phone, they switch that number to the Business app once. Suppliers see no change. (2) Coexistence is set up through a WhatsApp provider (BSP) that supports it, and the client pays that fee, about ₹999 to ₹2,599 a month ([RichAutomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/whatsapp-api-pricing-comparison-india-2026)). Add it to your quote. Tally connection: see "How you connect to Tally" below.

**d. Volume and time (estimate).** 30 to 150 open POs at any time. 10 to 40 follow-ups a day at 3 to 5 minutes each, so 1 to 3 hours a day of one person.

**e. Who does it and cost.** A purchase executive at ₹12,000 to ₹30,000 a month (sources above). Chasing is only part of the job, so you save hours, not a salary. In rupees (26 days of 8 hours): 1 hour a day of a ₹12,000 executive is worth about ₹1,500 a month. 3 hours a day of a ₹30,000 executive is worth about ₹11,250 a month. Only the top of that range can carry a fee.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** Indian WhatsApp platforms like AiSensy and Interakt can send reminder templates for ₹999 to ₹2,599 a month ([RichAutomate comparison](https://richautomate.in/blog/whatsapp-api-pricing-comparison-india-2026)), and guides describe WhatsApp reminders for vendor follow-up ([RichAutomate: manufacturing](https://richautomate.in/blog/manufacturing-supply-chain-whatsapp-automation), [CampaignHQ](https://blog.campaignhq.co/whatsapp-for-manufacturing-india)). Tally's own Docs by Ira can turn a PDF or photo into a draft Purchase Order, Receipt Note or other voucher ([TallyHelp: Docs by Ira](https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs-by-ira/)). But it works on documents you upload, one transaction per document. Nothing I found says it reads WhatsApp replies, tracks dispatch dates against open POs, or chases silent suppliers. **The gap:** those tools send messages or make vouchers, but none that I found reads the supplier's reply, matches it to the open PO line in Tally or Excel, updates the pending sheet with a human approving changes, and chases the ones who went quiet. Enterprise procurement suites do this, but not at a Ludhiana SME price. **Brutal note:** the paid Indian market here is small. Its real value is a live case study for E1.

**g. Realistic price.** First client: near free, in exchange for publishing the numbers and a video. After that, paid only for medium firms that pass the test in section e: ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 setup and ₹4,000 to ₹6,000 a month, plus the BSP fee the client pays directly. Check it pays: a ₹15,000 setup spread over 12 months plus ₹4,000 a month is about ₹5,250 a month. A ₹25,000 setup plus ₹5,000 a month is about ₹7,100. The time saved must beat that. Two hours a day of a ₹30,000 executive, or three hours a day of a ₹20,000 one, is worth about ₹7,500 a month. So it pays only at that level of work, or when it prevents line stoppages. If the client cannot show one of those two, do not charge. Take the case study and move on.

**h. Sources.** Linked inline above.

#### I2. Customer PDF PO to Tally sales order, for manufacturers (demoted)

**Read this first.** TallyPrime now has **Docs by Ira**, a plug-in that turns PDF and image documents into **draft** vouchers, and Sales Order is one of the supported types. A person reviews each draft before it posts. It needs TallyPrime 7.0 or later with active TSS. Each eligible licence gets 100 free pages once, and after that it costs ₹200 plus GST per 100 pages ([TallyHelp: Docs by Ira](https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs-by-ira/), [Tally Solutions: Docs by Ira](https://tallysolutions.com/tally/docs-by-ira-ai-invoice-processing/), [TallyHelp: Docs by Ira FAQ](https://help.tallysolutions.com/docs-by-ira-faq/)). That is about ₹2 a page, inside the software every I2 prospect already runs. So the core of I2, "read the PDF and make a draft sales order in Tally", **is already solved**. By your own rule, reject it as a product.

**What is left, and when to still take it.** From what I found, Ira works on documents someone uploads, one transaction per document. I found nothing saying it (1) watches the email inbox, (2) reads Excel POs, (3) maps a customer's own item codes to your items, or (4) checks each line against that customer's rate contract. Test Ira on 20 of the client's real POs first. Take I2 only if it fails on (3) or (4) and those errors cost the client money. Even then, build it as a thin layer on top: your engine fetches the email, checks codes and rates, and flags mismatches. Let Ira or your Tally agent make the voucher. The details below are kept for that case.

**a. Who has it.** Component makers and job workers who sell to bigger companies (auto parts, fasteners, hosiery, cycle parts). **Both** sizes. My judgment, which you should check in person: the very largest OEMs push suppliers onto their own supplier portals, so the PDF PO pain sits mostly with suppliers to mid-sized buyers.

**b. Steps today.** A customer emails a PDF PO. A sales coordinator checks each line against the agreed rate list, enters a sales order or invoice in Tally, and emails back if a rate or item code is wrong.

**Evidence the job exists and is paid for:** Indeed India lists about 2,000 "order processing executive" jobs. Some ask the hire to "ensure customer purchase orders fully align with approved quotations" and to create orders in the ERP ([Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/q-order-processing-executive-jobs.html), [LinkedIn India, 4,000+ order processing jobs](https://in.linkedin.com/jobs/order-processing-jobs)). Ludhiana sales coordinator posts ask for "generating sales invoices in Tally accurately and timely as per sales/orders" ([Naukri: Ludhiana sales coordinator jobs](https://www.naukri.com/sales-coordinator-jobs-in-ludhiana)). Indian job ads also call this "order punching" ([Indeed India](https://in.indeed.com/q-order-punching-jobs.html)). **Honest limit:** these are India-wide search results and do not prove how many Ludhiana firms get PDF POs by email. Your week 1 visits settle that.

**c. Software.** Gmail or Outlook, then Tally Prime. Some use Zoho Books, which has a normal cloud API.

**d. Volume and time (estimate).** 10 to 80 POs a week. 10 to 20 minutes each, because there are many lines and the codes differ.

**e. Who and cost.** A sales coordinator at about ₹18,000 to ₹35,000 a month (estimate, in line with the Tally role ranges above).

**f. Tools and gap.** Logistify lists TallyPrime among the ERPs it connects to for PDF order entry ([Logistify](https://logistify.ai/blog/ai-pdf-sales-order-entry-for-unleashed-software)). It quotes custom prices and aims at US and global distributors ([SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/logistify-ai)). Karvan and Zotok focus on WhatsApp dealer orders, not rate contract checks on PDF POs. Tally integrators build custom TDL jobs priced per project ([IndiaMART](https://dir.indiamart.com/impcat/tally-customization-services.html)). Tally's own Docs by Ira makes the draft sales order for about ₹2 a page (see above). **The gap:** only the inbox watching, Excel POs, customer item code mapping and rate contract check. **Brutal note:** that gap is thin. Many owners will say "Ira is good enough", and they may be right.

**g. Price.** Only in the narrow case above: ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 setup, ₹2,000 to ₹4,000 a month. You cannot charge more than a small multiple of what Ira costs unless the rate errors are costly.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

#### How you connect to Tally (applies to I1 and I2)

Tally takes XML over HTTP on port 9000, but only while Tally is running with the company loaded ([Appycodes Tally integration guide](https://appycodes.dev/blog/tally-integration-tdl-guide-2026/), [community API docs](https://noumaanahamed.github.io/tally-prime-api-docs/)). So the design is:

1. **A small Java agent** (Developer A, about 2 to 4 days) on the PC that runs Tally, or on the server or cloud machine if the client uses TallyPrime Server or hosted Tally. It only makes outbound HTTPS calls to your server, so no ports are opened to the internet.
2. **Snapshots.** While Tally is open, the agent pushes stock, party outstanding balances and open POs every 15 minutes.
3. **Night messages still get worked.** The engine reads and drafts at any hour and checks each draft against the last snapshot. The review screen shows "checked against stock as of 7:40 pm". Approved entries wait in a queue and post when Tally opens next morning. Honest limit: the owner approves in the morning, not at midnight.
4. **Wrong company safety.** Before every write, the agent asks Tally which company is loaded. If it is not the client's company (for example, the accountant has another company open), it waits and alerts instead of posting.
5. **Busy is out of the first build.** Busy supports XML import and export from its Administration menu ([BUSY FAQ](https://busy.in/faqs/how-do-i-importexport-data-in-busy-answerid-67737/)). I found REST access only through third parties like BusyNotify and RootFi ([BusyNotify](https://busynotify.in/solutions/custom-apis), [RootFi](https://www.rootfi.dev/integrations/busy-accounting)), not an official public API. Serve Tally clients first. Add Busy later through XML import files or one of those connectors once a paying Busy client asks.

### Region 2: Europe (including the UK)

**Price check on your guess.** UK agency guides put simple workflows from about £500, mid range builds at £3,000 to £10,000, and monthly support at £200 to £800 ([AI Workforce UK](https://www.aiworkforce.co.uk/blogs/ai-automation-pricing-uk-small-businesses-2026)). Another survey puts single workflows at $400 to $1,200 and workflows with an AI step at $1,500 to $4,500 ([Lets Viz](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/ai-automation-agency-pricing-2026-what-buyers-pay)). One UK agency sells fixed sprints from £4,500 ([Axivon](https://www.axivon-ai.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-cost-uk)).

**My verdict on your guess:**
* **Build: $1,500 to $4,000 is right.** Small firms: $1,500 to $3,000. Medium firms: $3,000 to $6,000.
* **Upkeep: $400 to $1,500 a month matches the market.** The UK source above says £200 to £800 a month, which is about $265 to $1,065 at roughly $1.33 to the pound (estimate; check the day's rate). Your $400 floor sits inside that. For medium firms with high volume and promised response times, $600 to $1,500 is fair.
* **The real limit is not the market. It is each client's volume.** A client will not keep paying $400 a month to save $250 of staff time. So price small firms at $300 to $500 a month, and only sell to small firms whose volume passes the break even test in E1 (section g). A small firm that fails that test is not a cheap client. It is not a client.
* **The hard ceiling:** vendor add-ons are cheap. Cin7's own document reader starts at $79 a month for 600 documents ([Cin7 IDR](https://www.cin7.com/features/sales/idr/), [Cin7 pricing](https://www.cin7.com/pricing/)). SaaS order tools run about $399 to $799 a month ([OrderDrafter comparison](https://orderdrafter.com/alternatives/conexiom-alternative/)). Wherever such a tool exists, your monthly fee loses. That is why E1 and E2 are aimed where those tools do not reach.

#### E1. Supplier order confirmation check and chasing (build first)

**a. Who has it.** UK and Irish wholesalers, distributors, importers and light manufacturers that buy from many suppliers. **Medium** (50 to 250 staff, where a purchasing team exists) is the main target. **Small** (20 to 49 staff) firms fit **only if they send 50 or more POs a week**, which usually means importers and wholesalers with many suppliers. There the owner buys. A small firm with 20 POs a week has the pain but would lose money on the fee (see section g).

**b. Steps today.**
1. The firm raises a PO in its ERP and emails it to the supplier.
2. The supplier replies with an order confirmation (a PDF or email text). It may change the price, the quantity or the delivery date, or swap an item. Some suppliers never reply.
3. A purchasing assistant compares the confirmation to the PO line by line.
4. They update the ETA and price in the ERP and tell sales if a customer order will be late.
5. They chase suppliers who have not confirmed, and chase again as dates get close.

UK purchasing assistant job ads list these exact duties: chasing confirmations and dispatch dates, and updating ETAs and prices in the system ([Point Recruitment](https://pointrecruitment.co.uk/job-listing/purchasing-assistant), [Potential Recruitment](https://www.potentialrecruitment.com/role/purchasing-assistant/)).

**c. Software and APIs.**

| System | Who uses it | API for reading and updating POs? |
|---|---|---|
| Unleashed (now part of The Access Group) | UK, NZ and AU wholesalers | Yes. Its REST API ([docs](https://apidocs.unleashedsoftware.com/SalesOrders)) covers purchase orders as well as sales orders. It has a PO Kanban board and supplier contact emails, but it does not read supplier replies ([Unleashed purchase orders](https://www.unleashedsoftware.com/en-us/product/purchase-order-software/), [Unleashed help](https://help-unleashed.theaccessgroup.com/en/articles/11582055-sales-orders)) |
| Cin7 Core | Product businesses | Yes, REST with API key ([docs](https://dearinventory.docs.apiary.io/)). Its purchase IDR reads supplier **invoices and credit notes** against the PO, not confirmations before shipping ([Cin7 Core help](https://help.core.cin7.com/hc/en-us/articles/12119964382479-Extract-purchase-data-with-Intelligent-Document-Recognition-IDR)) |
| Sage 200 | UK medium firms | Yes, API with order endpoints ([Sage developer community](https://developer-community.sage.com/topic/19805-basic-sales-order-flow-with-apis/)) |
| Sage 50 desktop | Many UK small firms | **No native REST API.** Needs a connector on the same PC or network ([Hyperext](https://www.hyperext.com/sage-50-integration/does-sage-50-have-an-api-yes-heres-what-you-need-to-know/)). Avoid at first. |
| Email | Everyone | Microsoft 365 (Graph) or Gmail API |

That is 2 tools per client (email plus ERP), which fits your rule.

**d. Volume and time (estimate).** 20 to 150 POs a week. 5 to 10 minutes to check each confirmation. Chasing adds more: if about half of POs need one chase of about 5 minutes, that is about 2.5 minutes per PO on average. So plan on **about 10 minutes of work per PO** in total, and measure it on the first call. At the top end that is one person for most of the week. A vendor in this space claims mid-market buyers lose 15 to 20 hours a week each to supplier follow-up ([Leverage AI](https://tryleverage.ai/blog/pf/purchase-order-automation-mid-market-manufacturers-guide-1)). Treat that as a seller's number.

**e. Who does it and cost.** Purchasing assistants at about £25,000 to £29,000 a year (job ad sources above), roughly £30,000 to £35,000 with employer costs (estimate).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* **Leverage AI** automates PO confirmations but is built for mid-market manufacturers processing 500 to 5,000+ PO lines a month on Dynamics 365, SAP, NetSuite, Epicor or Infor ([Leverage AI](https://tryleverage.ai/), [Leverage integrations](https://tryleverage.ai/integrations)).
* **Didero and Tacto** target mid-sized to large manufacturers on SAP, Oracle and Dynamics ([Procurement Magazine on Didero](https://procurementmag.com/news/how-didero-is-putting-procurement-on-autopilot), [Tacto on Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10023900/Tacto/)).
* **Conexiom and Doxis** handle order acknowledgements at enterprise prices ([Conexiom](https://conexiom.com/blog/what-is-order-acknowledgement-and-why-is-it-important), [Doxis](https://www.doxis.com/en/solutions/order-confirmation-automation)).
* **The gap:** nothing I found reads supplier confirmations and chases non-replies for a 20 to 200 person firm on Unleashed, Cin7 Core or Sage 200.
* **Brutal notes.** (1) Cin7 could stretch its IDR to cover confirmations, so start with Unleashed and Sage 200 clients. (2) Many small suppliers never send a formal confirmation. The chasing half of this workflow matters as much as the reading half, so build both. (3) This saves cost and protects revenue: "you find out an order is late before your customer does." Lead with that line.

**g. Realistic price, and who it pays for.** Small (50+ POs a week only): $1,500 to $2,500 build, $300 to $500 a month. Medium: $3,000 to $5,000 build, $600 to $1,200 a month.

**Break even test (estimate, run it on every prospect).** A purchasing assistant costs about £32,500 a year with employer costs. Over about 1,650 working hours that is about £20, or $26 to $27, an hour. At 10 minutes per PO, each PO costs about $4.40 of staff time. A small firm deal of $2,000 build (spread over 12 months, about $167) plus $400 a month costs the client about $570 a month.

| POs a week | Staff time a month | Value at $4.40 per PO | Against $570 a month |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | about 14 hours | about $380 | **Loses money. Do not sell.** |
| 30 | about 22 hours | about $570 | Break even |
| 50 | about 36 hours | about $950 | Pays, about 1.7 times |
| 150 | about 108 hours | about $2,860 | Pays well, even at medium prices |

So **sell E1 at 50 or more POs a week**. Between 30 and 50, sell only if the client cares a lot about late orders, because catching a late delivery before the customer does protects revenue that this table does not count. Below 30, walk away.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

#### E2. Customer PO email to sales order, Sage 200 firms only (second product)

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers, distributors and manufacturers selling to trade customers. On Sage 200, that means mostly **medium** firms, where a customer service or sales operations head owns the process.

**b. Steps today.** A customer emails a PO (PDF, Excel or email text). A clerk finds the account, maps the customer's product codes to the firm's SKUs, checks price, stock and credit, keys the order line by line, and sends a confirmation.

**c. Software.** Outlook or Gmail, then Sage 200 sales order endpoints ([Sage developer community](https://developer-community.sage.com/topic/19805-basic-sales-order-flow-with-apis/)). A lookup table holds the customer code to SKU map.

**d. Volume and time (estimate).** 20 to 300 orders a day, 3 to 15 minutes each. A firm with 60 orders a day at 8 minutes spends 8 hours a day, so one full time person.

**e. Who does it and cost.** Totaljobs listed about 754 UK "sales order processor" jobs ([Totaljobs](https://www.totaljobs.com/jobs/sales-order-processor)). Pay is about £21,000 to £29,000, and Indeed gives an order processor average of £26,832 as of May 2026 ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries), [Prime Appointments](https://prime-appointments.co.uk/sales-order-processor)).

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** This is where the category is most crowded, so here is the ERP by ERP truth:

**g. Realistic price.** $3,500 to $6,000 build, $700 to $1,500 a month. Sell it as "less than a quarter of one order clerk."

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

#### E3. Remittance advice to payment allocation (conditional)

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and manufacturers that sell on credit to many trade customers. **Both** sizes, mostly **medium**, where a credit controller exists.

**b. Steps today.** A customer pays one lump sum covering many invoices and emails a remittance PDF. A credit controller reads it, matches each line to an open invoice, allocates the payment in the ERP, and chases short payments or unexplained deductions. The pain is real ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/cash-application-software)).

**c. Software.** Email plus the accounting or ERP system (Sage 200, Odoo or similar). Before quoting, confirm that the client's ERP API allows receipt allocation. If it does not, produce an allocation file the controller imports in one step.

**d. Volume and time (estimate).** 20 to 200 remittances a month. 5 to 20 minutes each, longer when one payment covers 40 invoices.

**e. Who and cost.** A credit controller at about £25,000 to £32,000 a year (estimate). Allocation is part of the job, not all of it.

**f. Tools and gap.** RemitClear already does this for Xero and QuickBooks Online ([Xero App Store](https://apps.xero.com/us/app/remitclear)). **Only sell this to firms on Sage 200, Odoo or other ERPs RemitClear does not cover.** You do not move money. You suggest the allocation and a person approves it.

**g. Price.** $1,500 to $3,000 build, $250 to $600 a month. Best as an add-on for an existing E1 or E2 client.

**h. Sources.** Inline above.

#### Europe: rules you must follow to get paid

1. **GDPR.** Sign a data processing agreement with each client. Host in the EU (the Hetzner server in the budget below is in Germany or Finland). Send documents only to an LLM endpoint in an EU region with no training on client data, and say so in writing.
2. **Avoid Business Central, Cin7 Core order intake, Odoo order intake and Sage 50 desktop at first.** The first three have built in or cheap readers. The last has no cloud API.
3. **Time zones work.** 9 am in the UK is 1:30 pm in Ludhiana. You can cover the UK working day in your afternoon and evening.

## Final top picks

**1. E1, supplier confirmation checking and chasing, for UK and Irish wholesalers and importers on Unleashed or Sage 200 that send 50 or more POs a week.** Why: firms already pay purchasing assistants for it, and at that volume the fee is clearly smaller than the time saved. The known tools target bigger ERPs and bigger firms. The ERP APIs are clean. And unlike customer order intake, no ERP vendor I checked has shipped a built in fix. Pick one ERP first so your second client is setup, not a rebuild. Unleashed is a good start because it is built for wholesalers and has a documented REST API.

**2. I1 in Ludhiana first, as the proof, on the buyer's own WhatsApp number.** Why: you can walk in, watch the purchase executive for a morning, and have real numbers within weeks. The engine is the same one E1 needs. Pick a medium manufacturer with 100 or more open POs so the numbers look like a European buyer's. Expect to do it near free. That is fine: you are buying a case study, not earning Indian revenue.

**3. E2 on Sage 200, as your second European product.** Its proof is the reading and matching half of I1. Do not plan on I2: Tally's Docs by Ira already makes draft sales orders from PDFs for about ₹2 a page.

**What I would not do:** customer PDF PO to Tally as a product (Docs by Ira), WhatsApp order taking for distributors, order intake on Cin7 Core, Odoo or Business Central, supplier invoice capture, GST reconciliation, or freight quoting. Details below.

**The hard truth:** your bottleneck is selling, not building. You can build E1 in a month. Finding the first European buyer will take longer. Start outreach in week 1, not after the build.
