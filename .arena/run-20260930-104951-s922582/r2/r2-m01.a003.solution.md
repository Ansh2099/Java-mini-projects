# Workflows Worth Selling: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

1. **Build one thing first: "customer order arrives by email or WhatsApp, and it becomes a checked sales order in the client's system."** It has the clearest proof of paid pain (a full time salary spent on typing), a clean human approval step, and it is the same job in India and Europe. That gives you a real case study.
2. **Be clear eyed: this is a crowded, proven market, not an empty gap.** For most popular systems a built in feature or a cheap app already reads PDF orders: Business Central (built in agent), Odoo (apps), Katana (LevelOps app), Cin7 Core (built in add on), Fortnox (Qrisp). Do not sell to those firms. **Pick your clients by how messy their orders are, not only by their ERP.** Your targets: (a) UK firms on Sage 200, where the rivals are an agency and an add on, not cheap apps; and (b) firms on any system with a working API whose orders arrive as a mix of PDF, Excel and plain email text, full of each customer's own part codes. That is what the cheap tools handle worst.
3. **Your second product, sold only to your first clients: "supplier order confirmation arrives, it is checked against our purchase order, and missing ones are chased."** It reuses most of the same code. The chasing and late supplier tracking are the parts ready tools cover least.
4. **Stay away from invoice entry, bank entry, GST reconciliation, payment reminders, supplier statement matching and freight quoting.** Cheap tools and built in features already do these.
5. **India is for proof, not profit, unless the client is big.** Below about 80 orders a day, your fee roughly equals the clerk time you save, so the owner gains little. Target Indian manufacturers that get purchase orders by email as PDF or Excel from OEMs and big buyers. That is the closest match to what European buyers need.
6. **Your Europe price guess needs one correction.** 1,500 to 4,000 USD is right only as an intro price for your first 2 or 3 clients. After that, list at 5,000 to 8,000 USD. A Dutch agency publishes order intake builds from EUR 8,500. Upkeep is 400 to 800 USD a month. 1,500 a month is too high except for heavy volume.
7. **The hardest part is not the code. It is getting the first European client to trust a two person team in Ludhiana.** An Indian case study with real numbers, plus your Finland client, is your way in.

## How to read this

* **Confidence labels.** Every workflow has a confidence level: High, Medium or Low. It says how sure I am that businesses will pay you for it.
* **"Estimate"** means I worked the number out myself. It is not from a source. Check it on your first 5 sales calls.
* **Sources.** All links were found through web search on 30 September 2026. Most pages could be seen only as search results, not opened in full, so I treat vendor numbers as vendor claims. Blog case studies are single stories, not surveys.
* **Money.** I use about ₹88 per USD, about 1.33 USD per GBP and about 1.15 USD per EUR. Check the rate on the day you quote.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK, the EU, Norway and Switzerland. The UK, the Netherlands, German speaking countries and the Nordics are the best targets because they buy software, answer email in English and pay on time.
2. "Build in a few weeks part time" means about 60 to 120 hours for the first client, and about half that for the second client with the same workflow.
3. You run the core pipeline in your own code (Java or Node). Use n8n only for glue, if at all. Check n8n's Sustainable Use License before you host it for clients.

**Worked examples:**
* **Big Indian distributor, 300 orders a day (9,000 a month):** model about 18 to 27 USD, WhatsApp about 11 USD, server 5 to 20 USD. Total about 34 to 58 USD. **That breaks your budget if you pay it yourself.**
* **Free Indian pilot capped at 100 orders a day (3,000 a month):** model about 6 to 9 USD, WhatsApp about 3 USD, server 5 to 20 USD. Total about 14 to 32 USD. Fits.
* **UK client, 40 orders a day (about 880 a month):** model about 1 USD. Server share and API access free at this stage. Easy fit.

**Three rules that are required, not optional:** (1) cap every free pilot at 100 orders a day; (2) paying clients pay model and WhatsApp costs at cost, on top of your fee; (3) set India monthly prices by volume band (below) so the fee always covers running costs many times over.

## Part 1: India

**The honest picture.** Indian owners will pay, but little, and only when the gain is obvious. An order entry clerk costs about ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 a month ([PW Skills](https://pwskills.com/blog/tally/tally-operator-salary-work-job-for-freshers)). Cheap Indian SaaS products already cover the most common jobs. **Bottom line: India has one well evidenced workflow for you, it pays only at volume, and it is crowded in the WhatsApp distributor segment. Treat India mainly as proof of work for Europe.**

**Two technical facts that shape every Indian build.**

1. **Tally is local.** TallyPrime talks to other software through XML over HTTP on port 9000 on the client's own computer. There is no cloud login ([AI Accountant guide](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/tally-api-integration-guide), [TallyHelp JSON integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime-integration-using-json-1/), [TallyPrime API Explorer](https://tallysolutions.com/tallyprime-api-explorer/)). It only answers while TallyPrime is open with the right company loaded. So build a small agent program for the client's Windows PC that:
   * pulls approved entries from your server into a local queue;
   * names the target company in every XML request, and checks first that Tally is running with that company loaded;
   * retries later if Tally is closed, and sends the operator a WhatsApp alert if an entry waits more than, say, 30 minutes;
   * logs every posted entry so nothing is posted twice.
   Best setup: run it on the PC that keeps Tally open all day, usually the accounts PC. BUSY has no default integration option and sends you to its channel partners ([BUSY FAQ](https://busy.in/faqs/third-party-services/integration/1/)), though third parties sell BUSY APIs ([BusyNotify](https://busynotify.in/solutions/custom-apis), [RootFi](https://www.rootfi.dev/integrations/busy-accounting)). Plan for Tally first. Treat BUSY as extra work.
2. **WhatsApp needs the Cloud API, but staff can keep their app.** The WhatsApp Business app has no API for reading messages. Use Meta's **Coexistence** feature: the client's existing number is connected to the Cloud API through Meta or a provider, and the Business app keeps working on the same number. Messages sync both ways, and up to 180 days of one to one history is imported ([YCloud](https://www.ycloud.com/blog/whatsapp-business-app-coexistence-meta-update), [360dialog docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/phone-numbers/coexistence), [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users/)). Limits to tell the client: the app must be opened at least once every 13 days, and **group chats do not sync** ([InstantReply](https://www.instantreply.co/blog/whatsapp-coexistence-what-actually-syncs-2026)). If buyers send orders in a WhatsApp group, that client is not a fit.

**Confidence: Medium** that the pain is real and paid for (job listings, published cases). **Low to Medium** that you can charge well for it, because owners compare you to a ₹12,500 clerk.

Two more honest points. First, the owner rarely fires anyone. The saving shows up as "we grew without hiring a second clerk", so sell to firms that are growing. Second, speed and errors often matter more than salary for manufacturers: a wrong rate on an OEM PO, or a missed line on a large order, can cost more than a month of the clerk's pay. Ask each prospect for their last costly order mistake. That is your pitch in India.

**Why it matters anyway:** this is the same job as Europe workflow 1. A case study that says "a Ludhiana manufacturer's emailed POs now go from inbox to Tally in 40 seconds with a human check, and typing errors fell from X to Y" sells directly to a UK wholesaler.

## Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

**The honest picture.** Europe pays well, and the order workflows below are clearly paid for today. But they are not secret. Tools, add ons and small agencies already sell them for most common ERPs. Microsoft Business Central now has a built in **Sales Order Agent** that reads customer emails and PDF purchase orders and creates quotes and orders ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent)), and a **Payables Agent** for supplier invoices ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/payables-agent)). **Rule for every sales call: find out which ERP the prospect runs, check it against the map below, then search "[their ERP] sales order automation" and know the rival's name before you talk.**

## Part 4: My top picks for you

### Pick 1: Order to ERP (Europe workflow 1, proven first in India workflow 1)

* **Why:** It has the clearest proof of paid pain (a £27,000 a year role that mostly types), published prices you can undercut, and a clean human approval step. Agencies like Werkritme, Zestcode and Netum sell exactly this as a service, which proves your business model works. It fits both of you: Developer A builds matching and ERP posting in Spring Boot with Spring AI; Developer B builds email and WhatsApp intake and the approval screen in TypeScript.
* **Who to sell to:** UK Sage 200 firms first, then firms on Exact, Unleashed or other API ready systems, always passing the four question qualifier above (API, mess, volume, no add on). Skip Business Central, Odoo, Katana, Cin7 Core, Fortnox, QuickBooks Online and Sage 50 firms.
* **Main risk:** you are one seller among many, and more ERP vendors will add this feature, as Microsoft, Cin7 and Katana already have. Reduce it by owning the messy part: each customer's codes, mixed file types, price rules and delivery rules. Cheap tools read clean PDFs; you handle everything else.
* **Chance it works:** about 20 to 35% chance of landing a paying European client within 6 months, if you get one strong Indian case study and use your Finland contact for introductions. This is a judgement, not a sourced number. I lowered it because more ERPs now have built in or cheap tools than I first found, which shrinks your pool. It is still the best odds of anything I checked, because nothing else has this much proof that buyers pay.

### Pick 2: Supplier confirmation check and chasing (Europe workflow 2)

* **Why:** It reuses most of Pick 1's code (my estimate: about two thirds): reading documents, matching lines, the approval screen and ERP APIs. The chasing and supplier slip tracking are the parts ready tools cover least.
* **When:** only as the second workflow for your Pick 1 clients. Do not lead with it. Skip Business Central firms that use Continia, and Odoo firms.

### Pick 3: Nothing else yet

The India dispatch chasing idea stays on the "test first" list. Freight quoting and everything else in the rejected tables are off the list. Two focused products beat five half built ones for a part time team.

### What to do in the next 30 days

1. **Find 2 Indian manufacturers for Pick 1** that receive POs by email as PDF or Excel from OEMs or big buyers, post into Tally, and handle at least 20 POs a day. Ludhiana auto parts, fastener and cycle parts firms are the obvious start. Ask each one: "What was your last costly order mistake?" Offer the build free or at cost for case study rights, capped at 100 orders a day.
2. **Before building, ask each for one week of real orders.** Count them and time 20 by hand. That checks my estimates and gives you the "before" number.
3. **In parallel, message 30 UK wholesalers and distributors on Sage 200.** Ask one question: "How many orders a day does your team retype from email, and how many come as Excel or plain email text?" Run the four question qualifier before any call.
4. **Set up the cost and data basics:** build on Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite for India and Mistral Small (or Vertex AI in an EU region) for European data, not on Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, which retires in October 2026. Register for Sage 200 API keys and confirm there is no fee. Prepare a simple data processing agreement. European buyers will ask about GDPR before price.
