# Workflows Worth Selling: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Here is the part you will not like: I did not find a proven, open gap in either region. In the UK, almost every clean finance workflow on a mainstream ledger (Xero, QuickBooks, Sage 50, Sage 200, Business Central) already has a cheap product. My best European idea survives only on ledgers those products skip, and even there, mid market "any ERP" tools sell the same thing by file upload, one of them with plans from about 100 USD a month. In India, my best matching idea has a direct local competitor too. So this report does not hand you a sure thing. It hands you one narrow bet, the exact test that kills it in four weeks if it is wrong, and one small local earner.

**Pick 1 (a bet: test it before you build it): supplier statement reconciliation on Exact Online in the Netherlands, sold as a done for you monthly service.** A supplier sends a monthly statement. Someone checks every line against the ledger, finds missing invoices, missing credit notes and wrong amounts, and emails the supplier. On Xero and QuickBooks, Dext already does this inside its normal plans. On Sage, Business Central and NetSuite, Lightyear does it, including the chase email, from about 149 USD a month. On any ERP, 360° AP, Open ECX, FlexTrap and Telic do it if someone uploads the statements and ledger data. What is left for you is thin and specific: a firm on **Exact Online** (675,000+ businesses, mainly Netherlands, Belgium and Germany), which none of those tools lists as a native connector, that **will not run another tool itself** and wants the reconciliation done for it every month, with reply drafts in Dutch. You do not write the product until 31 October, when 3 Dutch firms have passed the gate in Step 8.

**Pick 2 (India, the only local earner): export shipment document cross-check** for Ludhiana exporters. It checks the customs agent's checklist and the draft bill of lading against your invoice and packing list before anyone approves them. Useful and local. It will not help you sell in Europe.

**Practice ground, not a pick: party ledger reconciliation in Tally.** Same matching engine as Pick 1. By my own numbers it costs a trader less in labour than a monthly fee, and Firmway already sells it in India. Run it free for two firms to get accuracy numbers for the Dutch pitch. Do not plan on it for income.

**Not a pick, but know it: emailed purchase order to draft sales order.** Strongest labour case of anything here, and the most crowded. Business Central, QuickBooks, Cin7, Unleashed and Exact Online all have a product or add-on for it already. Only take it if the Step 2 check comes back empty for that client's ERP.

**Your Europe price guess is mostly right.** 1,500 to 4,000 USD for the first workflow is fine for medium firms. 400 to about 1,000 USD a month for upkeep is right for a workflow with a clear labour case; 1,500 needs high volume. Statement reconciliation is the exception: plan on €250 to €450 (290 to 530 USD) a month, because statement tools from about 100 USD a month set its ceiling.

**The hardest part is not the build.** It is proving Dutch firms really work through 50+ supplier statements a month, and then getting a Dutch finance manager to trust two unknown developers in Ludhiana with their ledger. The plan at the end tests the first before you write code.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK plus EU countries where finance teams work comfortably in English (UK, Ireland, Netherlands, Nordics, Belgium). Germany and France are harder for you because of language and local accounting tools.
2. Exchange rates used, rounded: 1 GBP = 1.33 USD, 1 EUR = 1.17 USD, 1 USD = 88 INR. Check the day's rate before you quote.
3. "Loaded" staff cost = salary plus about 20% for employer taxes and pension in the UK, 1,950 working hours a year. In the Netherlands: 12 months of salary plus 8% holiday allowance, plus about 20% employer costs **(estimate)**, over about 1,700 working hours.
4. Anything marked **(estimate, measure in discovery)** has no source. Do not quote it to a buyer until you have timed it at a real client.

Several sites blocked direct page opening from my research setup, so some figures come from the page text shown in search results. Open the three or four numbers you plan to put in a sales deck and check them yourself first.

**What survives, and why.** The pattern is simple. Once a workflow is generic enough to describe in one sentence, a product builds it for the three or four biggest ledgers first (Xero, QuickBooks, Sage, Business Central). Products get to the smaller and regional ledgers late, because each connector costs them the same work for fewer customers. That gap is your only honest opening in Europe: **the same proven workflow, on a ledger the products have not reached yet, for a client who wants it done rather than a tool to run.** Be clear about how thin that is. The "any ERP" tools can still reach an uncovered ledger if the clerk exports a file each month. So your real competitor on Exact is "a tool with plans from about 100 USD a month, plus 30 minutes of the clerk's time exporting and uploading." You win only with clients who refuse that. It is a smaller market, and it closes over time, so you test fast and pick one ledger.

## Step 2: Europe (including the UK)

### The 5 step competitor check (run it before every sales call)

The brief says reject anything a built in feature or cheap tool already solves. For your workflows, the answer depends on the client's ledger and on whether the client will run a tool itself, so you check per client, not once.

1. Is the ledger Xero or QuickBooks Online? Then Dext covers statement reconciliation. Stop.
2. Is the ledger on Lightyear's integrations list (Xero, QBO, MYOB, Sage 50, Sage 200, Sage Intacct, Business Central, NetSuite, AccountsIQ, Iplicit and others)? Open the current list at [lightyear.cloud/features/integrations](https://www.lightyear.cloud/features/integrations). If it is there, stop.
3. Does the ledger vendor sell its own add-on? Search the vendor's app store (for Exact: [exact.com/nl/app-store](https://www.exact.com/nl/app-store)) for "statement", "afschrift", "afstemmen" and "reconciliation" (or "order" and "PDF" for order entry).
4. Search "<ledger name> supplier statement reconciliation" and the local language version. If a product under about 300 USD a month shows up, stop.
5. **Check the tools that never name the ledger.** Step 4 cannot find them, because they say "any ERP." Search "supplier statement reconciliation any ERP" and "PDF upload", then open the current pages of 360° AP (AP Accurate), Open ECX, FlexTrap and Telic (for India: Firmway and AI Accountant). Note the published price for the statement module, or ask the prospect if they have been quoted. Then ask the prospect one question: **"Would your team export a supplier ledger file every month and upload it into a tool?"** If a tool is priced under about €300 a month for their volume **and** they answer yes, stop. They should buy the tool. You continue only if they answer no.

If all five come back clear, the client qualifies. Save a screenshot of each search, and write down their answer to the step 5 question, in the client folder. When the finance manager asks "why not just buy a tool?", you show them, and you remind them of their own answer.

### E1. Supplier statement reconciliation on uncovered ledgers, done for the client (TOP PICK, but a bet to test first)

**a. Who has it, and size.**
Wholesalers, distributors, manufacturers, construction contractors and multi-site hospitality groups that buy on credit from many suppliers. **Medium (50 to 250 staff): the target.** Small (5 to 49): only if they pass the same qualifier as everyone else: **50 or more supplier statements received a month, and they already reconcile at least some of them today.** The math behind 50 is in section e. The second half matters because of your own rule 7: a firm that reconciles none today has a fully manual (or absent) process, and you would have to create the habit, not speed it up.

Where to find them:
* **Netherlands, on Exact Online.** Exact serves more than 675,000 businesses, mainly in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany ([Exact about us](https://www.exact.com/about-us), [Wikipedia on Exact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exact_(company))). Neither the Dext statements feature (Xero and QBO) nor the Lightyear lists I found include Exact. Dutch B2B e-invoicing is only mandatory from 1 July 2030, so PDF invoices and PDF statements stay common for almost four more years ([Sovos](https://sovos.com/blog/global-vat/netherlands-b2b-e-invoicing-mandate-2030/), [vatcalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/netherlands/netherlands-reluctant-on-domestic-e-invoicing/), [Meijburg](https://www.meijburg.com/news/dutch-government-opts-mandatory-e-invoicing-and-digital-reporting-b2b-transactions)). Belgium is weaker: e-invoicing has been mandatory there since January 2026, so fewer invoices go missing.
* **Other ledgers to run the check on:** AFAS and Twinfield (Netherlands), Visma and Fortnox (Nordics), Procountor and Netvisor (Finland, where Developer A already has a client), Odoo, and older UK mid-market ledgers. I have **not** verified these. Run the 5 step check for each.

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: a supplier emails a statement (PDF, sometimes Excel) to the accounts inbox, usually in the first week of the month.
2. A purchase ledger clerk opens that supplier's account in the ledger.
3. They tick each statement line against the ledger: invoices, credit notes, payments.
4. For lines on the statement but not in the ledger, they search email for the invoice or ask the supplier for a copy.
5. For amounts that differ, they check the original invoice and the purchase order.
6. They email the supplier about missing credits or wrong amounts.
7. End: the reconciliation is saved (often in Excel), and the account is marked "agreed" or "differences open."

A UK vendor in this space, Fiscal, says most teams only manage to reconcile their top 10 to 20 suppliers and the rest go unchecked ([Fiscal guide](https://fiscaltec.com/supplier-statement-reconciliation-ultimate-guide/), [Fiscal on the process](https://fiscaltec.com/supplier-statement-reconciliation-what-it-is-why-it-matters-for-ap/)).

**c. Software on each side.**
* Inbox: Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace. In the default design you need **no access to it** (see build step 1).
* Ledger:
  * **Exact Online:** REST API. Standard limits are 60 calls per company per minute and 5,000 per day, which is plenty for one reconciliation run a month ([Exact API limits](https://support.exactonline.com/community/s/article/All-All-DNO-Simulation-gen-apilimits?language=en_GB), [Apideck Exact guide](https://www.apideck.com/blog/guide-to-exact-online-api-integration), [Peliqan on Exact API](https://peliqan.io/blog/exact-online-api/)). API access follows the rights of the Exact user who authorises the app, so the client creates a user with read only rights on financial data **(check the exact role names in the client's Exact admin during setup)**.
  * Odoo Online: external API only on the Custom plan, not Standard. Check before you quote ([Odoo external API docs](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/19.0/developer/reference/external_api.html), [Odoo forum](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/does-the-external-api-really-require-the-custom-plan-on-odoo-online-or-is-there-a-trial-window-306032)).
* That is 2 tools (inbox plus ledger), 3 if they keep a shared Excel reconciliation file.

**d. How often and how long.**
Each supplier, once a month. Fiscal puts an average manual statement at about 30 minutes and a complex one at around 2 hours ([Fiscal on cost of errors](https://fiscaltec.com/the-cost-of-errors-in-statement-reconciliation-what-ap-teams-must-know/), [Fiscal fact sheet, May 2026](https://fiscaltec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Statement-Reconciliation-Fact-Sheet-May-2026.pdf), [Klearstack](https://klearstack.com/blogs/supplier-invoice-reconciliation-process-best-practices)).

**e. Who does it and what it costs.**
A purchase ledger clerk. UK proof that firms pay for this role: average salary £26,802 (Indeed, updated 1 August 2026), £26,000 to £32,750 outside London and £35,250 to £44,500 in London (Robert Half), and job ads name "supplier statement reconciliations" as a duty ([Robert Half](https://www.roberthalf.com/gb/en/job-details/purchase-ledger-clerk), [Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/purchase-ledger-clerk/salaries)). Westgate Moore sells supplier statement reconciliation as an outsourced service, which shows firms pay outsiders to do it ([Westgate Moore](https://www.westgatemoore.co.uk/statement-recs/)).
For the Netherlands: a "medewerker crediteurenadministratie" (accounts payable clerk) earns about €2,940 gross a month on Glassdoor NL (May 2026) and €3,248 on Indeed NL ([Glassdoor NL](https://www.glassdoor.nl/Salarissen/medewerker-crediteurenadministratie-salarissen-SRCH_KO0,35.htm), [Indeed NL](https://nl.indeed.com/career/medewerker-crediteuren/salaries)). Loaded (Assumption 3): €2,940 x 12 x 1.08 x 1.20 / 1,700 hours = **about €27 an hour**. So one statement at 30 minutes costs about **€13.50** in the Netherlands and about **£8** in the UK (£26,802 x 1.2 / 1,950 = about £16.50 an hour).

What I did **not** find: a Dutch job ad that names monthly supplier statement reconciliation as a duty, or a Dutch firm that sells it as an outsourced service. Dutch AP ads I found list booking and checking invoices, reminders and supplier contact by email and phone ([Indeed NL AP vacancies](https://nl.indeed.com/Medewerker-Crediteurenadministratie-vacatures), [ElectronicPartner vacancy](https://electronicpartner.nl/vacature/medewerker-crediteurenadministratie-m-v/)). Treat that as a warning sign, not proof either way. Dutch firms may work from their own open items list and chase suppliers' reminders, rather than tick monthly statements the British way. Step 8 tests this first.

**The brutal math, redone.** Your fee is €250 to €450 a month.

| Case | Statements a month | Manual hours | Labour (NL, €13.50 each) | Fee as a share of that labour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Break even with full coverage | 19 to 33 | 9.5 to 16.5 h | €250 to €450 | 100% |
| **Qualifier floor, all reconciled** | **50** | **25 h** | **€675** | **37% to 67%** |
| Larger firm, all reconciled | 100 | 50 h | €1,350 | 19% to 33% |
| Typical today: only the top 20 done | 20 | 10 h | €270 | 93% to 167% |

Read the last row honestly. Compared to what a firm **spends today** on its top 20, your fee is about the same or higher. So labour saved does **not** pay for you, and it will not win the renewal. You sell on two things:
1. **Coverage:** every statement checked, not the top 20. At 50 statements, doing the whole job by hand would cost €675 a month, so your fee is clearly the cheaper way to get full coverage. That is why 50 is the floor: below about 35, a firm could simply ask its clerk for a few more hours.
2. **Money found:** Fiscal says its users find missing credits averaging £13,000 and missing invoices averaging £8,000. These are a vendor's own marketing claims, not audited facts ([Fiscal fact sheet](https://fiscaltec.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Statement-Reconciliation-Fact-Sheet-May-2026.pdf)). **Renewal depends on this number, and you have no independent source for it.** So every pilot must report "money found" in euros each month (the capture list in Step 4). If a pilot finds less than its fee in 3 months, expect the client to cancel, and treat it as evidence against the whole pick.

Under 50 statements a month, or zero reconciled today: walk away.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* Dext Supplier Statements: Xero and QBO only. Included in all Dext plans.
* Lightyear: statement matching plus supplier chase emails on Xero, QBO, Sage 50, Sage 200, Business Central, NetSuite, Sage Intacct and more. Not Exact Online on the lists I found.
* Fiscal, Xelix, Basware, Kefron: "any ERP" statement tools, sold inside full accounts payable platforms and aimed at larger finance teams ([Fiscal](https://fiscaltec.com/solutions/statement-reconciliation/), [Xelix](https://xelix.com/xelix-supplier-statement-reconciliation), [Basware](https://www.basware.com/en/solutions/ap-automation/statement-matching), [Kefron](https://kefron.com/finance-automation/accounts-payable-automation/features/automated-supplier-statement-reconciliation)).
* **Mid market "any ERP" tools, the real threat.** 360° AP's AP Accurate takes statements "in any format" (PDF, Excel, CSV), matches every line against "your ERP", flags near matches for review, and offers a free proof of concept on your own statements. Its pricing page, titled "Plans From $100/mo", lists five modules in Starter to Enterprise tiers; I could not see the AP Accurate price itself ([AP Accurate](https://360-ap.com/ap-accurate), [360° AP pricing](https://360-ap.com/pricing)). Open ECX names COINS, IFS, Oracle, Business Central, SAP and Sage ([Open ECX](https://www.openecx.com/statement-reconciliation)). FlexTrap matches "across every connected ERP", and one case study had the client upload SAP data through a secure link ([FlexTrap](https://flextrap.flextecs.com/statement-reconciliation)). Telic names SAP, Oracle, Dynamics and NetSuite, with custom pricing ([Telic](https://telic.digital/automated-supplier-statement-reconciliation/)). **None of the pages I found names Exact Online as a native connector**, so on Exact they work by file export and upload.
* SupplierRecon: a browser tool that matches a CSV or Excel statement against a ledger export you upload by hand. No PDF reading, no ledger connection, no supplier email ([SupplierRecon](https://supplierrecon.com/)).
* Exact Online itself: matches bank statements to open items ("afletteren") and gives an open items list per supplier that exports to Excel, but does not read supplier statements ([Exact docs on afletteren](https://www.exactsoftware.com/docs/DocView.aspx?DocumentID=%7B91e11175-f81f-4644-a9d0-751a18115e0e%7D), [Exact open items for creditors](https://support.exactonline.com/community/s/article/All-All-HNO-Task-financial-purchase-finpurch-apovrvwgent?language=nl_NL)).

**The gap, stated honestly (and it is thinner than I first thought):** a medium Dutch firm on Exact Online **can** buy a statement tool today. It would export its supplier ledger from Exact each month, upload it with the statements, and have its clerk work the exceptions inside that tool. What it cannot buy, from what I found, is: (1) a connection that pulls the Exact ledger by itself, with no export, (2) Dutch reply drafts to suppliers, and (3) someone else doing the monthly run while the clerk only approves exceptions. That is a service gap, not a software gap. It is real only for firms that answer "no" to the step 5 question. It closes if Lightyear, Dext or 360° AP adds an Exact connector, so check their lists every quarter, and sign clients on 12 month terms.

**Two open risks, both tested before you build (Step 8):**
1. **Volume.** I found strong UK evidence that clerks reconcile supplier statements. I found no Dutch evidence of the same habit. First question on every Dutch call: "How many supplier statements do you receive a month, and how many do you check?"
2. **Tool price.** If the AP Accurate module (or any other) is priced under about €300 a month at 50 to 100 statements, most firms will choose it. Read the current pricing page on 1 October and write the number down.

**How you would build it (3 to 4 weeks part time).**

1. **Intake with no mailbox permission.** The client adds one Outlook or Gmail rule that forwards supplier statements to `statements+<client>@yourdomain.com`. An n8n IMAP trigger on your own mailbox picks up the attachment. You never touch the client's Microsoft tenant.
2. **Extraction and the right balance check.** The LLM returns JSON: `supplier_name`, `supplier_vat_or_kvk`, `statement_date`, `statement_type` (`open_item` or `balance_forward`), `opening_balance` (null for open item), `rows[] {doc_date, doc_number, doc_type, amount}`, `closing_balance`. Then plain code checks:
   * open item statement: sum of rows = closing balance (within 0.01)
   * balance forward statement: opening balance + sum of rows = closing balance
   If the check fails, a human sees it. The brought forward line is a field, never a row, so it does not break the sum.
3. **Find the supplier.** Match the statement to one ledger account by VAT or KvK number, then IBAN, then fuzzy name. Save the mapping so it only happens once per supplier.
4. **Pull the ledger for that one supplier, as of the statement date.** In Exact Online, read the transaction lines filtered to that supplier account and dated up to the statement date plus 10 days (for payments in transit). Check the field names in Exact's REST reference when you build it.
   * For an **open item** statement: rebuild which invoices and credit notes were still open on the statement date (document amount minus payments dated on or before that date). Compare the statement rows to that open list. Do not compare to "this month's entries." That is what caused false alarms in naive designs: an unpaid May invoice on a September statement is correct, not missing.
   * For a **balance forward** statement: compare the ledger balance on the day before the period to the statement's opening balance, then compare the period's entries line by line.
5. **Matching engine (Developer A, Spring Boot and PostgreSQL, plain code, no AI).** Normalise document numbers (strip spaces, prefixes, leading zeros). Match on number plus amount, then amount plus date within 5 days. Tag every leftover as one of: missing invoice, missing credit note, amount difference, **payment in transit** (we paid within 10 days before the statement date, supplier has not booked it), payment not on statement (older than 10 days), on our ledger but not on their statement, or possible duplicate.
6. **Exception screen (Developer B, Node and TypeScript).** The clerk sees only the differences. Buttons: Approve, Edit, Ignore. Every click is logged with user and time.
7. **Reply to the supplier.** On approve, the screen shows a ready email (in Dutch or English) with the missing documents listed, and an "Open in Outlook" button that fills a new message. The clerk sends it from their own mailbox. You never send email.
   * If a client insists on drafts appearing in a shared mailbox, register an app with Graph Mail.ReadWrite, but **scope it**: in Exchange Online, create a management scope for that one mailbox (`New-ManagementScope`), assign the Application Mail.ReadWrite role to your app with that scope (`New-ManagementRoleAssignment ... -CustomResourceScope`), test with `Test-ServicePrincipalAuthorization`, and **remove the unscoped Mail.ReadWrite consent in Entra ID**. Microsoft warns that Entra and Exchange grants add together, so leaving the Entra consent in place means no scoping at all. RBAC for Applications replaces the older Application Access Policies ([Microsoft Learn: RBAC for Applications](https://learn.microsoft.com/exchange/permissions-exo/application-rbac), [Microsoft Learn: Application Access Policies (legacy)](https://learn.microsoft.com/exchange/permissions-exo/application-access-policies)).
8. **Read only on the ledger.** You never post, change or pay anything.

**g. Realistic price.**
* Build: €1,800 to €3,000 (about 2,100 to 3,500 USD).
* Monthly: €250 to €450 (about 290 to 530 USD), 12 month term, including hosting, model costs, new supplier formats and a monthly "money found" summary.
* Pitch line: "We check every supplier statement against Exact every month. Nobody on your team exports or uploads anything. Your clerk only sees the differences and the reply emails in Dutch."

**h. Sources:** inline above, all listed at the end.

**The honest frame first.** Indian SMB software is priced very low: Vyapar TaxOne from ₹899 a month, Takkada ₹2,900 to ₹8,500 a year. ### I1. Party ledger reconciliation in Tally (PRACTICE GROUND for E1, not an income pick)

I tested this one against the same reject rule as everything else, and it does not pass as a paid product for traders. Sections e, f and g show why. I keep it for one reason: it runs the exact matching engine you need for E1, on messier data, for firms you can reach by car. That gives you accuracy numbers for the Dutch pitch.

**a. Who has it, and size.** Traders, distributors, manufacturers, and their CA firms. **Small and medium.** Businesses reconcile big parties quarterly and everyone at year end. CA firms need party balance confirmations for audits, which run from April to the tax audit deadline. This year CBDT moved the tax audit report date from 30 September to **21 October 2026** and the ITR date for audit cases to **21 November 2026** ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/cbdt-extends-ay-2026-27-tax-audit-date-21-october-2026.html), [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-cbdt-extends-itr-due-date-tax-audit-cases-ay-2026-27-47142.html/), [CAclubindia](https://www.caclubindia.com/news/cbdt-extends-tax-audit-due-date-for-ay-2026-27-to-21st-october-2026-itr-deadline-moved-to-21st-november-26821.asp)). So CA firms are in their final crunch now and will not try a new tool until December. Plan around that (Step 8).

**b. Steps today.**
1. Start: a supplier or customer sends their ledger of your account (PDF or Excel) by email or WhatsApp, or the auditor asks for it.
2. The accountant exports the same party's ledger from Tally to Excel.
3. They match line by line in Excel.
4. They explain differences. Common Indian causes: TDS deducted by one side and not booked by the other, GST adjustments, debit or credit notes not booked, payments in transit, typing errors ([OneFinOps](https://onefinops.com/blog/vendor-payment-reconciliation-guide), [Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/debtors-creditors-reconciliation-india/), [ClearTax on 26AS](https://cleartax.in/s/26as-reconciliation)).
5. End: correction entries in Tally (after owner or CA approval) and a confirmation or difference list sent to the party.

**c. Software.** WhatsApp or Gmail on one side, TallyPrime on the other. Tally talks XML over HTTP, by default on port 9000 of the PC running Tally. **Practical catch:** Tally usually runs on an office PC, so you need a small connector on that PC, or the client uses Tally on a cloud server. Sort this out in the first meeting.
Same two statement types as Europe: if the firm keeps bill-wise details in Tally, compare an open item ledger against the party's bill-wise outstanding as on the statement date. If not, treat it as balance forward: opening balance plus the period's vouchers.

**d. How often and how long.** Big parties quarterly, all parties at year end. **(Estimate, measure in discovery): 20 to 60 minutes per party.**

**e. Who does it, and what it costs.** The in-house accountant (₹15,000 to ₹45,000 a month in Ludhiana, so about ₹75 to ₹225 an hour over 200 working hours), or article assistants at the CA firm.

The math, for a typical trader with 200 parties, at 40 minutes a party **(estimate, measure in discovery)**:

| Work | Hours a year | Labour a year at ₹75 to ₹225 an hour |
|---|---|---|
| Top 30 parties, 3 quarters | 60 h | ₹4,500 to ₹13,500 |
| All 200 parties at year end | 133 h | ₹10,000 to ₹30,000 |
| **Total** | **about 193 h (16 h a month)** | **about ₹14,500 to ₹43,400** |

A monthly fee of ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 is ₹36,000 to ₹72,000 a year. That is **more** than the labour it replaces for almost every trader. I found no error cost or speed cost for a trader that closes that gap. So a monthly fee for traders fails your India test. The one place speed matters is the **CA firm in audit season**: 500 party ledgers at 30 minutes is about 250 hours, worth ₹18,750 to ₹56,250 at the same rates, all squeezed before the audit date, when the firm cannot simply hire another person for three weeks.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
* Tally Auditor edition has an external confirmation report, but it shows only the balance difference, not which lines cause it ([TallyHelp](https://help.tallysolutions.com/article/Tally.ERP9/Auditor_Edition/India/Statutory_Audit/External_Confirmation_third_Party.htm)).
* Takkada keeps customer ledgers close to matched by linking UPI payments to bills, but only on the receivable side for distributors ([Takkada](https://takkada.com/blog/ledger-reconciliation-tally-distributor/)).
* **Firmway** is the direct competitor. It collects ledgers from parties, tracks balance confirmations with reminders, gets sign offs, and auto reconciles ledgers "from any accounting software", for SMEs as well as enterprises. Price is not public ([Firmway](https://firmway.in/), [Firmway ledger reconciliation](https://firmway.in/ledger-reconciliation-software/), [Firmway balance confirmation](https://firmway.in/balance-confirmation-software/)).
* AI Accountant reads PDFs, Excel files and scanned documents "even when vendor statements are poorly formatted", syncs with Tally and Zoho Books, and its virtual accounting service costs ₹4,000 to ₹6,000 a month ([AI Accountant statement reconciliation](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/automated-statement-reconciliation-india), [AI Accountant pricing](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/virtual-accounting-pricing-india)). Most of its reconciliation pages are about bank statements, but the overlap is real.
* Terra Insight also writes about debtor and creditor reconciliation for India ([Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/debtors-creditors-reconciliation-india/)).
* **Gap: I cannot show one.** Between Firmway and AI Accountant, reading a party's ledger and matching it to Tally is sold in India today. By the brief's rule, I1 fails as a product. It survives only as your free test bed.

**g. Realistic price.**
* First 2 clients (one trader, one CA firm): free, **in writing, in exchange for the right to publish real numbers and name the client.** This is the whole point of I1.
* Trader after that: no monthly fee. A year end run at **₹8,000 to ₹15,000 a year**, which sits under the ₹14,500 to ₹43,400 of labour above. Take it only if a pilot client asks for it.
* CA firm: ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 per audit season for up to about 500 party ledgers, **only if** the firm has checked Firmway and still prefers you (for example because you do the run for them). Ask them for Firmway's quote first.

**h. Sources:** inline above.

### I2. Export shipment document cross-check (TOP INDIA PICK for income, India only)

**a. Who has it.** Ludhiana and Jalandhar exporters of hand tools, hosiery and bicycle parts. **Small is the target; medium only if it has not bought an EXIM automation platform** (see f). **c. Software.** Email or WhatsApp, the export document tool or Excel, and Tally for the export invoice.

**d. Frequency and time.** 5 to 40 shipments a month. **(Estimate, measure in discovery): 30 to 60 minutes per shipment.**

**e. Who does it.** An export documentation executive, ₹12,000 to ₹35,000 a month in Ludhiana. **f. Existing tools and gap.** The tools in Step 1 **generate** export documents; Eximly adds validation rules for HS code, GST and IEC errors on the documents you make yourself ([Eximly](https://www.eximly.in/blog/export-documentation-checklist-india)). One product does check the documents others send back: **COVORO's EXIMhero** validates export and import documents, including shipping bills, against checklist parameters, and says it works with mid and large exporters with multiple plants, CHA partners and banks ([COVORO checklist validation](https://covoro.ai/exim/exim-checklist-validation-automation/), [COVORO EXIM](https://covoro.ai/exim/)). Price is not public. So the gap is **small exporters** (one plant, 5 to 40 shipments a month) who will not buy a platform built for multi plant firms. A mistake caught after the bill of lading is issued means amendment fees and delay; an HS code or weight mismatch between invoice and packing list can trigger a physical examination ([Skydo on packing lists](https://www.skydo.com/blog/packing-list-export)).

**g. Price.** ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 setup, ₹3,000 to ₹5,000 a month.

**h. Sources:** inline above.

**Does it beat hiring?** At 20 shipments and 45 minutes each, that is 15 hours a month, worth about ₹900 to ₹2,600 of a ₹12,000 to ₹35,000 salary. So on labour alone it does **not** beat a person. It makes sense only where a wrong bill of lading has actually cost the exporter money. Ask every exporter: "What did your last BL amendment or customs hold cost you, and how many did you have this year?" Sell only where that number is bigger than ₹36,000 a year (12 months at ₹3,000).

**Why it is only a local pick:** it earns local money and referrals, but a European buyer does not care about Indian shipping bills.
