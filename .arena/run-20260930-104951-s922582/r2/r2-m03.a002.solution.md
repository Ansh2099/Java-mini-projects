# Workflow automations SMEs will pay for: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Build one thing: **customer orders that arrive as messages become draft sales orders in the client's ERP or accounting system, and a person clicks approve.**

* **Europe (your paying market):** emailed purchase orders (PDF, Excel, or typed in the email body) become draft sales orders in **Procountor or Netvisor, the two big Finnish small business systems.** Start in Finland. Developer A already has a paying Finnish client, both systems have free partner programmes and free test environments, and I found no order tool built for either of them.
* **UK: second, not first.** The UK system with the clearest gap, Sage 200, charges developers about £5,000 a year to build against its API. That breaks your $50 a month budget. Add Sage 200 only after you have revenue, or through a Sage reseller who already holds that licence.
* **Do not target** Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central (Microsoft ships its own Sales Order Agent), Fortnox in Sweden (Qrisp and Gung already sell AI order scanning there), Cin7 Core (built in AI order import), Unleashed (Logistify), Exact Online or Odoo (crowded).
* **India (your case study):** the same engine, with WhatsApp orders (text, photos of handwritten lists) becoming sales orders in TallyPrime for one Ludhiana distributor. Be clear with yourselves: this is **not an Indian product**. Zotok, Karvan and HublerX already sell it in India, and the saving at Indian wages only pays for itself at high volume. You build it once, at cost, to get real before and after numbers.

Four hard truths up front:

1. **The order entry market is being squeezed from both sides.** ERP makers build it in (Microsoft), and ready tools cover the popular systems, often sold inside the ERP's own app store (Qrisp on Fortnox). Your opening is the systems neither has reached yet, plus setup, item matching and custom rules. That window may last one to two years. Build the engine so the ERP connector is a small, swappable part, and re-check competitors before every pitch.
2. **Some ERPs charge you just to build.** Always check the developer cost before choosing a target. Sage 200 failed this check. Procountor and Netvisor pass it.
3. **Your price guess is roughly right.** A build fee of $1,500 to $4,000 fits small firms; medium firms can pay up to about $6,000. Monthly upkeep of $400 to $1,500 holds, but small firms sit at the bottom ($400 to $600), and only when their measured saving is at least twice the fee.
4. **Your real problem is not the build. It is getting the first European client from Ludhiana with no case study.** The 16 week plan at the end deals with that and fits about 20 combined hours a week.

## Assumptions

1. **Exchange rates** (rough working rates, check before you quote): £1 ≈ $1.33, €1 ≈ $1.16, $1 ≈ ₹88. All prices exclude VAT or GST.
2. **"A few weeks of part time work"** means about 100 to 120 hours for one workflow. At 20 combined hours a week, that is 5 to 6 calendar weeks per build. The plan assumes **one build at a time**.
3. **"Europe" includes the UK.** Start in Finland (warm contact, free developer access, Finnish orders that Microsoft's agent does not officially support), then add the UK once revenue can carry a Sage developer licence.
4. **How I researched.** I used web search and Microsoft Learn. Direct page fetching was blocked for most sites in my setup, so for most links the facts I quote are what the search results showed from that page. The Microsoft Learn pages I read in full. Vendor numbers are vendor claims, not independent studies. Where I have no source, I write **"estimate"** and say how to check it.
5. **Order volume has no public source for any one firm.** Nobody publishes "orders a day" for Finnish or UK wholesalers. So every money figure below is shown at several volumes, and you measure the real number in week one. The worked example gives the minimum volume at which a sale makes sense.
6. **Time per order** comes from order entry benchmarks (vendor sourced, see the worked example). Measure it at each client.

## Part 1. One real example, worked end to end

I solved one concrete case first, then kept only the ideas that behaved like it.

### Worked example: a Finnish HVAC and electrical wholesaler on Procountor

**The business.** 25 staff (small, by your definition), one warehouse near Tampere, sells to installers and small contractors. Runs Procountor for accounting, sales orders and invoicing. One order processor ("tilauskäsittelijä"), with sales staff helping at busy times. The owner decides.

**What arrives.** Customer orders by email. Some are PDF purchase orders from the customer's own system. Some are Excel sheets. Many are typed in the email body, in Finnish ("10 kpl 22mm kulmayhde..."). Customers use their own part codes or plain words, not the wholesaler's codes.

**What the person does today.**
1. Opens the email and the attachment.
2. Finds the customer in Procountor.
3. For each line, works out which product it means, types code and quantity, checks price and stock.
4. Saves the sales order and emails the customer a confirmation.

**Time per order.**
* Published benchmarks for manual order entry: "5 to 8 minutes to process end to end: opening the email, identifying the customer, looking up SKUs, checking inventory, applying pricing, entering the order into the ERP, and sending a confirmation" ([Mirage Metrics](https://miragemetrics.com/blog/true-cost-manual-order-entry/)). Suppliers interviewed by Hobson & Company put it at 8 to 12 minutes an order ([TradeCentric](https://tradecentric.com/blog/manual-order-processing-cost/)). Both come from vendors that sell automation, so I take the **low end**.
* **Base case: 5 minutes an order by hand. Low case: 3.5 minutes.** Reviewing a prepared draft takes about 1.5 minutes (estimate). So we save **3.5 minutes** an order (base) or **2 minutes** (low).

**Cost of the person (sourced).**
* Average Finnish order processor pay is **€3,104 a month** (Duunitori salary comparison, 2024 data, 99 reports) ([Duunitori](https://duunitori.fi/palkat/tilauskasittelija)). A sales secretary averages about €2,500 ([Palkkavertailu](https://palkkavertailu.com/palkka/myyntisihteeri)).
* €3,104 × 12 = €37,250. Add about 20% for employer pension and social costs (estimate; check with the client's accountant) = about €44,700. Over about 1,650 working hours, that is **about €27 an hour**.

**The count, at three volumes** (about 20.8 working days a month):

| Orders a day | Hand typing time a day (base) | Saving a month, base (3.5 min saved) | Saving a month, low (2 min saved) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 1.7 hours | €656 | €374 |
| 40 | 3.3 hours | €1,312 | €749 |
| 80 | 6.7 hours | €2,624 | €1,498 |

(Each order a day is worth about €33 a month in the base case and €19 in the low case.)

**What we would charge.** €2,000 build (about $2,320) plus €350 a month (about $406).
* At 40 orders a day: the client keeps €962 a month after our fee (base), so the build **pays back in about 2 months**; in the low case it keeps €399, so **about 5 months**.
* At 20 orders a day in the low case: the client keeps €24 a month. **Do not sell that client.**
* **Qualifying rule:** the measured saving must be at least twice the monthly fee. At €350 a month, that means about 22 orders a day if each takes 5 minutes by hand, or about 38 if each takes 3.5 minutes. To be safe, look for **40 or more orders a day**, or a firm where wrong items and late dispatch are the real pain.

**The UK version of the same case, and why it lost.** A 30 person UK plumbing wholesaler on Sage 200 with 50 orders a day. UK order processor pay averages £26,832 a year ([Indeed UK](https://uk.indeed.com/career/order-processor/salaries)), about £19 an hour with employer costs (estimate). The saving is about £660 (low) to £1,160 (base) a month. A £300 a month fee works for the client. But to build on Sage 200 you must join the Sage 200 Developer Programme ([Sage KB: Sage 200 API common queries](https://gb-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=200427112454709)), priced at **£416 a month or £4,995 a year** in July 2025 ([Sage developer pricing sheet](https://downloads.sage.co.uk/download/?did=cd53b8a9-506f-4d67-8ef7-4a016d7a8197)), with a price increase from 1 August 2025 ([Sage notice](https://ie-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/view2.jsp?k2dockey=250725123504990)). With one client, you pay Sage more each month than the client pays you. You need at least two Sage 200 clients just to cover the licence. So Sage 200 waits.

**What this example taught me (the rules I then used on every other idea):**
1. **The saving is time, not a head.** Nobody gets fired over 3 hours a day. The owner buys for other reasons: same day dispatch, fewer wrong items sent, no backlog when the order person is off sick. Sell those, with the hours as proof.
2. **The hard part is item matching, not reading the PDF.** Any AI model can read the PDF. The value is mapping "22mm kulmayhde" to product 10457 for this customer and learning from each correction. Tools that only extract text are not a threat. Tools that do the matching are.
3. **The ERP decides if the job is buildable, and if it is sellable.** It needs an API that creates sales orders, and it must not already have a built in agent or a ready tool in its own app store.
4. **Count the ERP's gate cost.** A developer fee paid by you (Sage 200) can kill a target before the first sale. A free partner programme (Procountor, Netvisor) does not.
5. **The monthly price is capped twice:** by the client's measured saving (fee at most half of it), and by any ready tool that supports that ERP.
6. **Volume decides the sale.** Below about 40 orders a day, the numbers are thin. Measure first, quote second.
7. **Structured e-invoicing kills supplier invoice ideas but not order ideas.** Mandates cover invoices, not customer purchase orders, so orders stay messy.

Every workflow below is checked against these seven lessons and your seven rules.

## Part 2. India

**Honest framing.** India is your case study market. At Indian wages the labour saving is small, and funded Indian tools already exist for the main workflow. Treat paid Indian work as a bonus.

### Workflow I1 (case study build): WhatsApp orders to TallyPrime sales orders

**a. Who has it.** Wholesalers and distributors that sell to retailers: hosiery and garments, auto parts, bicycle parts, hardware, FMCG sub distributors, pharma stockists. All are common in Ludhiana. Fits **both** sizes on paper: small (owner decides) and medium (sales or accounts head owns it, often with 2 to 4 billing clerks). **In practice, only high volume small firms and medium firms can justify paying** (see the math in e and g).

**b. Steps today.**
1. Retailers or the firm's own salesmen send orders on WhatsApp: typed lists, photos of a handwritten list, sometimes voice notes, in Hindi, Punjabi or English.
2. A billing clerk reads each chat, finds the party ledger in Tally, and types each item and quantity into a sales order or sales invoice. Stock is checked.
3. The clerk replies on WhatsApp with a confirmation or "item not in stock".
4. Data ends up in TallyPrime (sales order, then invoice and e-way bill).

**c. Software.** The clerk works in **two business tools: WhatsApp Business and TallyPrime** (sometimes Busy or Marg). Our parts are the review screen and a small connector on the Tally PC. We count our own components as part of the build, not as client tools, the same way Microsoft Graph is simply the route into Outlook in E1. WhatsApp is reached through Meta's Cloud API (details in the technical notes).

**d. Frequency and time.** Estimate for a 15 to 25 person distributor: 50 to 150 orders a day, 4 to 6 lines each, about 4 minutes each by hand. Peaks are much higher in season (Diwali, and October to January for hosiery). **Check in one day** by sitting with the clerk and counting.

**e. Who does it and what it costs (sourced), and does it pay?**
* The job exists and is advertised. Naukri lists 96 billing job vacancies in Ludhiana ([Naukri](https://www.naukri.com/billing-jobs-in-ludhiana)), and Tally operator and billing executive listings ask for "Sale & Purchase Entry" and invoice making ([Naukri Tally jobs](https://www.naukri.com/tally-jobs-in-ludhiana), [SimplyHired](https://www.simplyhired.co.in/search?q=tally&l=ludhiana,+punjab)).
* Pay: Ludhiana billing executive listings show ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 a month, mostly ₹13,000 to ₹18,000 ([Apna](https://apna.co/jobs/title_billing_executive-jobs-in-ludhiana), [WorkIndia listing](https://www.workindia.in/jobs/billing_executive-sector39a-ludhiana-6349089/), [WorkIndia listing 2](https://www.workindia.in/jobs/billing_executive-miller_ganj-ludhiana-6484303/)).
* At ₹16,000 a month and about 208 hours (26 days × 8), a clerk costs about ₹77 an hour. If the clerk only reviews drafts (about 1 minute instead of 4), each order saves 3 minutes, about ₹3.85. **Each order a day is worth about ₹100 a month.**

| Orders a day | Time saved a day | Saving a month | Worth paying ₹4,000 a month? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 50 | 2.5 hours | ₹5,000 | **No.** Fee eats most of it. |
| 70 | 3.5 hours | ₹7,000 | **Weak.** Setup takes 5+ months to pay back. |
| 100 | 5 hours | ₹10,000 | **Yes.** ₹6,000 a month kept. |
| 150 | 7.5 hours | ₹15,000 | **Yes.** |
| Any volume where a second clerk is about to be hired | | ₹13,000 to ₹18,000 (the salary avoided) | **Yes.** |

* Firms also already pay for tools that do this job (see f). That is the strongest proof of spend.

**The jugaad test, done with numbers.** A second clerk costs ₹13,000 to ₹18,000 a month. Automation beats that only when (1) volume is 100 or more orders a day, or would otherwise need a second clerk, especially in season, (2) wrong items cost returns and angry retailers, or (3) the owner wants same day billing and dispatch. **A firm with one clerk handling 50 to 70 orders a day will not save enough to pay you. Do not sell to them.** They can still be your free case study.

**The honest verdict.** There is **no clear gap** for a plain "WhatsApp order to Tally" product in India. HublerX targets manufacturers with ₹200 to 2,000 crore turnover, above most Ludhiana distributors, but Zotok and Karvan aim at exactly the distributors you would visit, and Zotok's entry price is low. By your own rule ("if a cheap tool solves it, reject it"), **I1 fails as an Indian product.** It still passes as a **case study build**, for three reasons:
1. The European buyer never sees Zotok. What they see is your engine reading a messy order, matching it to the item master, and learning from corrections. That is exactly what you sell in Europe.
2. Some distributors will not have tried these tools, or will run Busy or Marg. Busy has no default third party integration and needs a partner or custom route ([Busy FAQ](https://busy.in/faqs/third-party-services/integration/1/)), so check each one separately.
3. A photo of a handwritten Punjabi order list working is a far stronger demo than a clean PDF.

**Your answer to "why not just buy Zotok?"** is: "Try it. If it works for you, use it. We will build yours free in return for your numbers and a video." If the distributor already uses Zotok or Karvan and is happy, move on.

**Technical notes.**
* Tally integration is proven. TallyPrime runs an HTTP XML server (usually port 9000), and open source code imports sales orders through it ([sales order import example](https://github.com/SatyamTechsolvo/tallyerp9import), [Node.js to TallyPrime](https://github.com/JeevanKN/Tally_Integration_with-EC2-Virtual-Window-RDP-), [TallyPrime SDK](https://github.com/labs-infinitum/tallyprime-sdk)).
* Tally usually sits on a Windows PC in the shop. You need a small local connector on that PC that pulls approved orders from your server and posts XML to localhost:9000. Developer A can write this in Java in a few days.
* **WhatsApp without a monthly provider fee.** Meta's Cloud API can be used directly from your own Meta app, with no provider in between ([Meta Cloud API get started](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/get-started), [Zoice guide](https://zoice.ai/blog/whatsapp-business-api-without-bsp/)). "Coexistence" lets one number run both the WhatsApp Business app and the Cloud API, so the client keeps its existing number; the app must be opened at least once every 13 days ([360dialog](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/onboarding/whatsapp-coexistence), [Whautomate](https://whautomate.com/whatsapp-coexistence)). Linking an existing app number goes through Meta's Embedded Signup, which Meta offers to its registered partners. If you cannot set that up quickly, use a provider such as AiSensy (from ₹1,500 a month, [AiSensy pricing](https://aisensy.com/pricing)) for the first client only.
* **Message costs are near zero here.** The retailer messages first, and your confirmation is a reply inside the 24 hour service window, which is free. Only messages you start yourself (templates) are charged.

**g. Realistic price (INR), checked against the saving.**
* Small (5 to 49 staff): **₹15,000 to ₹25,000 setup, ₹2,500 to ₹4,000 a month**, and **only** at 100 or more orders a day or when a second clerk is about to be hired. At 100 orders a day the client saves about ₹10,000 a month and keeps ₹6,000 to ₹7,500 after our fee; setup pays back in 2 to 4 months.
* Medium (50 to 250 staff, 2 or more clerks, 200+ orders a day): ₹50,000 to ₹1,00,000 setup, ₹8,000 to ₹12,000 a month. Saving is ₹20,000 a month or more. Only where you add something the tools do not (Busy or Marg, several companies in one Tally, custom scheme and discount rules).
* Your first client: free or at cost, in return for real numbers, a named case study and a video testimonial.

### Workflow I2 (add on only, validate first): payment screenshots to Tally receipts

**a. Who has it.** The same distributors. Retailers pay by UPI, NEFT or cheque and send a screenshot or message ("sent 25,000 for bills 1142 and 1150"). Both small and medium.

**b. Steps today.** The accountant matches the bank credit to the party, creates a receipt voucher in Tally, and allocates it against the right bills. Short payments are chased on WhatsApp.

**c. Software.** WhatsApp plus the bank statement in, TallyPrime out. That is 3 tools.

**d. Frequency and time.** Estimate (no public source): 30 to 80 receipts a day for a mid sized distributor, 2 to 4 minutes each. Count them in one day at your case study client before deciding.

**e. Who and cost.** An accounts clerk in the same pay band as I1 (₹10,000 to ₹20,000 a month in Ludhiana listings, sources in I1 e). At 50 receipts × 2 minutes saved, about ₹3,300 a month: only worth it bundled with I1.

**f. Existing tools and gap.** Crowded too. Vyapar TaxOne (formerly Suvit) says its AI "will extract data from images, create accurate vouchers and push them directly to Tally" ([Vyapar TaxOne](https://taxone.vyapar.com/gst-reconciliation-feature)). Karvan parses bank SMS and matches payments to orders ([Karvan](https://www.karvan.ai/)). The only possible gap is bill wise allocation from the retailer's own WhatsApp note. **Test TaxOne and Karvan before building.** If either does bill wise receipts, drop this.

**g. Price.** Only as an add on to I1: ₹10,000 to ₹20,000 setup, ₹1,500 to ₹2,500 a month (estimate, sized to the ₹3,300 saving).

**h. Sources.** Linked above.

### Workflow E2 (validate before building): remittance advice to cash allocation

**a. Who has it.** Suppliers to big retailers and national accounts: food and drink producers, consumer goods wholesalers, building product suppliers. Mostly **medium**; small only if they sell to big chains.

**b. Steps today.** A big customer pays one lump sum and emails a long remittance (PDF or spreadsheet) listing many invoices, with short payments and deductions. A credit controller matches each line to open invoices, posts the allocation, and raises queries on deductions.

Frequency and time.** Frequency is sourced by a vendor example: a food wholesaler supplying a national grocer "may receive a payment once a fortnight with a 12 page remittance covering 180 odd invoices" ([RemitClear](https://remitclear.com/solutions/remittance-automation-software)). ## Part 5. Final top picks

1. **Build E1 for Procountor and Netvisor in Finland as your European product.**
   Why: it passes all seven rules; firms pay for this job today (a named job title with an average of €3,104 a month, and paid tools on neighbouring ERPs); both systems have free partner programmes and free test environments, so it fits the $50 budget; I found no tool built for them; orders are not affected by e-invoicing; Finnish orders are outside Microsoft's supported languages; hundreds of Finnish accounting offices work inside these systems and can introduce you; Developer A has a Finnish client for a warm first contact; and it fits your skills (Spring Boot, Postgres and Spring AI for Developer A, the review screen in TypeScript for Developer B).
   Risks, stated plainly: Finland is a small market; Zimply may already cover Netvisor or Procountor through its "Visma" connector; Finnish buyers prefer Finnish speaking support. Ask Zimply and OrderPilot about coverage before your first pitch, keep the ERP connector thin, and sell setup, matching, custom rules and support rather than "AI reads PDFs".

2. **Build I1 (WhatsApp orders to TallyPrime) once, for one Ludhiana distributor, as your case study.**
   Why: the same engine, cheap to run, local and in person, and it gives real before and after numbers. Pick a distributor with 100+ orders a day so the numbers impress. Do not expect Indian revenue from it: below 100 orders a day the saving does not cover even a low fee, and Zotok and Karvan already sell it.

3. **Add Sage 200 (UK) as your second European connector after you have revenue,** or earlier through a Sage 200 reseller who already holds the developer licence. The gap there is real (only a service firm sells it), and UK buyers are English speaking and larger.

4. **Keep E2 (remittance to cash allocation) as an upsell** only if an E1 client asks.

5. **Do not build:** order entry on Business Central, Fortnox, Cin7, Unleashed, Exact Online or Odoo; supplier invoice capture; statement reconciliation; PO matching in Xero; freight quoting; customs entries; timesheets; GST 2B tools; WhatsApp to Tally as an Indian product.
