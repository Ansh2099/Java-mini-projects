# Workflow automations to sell: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

Sell one engine: **a messy order message comes in, and a checked draft goes into the business system for a human to approve.** It has a clear start and end, touches 2 tools plus your approval screen, has one owner, and is easy to count.

Now the brutal part, which you need before anything else. **Email order entry is not an empty market in Europe.** Funded products already sell it: Hyperfox (about €0.07 per approved order line, native Exact Online, Odoo and Business Central connectors), Turian, Conexiom, Y Meadows, Ordermind, Logistify, cheap Odoo App Store modules, and Microsoft's own Sales Order Agent inside Business Central. A UK firm, Zestcode, already sells it as a service for Sage 200. The same is true of supplier confirmation checking (Turian, Doxis, Tradecloud). So you cannot pitch "nobody does this". You can only win in the narrow gaps below, and you must walk away from everyone else.

1. **Lead offer in Europe, medium firms: "Customer order email to draft sales order".** Target these gaps and nothing else:
   * **A. High line volume, 20,000+ order lines a month** (about 80 orders a day at 12 lines). At that volume Hyperfox costs about $19,000 a year. Your price of $3,000 setup plus a flat $1,000 a month is $15,000 in year 1 (21% cheaper) and $12,000 in year 2 (37% cheaper). Below about 16,000 lines a month you are not cheaper in year 1, so do not sell on price there.
   * **C. Business Central firms whose orders break Microsoft's agent:** more than 15 lines, item variants, or orders sent from many email addresses.
   * **B. UK firms on Sage 200, only when the client's Sage partner gives you a test company.** Sage 200 has a documented REST API that creates sales orders, and the only rival I found selling this for Sage 200 is one small UK service firm. But you cannot get a Sage 200 test system inside your budget on your own.
2. **Small firm offer in Europe (conditional): "Emailed order to Sage 50 sales order" for UK small wholesalers on Sage 50 Accounts desktop.** The cloud products I found do not name the UK desktop Sage 50, and the Sage 50 add-ons I found only import tidy files. It is the same engine and the same "desktop connector" pattern as Tally. Test it with 10 calls before you build.
3. **Proof of work in India: "WhatsApp order to Tally sales order"** for one or two Ludhiana distributors. Be clear about what this is. Zotok already sells this cheaply, so by your own "reject if a cheap tool solves it" rule, it is **not a product to sell in India**. It is a case study build, done free or at a token price, because it trains the exact engine you sell in Europe.
4. **Second workflow in Europe, sold to the same medium clients: "Supplier order confirmation check and chasing".** Email only, no supplier portal, priced below the mid market products.

**Walk away from:** small European firms on Exact Online, Odoo, Business Central, or Xero with Unleashed (cheap tools cover them); generic "AI invoice processing" (crowded with cheap capture tools in the UK and the Netherlands, Microsoft's Payables Agent in BC, and e-invoicing laws in Belgium, Poland, France and Germany); and any prospect that already uses one of the products above.

If your first 10 European discovery calls show that most prospects already use or have trialled Hyperfox, Turian or a similar product, stop and rethink. That is the test that tells you whether the gap is real.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the EU plus the UK, Switzerland and Norway. First targets: the UK, the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany and the Nordics (Finland first, because Developer A already has a Finnish client). English works in these markets. The Microsoft agents also cover English, German, Dutch, Finnish and more, so you win on fit and price, not language.
2. "A few weeks of part time work" means 3 to 6 weeks for two people at about 15 hours a week each, so about 90 to 180 hours per build.
3. Where a volume or time per task has no link, it is a labeled **estimate** to check on the first sales call.
4. **Research limits, stated plainly.** The Microsoft Learn and GitHub pages in the Sources list were opened and read. For every other site, page fetch was blocked, so those facts (prices, salaries, competitors, job ads, WhatsApp and Sage details) come from the text of web search results, and the Sources section marks this. "I found no rival" always means "none showed up in my searches", not "none exists". I did not invent any source. "What I could not verify" lists what to check next.
5. **Currency, 30 September 2026 (search result for market rates):** about ₹96 per US dollar, $1.32 per pound, and $1.134 per euro. Every conversion below uses these. Check the day's rate before you quote.

## How I judged each idea

A workflow made the list only if it passed all of these:

1. It meets your 7 rules: one process with a clear start and end, 2 or 3 tools, one owner, a human approves exceptions, a few weeks to build, countable, and already partly digital.
2. Businesses already spend money on it: salaries, job ads that list the task, paid tools, or paid services.
3. **No built in feature or cheap tool already covers it for that buyer.** I checked this per buyer segment, not per workflow. When a cheap tool covers a segment, that segment is rejected even if the workflow survives for others. The one exception is the India proof build, which fails this test and is kept only as a case study, never as a product. I say so where it appears.
4. It does not move money or make legal, medical or lending decisions.
5. I checked e-invoicing laws country by country. They turn supplier invoices into structured data, but only where a mandate is in force. They do **not** cover customer orders or supplier order confirmations, which is one reason my picks sit on the order side. Where no mandate applies yet (the UK and the Netherlands), I judged invoice capture on competition instead.

## Part 1: India

### India workflow 1: WhatsApp order to Tally sales order (proof build, not an Indian product)

**Verdict up front.** Zotok sells WhatsApp ordering with a Tally link, and a software listing says it "starts at ₹6,000". By your own reject rule, this workflow fails as something to sell to Indian distributors. Keep it for one reason: it is the fastest way to get a real, measured case study of the exact engine you sell in Europe, 20 minutes from your desk. Do it for 1 or 2 distributors, free or at a token price. Do not build a sales pipeline for it in India.

**a. Who has it, and size.** FMCG, pharma, hardware, auto parts, electrical and textile distributors and wholesalers who take retailer orders on WhatsApp and phone and bill in TallyPrime. Fits **both** sizes. Small (5 to 49 staff): the owner decides, and 1 or 2 billing staff key orders. Medium (50 to 250): the sales operations or billing head owns it. Vendor blogs describe order desks of 10 to 15 people at the larger end. For your proof build, pick a small or lower medium distributor: the owner can say yes in one meeting.

**b. The steps today.**
1. A retailer or salesman sends an order on WhatsApp. It may be a text list, a voice note, a photo of a handwritten list, or a forwarded PDF. Local item names, pack sizes ("5 peti", "2 dozen") and mixed Hindi, Punjabi and English are normal.
2. A billing person reads it, works out which items and pack sizes are meant, checks rate and stock, and keys a sales order or invoice into Tally.
3. They reply on WhatsApp to confirm, often late, and send the invoice PDF.
4. The data ends up in Tally. The WhatsApp chat is the only record of what was asked for.

Vendor blogs describe this exact pattern: orders from calls and WhatsApp keyed into Tally by hand, "error-prone and hours late", and at mid market firms "hundreds of messages per day" handled by teams of 10 to 15 people. These vendors sell a fix, so treat their numbers as a claim.

**c. Software on each side, and exactly how you connect.**

*Input: WhatsApp.* The free WhatsApp Business app has no API to read messages. Use one of these routes:

1. **Main route: WhatsApp Coexistence.** Since May 2025, Meta lets a number that is already on the WhatsApp Business app also be connected to the Cloud API, through Meta's Embedded Signup flow run by a provider. The owner keeps the same number, the same phone and the chat history. New messages on either side are copied to the API through webhooks, so your system sees every new order. **Retailers change nothing**, which keeps you inside your rule 7. India is a supported country.
   * **Limits you must check on the first visit.** Group chats do **not** sync, so orders sent in WhatsApp groups are invisible to you. View once media is turned off. Broadcast lists become read only. Someone must open the Business app at least every 14 days or the link drops. The number must still be on the app, not already on the API.
   * **Costs.** Incoming messages are free. Keep all replies in the phone app, as the clerk does today, and your Meta message cost is near zero. (A pricing blog says Meta starts charging for service replies sent through the API from 1 October 2026, and Meta has a page on "upcoming pricing updates" for service and utility messages. This only matters if you later send replies through the API.) The provider charges a platform fee: AiSensy starts at about ₹1,500 a month and Interakt's Growth plan at ₹2,799 a month (search results). Confirm that the provider you pick supports Coexistence onboarding, and pass the fee to the client at cost.
   * **Paperwork.** The client needs a Meta Business account; business verification can take days, so start it in week 2 of the plan below.
2. **Fallback for data and for shadow mode.** WhatsApp's built in "Export chat" (with media) gives you past orders on the day you visit, with no setup. For a live pilot without Coexistence, the clerk forwards each order to a separate number you run on the Cloud API. This changes one small step for the clerk only, not for retailers. Use it only until Coexistence is live.

*Voice notes.* Add a speech to text step. Sarvam AI offers speech to text for Indian languages including Punjabi and Hindi; search results give its price as about ₹1.5 a minute (one source says lower). A 30 second voice note costs under ₹1. Log accuracy per language in shadow mode.

*Photos of handwritten lists.* Send them to a vision capable LLM. Accuracy on Punjabi or Hindi handwriting is unknown to me. Measure it in shadow mode, and if it is poor, route photos straight to the human.

*Output: TallyPrime.* Tally accepts data through XML over HTTP (TallyPrime must be running with its HTTP server on, for example port 9000, with a company loaded). Since TallyPrime 7.0 it also accepts native JSON. Tally is a desktop program, so you build a small Windows service on the client's PC that pulls approved orders from your cloud and posts them to Tally locally.

That is 2 tools (WhatsApp and Tally) plus your approval screen. **Scope is Tally only.** I found no integration route for Busy or Marg, so do not promise them.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** Small distributor: 30 to 150 orders a day. Medium: 200 to 1,000 or more. Keying one order of 5 to 20 lines takes about 2 to 6 minutes, plus back and forth when an item is unclear. Example: 100 orders a day at 4 minutes each is about 6.7 hours a day, close to one full time person. **Measure it in week 1:** count two weeks of exported order chats and watch the clerk key 20 orders with a timer.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** Billing executives. Live Ludhiana job ads show ₹10,000 to ₹19,000 a month (Toyz Island), ₹13,000 to ₹15,000 (Commercial Traders, Miller Ganj), and ₹14,000 to ₹18,000 on Apna. So one clerk costs about ₹15,000 a month (about $156). **Labor savings alone will not sell this in India.** Speed (confirm and dispatch within the hour), fewer wrong items and returns, and no bottleneck in peak season are the only real selling points.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. TallyPrime (Release 4.0 and later) can **send** invoices, orders and reminders on WhatsApp. It does **not** read free text orders and turn them into vouchers.
2. **SalesOn** costs ₹208 per user a month on a yearly plan, with no setup fee, and posts orders to Tally. But it is a salesman order booking app: someone must enter the order in the app. It does not read the WhatsApp messages retailers already send.
3. **Zotok** says customers can order by text, image or voice note on WhatsApp and that it connects to Tally. **What its price means, as far as I can tell:** a software listing says "starts at INR 6,000" and does not say per month or per year. Zotok's own fair usage policy says it sells monthly and annual plans priced by "active customers" (customers with at least one conversation a month), with extra use charged at ₹10 per extra active customer or ₹2.5 per extra conversation. Its partner page pays partners a share of "setup fees", so Zotok also charges setup. HublerX makes a similar claim for larger manufacturers, and Interakt lists a Tally integration.
4. **Year 1 cost comparison, both ways.** Your price below is ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 setup plus ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 a month, so ₹51,000 to ₹102,000 in year 1, plus about ₹18,000 to ₹34,000 of WhatsApp provider fees. If Zotok's ₹6,000 is per month, Zotok costs about ₹72,000 in year 1 plus setup, which is in your range. If it is per year, you are many times dearer. Either way you are not clearly cheaper, and Zotok is a finished product. **That is why this fails the reject rule as an Indian product.**
5. **What is left, honestly.** Only a custom fit to one distributor's messy item names and approval rules, delivered in person, for a firm that has not bought Zotok. That is enough for a case study. It is not a business. If a prospect already uses Zotok, HublerX or SalesOn, skip them.

**g. Realistic price.**
* Proof clients (1 or 2): free or a ₹10,000 token setup, and the client pays the WhatsApp provider fee directly. In return: a written case study, permission to publish the numbers, and a recorded testimonial.
* If an Indian firm insists on paying: setup ₹15,000 to ₹30,000 and ₹3,000 to ₹6,000 a month, plus provider fees at cost. Do not chase these deals.

**h. Sources.** Tally XML, JSON and WhatsApp pages; Meta and 360dialog Coexistence pages; Coexistence limits pages; Meta and Indian WhatsApp pricing pages; AiSensy and Interakt pricing; Sarvam AI pricing and Punjabi speech to text; Interakt, Fast2SMS, HublerX and Sunray Datalinks blogs; SalesOn pages; Zotok distributor, fair usage and partner pages and the Techjockey listing; Apna and WorkIndia job ads. Links are in the Sources section.

### India workflow 2: Supplier PO confirmation and dispatch follow up (manufacturers)

**Verdict: do not build this unless a plant asks for it.** It is weak in India.

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers that buy many inputs from many suppliers: bicycle parts, auto components, hosiery, hand tools, machinery (all big in Ludhiana). Mostly **medium**.

**b. The steps today.** A purchase order is made in Tally (or Excel) and sent to the supplier as a PDF on WhatsApp or email. The supplier replies on WhatsApp with a confirmation, a changed rate, a part quantity or a new date. A purchase executive reads it, updates an Excel tracker (sometimes the Tally due date), and phones suppliers who are late. Data ends up in Excel and Tally.

**c. Software.** WhatsApp or Gmail in, Tally or Google Sheets out. 2 tools. The WhatsApp route is the same as India workflow 1.

**d. Frequency and time (estimate).** 20 to 100 open POs a week at a medium plant, 3 to 5 minutes per confirmation, plus daily chasing.

**e. Who does it and cost (estimate).** A purchase executive, not full time on this. The real cost is a stopped line when a late supplier was not chased.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. Tally already has a **Pending Purchase Orders** report that lists open POs by party and date. The "which POs are late" half is built in.
2. A Tally WhatsApp plugin (Fast2SMS) already **sends** POs to suppliers from Tally; the plugin is free and you pay per message.
3. HublerX claims to handle unstructured WhatsApp messages with ERP and Tally links for larger manufacturers.
4. What is left is reading the supplier's reply and updating the PO. That is a thin gap, at a firm where "my purchase boy does this" costs ₹18,000 to ₹35,000 a month (estimate, not sourced).
5. **No demand source.** I found no Indian job ad, study or paid service that shows firms spend money on this exact step.

**g. Price (estimate).** Only if a plant has lost production days to late material and asks: setup ₹20,000 to ₹40,000, monthly ₹3,000 to ₹6,000.

**h. Sources.** Tally Pending Purchase Orders help page, Fast2SMS PO from Tally page, HublerX manufacturers blog. No India demand source. Validate before you build.

## Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

### What already exists (read this before pitching anything)

**Microsoft, inside Dynamics 365 Business Central (BC).** BC is one of the most common ERPs for European firms with 20 to 250 staff.

1. **Sales Order Agent** watches a shared Exchange mailbox, reads the email body and PDF or image attachments, finds the customer by email address, checks stock, and creates a sales quote and then an order. A human reviews each step. Microsoft's cost example is 1,650 Copilot Credits for 100 order emails a month, about **$16.50 a month** at the $0.01 per credit rate in its examples. It works in English, German, Dutch, French, Finnish, Danish, Swedish, Norwegian, Italian and Spanish.
2. **Its documented limits:** at most **15 item lines** per document (more "might result in lower-quality output"), item lines only, **no variant codes**, no new customers or items, customers matched **only by the sender's email address**, only an Exchange shared inbox, no price or discount changes.
3. **Payables Agent** turns emailed PDF invoices into draft purchase invoices, focused on overhead costs. Microsoft's example is 6,500 credits for 100 three line invoices, **about $65 a month.** Better PO matching was "moved to the next release wave" in August 2026.
4. **Procurement Agent (supplier communications)** reads supplier confirmations and chases late POs, but only in **Dynamics 365 Supply Chain Management**, Microsoft's large company ERP, as a "production-ready preview". I found no BC equivalent in BC's update 29.0 feature list.

### Europe workflow 1: Customer order email to draft sales order (medium firms)

**a. Who has it, and size.** B2B wholesalers and distributors (industrial supplies, packaging, food service, building materials, spare parts, electrical) and make to stock manufacturers that get orders by email as PDF purchase orders, Excel sheets or plain text.

* **Medium (50 to 250 staff): the target.** The customer service or sales operations manager owns it, with 2 to 6 order entry staff.
* **Small (10 to 49) on cloud ERPs: rejected.** Small firms on Exact Online, Odoo, BC, or Xero plus Unleashed are covered cheaply: Hyperfox at low volume costs about €176 a month (worked below), the Odoo modules are a one time purchase, Logistify covers Unleashed, and BC has Sales Order Agent. The one small firm segment I found uncovered is UK Sage 50 desktop users, which is Europe workflow 3.

**Target only these medium buyers:**

| Segment | Why the gap is real | How to check on the first call |
|---|---|---|
| A. 20,000+ lines a month, any ERP with an API and a free test route (BC, Exact) | Hyperfox at €0.07 a line costs about $19,000 a year at 20,000 lines. You cost $15,000 in year 1 and $12,000 a year after. Full table below. | Orders a day times average lines times working days. Ask what per line price they were quoted. |
| C. BC firms whose orders break Sales Order Agent | More than 15 lines, item variants, many sender addresses, customer part numbers. | Ask for 20 real orders and count how many break a documented limit. If under a third, walk away. |
| B. UK firms on Sage 200 (conditional) | Sage 200 has a documented REST API with a POST for sales orders (Sage developer portal). The only Sage 200 rival I found is Zestcode, a small UK service firm. | Which Sage 200 edition and version? Is the API enabled? **Will their Sage partner set up a test company for you?** If not, walk away. |

**Segment A: the full cost check (EUR/USD 1.134).** Hyperfox is €0.07 × 1.134 = about $0.079 per line. I assume Hyperfox has no setup or platform fee, which is the worst case for you. Your Segment A price is $3,000 setup and a flat $1,000 a month up to 30,000 lines, then $40 per extra 1,000 lines.

| Lines a month | Hyperfox per year | You, year 1 ($3,000 + 12 × $1,000) | You, year 2 | Your saving, year 1 | Your saving, 2 years combined |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15,000 | about $14,300 | $15,000 | $12,000 | **none: you are 5% dearer** | 6% |
| 20,000 | about $19,100 | $15,000 | $12,000 | 21% | 29% |
| 30,000 | about $28,600 | $15,000 | $12,000 | 48% | 53% |

* **Break even in year 1 is about 15,700 lines a month.** So sell Segment A only at 20,000 lines or more, where the saving is big enough to matter.
* **The quote you must beat.** At 20,000 lines, you win year 1 only if their Hyperfox quote is above about €0.055 a line ($15,000 ÷ 240,000 lines = $0.0625). Large buyers may get a discount. If they were quoted less, you lose on price and must win on fit and service, or walk away.
* **Your running cost at this volume.** 20,000 lines is about 1,700 orders. At my estimate of 1 to 2 US cents of LLM cost per order, that is about $17 to $35 a month, which is small next to $1,000. Human review time sits with the client and is the same for both options.
* **Brutal truth about Segment A.** It is a price argument against a funded product with more features. It only works if prospects at this volume have not already signed.

**Kill rule.** If a prospect already uses or has signed with Hyperfox, Turian, Conexiom, Y Meadows, Ordermind or Logistify, do not compete. If your first 10 calls show most prospects already have one, the gap is smaller than this report assumes, and you should stop and rethink.

**b. The steps today.**
1. An order arrives in a shared mailbox (orders@...). It may be a PDF from the customer's ERP, an Excel sheet, or a typed email.
2. A clerk finds the customer in the ERP, translates the customer's part numbers or descriptions to their own item codes (often from an Excel cheat sheet), checks price, stock and delivery date, and types each line into a new sales order.
3. They email an order confirmation back.
4. The data ends up in the ERP sales order. Mismatches (wrong price, unknown or discontinued item) are sorted out by phone or email.

**c. Software and APIs.**

| Tool | Usable API? | How I know |
|---|---|---|
| Microsoft 365 / Outlook (input) | Yes | Microsoft Graph sends change notifications for new messages; shared mailboxes need the Mail.Read application permission. (Opened) |
| Gmail (input) | Yes, Gmail API | Not re-verified here |
| Business Central (output) | Yes | API v2.0: POST salesOrders and salesOrderLines. (Opened) |
| Sage 200 (output) | Yes | Sage developer portal lists a POST for SOP orders (Sage 200 Professional); OData style REST with OAuth 2.0. (Search result) |
| Exact Online (output) | Yes | Community PHP client covers SalesOrder, PurchaseOrder and PurchaseInvoice. (Opened, GitHub) |
| Odoo (output) | **Only on "Custom" plans** | Odoo docs: no external API on One App Free or Standard. (Opened, GitHub) |
| Unleashed (small firm output) | Yes | SalesOrders resource can list, view and create; JSON or XML; no partial updates. (Search result) |
| Xero | Yes, official SDKs | No full sales order module of its own. (Opened, GitHub) |
| SAP Business One, Access Dimensions, Pegasus Opera, weclapp, Xentral | Not verified | Check before you pitch |

That is 2 tools (mailbox and ERP) plus your approval screen. Teams or Slack alerts would be a third.

**What you build.** Mailbox webhook, then text extraction (PDF, Excel, email body), then an LLM that pulls out customer, lines, quantities, dates and PO number. Next **a matching layer**: a customer part number to item code table that learns from every human correction, plus price and stock checks through the ERP API. Then a draft order on an approval screen next to the original document. Nothing posts until a human clicks approve. Developer A builds the core in Spring Boot with Spring AI and PostgreSQL. Developer B builds the approval screen and mail connectors in TypeScript. The extractor, matcher and approval screen are the same code you build for the India proof; only the input connector and the ERP connector change.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** Medium: 50 to 300 orders a day. Keying a 10 to 30 line order takes about 4 to 10 minutes, more when part numbers need translating. Tech Ultra Solutions, which sells an Odoo module, claims order entry drops "from 8 to 15 minutes per PO to 1 to 2 minutes" (vendor claim). Example: 60 orders a day at 7 minutes is 7 hours a day, about one full time person. **Measure it:** export one month of the orders@ mailbox and time 20 orders being keyed.

**e. Who does it and what it costs (evidence of spend).**
* **UK salaries (Reed, search results):** average sales processor salary £28,921. Live ads: Worsley £16.24 an hour (about £31,500 a year), Stockport £24,000 to £30,000, North London £28,000 to £32,000 plus 20% bonus, senior sales order processor £35,000 to £40,000. Reed lists sales order processor jobs across London, Leeds and remote, so firms hire people for exactly this task.
* **Netherlands (Werkzoeken, search result):** orderverwerker average €2,545 gross a month, range €2,165 to €2,930, from 10,000+ salary entries.
* Employer costs (National Insurance, pension, holiday) add more on top. I could not open a source for the exact UK rates, so assume 15 to 25% extra and confirm.
* **Paid tools and services:** Hyperfox, Turian, Conexiom, Y Meadows and Zestcode all sell this, and Hyperfox raised €3.2M to do it. Companies are paying for the fix today.
* So one full time UK order clerk costs a firm about £2,410 a month in salary alone (£28,921 divided by 12), and a medium firm with 3 clerks spends over £7,000 a month on this job.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.** See the landscape table above. In short: BC Sales Order Agent is cheap for simple BC orders; Hyperfox and the Odoo modules cover Exact, Odoo and BC cheaply below about 16,000 lines a month; Logistify covers Unleashed; Zestcode covers some Sage 200 firms as a service. Your gap is segments A, C and (conditionally) B. EDI solves this only for large customers; the long tail still sends PDFs. E-invoicing laws do not cover purchase orders, so the pain stays.

**g. Realistic price.** Segment A: $3,000 setup, flat $1,000 a month up to 30,000 lines. Segments B and C: $3,000 to $4,000 setup, $600 to $1,000 a month including up to 1,500 order documents, then $50 per extra 100. See Part 3 for how these were checked.

### Europe workflow 2: Supplier order confirmation check and PO chasing (medium firms)

**a. Who has it, and size.** Manufacturers, wholesalers and importers that place many POs with many suppliers: engineering, food production, furniture, electrical and industrial distribution. Mainly **medium** (1 to 5 buyers). The owner is the purchasing manager or head buyer. Small firms rarely have enough POs.

**b. The steps today.** The buyer sends a PO from the ERP as a PDF by email. The supplier replies with an order confirmation that may confirm, change the price or quantity, split the delivery, or move the date. The buyer finds the PO, compares line by line, updates the expected receipt date and flags price changes. Unconfirmed or late POs get chased by email. Data ends up in the ERP PO lines. Price changes nobody noticed become invoice disputes later.

Microsoft describes this pain in its own documentation: "Purchasing personnel spend a large part of their day on manual tasks that are related to communicating with vendors, following up with them, and maintaining changes that are related to purchase orders," and this communication "is often unstructured and conducted via email, even for companies that implement electronic data interchange (EDI)."

**c. Software and APIs.** Mailbox (Graph or Gmail) and the ERP. In BC the purchaseOrderLine resource has an expectedReceiptDate field and supports PATCH (opened). Exact Online has PurchaseOrder entities (opened). Unleashed documents a Purchase Orders resource (search result). 2 tools plus your approval screen. Same test routes as workflow 1.

**What you build.** Classify supplier emails (confirmation, change, delay, other). Extract lines. Match to the open PO. Show a diff (old against new: price, quantity, date). The buyer approves, then you PATCH the PO line. On a schedule, draft chasers for POs unconfirmed after N days or overdue, and the buyer sends them. Price changes above a threshold always go to a human.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** 30 to 200 POs a week at a medium firm, 3 to 8 minutes per confirmation, plus chasing. Example: 100 confirmations a week at 5 minutes is about 8 hours a week, plus 2 to 4 hours of chasing. Count one month of supplier replies and time 20.

**e. Who does it and cost (evidence of spend).**
* Indeed UK: purchasing assistant average £25,869 a year. Reed: London £27,500, Leeds £30,250 (search results).
* UK job descriptions for purchasing administrators list "expediting order confirmations and agreeing delivery dates with suppliers", "using in-house systems to reschedule purchase orders" and "checking order confirmations to ensure all information is correct" (Indeed job description page and Glassdoor listings, search results). Firms hire for this task.
* Turian, Doxis and Tradecloud sell products for it, which is spend.
* The bigger cost is late material and missed price increases. Ask each client for one real example of each; that story sells better than hours.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. **Turian** processes supplier confirmations, handles partial deliveries and price unit conversions, and sends only critical deviations to people. Price on request, mid market. This is the closest rival.
2. **Doxis** compares confirmations to the PO line by line. It is an enterprise document platform.
3. **Tradecloud** moves confirmation onto a supplier portal. Suppliers must log in, which many small suppliers will not do.
4. **Microsoft Procurement Agent** is only in D365 Supply Chain Management, in preview, and does not yet support split deliveries or site changes. No BC version found.
5. **Your gap:** medium firms on BC, Sage 200 or Exact that want email only (no supplier portal), a flat fee, and no mid market sales cycle. It is a real gap but a narrow one, which is why this is the second workflow, sold to clients who already trust you.

**g. Realistic price.** As a second workflow: setup $2,000 to $3,000 (connector and approval screen are reused), upkeep $400 to $900 a month. As a first workflow: $3,000 to $4,000.

**h. Sources.** Microsoft Learn: Procurement Agent overview, supplier communications, follow up; BC update 29.0; BC purchaseOrderLine. Search results: Turian order confirmations and supplier communication pages; Doxis; Tradecloud; Indeed and Reed salaries; Indeed job description; Glassdoor listings.

### Europe workflow 3: Emailed order to Sage 50 sales order (UK small wholesalers, conditional)

**Verdict up front.** This is the only small firm European workflow I found where no cheap tool showed up in my searches. The evidence is thinner than for workflow 1, and the money is smaller. Treat it as a test: if 10 calls to UK small wholesalers on Sage 50 find 3 that key more than 20 emailed orders a day and use no order tool, build it. If not, drop it.

**a. Who has it, and size.** **Small** (5 to 49 staff) UK wholesalers and distributors (trade supplies, food and drink, packaging, spare parts) that run **Sage 50 Accounts** on a desktop and get orders from trade customers by email as PDF, Excel or typed lists. The owner decides. A sales administrator or office manager keys the orders. Sage 50 is common here: one search result says over 140,000 UK and Ireland customers (I could not pin this to one page, so treat it as rough), and a market data site says 51% of Sage 50 users have fewer than 50 employees. A Sage 50 guide says it suits small wholesalers "with proper stockrooms". Sales orders need the Professional version of Sage 50 Accounts (from memory; confirm on the call).

**b. The steps today.** An order email arrives. The admin opens the attachment, finds the customer account in Sage 50, looks up each product code (often from the customer's own description), types the lines into a Sage 50 sales order, and emails a confirmation. Data ends up in Sage 50. It is partly digital (email and Sage 50) with a manual middle.

**c. Software, API and build path.** Outlook or Gmail in (Graph or Gmail API), Sage 50 Accounts out. Sage 50 is a desktop program with no simple cloud API, which is exactly why the cloud products skip it. There are three ways in:
1. **Recommended: an import add-on the client buys.** WebXeL Order Importer imports orders and invoices into Sage 50 from files, and emails when an order fails its checks. Adept Tools also sells Sage 50 import tools; its order entry tool is £360 plus VAT with a 20% yearly renewal. Your system writes approved orders as a file in the add-on's format, and a small Windows job on the client's PC hands it to the add-on. This is the same pattern as your Tally connector.
2. **Hyperext**, a UK firm that sells a REST API for Sage 50 Accounts on a yearly subscription (price not public; sandbox £300 plus VAT for 3 months).
3. **Sage Data Objects (SDO)**, Sage's own COM library. The documentation needs Sage's paid developer programme, so skip it until you have revenue.

That is Outlook plus Sage 50 plus the import add-on: 3 tools, which is your limit. Do not add anything else.

**d. How often and how long (estimates to verify).** 15 to 60 orders a day at 3 to 8 minutes each. Example: 30 orders a day at 5 minutes is 2.5 hours a day, about a third of one person.

**e. Who does it and what it costs.** A sales or office administrator. Using Reed's UK average for sales processors (£28,921 a year, about £14.80 an hour at 37.5 hours a week), 2.5 hours a day for 21 days is about £780 a month of staff time. If you remove 60% of it, that is about £470 (about $620) a month of value. Evidence of spend: a Sage community thread titled "Sage 50 Accounts, Sales Order generation, Automation software" shows users asking for this, and several firms sell Sage 50 order import add-ons, so small firms already pay for part of the fix.

**f. Existing tools and the gap.**
1. WebXeL Order Importer and the Solutions for Accounting Sales Order Import Module import orders from **structured** sources (web shops, flat files, databases). They do not read a messy PDF or email.
2. Adept's order entry tool makes manual keying faster. It does not read documents.
3. Hyperext gives an API and builds custom projects, priced on quote. It is a building block, not an order reader.
4. OrderEase captures PDF and email orders into Sage 50, but its pages name the **Canadian** edition. Check whether it supports UK Sage 50 Accounts; if it does, this workflow is dead.
5. Sage 50 Accounts has added AI for **purchase** invoices, not customer orders.
6. **Your gap:** reading messy emailed orders into UK Sage 50 through a cheap import add-on, with an approval screen, for a flat monthly fee.

**g. Realistic price.** Setup $1,500 to $2,500. Upkeep $250 to $450 a month including up to 600 orders. At $350 a month (about £265) the client keeps about £205 a month of the £470 value, and setup pays back in about 6 to 9 months. **That is thin.** The real selling points for a small owner are orders confirmed the same hour, fewer keying errors, and the admin freed for other work. Do not quote above $450 a month to a small firm.

**h. Sources.** Search results: Sage community thread; Sage 50 market pages; WebXeL Order Importer; Solutions for Accounting import module; Adept Tools; Hyperext API and sandbox; OrderEase Sage 50 pages; Sage 50 developer and SDO pages; Reed salary.

### Practical rules that protect you

1. **n8n licence.** The n8n Sustainable Use License allows use "only for your own internal business purposes", and providing it to others only "free of charge for non-commercial purposes". Do not host one n8n instance and charge clients for workflows on it. Install n8n in the client's own account, or write the product in your own Spring Boot and Node code. Email license@n8n.io before you sell anything that runs on n8n.
2. **GDPR.** EU and UK client data processed by you in India is a transfer out of the EEA or UK. India is not on the European Commission's adequacy list (check the Commission list linked from the Microsoft guide in Sources). Sign a Data Processing Agreement with **Standard Contractual Clauses** (plus the UK addendum for UK clients). Keep data in an EU region such as AWS Frankfurt or Ireland. Keep a written list of subprocessors, including your LLM provider and, for India, your WhatsApp provider and speech to text provider.
3. **Budget under $50 a month.** One small EU cloud VM or container (about $10 to $20), one PostgreSQL database on the same VM, and pay as you go APIs: LLM (my estimate: 1 to 2 US cents per order document; log real token use from day one) and Sarvam speech to text (under ₹1 per voice note; free signup credits first). The WhatsApp provider fee (₹1,500 to ₹2,800 a month) is paid by the client, not you. Test systems are free (BC, Exact, Tally, 30 day Sage 50 add-on trials). The Hyperext sandbox (£300 for 3 months) and any Sage developer programme fee wait until a client pays.
4. **Desktop connectors.** Tally must be running on the client's machine with the HTTP server on. Build a small Windows service that pulls approved orders from your cloud and posts them to Tally locally, using JSON on TallyPrime 7.0 and XML otherwise. Reuse the same service for Sage 50 by swapping the "post to Tally" step for "hand file to the import add-on".
5. **Constraints.** Nothing here moves money or makes legal, medical or lending decisions. Make "human approves before anything posts or sends" a hard rule in the code, not a setting.
