ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The lead pick's price case rests on an invented "15 minutes per confirmation"
Where: Europe pick 1, section d: "A German case: buyers spent about 15 minutes per confirmation ... At 15 minutes each, 25 to 30 hours is about 100 to 120 confirmations a week." Also the section g table heading "(formula, 15 minutes each)" and "Your price guess, checked": "$400 needs ... about 65 to 95 confirmations a week."
Problem: The cited esw press release (Mittelstand Cafe / SCM / Elektropraktiker, all the same Perzeptron text) gives 25 to 30 hours a week and says OCC.AI cuts time per confirmation "from several minutes to a few seconds" ("von mehreren Minuten auf wenige Sekunden"). The only "15 minutes" in these search results is Perzeptron's offer of a 15 minute online demo. The same release says esw has only 14 suppliers connected, and they make up more than 80 percent of goods receipt volume. That makes esw a high volume EMS parts buyer, not a typical medium firm. Rerun the document's own formula at 5 minutes a confirmation and the lead product falls apart. At 100 a week: 8.3 hours a week, buyer saving about £470 a month, so the fee cap is about £157 ($207). Done by an assistant, it is about £110 ($145). The $400 line moves from 65 to 95 a week up to about 195 (buyer) or 275 (assistant) confirmations a week. The line "Six months of saving at the qualifying volumes is $7,500 or more" also fails. So the headline "$400 to $650 a month at about 100 a week" and the verdict "your lead European product" rest on a number that no cited source gives. The user would quote a fee that most medium firms' real volume cannot carry.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] "No agent for this" skips Business Central's own custom agent builder, which went GA in 2026
Where: Europe pick 1 table, Business Central row: "No agent for this. The Sales Order Agent 'can't create or work with' purchase documents ... Sell it? Yes, best target." Also Part 4 Pick 1: "It is not built in." Also filter rule 8: "This includes the ERP vendor's own AI agents."
Problem: The document checked only the two prebuilt agents. Business Central's Agent Designer, for building custom agents, reached general availability in May 2026. Update 28.1 (May to June 2026) lets customers deploy custom agents to production (search results for Tigunia's Wave 1 2026 guide and Microsoft Learn "Envision and design AI agents in Business Central"). So a BC customer, or more likely its existing BC partner, can now build an in product agent that reads a supplier confirmation email and checks it against the PO. That runs inside BC on Copilot Credits, just like the Sales Order Agent that the document uses to rule out Pick 2. By the document's own rule 8, this had to be checked and was not. Pick 1 names BC as the "best target", yet it never says why a buyer would pick two part time developers in India over their own BC partner using Microsoft's builder. The only risk it lists is that "Microsoft could add a purchase side agent", and the tool to make one already shipped. The negative claim also rests on "search result text" from one FAQ page about a different agent.
