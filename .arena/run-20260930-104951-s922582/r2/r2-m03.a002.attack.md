ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] Segment A of the lead pick ("high line volume, flat fee is cheaper than Hyperfox") fails its own arithmetic
Where: Short answer: "A distributor keying 15,000+ order lines a month pays roughly €1,000+ a month on Hyperfox's per line fee ... Your flat fee of $500 to $1,200 a month is cheaper." Segment table: "Above about 12,000 lines a month, per line pricing costs more than you." Part 3: upkeep "$600 to $1,200, tied to volume" plus setup "$3,000 to $4,000".
Problem: The break-even is stated two different ways (15,000 lines in the summary, 12,000 lines in the table), the fee floor changes ($500 vs $600), and no EUR/USD rate is given, so the comparison never includes the setup fee. Worked example at EUR/USD 1.15 using the report's own numbers. At 15,000 lines a month, Hyperfox costs €1,050 a month, or about $14,500 in year 1. Because the report ties upkeep to volume, a high volume buyer sits at the top of its range: $3,500 setup plus 12 × $1,100 comes to $16,700, which is MORE expensive. At 12,000 lines (€840, about $970 a month) the report's upkeep alone costs as much as or more than Hyperfox before setup is added. Even at 20,000 lines, Hyperfox year 1 is about $19,300 against $17,900 for the team, so the saving is about 7%, not a clear win. The report's LLM and review cost per line also rises with volume. The headline segment of Pick 1 is therefore not shown to exist. The report should compare 12 month total cost including setup, and set the threshold at the volume where the team is actually cheaper.

If Zotok is ₹6,000 a month with no setup, the team's year 1 price (₹15,000 to ₹30,000 setup plus ₹36,000 to ₹72,000) is at or above it, not near it. The stated gap is "their Busy or Marg setup". That contradicts the workflow itself, which is titled and built "to Tally" with only a Tally connector (Practical rule 4). No Busy or Marg integration path or API is given anywhere.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] The WhatsApp input has no working automation path
Where: India workflow 1 c: "In: WhatsApp Business app, or the WhatsApp Business API through a provider." Step b.1: "a voice note, a photo of a handwritten list ... mixed Hindi, Punjabi and English".
Problem: The WhatsApp Business app has no API to read incoming messages. An automation can only read orders if the distributor moves the number retailers already message onto the Cloud API through a provider, or through Meta's coexistence onboarding. That requires Meta business verification, a provider account, and per message costs. The report does not say which route to use, what it costs (it lists WhatsApp prices as "could not verify"), or whether it changes anything for the retailers (task rule 7). Voice notes in Punjabi or Hindi need a speech to text step. That step appears in neither "what you build" nor the budget. As written, a developer cannot build the input side without guessing, and this is the input for the one case study the whole European pitch depends on.

Where: "Days 1 to 3: visit 5 Ludhiana distributors ... Ask for a week of forwarded WhatsApp orders and time 20 entries." / "Days 4 to 5: Developer A builds the extractor and matcher ... Developer B builds the approval screen." / "Week 2: shadow mode."

Week 2 shadow mode needs a live WhatsApp feed, which Attack 3 shows is not defined. The user would start this plan and miss every date in it.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] The e-invoicing reason for rejecting invoice capture is wrong on the dates and does not cover the report's own target countries
Where: "Poland KSeF from 1 February 2026 for large taxpayers and 1 April 2026 for all" / "Those laws turn invoices into structured data, which kills PDF reading for invoices" / Walk away: "generic 'AI invoice processing' (Microsoft's Payables Agent and e-invoicing laws are killing it)".
Problem: KSeF from 1 April 2026 is not "for all". The smallest taxpayers were given until 1 January 2027. Germany's 2025 rule is a duty to receive e-invoices. Issuing them only becomes mandatory from 2027 and 2028, so German suppliers can still send PDFs in 2026. France's September 2026 phase makes only large and mid-size firms issue e-invoices; small firms follow in September 2027. More importantly, the report's first targets are the UK, the Netherlands and the Nordics (Assumption 1), and none of them has a B2B e-invoicing mandate in force in 2026. So "e-invoicing laws are killing it" does not justify rejecting invoice capture for UK or Dutch medium firms on Sage 200 or Exact. That segment is never actually evaluated.
