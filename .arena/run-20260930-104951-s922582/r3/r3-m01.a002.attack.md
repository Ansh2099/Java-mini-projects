ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The top pick's budget advantage over Sage 200 is false: Unleashed is not free to build and demo on
Where: Short answer point 3 ("its API works on a free trial account, and it gives you a free 21 day sandbox. So you can build and demo it inside your 50 USD budget"); ERP table, Unleashed row, "Cost for you to build on it: Free"; Final top pick 1, reason 3 ("You can build and demo it for free on a trial account and sandbox, so it fits your budget"); "Later, not now: Sage 200 ... the developer programme costs about 260 GBP a month".
Problem: Worked example with the solution's own timeline. The Unleashed free trial is 14 days with no card (Software Advice / Capterra listings, found via search: https://www.softwareadvice.com/distribution/unleashed-profile/). The sandbox is described by Unleashed as giving "full access to Unleashed features for 21 days, regardless of your current plan", which means it is spun off an existing account, and it "will expire and be deleted 21 days after being created". The solution itself says the Unleashed build takes 3 to 4 weeks of part time work, and then plans 100 prospect contacts and 10 discovery calls in parallel, which means a demo environment must exist for months. After day 14 to 21 the team needs a paid Unleashed plan, listed at about 366 to 729 USD a month (CostBench: https://costbench.com/software/inventory-management/unleashed-software/; Medium plan about 380 USD a month for 3 users per Software Advice). That is 7 to 14 times the 50 USD budget, and more than the 260 GBP (about 340 USD) Sage developer programme the solution rejects Sage 200 for. The main stated reason to lead with Unleashed instead of Sage 200 (the ERP where all the job ad evidence sits, see Attack 2) therefore collapses. The user following point 3 hits a paywall mid build or mid sales cycle.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] Proof of spend is for Sage users, but the recommended buyer is Unleashed users, and their number is unknown
Where: Europe workflow 1, section b and e ("taking orders via email and collating them through Sage", "validating customer orders in the system (ERP Sage)", "processing sales orders and maintaining accurate records on Sage", Totaljobs 754 jobs); Final top pick 1, reason 1 ("Clear proof of spend: hundreds of UK job ads for this role") and weakness ("I did not find how many UK firms run Unleashed").
Problem: The task requires "Evidence that businesses really have this problem and already spend money on it today" for every workflow, and asks for top picks "with reasons". Every quoted job ad names Sage. Not one cited ad, salary figure or case names an Unleashed user doing manual order entry. The only Unleashed specific evidence is Logistify's own marketing blog, a rival selling there. So the top pick is justified with evidence from a different buyer (Sage 200 firms, which the solution tells the user not to sell to), while the size of the actual target pool, UK and Irish Unleashed wholesalers with 30 or more emailed orders a day, is admitted to be unknown. The user cannot tell whether 100 such firms even exist before spending months on the build. The solution's own fallback ("if Unleashed prospects are too few after 100 contacts") shows the pick rests on a guess.

ATTACK 3 [MAJOR] Workflow 2's "sell only above 1,000 PO lines a month" threshold does not follow from its own numbers
Where: Europe workflow 2, section d ("200 to 2,000 PO lines a month. 3 to 10 minutes per confirmation"), and the price table row "Saves 50 to 60% of one administrator (about 1,000 or more PO lines a month)".
Problem: The section mixes units (lines versus confirmations), so the qualifying test the user is told to apply is wrong. Worked example: 1,000 PO lines a month at a modest 4 lines per PO is 250 confirmations. At 3 to 10 minutes each that is 12.5 to 42 hours a month. One administrator works about 160 to 170 hours a month, so the task is about 8% to 26% of the job before any automation discount, and Assumption 4 says "All payback math uses" a 70% capture rate, which gives about 5% to 18% saved. That lands in the solution's own "Fail. Do not sell" row (30%), not the 50 to 60% "Pass" row. The 50 to 60% figure is only reachable if every line is its own confirmation at the top of the time range. A user who qualifies a prospect by "1,000 lines a month" will sell a deal that fails the solution's own payback rule.

ATTACK 4 [MAJOR] Workflow 2's "no cheap tool" check misses a cheap Business Central app for order confirmations
Where: Europe workflow 2, rule check "E2 yes for medium firms on Business Central", and section f, which lists only Hypatos, Doxis, Leverage, Nordoon and Microsoft's enterprise Procurement Agent; Final top pick 3, reason 2 ("BC has no built in agent for it").
Problem: The task says "If it is already solved by a built in feature or a cheap tool, reject it." Abakion's Purchase Order Management app is sold on Microsoft AppSource for Business Central at about DKK 120 per user per month (about 16 EUR). It tracks every change on each purchase line, shows "what was actually agreed and what was only asked for but not yet confirmed", and has a web module where suppliers enter their confirmations straight into the purchase journal (https://appsource.microsoft.com/en-us/product/dynamics-365-business-central/PUBID.abakion%7CAID.purchase_order_management%7CPAPPID.88681068-5f10-444f-bd26-84c59b1dfa82?tab=Overview). It does not read PDFs with AI, but it covers the tracking, comparison and chasing steps that the solution sells, for the exact buyer (medium BC firms) it targets. The E2 verdict was reached without checking the BC app store, and the gap left (PDF reading only) is narrower than stated, which weakens pick 3 and its 3,000 to 4,000 USD price.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The India buyer profile and the whole case study bridge rest on an unsourced guess
Where: India workflow 1, section d ("Volume: 30 to 150 orders a day for the firms above (my estimate)"); "What to do this month" step 1 ("Find one Ludhiana B2B supplier on Tally whose business customers email 30 or more PDF or Excel POs a day in their own item codes"); Both regions section, point 1.
Problem: The recommended Indian client must have three traits at once: orders by email (not WhatsApp or phone), 30 or more a day, and in customers' own codes and many layouts. None of the sources shows that such Ludhiana firms exist in useful numbers. The "22 order processing jobs" count says nothing about the email channel, and the solution's own India sources (HublerX, Zenxos, Rupyz, Fast2SMS) all describe Indian B2B ordering as WhatsApp heavy, which is why workflow 1 pushes WhatsApp to version 2. Ludhiana auto and cycle part makers that supply large OEMs usually get POs through the buyer's portal or ERP, not as varied emailed PDFs. If the first prospect turns out to take orders on WhatsApp, the plan gives no alternative Indian workflow (the only one offered) and no case study for Europe. The user is told to go find a firm of a shape the report never showed exists.

ATTACK 7 [MINOR] Small factual and arithmetic errors that change what the user would quote
Where: (a) Europe workflow 1, f.6 ("UK and Irish office hours of 9:00 to 17:30 fall at about 13:30 to 23:00 in Ludhiana in winter"); (b) India price table, medium row, "₹66,000 to ₹98,000 ... Pass"; (c) Sources list, Indeed NL link.
Problem: (a) In winter the UK is on GMT (UTC+0), so 9:00 is 14:30 IST, not 13:30; 13:30 is the summer (BST) figure. (b) The solution's own rule is that year one price must be at most one third of saving, and for Europe it says "Each row was checked at its worst case". At the bottom salary the one third cap is ₹67,000, but the top price is ₹98,000, so the row fails its own rule at worst case while being labelled "Pass"; the hedge "use the top price only for firms at the top of the salary range" is not reflected in the verdict. (c) The Indeed NL link is given as ".../q-medewerker-orderverwerking-vacatures.html" inline but ".../q-medewerker-order-verwerking-vacatures.html" in the Sources list, so at least one cited link is not the page that was actually opened.
