ATTACK 1 [MAJOR] India pick fails its own "beats hiring a clerk" test at the volume its target segment actually has
Where: Part 1, "Does automation beat hiring one more clerk? Only at volume." table; row d "Estimate: 20 to 150 POs a day for a manufacturer"; 30 day plan step 1 "handle at least 20 POs a day"; short answer point 5 "Below about 80 orders a day, your fee roughly equals the clerk time you save".
Problem: The task says an Indian workflow "only makes sense where automation clearly beats simply hiring another person". By the solution's own table, 40 orders a day is "Break even at best" and 150 a day is only a "Small win" in year one. The only "Clear win" row is 300 orders a day, and the only 300 a day example in the document is the "Big Indian distributor" in the running cost section, a segment the solution tells the user to skip ("Not your target: FMCG distributors"). So for the segment it actually recommends (manufacturers with OEM POs, 20 to 150 a day, qualifier "at least 20"), its own numbers show no clear win. The inputs are also borrowed from the wrong segment: the 4.5 minutes per order and the ₹12,500 clerk come from a WhatsApp FMCG distributor story, not from OEM POs. The 20 to 150 OEM POs a day figure has no source, and it clashes with how OEM supply works: OEMs run on a blanket PO plus a delivery schedule that changes weekly, often pulled from the OEM's own supplier portal (Maruti's is e-Nagare), not dozens of fresh emailed PDFs a day ([oemup: ERP for auto component makers](https://www.oemup.app/blog/erp-for-auto-component-makers), [Terra Insight: Maruti supplier settlement](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/maruti-suzuki-supplier-settlement-process-india/)). A Ludhiana fastener firm with 30 staff and 3 OEM buyers fails the solution's own qualifier. The India pick rests on a volume assumption that its own math and its own target list contradict.

ATTACK 2 [MAJOR] The running cost budget leaves out the WhatsApp provider fee, so the "fits the budget" pilot breaks the 50 USD cap
Where: "Running costs, checked against your 50 USD a month budget", "Free Indian pilot capped at 100 orders a day ... Total about 14 to 32 USD. Fits."; the Coexistence section, which routes the number "through Meta or a provider" and cites 360dialog's docs.
Problem: Task constraint 6: "Our budget is under 50 USD a month until we have revenue." The cost lines count only Meta's per message fee, the model and the server. Coexistence onboarding runs through a Business Solution Provider or tech provider, and the provider the solution cites, 360dialog, charges about 49 EUR / 59 USD per number per month before any Meta fees ([360dialog pricing](https://360dialog.com/pricing), [chatmitra summary: EUR 49 to 249 per number](https://chatmitra.com/chatmitra-vs-360dialog/)). Indian BSPs also add a platform fee plus a 10 to 30% markup on messages ([MyOperator India 2026](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-india-2026)). Add 59 USD to the "14 to 32 USD" pilot and you get about 73 to 91 USD a month, which is over budget, and that is on a free pilot where the solution says "you pay it yourself". The solution never names a zero fee route (becoming a Meta tech provider yourself) or what it takes to get there, so the user cannot run the India pick within budget without guessing.

ATTACK 5 [MAJOR] The buyer ROI math overstates savings and can be negative for the solution's own sample client
Where: Europe workflow 1 row g "removing half the typing for one processor saves about £16,000 a year (about 21,000 USD) against about 9,800 to 17,600 USD in year one cost at list price"; sample quote "40 orders a day".
Problem: £16,000 is half of a fully loaded processor (£32,000 to £36,000), which assumes the whole job is typing. Row b of the same table says the processor also checks stock, checks prices and sends confirmations. Using the solution's own inputs (row d: 3 to 15 minutes per order; sweet spot "30 or more emailed orders a day"): 30 orders × 3 minutes = 1.5 hours a day, about 20% of one person. Half of that is about £3,400 a year (about 4,500 USD), against 9,800 to 17,600 USD year one at list price. Even the sample client at 40 orders × 5 minutes saves about £7,000 (about 9,300 USD), below the low end of list cost. The claim that list pricing of 5,000 to 8,000 USD pays for itself holds only at the top of the ranges. This matters because the main correction the solution makes to the user's price guess ("Raise it: 5,000 to 8,000 USD") rests on this math.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] Sage 200 API access is treated as free while the same kind of fee was used to reject Sage 50
Where: Running costs "Sage 200: ... I found no public fee for the keys. Confirm before your first quote." vs "Sage 50: writing sales orders needs the Sage Developer Programme, from about £3,100 plus VAT a year ... skip Sage 50".
Problem: Sage's own knowledge base states "Development with the Sage 200 API requires membership of the Sage 200 API Developer Programme" ([Sage KB: Sage 200 API FAQ](https://gb-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/viewsolution.jsp?solutionid=200427112454709)), and Sage runs a joint "Sage 50/200 UKI Developer Programme" with a subscription fee that was repriced in August 2025 ([Sage KB notice](https://ie-kb.sage.com/portal/app/portlets/results/view2.jsp?k2dockey=250725123504990)). Other sources say API keys need only portal registration ([Realnet](https://www.realnetintegrations.com/api-questions/how-to-get-my-sage-200-api-developer-subscription-keys/)), so the answer is unsettled. Either way, the lead European target may sit behind the same fee that got Sage 50 cut for budget reasons. That risk is left as a footnote instead of being stated as a go/no go check before any Sage 200 outreach (30 day plan step 3 starts outreach in parallel with step 4's key registration).
