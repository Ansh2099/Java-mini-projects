# Workflows Worth Selling: India and Europe (September 2026)

## The short answer

1. **Build one thing: "customer order arrives by email, and it becomes a checked sales order in the client's system."** It has the clearest proof of paid pain (staff paid to retype orders), a clean human approval step, and it is the same job in India and Europe. That gives you a real case study.
2. **This is a crowded, proven market, not an empty gap.** For many popular systems a built in feature or a cheap app already reads PDF orders. So you pick clients with one rule, the **ERP traffic light** (defined once, in Part 2, and used everywhere):
   * **Go:** UK firms on **Sage 200** (only after the API fee check in step 1 of the 30 day plan passes) and firms on **Unleashed**.
   * **Warm lead only, and only if their orders are messy:** Exact Online, Business Central, Cin7 Core.
   * **Stop:** Odoo, Katana, Fortnox, QuickBooks Online, Sage 50, Xero on its own.
3. **Volume decides the price, not hope.** A European firm needs about 30 emailed orders a day (about 2.5 hours of typing) before your intro price pays back, and about 60 a day (about 5 hours) before a full list price pays back.
4. **Your Europe price guess is mostly right.** 1,500 to 4,000 USD setup fits most small firms. Go to 5,000 to 8,000 USD only for firms with 60 or more orders a day, or after you have case studies. Upkeep of 400 to 800 USD a month is right; 1,500 a month is too high except for heavy volume.
5. **India is for proof, not profit.** On salary alone, automation clearly beats hiring a clerk only when order typing takes about two full people today. Few of your targets are that big. Sell Indian firms on fewer costly mistakes, or build the first one free for case study rights. Target firms whose customers email POs (exporters, suppliers to mid sized manufacturers, industrial distributors). Skip suppliers whose big buyers push schedules through a portal, and skip FMCG WhatsApp distributors.
6. **No second product yet.** Supplier order confirmation checking is already sold with reminders included, even by enterprise tools. Keep it as a possible add on for a happy first client, after two checks.
7. **Stay away from invoice entry, bank entry, GST reconciliation, payment reminders, supplier statement matching and freight quoting.** Cheap tools and built in features already do these.
8. **The hardest part is not the code. It is getting the first European client to trust a two person team in Ludhiana.** An Indian case study with real numbers, plus your Finland client, is your way in.

## How to read this

* **Confidence labels.** Every workflow has a confidence level: High, Medium or Low. It says how sure I am that businesses will pay you for it.
* **"Estimate"** means I worked the number out myself. It is not from a source. Check it on your first 5 sales calls.
* **Sources.** All links were found through web search on 30 September 2026. Many pages could be seen only as search results, not opened in full, so I treat vendor numbers as vendor claims. Blog case studies are single stories, not surveys.
* **Money.** I use about ₹88 per USD, about 1.33 USD per GBP and about 1.15 USD per EUR. Check the rate on the day you quote.

## Assumptions

1. "Europe" means the UK, the EU, Norway and Switzerland. The UK, the Netherlands, German speaking countries and the Nordics are the best targets because they buy software, answer email in English and pay on time.
2. "Build in a few weeks part time" means about 60 to 120 hours for the first client, and about half that for the second client with the same workflow.
3. You run the core pipeline in your own code (Java or Node). Use n8n only for glue, if at all. Check n8n's Sustainable Use License before you host it for clients.
4. "Order handling time" means the minutes a person spends per order on reading, finding the customer and items, checking price and typing. I assume your system removes about 60% of that in Europe and about 70% in India (estimate), because a person still reviews every order.

**Worked examples:**
* **Free Indian pilot, email only, capped at 100 orders a day:** model about 6 to 9 USD, server 5 to 20 USD. **Total about 11 to 29 USD. Fits.** No WhatsApp in the pilot.

**Three required rules:** (1) free pilots are email only and capped at 100 orders a day; (2) paying clients pay model, WhatsApp and any provider fees at cost, on top of your fee; (3) no Sage 200 outreach until the API fee check is done.

## Part 1: India

**The honest picture.** Indian owners will pay, but little, and only when the gain is obvious. An order entry clerk costs about ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 a month ([PW Skills](https://pwskills.com/blog/tally/tally-operator-salary-work-job-for-freshers)). Cheap Indian SaaS products already cover the most common jobs. **Bottom line: India has one well evidenced workflow for you. It beats hiring on salary only at large volume, so its real job is to give you a case study for Europe.**

**Two technical facts that shape every Indian build.**

1. **Tally is local.** TallyPrime talks to other software through XML over HTTP on port 9000 on the client's own computer. There is no cloud login ([AI Accountant guide](https://www.aiaccountant.com/blog/tally-api-integration-guide), [TallyHelp JSON integration](https://help.tallysolutions.com/tally-prime-integration-using-json-1/), [TallyPrime API Explorer](https://tallysolutions.com/tallyprime-api-explorer/)). It only answers while TallyPrime is open with the right company loaded. So build a small agent program for the client's Windows PC that:
   * pulls approved entries from your server into a local queue;
   * names the target company in every XML request, and checks first that Tally is running with that company loaded;
   * retries later if Tally is closed, and alerts the operator by email if an entry waits more than, say, 30 minutes;
   * logs every posted entry so nothing is posted twice.
   Best setup: run it on the PC that keeps Tally open all day, usually the accounts PC. BUSY has no default integration option and sends you to its channel partners ([BUSY FAQ](https://busy.in/faqs/third-party-services/integration/1/)), though third parties sell BUSY APIs ([BusyNotify](https://busynotify.in/solutions/custom-apis), [RootFi](https://www.rootfi.dev/integrations/busy-accounting)). Plan for Tally first. Treat BUSY as extra work.
2. **WhatsApp is an add on, not the core.** The WhatsApp Business app has no API for reading messages. Meta's **Coexistence** feature connects the client's existing number to the Cloud API through Embedded Signup, and the Business app keeps working on the same number. Messages sync both ways, and up to 180 days of one to one history is imported ([YCloud](https://www.ycloud.com/blog/whatsapp-business-app-coexistence-meta-update), [360dialog docs](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/phone-numbers/coexistence), [Meta for Developers](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/embedded-signup/onboarding-business-app-users/)). Only a provider or a Tech Provider can run that signup, so it costs money or setup time (see running costs). Limits: the app must be opened at least once every 13 days, and **group chats do not sync** ([InstantReply](https://www.instantreply.co/blog/whatsapp-coexistence-what-actually-syncs-2026)).

### India workflow 1: Customer purchase order by email becomes a sales order in Tally

**Confidence: Medium** that the pain is real and paid for (job listings, a published agency case). **Low** that you can charge enough to beat a clerk on salary alone, except for large firms.

**Does automation beat hiring one more clerk?** (Estimates. A clerk works about 2,400 hours a year. A ₹12,500 a month fresher costs about ₹62 an hour; a ₹2.7 lakh coordinator about ₹112 an hour. You remove about 70% of the handling time.)

| Order typing today | Example | Value saved per year (fresher to coordinator pay) | Your price, year one | Verdict on salary alone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 hours a day | 25 POs at 7 minutes | about ₹39,000 to ₹71,000 | ₹25,000 + 12 × ₹3,000 = ₹61,000 | **Loses or breaks even.** Case study only. |
| 8 hours a day (one full person) | 80 POs at 6 minutes | about ₹1 lakh to ₹1.9 lakh | ₹60,000 + 12 × ₹5,000 = ₹1.2 lakh | **Break even to small win.** Not "clearly" better than hiring. |
| 16 hours a day (two people) | 160 POs at 6 minutes | about ₹2.1 lakh to ₹3.8 lakh | ₹60,000 + 12 × ₹8,000 = ₹1.56 lakh | **Clear win.** |

**What this means, bluntly.** On salary alone, you clearly beat "hire one more clerk" only when order typing already takes about two full people. Most Ludhiana firms you can reach are smaller than that. So there are only two honest ways to sell in India:
1. **Errors and speed.** For firms selling to exporters' buyers or big dealers, one wrong rate or missed line can cost more than a month of the clerk's pay. Ask each prospect: "What was your last costly order mistake, and what did it cost?" If mistakes cost more than about ₹50,000 a year, the ₹61,000 year one price is easy to justify. If they cannot name one, do not expect them to pay.
2. **Free or at cost, for case study rights.** This is the main use of India for you. The 20 POs a day minimum in the 30 day plan is only there so 4 weeks of data (about 400 orders) is enough to publish. It is not a paid fit test.

Also, the owner rarely fires anyone. The saving shows up as "we grew without hiring a second clerk", so pick firms that are growing.

**Why it matters anyway:** this is the same job as Europe workflow 1. A case study that says "a Ludhiana exporter's emailed POs now go from inbox to Tally in 40 seconds with a human check, and typing errors fell from X to Y" sells directly to a UK wholesaler.

## Part 2: Europe (including the UK)

**The honest picture.** Europe pays well, and order entry is clearly paid for today. But it is not a secret. Tools, add ons and small agencies already sell it for most common ERPs. Microsoft Business Central now has a built in **Sales Order Agent** that reads customer emails and PDF purchase orders and creates quotes and orders ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/sales-order-agent)), and a **Payables Agent** for supplier invoices ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/dynamics365/business-central/payables-agent)).

### The ERP traffic light (the one rule for whom to sell to)

Use this table and nothing else to decide. Every other section of this report follows it.

| Light | ERPs | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Go** (cold outreach is fine) | **Sage 200** (UK), only after the API fee check passes. **Unleashed.** Any other ERP with a sales order API that is not listed here, after a 10 minute search for "[ERP] sales order automation" finds no cheap app. | Pitch if the prospect passes the volume test and does not already own the add on named in the map. |
| **Warm lead only** | **Exact Online** (at least five Dutch sellers already). **Business Central** (built in agent). **Cin7 Core** (built in add on). | Pitch only if you have an introduction **and** the mess test passes: at least a third of their orders arrive as Excel files or plain email text (or, for Business Central, as forwarded emails), which the built in tools handle badly. Otherwise stop. |
| **Stop** | Odoo, Katana, Fortnox, QuickBooks Online, Sage 50, Xero on its own. | Do not pitch. Cheap apps cover them, or the API is missing or too costly. |

**The one call qualifier (use every time, in this order):**
1. **Light:** Go, or Warm lead with an introduction. Stop means stop.
2. **Mess:** required for Warm lead firms (a third or more Excel or email text orders). For Go firms it is a plus, not a must.
3. **Volume:** ask "How many hours a day does your team spend keying orders from email?" Under 2.5 hours (about 30 orders): not a paying fit yet. 2.5 to 5 hours: intro price. Over 5 hours (about 60 orders): list price.
4. **No existing add on:** they do not already own Sicon, Continia, Cin7 IDR, Logistify or similar.

### Europe workflow 1: Customer purchase order by email becomes a checked sales order in the ERP

**Confidence: High** that the pain is real and paid for (many sellers, published prices, a named job role). **Low to Medium** that SMEs will pick you over the existing sellers without a case study.

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| a. Who has it | Wholesalers and distributors (food and drink, building, plumbing and electrical supplies, industrial parts, packaging). B2B manufacturers with repeat trade customers. **Size: both.** Sweet spot: 20 to 150 staff with 30 or more emailed orders a day (60 or more for list price). Small: the owner or office manager buys. Medium: the customer service or sales office manager owns it. |

## Part 4: My top picks for you

### Pick 1: Order to ERP (Europe workflow 1, proven first in India workflow 1)

* **Why:** It has the clearest proof of paid pain (a £27,000 a year role that spends much of its day keying orders), published prices you can undercut, and a clean human approval step. Agencies like Werkritme, Zestcode and Netum sell exactly this as a service, which proves your business model works. It fits both of you: Developer A builds matching and ERP posting in Spring Boot with Spring AI; Developer B builds email intake and the approval screen in TypeScript.
* **Who to sell to:** follow the ERP traffic light. Go: Sage 200 (after the fee check) and Unleashed. Warm lead with messy orders: Exact Online, Business Central, Cin7 Core. Stop: everything else listed. Then apply the volume test: 30+ orders a day for intro price, 60+ for list price.
* **Main risks:** (1) You are one seller among many, and more ERP vendors will add this feature, as Microsoft, Cin7 and Katana already have. Reduce it by owning the messy part: each customer's codes, mixed file types, price and delivery rules. (2) If Sage 200 API access carries a fee near the Sage 50 level, your best UK target waits until you have revenue, and Unleashed (a smaller user base) becomes your cold outreach target.
* **Chance it works:** about 15 to 30% chance of landing a paying European client within 6 months, if you get one strong Indian case study and use your Finland contact for introductions. This is a judgement, not a sourced number. I lowered it because the Sage 200 fee is unsettled and small firms can only carry intro prices. It is still the best odds of anything I checked, because nothing else has this much proof that buyers pay.
