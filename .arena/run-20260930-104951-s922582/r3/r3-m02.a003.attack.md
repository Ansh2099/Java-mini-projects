ATTACK 1 [FATAL] The 0.7 savings factor that the whole price verdict rests on is contradicted by the answer's own evidence
Where: Assumptions 6: "The 0.7 is because a person still reviews every draft, so you remove about 70% of the time ... Workist's own figure of 2.9 minutes saved per document at one client, against 8 to 12 minutes of keying, shows savings are partial." Also "How to read the numbers": "[Estimate] ... is never used as a gate."
Problem: The answer's own numbers give 2.9 / 12 = 24% to 2.9 / 8 = 36% saved, not 70%. The only data point it cites for the factor undercuts the factor by half. Rerun its own Europe table at 0.3: 4 hours of keying a day saves 3,250 x 0.5 x 0.3 = about 490 USD a month, so upkeep at a third to a half is 160 to 245 USD. That is below the 400 USD floor the answer says it "confirms". The 400 USD floor now needs about 7 to 10 hours of keying a day. The 1,500 USD ceiling needs about 25 to 37 hours a day, which is 3 to 5 full time people, not "two or more". India breaks the same way: 6 hours at ₹25,000 gives ₹5,600 saved and ₹1,900 to ₹2,800 upkeep, under its ₹5,000 minimum. The "a third to a half of the saving" rule is also untagged and unsourced. So the "walk away below 4 hours / 6 hours" gates are built on two estimates, which breaks the answer's own promise that no estimate is used as a gate. The task explicitly asks to "Confirm or correct" the 1,500 to 4,000 / 400 to 1,500 USD guess. The confirmation here does not follow from the evidence given, and the user would reject real prospects or overprice them based on it.

ATTACK 6 [MINOR] "At every volume, Hyperfox's line fee is about 40% to 65% of your upkeep" holds only for one assumed speed
Where: Part 4, "The Hyperfox check", and the last column of the Europe value table ("at the 8 min volume (5 lines per order [Estimate]...").
Problem: The fee is worked out only for the 8 minute column and an estimated 5 lines per order. Use the table's own 4 minute column: 4 hours equals 60 orders a day, x 5 lines x 21 days x EUR 0.07, which is about EUR 440 (about 485 USD), or 85% to 128% of the 380 to 570 USD upkeep. At 12 minutes it is about EUR 147, or 28% to 43%. So the ratio runs from under a third to more than the user's price, depending on inputs the answer admits it does not know. The line "Hyperfox quoted us less ... let them go" is therefore a guess. The user needs to be told to compute Hyperfox's fee from the prospect's measured lines per month, not from this fixed ratio.
